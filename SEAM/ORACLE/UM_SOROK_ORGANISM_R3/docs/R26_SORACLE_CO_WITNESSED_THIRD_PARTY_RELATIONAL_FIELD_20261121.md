## User-directed theoretical correction

A bounded household is a network of **different historically constituted people**, multiple activities, institutional conditions, asymmetric identifications, collective practices and sometimes contradictory social relationships. A person's model of a parent may change not because that parent addressed the person, but because they personally witnessed how that parent treated another. The family is not a sovereign agent with its own mind; the field is a historically persisting, materially constrained *relation of relations*. No scalar 'family mood' or rule 'infidelity -> disappointment' is legitimate.

Example: Nadia admired her father's work and interpreted her mother's complaints as ingratitude. A later act against the mother might call her own prior identification into question without erasing genuine affection for father. Her brother could independently interpret the same act differently, perhaps because of contrary loyalties, interests or earlier access. This construction is **not yet a cheating scene engine**, and neither default rule is a claim about actual people.
