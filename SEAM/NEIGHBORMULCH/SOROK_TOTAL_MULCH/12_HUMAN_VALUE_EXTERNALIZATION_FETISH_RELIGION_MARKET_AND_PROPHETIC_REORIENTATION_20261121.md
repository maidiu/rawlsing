## II. What the criticism forces us to correct

The previous assistant said it would distinguish Marx's account of commodity value from the wider human psychological investment in ideals/objects. Distinguishing economic forms is legitimate, **but it was offered as though the human externalization–alienation–fetishization movement were merely an analogy imported into psychology from commodity economics**. That missed the user's long-running claim: a broader, materially rooted Marxian operation appears already in critique of religious alienation, labor/product alienation and commodity fetishism. Theological authority, legal rights, money, market autonomy, collective progress-ideals and personal cherished objects can all be occasions for human powers becoming external forms, including forms that govern those who sustain them. The broad comparison is not a new reduction of everything to market exchange.

**Corrected conception, under discussion:**

1. **Human activity/valuation:** actual labor, sacrifice, care, collective practices, memory, recognition and imaginative activity create or transform things, institutions, obligations and concepts; they can be concretely bound to persons and their historical projects.
2. **Externalization and objectification:** those activities leave consequences and products outside a person's immediate consciousness/body (firewood, shelter, songs, rituals, representations, laws, religious concepts, institutions, shared language), which have their own material persistence and may preserve real human investments. Externalization is not automatically alienation or fetishism.
3. **Social objectivity:** as products circulate, they acquire powers conditioned by other people's recognitions, institutional rights, resource control, reproduction practices and coercion. The subjective author's idea of value and others' recognition may diverge; one person's labor cannot alone legislate all ownership rights.
4. **Alienation:** the human's own powers, social arrangements or products may appear outside their agency, strange to them, or as controlling objects; the genesis of the relation becomes obscured, inaccessible, disavowed or enforced.
5. **Fetishization/reification:** in the user's broad formulation, an externalized product/idea is treated as **inherently, independently and unanswerably authoritative/value-bearing**, to the point of subordinating people whose practical histories sustain it. This is a **qualitative social relation** with many degrees and domains, not the product merely being loved, valued or physically durable.
6. **Countermovement:** people may confront or reclaim the human conditions of the object/institution/God-concept (e.g. justice for the weakest; ordinary human needs above sacralized Sabbath form) and reform or overthrow the authority it has come to exercise. This is no guaranteed progress; humanizers can be appropriated or canonized into a subsequent autonomous command structure.
7. **Reproduction by class/power:** wealth, employers, clergy, states and other materially organized powers can use accreted symbolic legitimacy to discipline others and stabilize an asymmetrical extraction/obedience relation. It is not enough to correct a character's *idea* when the material institution still punishes refusal.

**Important historical/analytic qualification—not a refutation or silent rewrite:** Marx's commodity **value** in the critique of political economy is a specific historical-social form of abstract labor and exchange, and legal property rights to public-forest wood do not follow solely from physical labor. The user's firewood example establishes a persuasive **human-practical source of significance and a plausible moral claim** (someone's effort and life are objectified in the wood); whether taking it constitutes *legal theft* depends on a governed property/commons regime and jurisdiction. The novel generalization should never convert Marxian labor theory of value into an unhistorical rule that every labor-produced use-object contains a private property title. The shared structural family is historical human activity becoming objectified and potentially socially autonomous; **formal equivalence across religious alienation, labor-product alienation and commodity fetishism remains a philosophical reconstruction, not a proven identity of their causal machinery**.

## III. God, prophets, Sabbath: two registers cannot be collapsed

## VI. Two genuine philosophical objections to retain, not use as dismissive slogans

1. **Objectified value is not always universal market value, nor even positive private significance.** A child may deeply care for something without laboring to produce it; an exchange commodity can carry exchange value without its producer having a loving psychological relation to it. The philosophical operation generalizes *externalized human power*, not a single measurable “amount of life” in things.
2. **Not every sacred/categorical ideal is merely an alienation**. Ideas and institutions can be vehicles of collective coordination, reciprocity, emancipation and sustained memory. What matters is when a representation becomes materialized as authority that refuses accountability to people and reality. An institution's purportedly independent legitimacy has to be tested against actual practice and power; the divine's metaphysical reality remains undecidable by a fictional historical engine.
