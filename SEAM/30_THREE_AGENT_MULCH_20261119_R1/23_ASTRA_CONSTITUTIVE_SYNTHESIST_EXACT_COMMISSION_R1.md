## PART 4 — THE ESSENTIAL DIALECTICAL OPERATION: RELATION CHANGES WHAT A AND B ARE

Do not synthesize A and B as a paragraph containing both their names. Determine whether an *actual historically possible relation* exists, by what mediation, and which form of difference it constitutes. Explore at least the following **nonexclusive outcomes**, without assuming each is present in every case:

- **No new operative relation at this actor/time/grain**, despite both belonging to a materially continuing life. Distinguish true absence of acquired link from unknown or inaccessible link.
- **Mutual or asymmetric facilitation**, where one makes another exercisable or significant without becoming its motive.
- **Inhibition, displacement, competition, coexistence or material impossibility**, none of which necessarily makes a new integrated psychic thing.
- **Contradictory practical organization**, in which the same act serves incompatible projects or one learned value becomes the material of its own contestation.
- **New directed relation or changed susceptibility**, where earlier experience alters how an object, person, criticism or practical possibility can affect the actor.
- **Historical reorganization or emergent capacity**, where a new way of discriminating, anticipating, imagining, caring, refusing, practicing or interpreting becomes possible through actual activity and cannot be explained adequately as the sum of two previously fixed intensities.
- **Decay, extinction or discontinuity**, where previous organization ceases to function, without falsely asserting that its historical occurrence was erased.

For any claimed emergent organization D from A↔B, establish:

1. A and B's independent histories and prior forms.
2. The *specific* shared practical history, objective means and Others through which the new relation might form; not magical semantic juxtaposition.
3. What the new organization can do **that cannot be adequately explained by the previous available operations alone**.
4. What has changed in A, in B, in the relation, in the actor's reachable world, and what has not.
5. The actual conditions and time of acquisition; possible inhibition, decay, scope and later recruitment.
6. A counterhistory with A alone, B alone, the same external situation without joint formation, a similar-looking outward act from a different history, an independent material disruption, and NO_CHANGE.
7. Alternative explanations that deserve not to be discarded.
8. Its evidentiary grade: scientific, theoretical hypothesis, fictional plausible, or current implementation.

If the evidence only supports A and B as co-present, say so. If a historically specific D is defensible, use D in the next analysis as a **new revisable determination**, and then ask what happens when C encounters the transformed A–B–D organization. This is recursive **without** a preinstalled AB-fusion algorithm. An initially promising new object may be defeated by C. D may itself change A's meaning or the interpretation of its own ancestry. A real world response may affect another person differently. Revision propagates *backward in theory*, not backward in objective fictional event time.

**Do not confuse a theoretical contradiction with a causal mechanism.** A merely verbal contradiction between definitions may reveal a bad category rather than a generative social relation. Do not make every tension resolve through Hegelian sublation, Deleuzian differentiation, psychoanalytic defense or any other preferred vocabulary. Use each as a possible explanatory interlocutor at its justified scope.
