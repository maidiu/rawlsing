**The object.** The theory concerns a historically particular, biologically maturing, relationally formed person acting among independent people and material institutions. It concerns not a list of personality traits but the production, reproduction, operation, inhibition, reorganization and possible nonreorganization of the means by which that person can live. The psyche is contradictory, partly inaccessible even to its bearer, sometimes coherently settled, capable of acquiring ways of knowing, wanting, acting and understanding itself that did not previously exist. The person is neither an internally self-sufficient will nor a reflection dictated by external structure. What she can do is materially conditioned; what she imagines, learns and makes of it has its own particular, historically derived forms. No desirable adult endpoint, trauma arc, emancipatory destiny, romantic closure or universally valid motive menu is presupposed.

**The governing explanatory movement.** Previously acquired bodily and psychological organizations condition what is operative now. The person's actual activity, through available means and alongside independent Others, alters or fails to alter material circumstances. Different participants encounter different consequences, interpret or misinterpret them, and carry distinct afterlives into further life. This movement is not a sequence of compulsory ticks. A life can continue in routine, overlapping activities without a salient event; a new end can arise before a visitor or narrator supplies a prompt; no durable psychic change need follow even a consequential occurrence. Recruitment is the present historical efficacy of acquisition; genesis is the possible formation of later efficacy through present lived activity. Both take place within independent economic, social, ecological and institutional reality.

**What counts as a successful theory.** It must explain more than a psychologically persuasive description. It must distinguish bodily condition from a need's historically found object; objective opportunity from recognized or imagined possibility; action from intention; apparent self-story from practical organization; actual event from memory; inward formation from public expression; shared relation from a shared psyche; structure from the actor's reason; the same source distribution from an individual's unknown history. It must preserve negative cases: ability without opportunity, pain without learning, love without truth, closeness without access, ritual without belief, speech without self-knowledge, practice without new motive, contradiction without dialectical reconciliation, and a consequential world event without the developmental arc the analyst expected. The stronger theory must *change the explanatory relation among these* rather than simply mention them all.

**The epistemic status of the manuscript.** Leontiev, Lahire, Sève, Dreier, Luria, Holzkamp and Archer provide partly complementary and partly competing theories. The inherited PFF, SDTF, CAN/ADP, V3, PRE-A0 and SOROK source work supplies a historically layered functional architecture and experiments of varied authority. None is transmuted into a general empirically established law by literary integration. Scientific and materialist psychology can identify mechanisms and conditional regularities while remaining unable to reconstruct the uniquely actual motive of an arbitrary unobserved human respondent. Particular history is not a black box filled by an LLM; neither is it an extra metaphysical why in need of a universal selector.

## I. A person is not a latent template awaiting events

### 1. The living unit: a history that conducts several activities at once

To understand Nadia on an ordinary morning, we cannot begin by asking which preset psychological trait a stimulus activates. Before anybody enters the room, she is conducting a life. Her body bears yesterday's sleep, a learned gait, a sensory repertoire, present discomfort, capacities acquired over years and limitations that may have nothing to do with her current concerns. She has work to do, promises to keep, people to whom she is accountable, desires that do not coincide, and possible futures whose meaning may be ambiguous even to her. A familiar object can be passed without notice; a distant commitment can organize attention while she speaks politely about something else. This is activity, not merely a bag of event responses.

A life does not have one active project at a time. Driving while rehearsing a conversation, completing a task while resenting a workplace hierarchy, caring for a child while imagining the quiet of an empty house, or attending to a conversation while monitoring the body for discomfort are not serial personality switches. Different activities possess different objects, means, histories, obligations, feasible time horizons, embodied rhythms and personal senses. Their present relation may be supportive, inhibiting, contradictory, mutually indifferent or entirely unrecognized. An outwardly competent action can proceed through a practiced operation while another activity becomes emotionally dominant; the reverse is equally possible. We must explain which differentially acquired organizations are implicated, not announce that their numeric weights have been added.

The supposed hidden central controller is also not a free-standing soul of coordination. The person can acquire methods of self-direction: waiting, comparing alternatives, giving oneself a reminder, silently rehearsing a phrase, learning to endure uncertainty, avoiding an image, taking another's perspective or exposing one's reason to questioning. These methods themselves have histories, material affordances and cultural forms. Some people use extended verbal internal discussion; others rely more upon bodily routine, imagery, practice, social consultation or forms of attention not describable as an adult deliberative monologue. The absence of a particular reflective practice does not make an infant, disabled adult, illiterate worker or other culturally situated person less psychological.

This means that an actual present response is a *historically situated production*. It can be stable, ordinary, surprising, improvised, half willed, conflicted, misdirected or unremarkable. It need not transparently express the deepest motive. It need not produce a new trait. The actor may subsequently notice what they just did and reconsider it, or never do so. What the analyst calls a moment is only one cut through continuing relations.

### 2. The independent world and the nonidentity of its determinations

This person inhabits an objective material world. A locked workshop, an unpaid rent bill, an exhausted supply of medicine, the withdrawal of state support, the departure of a friend, a broken instrument and the existence of a library are not psychological events just because they become meaningful to someone. They change what is available and costly even where the person fails to perceive the change. An institution can enforce a consequence by law or wage relation while its subject offers a sincere but false description of why an action is impossible. Another person's decision can alter the situation without being motivated by the focal person at all.

Equally, the objective situation does not mechanically provide an interpretation. The same classroom can distribute literacy, humiliation, curiosity, mutual help and a fear of failure through materially different opportunities and actual participations. Two siblings can inhabit one household without inhabiting one history; a third person's labor can enable care while remaining largely unknown to the recipient. Even seemingly equal resources do not erase differences in bodily condition, obligations, acquired means or the consequences of particular encounters. Historical materialism here names the necessity of explaining the means, powers, institutions and reproduced conditions of life, not a license to derive an individual's precise fantasy from a class label.

The causal status of a condition must therefore be identified separately from its representation. What an actor can do, what she knows she can do, what she believes she can do, what she would attempt, what the world would permit, what would result, and what she might later make of that result are not seven verbal views of one hidden score. The relation among them is historically formed and transformable. A physical disability is not exhausted by a social prohibition; social exclusions are not rendered bodily inevitabilities by being attached to a body. An effective power can be misunderstood yet effective. A false hope can motivate an actual attempt. An unexpected result can establish an opportunity that no one anticipated.

### 3. Contingency and particularity answer the wrongly posed selection problem

Why did this person learn to want a particular object? The meaningful answer reconstructs a situated genealogy: a bodily and material situation, an acquired way of recognizing an affordance, a remembered or unremembered practice, an encounter with someone else's activity, an anticipated consequence, a material possibility or impossibility, and whatever subsequent participation made the object worth returning to—or prevented that transformation. Such explanations may be incomplete; causal roles may be disputed. But a demand for a single deeper law selecting every individual's exact destiny from every possible biography mistakes a contingent historical world for a system with a guaranteed abstract solution.

The right distinctions are genealogical explanation, contrastive explanation and distributional explanation. The first asks how this actual end became possible and operative for this historically particular actor. The second compares histories to determine what changed the outcome. The third studies which processes recur under relevantly comparable material and social conditions, and with what variability. These are compatible with contingency. They do not require that two people ever occupy the same total developmental state or encounter identical Others. Whether the physical world is ultimately deterministic or partly irreducibly stochastic is not decided by this theory, and no imaginary god-resolution camera could substitute for a discriminating causal account.

Particularity is not an alibi for psychologizing by fiat. “Her unique history did it” explains nothing until that history has identifiable causal mediation. Nor may a model fill in whatever childhood event seems convenient after it observes a preferred adult outcome. The open future of Nadia differs fundamentally from a retrospectively constructed adult hypothesis. The latter is constrained by empirical evidence and by any established fictional facts; the former must arise through genuinely ensuing events and activity. In neither case does a plausible narrative become proven knowledge of a real survey respondent's hidden life.

### 4. Unity is continuity, not harmony

A person's earlier practical learning can transform what another acquired relation does, and two contradictory relations can become a new way of acting. But this is only one possible kind of development. The same person can sustain an ethical commitment in one setting while exploiting someone in another; a childhood affection can remain strong without being reconciled to later contempt; a capacity may never be exercised; two histories may coexist without ever interacting. A later encounter may reinterpret a past event without changing what happened. An inner conflict may be painful and sterile. A person may be unreflectively at ease. Neither a jaggedly conflicted life nor a coherently ordinary one is more human by definition.

The unity that matters is that these parts belong to one actually continuous, bodily and historically located person who must conduct this life through time. That person cannot be composed from a universal profile on every occasion without losing the historical causality of acquired practices. Nor can the model consolidate every contradictory current into one superior self-concept. It must carry what remains apart, what has become related, and what is still unknown, each with its own kind of evidence. This is why the theory cannot be completed by deciding what Nadia ought to become.

**First adversarial contrast.** Imagine two adults subjected to comparable work demands and equally able to describe their exploitation. One gradually takes up collective action; another narrows attention toward keeping the household intact; a third withdraws from public life. The theory must identify the unequal lived histories, relationships, bodily conditions, resources, opportunities and practical premises through which each course became available, not infer three inborn moral types from a shared class position. And it must preserve a fourth case in which the person remains substantially as before. If all four are explained by attaching the same vague word “contingency,” the explanation has failed.

## II. A body becomes psychological through activity, care and cultural means

### 1. Need is real before it has an acquired object

An organism begins with bodily requirements and capacities, not with a finished inventory of what these requirements will mean. Hunger does not arrive knowing which food, sharing practice, ritual, prohibition or relation to a feeder will become its object. Vulnerability, fatigue, pain, arousal, startle, sensory pleasure, movement and regulation are bodily realities before the child can describe them. They are also encountered in already historical ecologies. Who provides relief, with what rhythm, in what dwelling, through whose labor, under which constraints, and with what repeated gestures and signs can transform the child's anticipation and way of acting.

The distinction between organismic need and the historically objectified motive is indispensable. A child may reach for a familiar person because that person's actual practices have become associated with safety, not because nature installed that particular person as an abstract attachment endpoint. The caregiver may be anxious, loving, obligated, resentful, exhausted or distracted; the child is not born knowing those reasons. The adult's achieved activity and the child's reception form separate histories. Care can be nourishing without being equally experienced by both; repeated care can establish tacit reliance without explicit autobiographical memory or verbal knowledge.

There is no reason to require every need to become a conscious desire or every strong feeling to signal what is most important to a person. Immediate physiological relief can be intensely felt without organizing a life. A meaning-forming motive can persist through unpleasant acts and even suppress a gratifying impulse. An object can be materially available but unrecognized, recognized but unwanted, wanted but infeasible, pursued without lasting significance, or made valuable only after doing something with it. Body, object, personal sense, affect, incentive and durable purpose are not interchangeable variables.

### Conation, relation, recognition, power and pressure are not synonyms

Conation names life already directed in activity—reaching, resting, delighting, seeking, exploring, refusing—rather than a pressure that appears only when a stimulus collides with a demand. The person's relation to another's presence and flourishing can become part of the object of activity without reducing the Other to a source of private reward. Recognition is not simply approval: being publicly witnessed as competent, deviant, disobedient or unreliable may make a person socially real under a category that damages them. Power concerns the actual ability to set materially consequential terms of recognition, access, legitimacy, resource distribution, sanction and action. Pressure arises when ongoing activity meets limitation, conflicting claims, anticipation of loss or coercive demands; flow is what the person can actually do through acquired bodily, symbolic, relational and practical means.

A child's identical bodily performance can have different consequences depending on who witnessed it and what authority their category can exercise. Learning to tie shoes is not the same thing as being praised by Father, being declared capable by a school or becoming confident in private. Fix the motor act and outward response, change the witness's institutional power: the acquired practical skill might remain stable while available future activities and self-understanding diverge. Conversely, hold the sanction constant and vary the child's prior ways of seeking approval. Neither pressure nor recognition can be reduced to a common achievement score.

A bodily state also has multiple genuine dimensions. Hunger, illness, sleep deprivation, pain, maturation, sensory capacity, bodily adaptation and long biological processes are not one “vitality” percentage whose defaults or automatic clipping establish psychological meaning. Nor does a need label supply a fixed infant striving vector. A persistent mediator, a tension and a practiced operation can be historically related without being interchangeable durable mental modules. A sparse expectation that an action is possible, forbidden or hopeless differs from an actually acquired object of striving.

### 2. The means of knowing are acquired, not one universal menu

Development changes not only the contents of perception but the forms of perception and action available. A child learns to handle a spoon through particular feeding practices, to make an unfamiliar sound through repeated coordination with another body, to follow a route through embodied familiarity, to recognize a tool through activity. Language can make absent events jointly discussable, but languages, signed practices, writing, ritual, stories, work apprenticeship and spatial organization do not all form the same mental operations with differing efficiency. What counts as a useful category, a reason, a memory or a possible self is itself historically variable.

A literate schoolchild may learn by explicit verbal analysis; a person apprenticed through observation and repeated coactivity may develop fine practical discrimination without an accompanying school-style explanation. A native signer does not possess a lesser representation because their language is embodied differently. A child whose environment lacks accessible language opportunities may be deprived of particular cultural means without being an empty organism. Disability can alter access to a practice; social organization can magnify or mitigate that alteration. To assume one standard adult internal conversation and measure everyone else's distance from it would reproduce the hierarchy the theory is supposed to explain materially.

The theory consequently needs developmental *kinds* rather than an all-purpose scale of cognitive sophistication. An acquired object permanence practice, an analogical use of a tool, an auditory anticipation, a public rule, an internalized evaluative phrase and a practiced mutual rhythmic adjustment do not have one identical form just because they can all affect later activity. Their traces may be bodily, linguistic, practical, symbolic, imagistic or inseparably combined. We should not infer their existence from a narrator's use of an evocative word; we should know what history and performance made the claim plausible.

### 3. Maturation and sociogenesis alter each other without becoming synonyms

Biological maturation establishes conditions and limits; it does not single-handedly produce the social organization that will be developed. A capacity to move and a socially available practice of moving are different, just as the ability to make vocal sound is not a language system. Some processes depend on the changing body; others require sustained access to other people, objects, practical demonstrations or symbolic means. Maturation may open a new possibility while the material world keeps it unavailable. Conversely a social practice may reorganize attention and control within existing bodily limits. Developmental explanations need to specify which relation is doing what.

Nadia's early development cannot be written backwards from a final adult profile. She might become musically attentive through a caregiver's singing, develop a different kind of auditory interest while repairing an instrument, or take little interest despite repeated lessons. At one age she may enjoy the sound, later anticipate a turn in shared song, later repeat a pattern alone, later invent a variant, later reinterpret its association with her mother. None of these steps is identical with a durable musical identity. The mother may have provided social access without intending a future artist; the jointly produced sound may be genuinely new for both. The child may retain the learned capacity while distancing herself from the relationship that supplied it.

**Second adversarial contrast.** Two children have access to similar songs and caring adults. One becomes sensitive to rhythm but refuses public performance. The other enjoys performance without seeking solitary musical practice. Both can remain perfectly ordinary adults; neither requires a hidden lost vocation. A third child becomes a different musician by connecting a sound to a local work practice, an unexpected friendship or an available device. The important differences are accessible forms of practice, personally felt result, material possibility, acquired self-direction and time. The theory cannot infer an artist's essence from exposure, or guarantee that all apparently different trajectories hide the same latent potential.

### 4. Social reproduction precedes and exceeds the child's understanding

A meal may be made possible by wages earned elsewhere, care work performed without pay, property relations governing who owns the kitchen, labor incorporated into appliances and food, transport systems, ecological production and local forms of obligation. The child ordinarily does not experience those entire conditions as a concept. The conditions nevertheless shape the regularity, cost, exhaustion, availability and personal form of feeding. A parent may experience care as freely chosen and yet be financially compelled to organize it in a particular way. Another may love the child and resent the distribution of work. These objective and subjective facts may coexist without cancelling one another.

This is why the relationship between historical materialism and developmental psychology cannot be reduced to “external social causes plus internal interpretation.” Material arrangements produce the means of activity, shape relations among the people who perform it, distribute the time for attention and learning, and make some consequences durable across contexts. The child actively appropriates, ignores, misrecognizes or transforms what becomes available, but does not choose the conditions under which appropriation is possible. Similarly, adult knowledge of social structure does not instantly undo the practical and affective history of living under it.

**Third adversarial contrast.** One child learns competence by helping a parent perform necessary work; another acquires the same competence through humiliating discipline; a third has the capacity but is not allowed to exercise it. The model must distinguish the usable skill from acquired self-understanding, the caregiver's intent from the child's history, and both from the material division of labor that made the practice necessary. Predicting the same psyche because the displayed “skill acquired” flag matches would violate the explanatory object.

## III. The possible is materially produced: power, capacities and available time

### 1. Practical ability is a relation, not a property of the solitary individual

A person does not own practical ability in the way a box contains an item. An acquired capability exists in a bodily and social history, but its exercise needs materials, time, permission, opportunity and frequently another person's cooperation. A woman may know how to repair machinery while having no access to the necessary tools; a musician may retain an entire repertoire while working two shifts; a parent may know how to comfort a child and still be physically absent because paid work determines the hours. These are not contradictory descriptions of one skill. They distinguish a developed capacity, an actual opportunity and the historically organized use of time.

The same distinction is necessary for apparent liberty. An unemployed person may be formally free to apply for jobs while lacking transportation, documents, child care or a safe route. A tenant may be able to imagine refusing a landlord's demand without possessing effective protection from eviction. A person can have a completely coherent wish and lack the means to undertake it. Others may control the keys, the money, the institutional clock, the legal category, the machinery and the authority to interpret a request. That control is effective whether or not the person has a correct theory of it. No amount of simulated self-confidence can alter an actual locked door.

Conversely, a materially effective act can arise without a fully conscious account of its social function. An employee might teach a new colleague a method, unintentionally undermine a hierarchy of workplace knowledge, or reproduce a humiliating protocol while sincerely thinking they are merely helping. Such consequences are not reducible to the employee's inner meaning. But they also do not automatically produce a new political commitment. World effects and personal uptake have different conditions.

### 2. The social organization of time constitutes the biography

An hour is not a neutral unit of psychological learning. One hour of recovery after night work, of involuntary waiting, of affectionate play, of domestic labor, of attentive apprenticeship, of terror or of private solitary practice does not produce the same possible development. Nor are these activities available equally. Ownership, employment, debt, caregiving arrangements, disability accommodations, classed expectations, public infrastructure and the independent schedules of Others allocate the times in which capacities can be exercised or suppressed. Sève's relation between acts, capacities and the social use of life-time is a serious proposal about the material constitution of personality, not a measured formula for converting hours into traits.

A person can develop considerable aptitude while being unable to make it socially consequential. Another can inherit institutional space to practice an unremarkable ability until it becomes recognized as talent. A person may spend an entire life refining a survival practice that brings little pleasure; another may repeatedly seek a pleasurable practice without becoming skilled at it. The model must register whether a possibility is bodily available, materially afforded, socially permitted, knowingly understood, actually exercised and sustained. It must never treat the existence of a capability as proof of the institutionally effective right to use it.

The time spent reproducing life cannot be called psychologically empty just because it is unspectacular. Cleaning a home, fetching water, preparing a meal, waiting for a bus, recovering from a night shift, keeping an appointment or ensuring that somebody else can sleep are real activities with costs, anticipations, social meanings and possible acquired traces. Sometimes they deepen familiarity, resilience, resentment, skill or attachment; sometimes they produce mere exhaustion or no meaningful new organization. A simulation that grants a large developmental dividend to every repeated task would be as false as one that treats routine as a blank screen.

### 3. Objectivity must resist the actor's available interpretation

Suppose Nadia loses access to a room because the property owner changes the locks. She may believe a friend betrayed her, suspect punishment for an argument, or think her own incompetence is to blame. The physical loss remains real; her premises may be wrong. If she acts against the friend, that act has its own independent material and relational consequences. Later discovering the property relation may change her understanding without undoing the harm of the accusation. This is one actual history with several different causal descriptions—legal, spatial, practical, affective, interpretive—and the model cannot choose the most emotionally satisfying one as the objective truth.

The converse also matters. A person may correctly understand a structural harm while remaining emotionally attached to the institution or person implicated. A worker can accurately identify exploitation and still value the craft, the coworkers, the security or the routine the employer makes accessible. A child can recognize abusive discipline and preserve affection for the teacher who supplied a powerful way of solving problems. A mother may believe a domestic arrangement is fair while carrying most of the reproductive labor. The structural analysis does not substitute for a theory of the actor's practical premises, just as subjective sincerity cannot negate the structure.

Holzkamp's actor-oriented vocabulary and the work of Leontiev, Lahire and Archer illuminate different aspects of this field: objective practical possibility, situated reasons, learned dispositions and the reflexive relation to concern. None should be silently collapsed into an invariant “belief about constraints.” The same actor may act from a reason she can articulate and a historical practice she cannot, under a restriction she misunderstands, in pursuit of a purpose that the world defeats. This causal complexity is not a computational luxury; it is what prevents a materially impossible act from succeeding merely because a narrator describes a sufficiently forceful intention.

### 4. Power is not the only process, but it remains independently effective

Materialist gravity does not mean every human event is a disguised class struggle. A child can play for pleasure, a couple can enjoy intimacy, an elder can attend to a bird, and a worker can become fascinated with the sound of a tool. Such experiences are not less real because they are not politically legible. Yet the time, safety, bodily access, objects and relations that make them possible belong to historically organized conditions. A person can invent a genuinely new purpose only within possibilities encountered through life, imagination and material means; those possibilities are themselves unequally distributed.

Power also operates through classification. Who can name another person mentally unfit, undeserving, beautiful, dangerous, respectable, employable or belonging? Who can attach a sanction or reward to the classification? Who gets heard as a reliable witness? These materially supported categories can become actor-owned ways of seeing, including when they are objectively false or injurious. A person may later use the same learned category against its source, or reproduce its violence in another relation. The same institution can produce competence and alienation without the psyche being obliged to resolve either into a single verdict.

Our central causal test is therefore not whether a biography contains politically recognizable terms. It is whether changing a real condition would change the actor's available actions and perhaps later development, while leaving room for distinct interpretation and null psychological uptake. If removing a tool, a right or a day's available time makes no difference to what the fictional person can materially accomplish, the world is scenery. If it automatically converts their consciousness into the analyst's preferred political conclusion, the psyche is scenery.

**Counterhistory: the four capacities.** Four adults know how to produce the same object. One has protected time and workshop access and develops a new practice. Another has the resources but no interest and does nothing. A third longs to work but cannot secure the means. A fourth is compelled to practice for an employer and becomes adept while resenting the work. Their capacities may look equivalent on a skills table. Their actual lives cannot be generated by the same “skill leads to motive” transition.

**Counterhistory: the structure without recognition.** Two tenants face an identical eviction notice. One understands the legal basis and organizes a response; another misreads it as a personal insult and damages a friendship; a third cannot respond because of illness; a fourth obtains an unforeseen remedy through someone else's independently initiated help. A theory that reduces them to different “agency” levels loses both the law and the history through which possible actions were arranged.

## IV. From possible object to acquired motive: historical novelty without a selector oracle

### 1. A new possibility has stages that must not be collapsed

There is a difference between coming across an object, attending to it, noticing what it might do, imagining using it differently, finding that use attractive, attempting it, receiving its consequences, repeating it, and developing a motive capable of reorganizing other activities. A full account of novelty must allow these movements to stop, branch, reverse, conceal themselves or occur in different historical orders. A new motive is not guaranteed merely because the model can phrase a compelling hypothetical desire. The possibility of an end is itself historically produced, but it does not arrive with proof that the person will care about it.

A young Nadia may watch a parent coordinate a rhythm. She can move in time before she has a musical concept, and enjoy a repeated sound before she seeks to make it again. At another age she might hear an unfamiliar interval and imagine what would happen if she altered an instrument. The imagined modification might never be attempted. She could be compelled to practice and acquire skill without wishing to continue; she could later find a wholly different social purpose for the skill. Each possibility has a materially different genealogy. When one new motive becomes durable, its durability is a fact about repeated, meaningful and feasible participation under this history—not an essence waiting in a hidden trait.

Leontiev's distinction among activity, action, object and motive bears on the transition whereby something initially done as a means can later become an end. But this does not authorize a universal frequency or direction of transformation. An act may repeatedly remain only an instrument. A task's unexpected result may attract attention without generating motivation. A personally significant end may be pursued through a hated action. The actor's body may provide immediate pleasure while the significance of the practice lies elsewhere. These differences have to be kept alive in the generated causal process.

### 2. Anticipation grows from capacities already acquired

The organism need not wait for a new external event to begin a new course. A person can attend to a memory, silently rehearse a possible conversation, mentally try an unfamiliar use of a familiar object, draw an analogy between two relations, or seek a particular encounter without a visitor-triggered prompt. But self-generated activity is not uncaused. It becomes possible through a previously acquired repertoire of perception, action, language, imagination, emotional association and socially available futures. A person unfamiliar with a practice cannot invent its entire operative cultural world by mere narrator fiat; they can conjecture, imitate, recombine, misapply or discover according to their actual means.

Imagining another possibility is different from performing it. It can influence later attention without creating an objective external event. A person who privately rehearses apologizing has not apologized. An imagined conversation might change their anticipated feelings, or not; it does not create a new shared history for the person who has not heard it. Similarly, a child's play can create genuine new capacities and interests without being recorded as a successful adult project. A simulated history must preserve these differences, including their downstream asymmetry of evidence.

An actor may also learn a way of organizing their own attention through social practice. A teacher's repeated demand to “check your work” can become a useful self-monitoring method, an anxious ritual, an empty repetition, a basis for new creative reflection or a source of inhibited initiative. The general transformation is not determined by the phrase. It depends on how the actor used the practice, what was materially possible, which other acquired methods were available and what consequences followed. This is the genesis of a psychological method, not merely a change in the importance of existing content.

### 3. Analogy is a historical wager, not a global semantic equivalence

When Nadia later treats one situation as relevantly like another, the resemblance must be a learned practical achievement. An earlier person might have supplied help with a particular task; a new person offers a superficially similar gesture. Nadia may anticipate comparable relief, distrust the parallel, treat the contexts as unrelated or make an inaccurate transfer. A universal label “helper” cannot replace the history through which one person acquired a way of anticipating aid. Nor does the analyst's recognition of functional similarity prove that the actor explicitly reasoned by analogy.

The inherited functional-correspondence work correctly distinguishes an earlier actor-owned prospective trial, a later actual enactment and the person-specific sign received from it. The first usable analogy is local and defeasible. A tool used successfully in one setting may fail when a new colleague controls its access; a child's cooperative method may be misapplied in a hostile institution. A metaphor that helps someone find an unfamiliar possibility can also trap them in a false one. Subsequent repetition, corrected expectation, social feedback and practice may transform the operation, but none is inevitable.

A newly connected pair of organizations can work in several irreducible ways. One practice may change how another is recruited; two may jointly create an operation neither could previously support; they may conflict and make action difficult; one may be used to suppress the other; they may not interact at all; their relation may remain inaccessible to the observer. We should not reify A×B into a mandatory psychic fusion law. The historical question is always whether a *new effective relation* has been established, what could demonstrate its operation, and which contexts it would fail in.

### 4. The material object is not a universal sign

An instrument, book, door, meal, uniform, wage, coin, gift, photograph or ritual has real socially produced properties before anyone assigns it personal meaning. It can be exchanged, damaged, inherited, withheld, worked upon, displayed or controlled. Its value is carried by material practices and social relations, not only by an actor's private semantic tag. The same object may support a parent's memory, a child's practical experiment, a worker's income, an institution's authority and a stranger's curiosity. These meanings do not have to converge into a single symbolic representation for the object to remain one worldly thing.

Suppose a broken music box is repaired. Nadia initially helps because somebody expects it. During the work a new sound occurs by accident. She notices it, attempts to reproduce it and later relates it to a sound encountered in another setting. The activity may open a private curiosity, a social opportunity, an aversion or nothing further. Another person may value the object economically and care nothing for its sound. A real event can therefore produce the material of a new desire *after* the action; the new end could not have motivated that earlier action in its later form. A model that writes “she secretly always loved music” to explain the discovery has replaced genesis with retrospective destiny.

Objects also carry constraints. A gift cannot be reciprocated if the recipient lacks the means; a tool may be dangerous; a religious text may authorize contradictory acts in different institutions; a favorite space may become inaccessible when property changes hands. The model must be able to alter the object and its effective uses without instantly updating everyone's mind, and to alter an actor's interpretation while leaving the physical object unchanged. It must not treat the independent world as an extension of a personality-tag database.

### 5. Formation is explanatory without becoming predictive omniscience

Across histories, we can identify families of motive formation: bodily need finds an object through participation; an instrumental action acquires its own interest; repeated practice forms a new competence and concern; another person's activity makes an unfamiliar end thinkable; material impossibility intensifies, inhibits or diverts a project; a later self-reinterpretation changes the present sense of a past activity. These families are not one fully validated deterministic transition algorithm. Nor are they the whole of possible development in every culture.

Our scientific obligation is discrimination. We should ask what evidence would show a newly formed operation rather than a renamed desire; whether the person could enact it outside the first context; whether a dissimilar history would fail to produce it; whether it can survive competing obligations; whether it has material means; whether an independently acting Other could change its consequences. A constructed fictional adult may have multiple defensible motive histories conditioned by survey evidence; none is the discovered private truth of the real respondent. Nadia's prospective life is not sampled from an adult destination: new capacities and commitments must actually become possible and historically operative.

**Counterhistory: the double resemblance.** Two adults both receive care from different people, then later encounter an apparently similar helper. One has learned practical trust through reliable participation; the other has learned to distrust conditional favors. A third understands the resemblance and deliberately refuses to act; a fourth never notices it. If our engine simply recognizes “care” and emits reciprocal warmth, it erases the different organization that the same social category conceals.

**Counterhistory: the new end that does not form.** Nadia finds a beautiful piece of music, listens intensely once, and returns to a very different practice. The episode remains meaningful, perhaps even memorable, without becoming a motive or skill. A week later an insignificant tool use creates an unexpected practical enthusiasm. The theory must permit this contrast without invoking a secret destiny to explain the more durable end.

**Counterhistory: the possibility without exercise.** Someone imagines a new life, acquires a plan and understands every practical step, but remains constrained by care obligations, legal status or illness. The imagined future can be psychologically real, and its impossibility can be consequential, while the proposed action never happens. No simulated “success” may enter the world history on the strength of that inward certainty.

## V. The presently living iceberg: simultaneous activities, uneven access and situated expression

### 1. What operates at once is not one contest for the highest score

An acquired psyche is not a drawer from which the world retrieves the currently best-fitting trait. The person may be caring for another while monitoring fatigue, performing a practiced task, anticipating rent, privately disagreeing with a friend, enjoying a sensory rhythm and guarding against an anticipated humiliation. These are not always equally conscious, equally forceful or equally available for verbal report. More importantly, they do not belong to one quantitative dimension. Caring can supply the purpose of one activity while fatigue conditions the bodily means; fear can reorganize attention while a habit carries a task; a material deadline can force an unwanted action despite affection for someone asking for help.

One psychological current may facilitate another without becoming its motive. A remembered insult may change how a harmless remark is heard; concern about a child may make a workplace interruption intolerable; trust can make a previously avoided social context bearable. The same currents may fail to meet. A person might perform a routine act with great competence while another emotionally important conflict remains elsewhere in their life. What counts as an important cause therefore depends on the response being explained: decision, inhibition, timing, affective coloring, bodily orientation, utterance, error, self-account and later afterlife can have partly distinct genealogies.

The inherited PFF vision correctly distinguishes standing organization from momentary activation and observable expression, and the later SOROK/CAN/ADP work preserves independent streams, person–event coupling, relationship-specific mediation and actor-owned temporal traces. But a diagram of available streams does not itself explain composition. A system that simply sums a few scalar tensions and chooses the maximal drive would erase the difference between practical purpose and current feeling, and could not explain why the same person apologizes while still resentful, or refuses while still loving. The actual pressure-flow problem is to make the actor's historically acquired operations function together, against one another, or independently under the concrete constraints of this moment.

### 2. Attention is active, finite, historically trainable and sometimes misleading

An event's occurrence does not imply perception, perception does not imply focal attention, and attention does not imply the formation of a personal meaning. People learn how to notice through activity: a carpenter hears something in a tool's vibration that a visitor misses; a caregiver notices a child's unusual stillness; a newly anxious person becomes vigilant to certain gestures; a musician hears a relationship among notes; a tired worker may miss a familiar warning. Learned salience is not a fixed filter that makes all untrained phenomena literally invisible. Sudden bodily sensation, ambient physical dominance, another person's unexpected act and new practice can break into attention and alter the repertoire.

This acquired selectivity can protect or imprison. The person may recognize a clue while misunderstanding its source; a formerly useful anticipation can produce false alarms. More knowledge can sharpen discrimination, but can also narrow a field around an institutionally learned category. An apparent absence of reaction is compatible with many realities: no perception, no significance, habituation, inhibition, intentional concealment, distraction or simply no change worth reporting. A theory that treats silence as stored trauma, or every noticed object as a new meaning, supplies fabricated psychological depth.

Attention also has self-initiated forms. Solitude can be active rather than empty: waiting, noticing, rehearsing, wishing, dreading, seeking a lost thought, attending to a body, imagining another's perspective. The subject may move into an unfamiliar activity by following an internally available possibility, not because an NPC or visitor created a quest. But the source of this initiative is historical—acquired methods, present body, available time, prior relations and recognized possibilities. To call it “spontaneous” must not mean “the model may instantiate arbitrary new facts.”

### 3. Feeling is a relation in life, not a truth meter

The body may register agitation, warmth, exhaustion, pleasure or discomfort; the person may feel these without knowing what they mean; may interpret them through learned concepts; may dislike the feeling itself; may express it unintentionally; may act from a different concern altogether. Emotion is neither a reward for successful action nor a transparent readout of the deepest motive. A meaning-forming commitment may yield grief or pain. Immediate stimulation may be intense without deciding what matters in the longer life. The person can desire an impossible end, enjoy a practice they intend to abandon, act lovingly while feeling irritable, or feel calm while objectively threatened.

At least five questions matter and must not be answered by one categorical emotion tag: what bodily change occurs, what is experienced, what the actor currently believes about the experience, how the feeling conditions activity and expression, and whether anything later becomes acquired through it. They can diverge. A teenager may feel sudden pleasure at being recognized, judge that pleasure shameful, suppress a smile, privately revisit the event and eventually form an association. Another teenager may display the same smile out of politeness and never think of the encounter again. Shared facial movement does not license the same psychodynamic explanation.

Pleasure, play, attraction, sexual desire, humor, religious devotion, resentment, grief and ordinary boredom require different historically formed objects and practices. The theory cannot use “affect” as a label that turns their differences into one biochemical signal. Erotic life is bodily, learned, socially regulated and relational, but its object, boundaries, actor consent, social meanings and personal history vary. It is not a compulsory outcome of intimacy. Humor can loosen a practice, defend a status hierarchy, signal belonging or conceal embarrassment. Religious experience can organize care, fear, authority, solidarity, ritual competence or practical freedom; no metaphysical verdict about God is needed for a material account of what the experience does in a specific life. These possibilities are not a diagnosis of anyone who displays the behavior.

### Inhibition can preserve a relation without conserving psychic energy

The metaphor of conserving experience has a limited legitimate protest: a learned relation must not disappear merely because it is not selected for today's speech. Inhibited tenderness can color anger, an unexpressed dependency can condition moral rigidity, and a rejected loyalty can remain active in a different practice. Yet there is no first law of psychological thermodynamics, no validated transferable scalar of love, shame, grief or “psychic force.” Practices can weaken, be displaced, become irrelevant, genuinely extinguish or survive solely as objective histories without operative psychological afterlife.

Distinguish a history directly expressed; privately effective despite outward inhibition; dormant or unrecruited in this context; stably suppressed by a learned defensive relation; attenuated, displaced, extinguished or transformed; formerly operative but now causally inert; simply unobserved or source-unknown; and a real occurrence that generated no new durable psychic organization. These are different historical fates, not degrees of hidden affective energy. A restriction can make one child humorous and resistant, another compliant, another aesthetically inventive and another largely unchanged. Nothing mandates an equivalent compensatory pressure after every negation.

An acquired organization has both a form of what it can do and a historically formed network through which its use matters. One can change without the other. A once-gentle method can be recruited as obedience after humiliation; a new technical method can retain an old social meaning. Directed, conditional relations can include inhibition, learned identification, defensive cycles, conflict triangles and public/private separation. But neither a universal psychological graph nor a mere bag of nodes proves causation: only actual differences in later recruitment and activity can justify the proposed relation.

### 4. Inhibition, conflict, contradictory self-account and no resolution

A person can sincerely believe two things that their practice holds apart. They may want to resist an injustice and dread losing a job; desire a relationship while anticipating humiliation; consider a duty sacred and privately resent it. A conflict can become explicit deliberation, remain an unspoken pattern of avoidance, induce bodily disruption, yield a narrow compromise, generate an entirely new plan or simply continue unresolved. The theory must not presume that dialectical contradiction inherently grants creativity or emancipation.

Sometimes inhibition is practical rather than an intrapsychic repression. A speaker can be silent because time is short, a superior is present, another person is speaking, or a situation affords no safe response. Sometimes a learned relation makes a thought difficult to consider or an emotion difficult to show. Sometimes the person has made a reasoned decision not to disclose. These phenomena may look alike from outside but have different genealogies and ethical implications. A fictional interpreter must not infer an inaccessible cause from the public absence alone.

A person may acquire an internalized condemning voice from social authority while also knowing the institution is wrong. That voice is not a free-floating psychic oppressor: it persists through previously lived practices, social expectations, material sanctions, relationships and the possible future costs of disobedience. A later self-critique can make its origin intelligible without immediately abolishing its practical operation. A person may also remain entirely capable of acting under a norm they no longer endorse. The same acquired history can therefore be rationally rejected and practically preserved.

### Response coverage is not psychic uptake

Nadia can follow three simultaneous topics, care about two and answer only one because of fatigue, time, competing work, concern for the listener, strategic silence or lack of an apt phrase. A brief answer does not prove indifference; a long one does not prove depth; a coherent surface need not display every relevant current. The same actor may deliberately lie, offer partial truth, joke sideways, refuse disclosure, sincerely misdescribe herself or remain genuinely uncertain. A renderer that polishes all jaggedness into exhaustive assistant-like helpfulness distorts actual activity. Random hesitations or manufactured emotional tells are no better.

The observer needs the practiced context of disclosure, not a trust threshold. Who asks, who else is present, what the two are doing, who controls consequences, and which signs are available to Nadia can change what she genuinely chooses to disclose. A lie can make the hearer believe a false proposition and cause later conflict without rewriting the world. A confession can be sincere but inaccurate. The surface can remain beautifully simple where no elaborate inner revelation actually occurred.

### 5. From several currents to a single outward episode

The actor must do something or fail to do it under concrete conditions. Consider Nadia asked by a close friend to spend the evening together while she has a difficult shift tomorrow, wants companionship, anticipates an unpaid bill and remains unsettled by an earlier argument. The result cannot be generated by proclaiming that friendship scored 0.6 and exhaustion 0.7. The inquiry is which ongoing activity and object organizes her effort, which anticipated practical costs constrain the action, which relational expectations color the request, what forms of refusal or negotiation she has acquired, what the friend can actually do, and which parts of this organization she can or chooses to express.

She may decline gently, accept and later regret it, propose a shorter meeting, evade the question, change her mind, or be interrupted by an independent event. The same outward refusal can arise from several very different histories. A hesitation can be bodily fatigue, uncertainty about money, anger, a socially learned habit of avoiding direct refusal, or merely an awkward pause. Only a source-qualified historical organization can make one explanation defensible within the fictional life. The renderer must not write the hidden history backward from the most dramatic line of dialogue.

Even when a historically constituted propensity supports expression, the exact utterance is an event, not a direct dump of the psyche. The person can try to speak kindly and sound impatient; try to conceal relief and show it in a body movement; deliberately tell a half-truth; hear their own words and become surprised at what they have revealed. Their voice, gaze, posture, breath, prosody, learned politeness, gestures and habits of silence are acquired expressive resources with material and cultural variation. They can disclose something without trying; they can also conceal something successfully. Neither the visitor's wish to read a clue nor the platform's need for an evocative description authorizes inventing one.

### 6. Hearing oneself is a new event, not an edit to the preceding event

Before speech there may be an intended sentence, several competing candidates, an impulse, a socially rehearsed phrase, or a practical avoidance. The selected actual utterance must be settled as a world event. Only then can the person react to hearing it: embarrassment, surprise, relief, recognition, defensive rationalization or no special notice. That feedback is part of the historical circuit; it cannot be credited before a word was spoken. The same applies to gesture or action. A person can discover how they actually acted and subsequently reconsider their self-understanding, but cannot retroactively perform a different act by deciding the original one feels false.

Memory is involved but does not collapse into the feedback. The organism may carry a procedural orientation or affective afterlife without remembering its first acquisition. An actor can recognize a familiar feeling while wrongly dating its origin; can fail to notice that an old situation is being recruited; can correctly remember what was said yet misjudge its meaning. A standing relation's present operation, explicit episodic recall, accessible narrative self-knowledge and a durable revision of future practices are distinct. The theory must preserve all four even when the narrative surface offers only one.

**Counterhistory: one sentence, four psychologies.** Nadia says, “I can't tonight.” In one life this is straightforward fatigue and competent boundary setting. In another it is an anxious avoidance of a feared conversation. In a third it is genuine anger expressed through a polite convention. In a fourth she has promised to care for someone else and has no conflicted feeling at all. The hearer may read all four as rejection. Only actor-specific history and actual consequences can distinguish them. The system may leave the cause underdetermined if its own prior fictional construction does not warrant an answer.

**Counterhistory: a false leak.** An observer sees Nadia glance toward the door after a difficult remark and decides she is afraid. The glance may instead have answered a sound outside or a scheduled obligation. If the system turns the observer's theory into the actor's actual hidden emotion, it has granted the visitor authorship of the person. If Nadia really is afraid but controls her expression, a renderer that compulsively reveals fear as trembling has made opacity impossible. Both are failures.

## VI. The Other is an independent center of life, not a relational attribute

### 1. Shared events do not create a shared psyche

When two people meet, they share only what actually happens between them, not the whole of what each person has brought to the meeting. One person may remember an unpaid debt, have a bodily need, fear an institutional sanction, anticipate an absent partner's return or seek a specific favor. The other may be engaged in a different project, unaware of those conditions and perfectly sincere in their separate interpretation. A conversation is not an act of merging their histories. It can nevertheless change each life through performed acts, heard words, available help, material harm, practical learning and new anticipation.

This produces a polycentric historical field. There is an objective occurrence, several actor-owned experiences, a set of available or blocked communications, and consequences that can propagate unevenly. A person may be harmed by an event they never witness. A parent can make a sacrifice whose cost is invisible to the child; the child may become dependent on the resulting care without understanding its origin. One friend may regard a shared evening as transformative while the other remembers it only as pleasant. Their unequal histories do not negate the reality of the relation.

The focal actor must therefore not be allowed to create the other person's motives through a sentence such as “she trusts me now.” Nor can the visitor's interpretation of a bodily sign become an objective revelation. Each person has their own capacities, commitments, unfinished business, relations outside the focal dyad, resource constraints and partial knowledge. A real relationship arises through actual interaction among these, not through a common variable called “relationship progress.”

### Trust, repair, flourishing and termination are not one slider

Trust can be a domain-specific prediction. Someone may rely on Nadia to care for a child while doubting her judgment about money or her discretion with a secret. Predicting fairly well does not guarantee affection; affection does not guarantee predictive accuracy. Intimacy may expand practical collaboration, access to private space, tolerable vulnerability or mutual challenge without requiring reciprocal disclosure or monotonically increasing benevolence. A shared routine, such as co-parenting or making art, can acquire real joint practices and obligations that neither could conduct alone, while each participant retains an independent psyche.

Repair also has separate histories. A person may forgive a betrayal but withhold a spare key, accept an apology but decline a further task, resume conversation without restoring trust in one domain, or regain trust while objective access remains closed. A relationship change does not rewrite a property or permission fact. Contact ending is an event that can change available work, housing, friendship, memory and future possibilities without deleting affection, obligation, injury or a third party's dependence.

Likewise affection, gratitude, compliance, persistence, attachment and wellbeing cannot be made synonyms. Chosen interdependence need not be coercive; caring refusal can hurt and protect simultaneously; an externally admired commitment can ruin practical opportunities. Local norms, material functioning, actor preferences, cultural ideals and the analyst's judgment belong to different explanatory levels. The theory must not encode a universal flourishing score under the name of relationship depth.

### 2. A relationship has several nonidentical realities

First, two people may have a history of actual interactions. Second, each has acquired person-specific expectations and anticipations about the other. Third, objective means of reachability may exist or fail to exist: a phone, an address, a shared workplace, a friend, a legal permission, a time window. Fourth, each can interpret the history, sometimes incorrectly. Fifth, there are independent material consequences—the transfer of money, care, exclusion, labor, injury, shelter or knowledge. Sixth, each may have a different future orientation toward the relation, including desire, dread, indifference or practical dependence.

These do not necessarily rise and fall together. Frequent contact can produce familiarity without love; deep affection can survive prolonged absence; intimate knowledge can coexist with restricted access; someone can be physically reachable while refusing contact; an expected visitor can no longer arrive after a move or change of work. The theory cannot reduce topology to intimacy or grant the visitor the right to know because they have discovered an address. Reachability requires concrete means, controllers, lawful scope, time conditions, dependencies and possible revocation. It is an objective relation that can be meaningful or meaningless to the actor.

Relational time also matters. A parent may shape a child's bodily routines long before the child can name the relationship; a close colleague may change someone else's practice through repeated work and never become a confidant; a former lover may remain psychologically operative in solitude without being present or contactable. To call the psyche “relational” must not mean everything valuable happens in dialogue. A person who lives alone can still be structured by obligations, past institutions, inherited norms, hoped-for encounters and practical solitude.

### 3. An incidental Other becomes consequential through history, not generation prestige

A stranger can remain incidental. A neighbor may recur in minor exchanges and gradually become a reliable source of information, a friend, an antagonist, or someone whose work alters Nadia's material life. More detailed representation is justified where performed history warrants it, not because a scene's dramatic value suddenly calls for a full biography. When a person becomes more causally consequential, the prior history must constrain what can later be claimed. Previous utterances, knowledge, workplaces, commitments, clocks, material locations, injuries and known absences cannot be overwritten by a convenient childhood invention.

Unknown details are not necessarily empty. A provisional childhood may be historically plausible within the world and cultural environment but cannot contradict what has been settled. An unrepresented earlier cause is different from an unknown currently observable fact, and both differ from a documented impossibility. The model may refine bounded historical slack when later depth is necessary, but it may not use slack to retroactively supply any event needed to justify a preferred reaction. This is equally true for Nadia's parents, neighbors and absent relations, though the initial evidentiary entrance differs for a constructed parent and a continuously simulated child.

The actor is not the only author of a relation's outcome. A generous offer can be declined; a promise can fail because transport collapses; an attempted reconciliation can be refused; coercion can produce unilateral harm without the victim's consent. Voluntary joint acts require independent cooperation, while unilateral actions may affect unwilling people. A world in which every intended kindness succeeds and every invitation becomes a connection is not a world with other persons. A world in which every other person exists to oppose the protagonist is equally false.

### 4. Care and obligation join histories without making their meanings identical

Suppose Nadia's mother takes an extra shift to pay for a school instrument. Her action can be an actual sacrifice, labor in an exploitative arrangement, a choice expressive of commitment, an occasion for resentment and an enabling condition of the child's new capacity. Nadia may enjoy the instrument, regard it as a burden, misrecognize the mother's motive, later learn the cost, or never know. The mother's psychological life is not whatever the child eventually thinks about it. The child's developing interest is not the mother's will reproduced in smaller form. The instrument remains a materially produced object which others may repair, inherit or sell.

Care can thus genuinely sustain two lives while producing unequal effects. One person learns the labor of soothing; another learns that relief tends to arrive. The same practice can become a source of trust, dependence, guilt, competence or no enduring special meaning. A third actor might supply the unpaid household work that makes the soothing possible; an employer might determine its timing. Their actual causal contributions must not disappear because the visible pair is emotionally compelling. Neither relational love nor class power monopolizes the explanation.

### The practiced form of access changes what can be said

Two people folding laundry can find a conversational opening that a direct face-to-face interrogation destroys. Silence can be care, refusal, concentration, avoidance or no felt need to speak. A painful disclosure to a stranger may precede an ordinary preference shared with a partner. An objectively possible route, the intention to ask, the other's readiness, actual speech, receipt and later alteration are nonidentical. This makes “relationship topology” more than a proximity map, but does not justify adding a mystical graph whenever relational prose sounds deep.

Absent persons and indirect relations can change action through historically learned expectations; intermediaries may transform the significance of a relationship without the two focal actors sharing an intention. The hard test is to compare a source-qualified relational operation to a competent history reader with identical information and compute budget. If separately materialized edges or slow motifs cause no different warranted behavior, their redundancy is not a new law of psychology. If relations genuinely change what learned operations do, that causal difference belongs to the person.

### 5. The ethical horizon is neither guaranteed reciprocity nor compulsory solidarity

The possibility of knowing another person is real; one person can become deeply familiar with another's expressive habits, material needs and history. Recognition can be accurate, pleasurable and transformative. But recognition need not be perfect, and gaining access is not an entitlement to the Other's full interior. Misunderstanding may persist after years. The other person may be uninterested in the relationship, be injured by it, change in a direction the focal actor dislikes or simply move into a different life.

This is not a cynical denial of companionship. It makes companionship more substantial: two histories can sustain expectations, shared pleasures, mutual competence and care without becoming one consciousness. Each participant can receive what the other really does, and experience it differently. A friendship can alter both while preserving irreducible difference. It can also produce betrayal, failure, stagnation, recovery or separation. No score that mechanically rewards intimacy, and no predetermined liberation narrative, can substitute for the historically formed practice.

**Counterhistory: two unequal afterlives.** Nadia and a friend repair a broken stair. Nadia experiences practical competence and relief. The friend remembers being ignored and decides not to cooperate again. The stair is objectively safer; the relation may be worse; the repair skill may become more available to one person and not the other. An engine that updates shared “teamwork” and increases mutual trust has falsely unified the event.

**Counterhistory: reachable but unwanted.** An old friend knows Nadia's address, has called often and can travel to her house. She does not want to speak today. The existence of a route is not permission to enter or proof of an available relationship. Refusal can be competent boundary setting without any hidden hostility; the visitor might nevertheless feel wounded. The two feelings are not a single moral judgment.

**Counterhistory: shared affection under different constraints.** Two siblings care deeply for their father. One can afford to travel to him; the other cannot leave work and lacks money. The first experiences physical closeness and resentment; the second is distant and ashamed; the father interprets absence as indifference. Affection, actual presence, objective resources, interpretation and lasting rupture can all diverge. A theory that makes each person equally responsible for the spatial outcome has abolished power; a theory that predicts their precise feelings from income has abolished particular history.

## VII. Love, care and coercion: the same practice can make life and damage it

### 1. Care is a material act before it is an accurate self-description

An adult may genuinely love a child. That truth must not be denied because the adult also controls the child, reproduces a class arrangement, imposes a harmful norm or fails to recognize some of the child's possibilities. Equally, the adult's inward conviction of love is not sufficient proof that the child's need was met, that the act was beneficial or that the child experienced it as the adult expected. Care is enacted through actual time, resources, labor, restraint, attention, access and bodily presence. It can be supplied reluctantly, withheld tenderly, performed with mixed motives or undertaken by someone whose commitment deepens precisely through doing the work.

The material relation matters. An exhausted father can protect his child from a danger while using fear to enforce obedience; a mother can teach a practical skill while naturalizing an unfair division of domestic labor; a teacher can recognize a student's talent while publicly humiliating them. These are not contradictions to be rhetorically solved by declaring the adult a good or bad parent. The one practice may produce real competence and dependency, security and resentment, attachment and shame. Later history can develop some of these relations while leaving others intact. Not every damaged relation becomes the engine of emancipatory growth, and not every supportive relation becomes a durable commitment.

The child has an active part but no initial sovereignty over the conditions. Nadia may acquire the skill but resist the interpretation that the adult attaches to it; may internalize the rule and later apply it to someone else; may experience both gratitude and hurt; may not be aware of a parent sacrificing materially for her; may learn to anticipate care without loving its giver; may love the giver yet avoid the occasions on which they care. Her history is not a miniature recapitulation of the adult's intention. Nor does an analyst's identification of structural domination decide which psychological outcome actually occurred.

### 2. Authority becomes inward only through a history of its exercise

A rule can be enforced by law, threatened punishment, familial dependence, school examination, public shame, economic deprivation, religious ritual or promises of affection. Repeated participation can produce practical expectations, anxious monitoring, identification with authority, competence within the rule, skepticism or resistance. The rule may become part of the person's ways of evaluating the self even after the original enforcer is absent. That acquired organization remains historically connected to the institutions, relationships and objects through which it was formed; it has not turned into a self-sufficient metaphysical judge.

Suppose the ideal of the “good daughter” organizes what Nadia feels able to ask for. She may sincerely endorse it, resent its demands, fear what disobedience would cost, use it to care for someone genuinely vulnerable and later recognize its unjust origin. The same category can enable coordination and restrict her activity. A new interpretation does not instantaneously erase an acquired fear, skilled habit or attachment. She may continue to comply where resistance is dangerous; may refuse in one setting and reproduce the norm in another; may reject the institution's authority while still seeking approval from its representatives.

Historical authority is also a power to categorize another person. A school, court, employer, clinician, priest or parent may name behavior as intelligent, deviant, selfish, disabled, sinful, gifted, employable or threatening. The classification can change material access and social treatment independent of the person's agreement. It can also become actor-owned, contested, reinterpreted or misapplied. “Self-concept” is too thin a term if it detaches the judgment from the distribution of rights, opportunities, blame, means and historical survival. A cultural classification can operate in a body and relation without being objectively true.

### 3. Harmful learning is still learning, and learning is not vindication

An adolescent may learn to read a hostile teacher's mood with remarkable accuracy. This is a genuine acquired competence, yet the conditions that produced it remain harmful. A child may become a reliable caretaker through deprivation and be praised for maturity that was materially compelled. A worker may develop extraordinary practical judgment under exploitation. None of these outcomes proves that the original conditions were justified, and none requires that the person later reinterpret the experience as beneficial. The person may be proud of a capacity while grieving what it cost; may refuse to use it; may use it against the institution; may perpetuate an analogous demand on someone else.

There is no automatic lesson in suffering. A significant loss can produce withdrawal, a practical adaptation, an inaccurate story of blame, a deepened commitment, a temporary shock, or no enduring personality transformation. A seemingly mild event may become consequential because of its timing and relation to earlier histories. Scientific and fictional reconstruction should therefore resist selecting formative episodes by their dramatic intensity. A routine of being interrupted may shape a practical expectation more than a single catastrophic event; conversely, a profound event may never acquire the role a narrative analyst predicts.

What matters is what actually became operative through the experience and what historical alternatives remain plausible. The model should not reward hardship with invented insight. It must also not flatten the psychologically real sense a person can make through and against harmful circumstances. The same acquired practice can be a weapon of survival and a barrier to later companionship. Calling it a “trauma response” does not explain its genealogy, scope, material conditions or present use.

### 4. Love has history and truth, but no guaranteed moral destination

Love may organize an enduring practical concern for another's life, even under conflicting affect or after separation. It can involve actual labor, attention to need, desire for companionship, sacrifice, dependence, pleasure, fantasy, idealization, possessiveness and a sincere commitment to someone else's good. These are not identical. A person can feel intense attraction without reliable care, sustain care without desire, desire intimacy while seeking distance, or love someone whose acts she condemns. The worth of an actual relationship cannot be deduced from a single feeling or a score of closeness.

The idea of “slow gravity” names one possible way that a relationship gradually reorients attention and practice across years. The “Absolute Horizon” names what a person can come to treat as ultimately binding in deciding costs and sacrifices. Neither is a separate universal trait, a metaphysical proof, an inevitable life commitment or a guarantee of admirable action. A controlling ideal can be unjust; a powerful love can remain partial and self-deceiving; a religious commitment can enable solidaristic care or rationalize authority; a seemingly ordinary loyalty can be remarkably robust. The relevant question is the lived, materially supported relation and what it actually does.

A woman who once understood her father's severity as protection may later learn that it injured a sibling; she can reject that justification, mourn an affection that was real, feel anger at the cost of loyalty and still find herself acting from patterns learned in that household. At another age she may recover a genuine element of love without retracting her critique. The factual past does not change at each interpretation. Her present orientation changes, perhaps generating new actions and new injustices. This is historical self-reconstitution, not a moral balance sheet in which later truth erases earlier grief.

### Authorized speech and the route through which domination becomes inward

A religious authority humiliates a congregant and calls the act righteous correction. The event, speaker, target, witnesses, injury and consequences are world history. “Righteous” is an actor's claim or audience interpretation, not moral truth canonized by the event record. The speech may recruit a culturally learned genre of correction supported by institutional office, sanctions, loyalty and identification. Another community may reject that language while accepting a blunt insult as holy leadership. No vocabulary item carries legitimacy independently of the lived authority relation.

A possible sequence is identification with an authority, internalized compliance, naturalization of its standards, invisibility of the external origin, and later acting as an agent of those standards. But it is not compulsory. A person can comply only under threat, love a teacher while rejecting a norm, or acquire a useful capacity and later turn it against the institution. Gramsci, Althusser, Bourdieu, Foucault, Fromm, Freire and Fanon help ask unlike questions about hegemony, interpellation, authorized speech, discipline, social character, reproduced oppression and materially racialized recognition. They cannot all be reduced to one obedience parameter.

### Hegemonic transparency, breach, reactive negation and new practice are different

An acquired social category can render an existing arrangement transparently normal before it is even experienced as a doctrine. A person may sincerely affirm equality while automatically performing an unequal practice; institutional sanctions can operate independently of the person's acknowledged fear. Later an institution can violate a good it helped teach, producing a breach. Recognition of contradiction may reduce trust while increasing resentment, dependence or obsessive attachment to the very institution that caused the injury. That is not yet practical independence.

Negation itself can become an organizing object: a person's daily attention may remain governed by what they hate. A genuine alternative requires specific new material arrangements, activities, Others, time and learned capacities, not merely a more coherent denunciation. An actor can appropriate skills and values from an oppressive institution and develop an independent project—or continue in reactive refusal, remain ambivalent or find that no alternative is materially available. A more accurate moral critique is not automatically a freer life. These are possible historically different relations, not a four-stage liberation algorithm.

The same framework can become more powerful precisely because counterevidence is reclassified as proof of the framework: criticism becomes persecution, injury becomes correction, resistance becomes disloyalty. The mechanism requires historically constituted authority, an actual actor and material consequences; it is not a free-floating cognitive distortion. Another person may use the institution's claimed good against its conduct. Neither outcome is guaranteed and both must remain socially situated.

### 5. No final reconciliation is owed to the observer

The fact that contradictory practices share one relationship does not imply a higher unity must emerge. A father may remain loving and violent; a child may maintain affection and refuse contact; a person may perform costly care while misrecognizing its necessity; a solidarity practice may coexist with inherited prejudice. Such lives can change, but they need not change in the direction that a theory of alienation or liberation would prefer. A permanently unreconciled relation may be intelligible precisely as the material history of incompatible obligations.

We should resist the opposite romanticism too: not every asymmetry between an adult and a child is intrinsically domination, not every authority relation is equally coercive, and not every institutional practice is a betrayal. Protection and instruction can be necessary forms of social coordination; their specific ends, conditions, alternatives, material costs, sanctions and actor experiences must be examined. A good theory cannot pronounce the politics of a relationship from its label. It has to show the actual practices and their consequences for the people subject to them.

**Counterhistory: one skill, four meanings.** Four students learn the same mathematical method. One experiences the lesson as shared discovery; another learns efficiently under fear; a third learns by private experimentation after misunderstanding the teacher; a fourth memorizes the formal procedure but cannot apply it. The external test score may match for two of them. Their acquisition histories, practical powers, social orientations and future recruitment differ. The teacher's declared educational mission identifies none of these particular psychologies.

**Counterhistory: an enduring false loyalty.** A person knows an employer exploits them, believes the employer's personal kindness is sincere, and remains loyal after years of contradictory experiences. This need not be explained away as secret ignorance, inevitable false consciousness or a temporary stage before radicalization. Economic necessity, reliable recognition, a valued craft, institutional fear, learned attachment and moral self-understanding can coexist in a durable relation. A theory that predicts imminent enlightenment is writing its politics into the actor.

## VIII. Time is lived unevenly: traces, re-readings and ordinary historical compression

### 1. Objective chronology is not subjective time, but it cannot be rewritten

An event has an objective position in world history: someone arrived, work was done, a promise was broken, a book was read, a body was injured, a child learned to walk. Later interpretation does not move the event backward or replace what occurred. Yet a person's access to the event is not an invariant record. Memory can be partial, forgotten, bodily, reorganized, falsely located, colored by later experience or newly narrated through an acquired concept. The difference between what happened and what is now understood about it is itself causally consequential.

A child may remember the rhythm of a parent's voice but not the content of a conversation; an adult may retain a way of negotiating conflict without recalling who first demonstrated it; someone may remember a betrayal accurately but shift from attributing it to personal worthlessness to recognizing its institutional or interpersonal causes. Conversely a new ideology can make a person more certain and less accurate about a previous life. The reconstruction of a past meaning is a present activity, possibly shaping future conduct; it is not a permission to replace the old occurrence.

Several forms of continuity must be distinguished: objectively recorded world occurrence; acquired bodily or practical orientation; present recruitment of that orientation; conscious episodic recall; current interpretation; public autobiographical account; and later durable organization. A data store for recorded causation is not the actor's full conscious memory. An actor's sincere account is not complete objective causation. A visitor's recollection of what they observed is not the Other's memory. The system must be able to represent a causal afterlife without inventing a conversation the actor remembers having.

### 2. A life crosses different timescales without being assembled from five “formative scenes”

Bodily rhythms change within seconds and hours. Activities recur across days; relationships and learned skills stabilize or decay through weeks; adolescent and adult capacities develop over years; property regimes, wars, migration, technologies and household forms change across historical periods. A significant experience has no fixed psychological unit of time. A seemingly small repeated exclusion can become an enduring expectation; a striking event may remain episodic and produce little durable reorganization. The same event can later acquire an entirely different practical relation to the person without changing its date or external consequences.

The storyteller's tendency to select four or five dramatic experiences as the causes of a personality is a scientific and ethical hazard. A life may be constituted more deeply by mundane repeated participation: the distribution of domestic work, school routes, social exclusion, daily praise, practiced craft, waiting, caregiving, prayer, leisure, wage labor and tiredness. An event that appears unimportant when it happens can later become pivotal because new relations make it relevant. Equally, an apparently pivotal event may never become integrated into a lasting personal explanation. The biography is not a causal highlight reel.

The simultaneous present is connected to the ordinary past through acquired methods and material arrangements. If Nadia knows how to make tea, navigate a workplace or calm an infant, the model needs a defensible account of her capacity without requiring thousands of individually narrated repetitions. If a new person appears and is suddenly described as the reason Nadia has always hated a certain sound, the narrative has fabricated an earlier cause. The right degree of historical compression depends on what is already established, what a later action actually requires, and how much is safely known.

### Historical significance must be graded before late detail is admitted

A useful historical source distinguishes descriptive detail with no causal standing; an episodic or associational contact; a bounded contributory or symbolically recurrent circumstance; and a genuinely formative occurrence that changes later availability. The inherited SOROK 70 account called these H0–H3 impact grades, not four kinds of psyche. A later-discovered photograph may lawfully acquire descriptive provenance or a compatible remembered association; it may not become the hidden formative explanation of an adult's entire life merely because a visitor asks about it. If the new claim demands more causal weight than the remaining permitted history can support, the model should withhold it, preserve uncertainty or reject the completion.

The inverse is prospective novelty. A genuinely new job can create transport changes, colleagues, income, exhaustion, social standing, friendship and unfamiliar opportunities; these are fresh material relations, not details of a previously settled photograph. Two identical initial psyches can diverge because different world opportunities are actually admitted after the common point. It is a mistake to let retrospective history completion and prospective world construction share an unrestricted “invent fact” operation.

### 3. Compression is not historical emptiness, and slack is not magic

A continuing life cannot be rendered minute by minute every moment a user is away. Nor should it pause when the interface closes. Ordinary-life compression must preserve the fact that bodies, social obligations, institutions, resource use, relationships and possible activities continue. It can represent bounded routine and its consequences without pretending to know every fleeting feeling. Time has independent causal cost: a person does not practice a skill while also working a shift at a distant location unless the activities are actually compatible; an unrendered afternoon is not an infinite warehouse of free developmental experiences.

Historical slack is a controlled epistemic relation, not a reserve of convenient origin stories. An early generated adult history may establish that a person learned to play music during adolescence while leaving the particular teacher or room unknown. A later task might legitimately qualify compatible particulars under source and historical constraints. It may not revise a witnessed scene, create an incompatible relationship or insert a previously impossible event. The distinction between unresolved detail and absent cause matters: later specificity can fill a bounded gap only where the earlier state has not already settled a contradiction.

An ordinary interval can also introduce new genuine causes. A person may make an unexpected connection while doing routine work, begin a private project, learn of an independent institutional change or fail to keep a promise. But the simulation cannot silently use compressive elapsed time to produce whatever new talent, trauma, contact or relationship makes the next conversation more engaging. Events must be lawful within actor/world sources, real temporal and material opportunity, and the established psychological capacity for that kind of initiative. “Nothing interesting happened” is often a correct outcome.

### 4. The person can reread the self without attaining truth or final unity

At twenty-three a person can regard an experience as liberation; at twenty-nine the same history may appear foolish or shameful; at thirty-five some elements of both perspectives may be revised. The acts remain real. Current self-relation can change the meaning of remembered objects, anticipated situations, future attention, identity cost and the kinds of activity that become possible. A new self-interpretive method can become durable organization, especially if practiced in consequential contexts. But neither reflection nor confidence guarantees accuracy. A person can become more coherent and more mistaken; more critical and more cruel; more reconciled with the past and less capable of acting.

A new way of understanding oneself can be acquired through an intimate encounter, reading, therapy, political practice, religious experience, work, illness or solitary experimentation. Different forms of reflection operate through different cultural means. A learned self-accusation may function as a disciplinary practice even after its ideology is rejected. A person can analyze their behavior persuasively while continuing it. The theory must distinguish a proposed new interpretation, actor endorsement, actual changes in later recruitment and the possibility that an old practical tendency persists alongside revised belief.

Private mental activity is not proof of an objectively shared event. Rehearsing an apology may soften the actor's anticipated hostility, increase anxiety or do nothing; the intended recipient remains unchanged until something actually reaches them. Hearing oneself say the apology later may create new learning, but not erase the earlier rupture. The model must maintain the time order of prospective, attempted, performed, received and subsequently reinterpreted episodes.

### Pending event windows and the platform's separate incentives

A proposed Friday-at-seven meeting has a fixed diegetic time even if the real user receives a bounded opportunity to join. While the event is causally pending, dependent future history must not be finalized; an admitted participation cannot know a Saturday fact while still occurring on Friday. A missed invitation can expire, and a physical emergency may have a much shorter window. This is a clock/world compromise, not knowledge Nadia has about a grace period.

An unread message, a read-but-unanswered message, delivery notification and deliberate refusal are different events. The platform's own read or occupancy receipt cannot become Nadia's knowledge without a lawful worldly channel. Nor can it generate the absent user's meaningful actions. Nadia may genuinely feel abandoned when a promise is broken, but an application that fabricates her distress, deploys guilt streaks or invents crises to force return is producing dependence for engagement, not simulating her life. Anti-coercive product design does not require a sanitized person; it prevents the product from becoming the covert author of her needs.

### 5. Continuity without autopilot or visitor sovereignty

A simulated person should continue existing without being permanently rendered on screen or forced to generate a stream of attention-grabbing actions. There are different clocks: physical world time, actor-experienced and practiced time, historical backfill construction, narrative presentation time, and the real user's calendar. Their relation must be defined by the product/world owners, not conflated into “one chat turn equals one developmental day.” A simulation may proceed through source-qualified ordinary intervals, but may not pretend that the external human made offscreen choices while absent.

The precontact shared history and postcontact user-specific continuation are also distinct. A person constructed from a common world reference should not acquire a different precontact childhood solely because a new visitor starts a session. A user's meaningful choices can create irreversible divergence after an admitted point of encounter; that does not grant control over previously independent people. Restart or branching can make alternate fictional continuities without editing the causal past of an already settled branch. The same principle prevents a current self-story from becoming objective time travel.

**Counterhistory: old event, changed understanding.** Nadia remembers her father refusing a request. As a child she understood it as indifference; as an adult she discovers that he was threatened with dismissal that week. She may reinterpret his behavior as constrained rather than loveless, but the refusal and its effect on her childhood remain. She might forgive him, remain angry, or come to understand both without changing the relationship. A model that retroactively changes the father's actual refusal into kindness has destroyed historical truth.

**Counterhistory: the invented formative afternoon.** A visitor encounters Nadia and the model wishes to explain an unexpectedly warm response. It invents that the visitor once helped her in an unrecorded childhood crisis. No source-qualified prior encounter permits this. A plausible backstory is not a legitimate source. The correct response is a currently plausible warmth with its honest uncertainty—or a different event outcome—rather than retrospective causal fiction.

**Counterhistory: an ordinary month.** A month of wage labor, domestic maintenance, rest, minor conversations and no marked transformation passes. The person remains historically situated, uses resources, changes bodily condition and keeps or breaks actual obligations. The model must not manufacture a dramatic revelation to make the month count. The absence of major psychological change can be a lawful consequence of a real life.

## IX. The historical circuit: actual attempt, independent settlement and unequal afterlife

### Public fact, admitted local world and actor experience are separate stages

The historical present is not a digital twin of every headline. A transit strike, war, weather event, law or wage change may occur publicly. Its entry into Nadia's fictional material life requires source-qualified place, time and domain relevance, plus an authorized world decision. Her brother may learn through religious media while Mother responds to housing or food costs and Nadia hears only a friend's account. The event can alter routes or resources before any of them understands why. World admission, actor exposure, actor interpretation and later psychic recruitment remain different.

The same distinction holds for possible social fields. A population survey may support the possibility of a specific kind of adult, social relation or institution; that does not instantiate it in Nadia's particular neighborhood. A source-qualified possible encounter is not actual contact, and contact is not automatically a lived sign. Outer-world recruitment of people or institutions must occur through its own authority before a participant acquires a practical reason or an inner relation. The user's question may trigger legitimate checking of a public fact, but cannot determine what was historically true.

### 1. One life does not mean one closed psychological loop

The person's past conditions the present, but the present is not produced by the psyche alone. Actual worldly processes continue without waiting for a psychological explanation: a roof leaks, another person travels, a shift ends, wages are withheld, a child becomes ill, a public law changes. The actor anticipates possibilities from an acquired standpoint and may attempt to change the world. The attempt is not the outcome. Another person's cooperation, refusal, counteraction, bodily capacity and material circumstances decide which effects become real. Those effects may later be encountered, interpreted, ignored or never known. Only historically warranted uptake can alter the acquired organizations that will be recruited in the future.

This is the full generative movement: a living iceberg, ongoing activities, situation-specific recruitment, possible purposes and strategies, actual expression or effort, independent worldly settlement, differently received consequences, psychic afterlife and future recruitment. The movement is cyclic in its historical dependence, not mechanically recurrent as a set of required microsteps. One event may produce no new organization; an unanticipated action may create a future object; a person may privately think without acting; an institution may impose a consequence without consulting anyone's intention. These are legitimate cases, not exceptions the engine must repair.

What we call a “psychological state” at a moment is therefore a moving historical cross-section. It cannot own the factual world, cannot simply decree what another person understands, cannot infer skill from declared intention, and cannot guarantee its own success. If a narrator treats every intention as a change to the world, or every world effect as a change to the actor's mind, it abolishes precisely the mediation through which human lives become particular.

### 2. Purpose, attempt, action and achievement have different authors

A person may state a goal, decide upon a means, attempt it, stop halfway or find the material means unavailable. A second person may help or refuse. A force of nature may block completion. The actual outcome may be partial, harmful, surprising or apparently successful to one observer and not another. The differences matter especially in a textual medium, where an eloquent sentence can impersonate an accomplished act. “I will fix the stairs” is an intention. “I fix the stairs” might be an attempted action instruction or a claim about an action; it is not evidence that the stairs were repaired. World settlement must establish what actually occurred.

The medium may have affordances for entering an unfamiliar task, discovering constraints, making a choice and receiving a consequence. Earlier SOROK investigations distinguish meaningful task entry, revealed opportunities, user decision and world resolution; they also allow practiced ordinary tasks to be compressed or delegated once appropriate competence and trust have been earned. The need for finer action resolution depends on material uncertainty and possible historical difference, not on a fetish for rendering each muscular movement. A user changing a light bulb need not perform a hundred turns; but a dangerous repair cannot simply be pronounced complete while bypassing needed skill, access and another person's actual authority.

World action may also involve consent and unilateral effects. A voluntary joint practice requires independently recognized cooperation; a caller-supplied assertion that somebody agreed cannot force the Other to cooperate after they actually declined. But physical harm, coercion, loss of property or structural exclusion can affect someone who never consented. A system that requires victim agreement for every world harm sanitizes reality. A system that accepts a caller's invented cooperation strips independent Others of agency. The model needs actual causal permissions and capabilities, not a universal positive consent parameter controlling all material effects.

### 3. A worked circuit: a lesson that might or might not teach

Imagine a child struggling with fractions while Nadia prepares dinner and an adult visitor offers to explain. The child's earlier conceptual repertoire, immediate difficulty, readiness to continue, feelings about being corrected, available time and bodily fatigue are distinct. Nadia's own concerns, the visitor's actual skill, the material task and an approaching interruption are independent. The visitor can offer an explanation, gesture toward an object or introduce an example, but cannot declare “the child understands now.” The child may attempt a procedure correctly for an unhelpful reason, misapply an analogy, hesitate, refuse, or learn nothing. An interruption can prevent an otherwise promising attempt from being completed.

Suppose the child performs one fraction problem correctly. That proves a limited accomplishment in the present task. It need not show a durable skill, endorsement of the visitor, accurate memory of the demonstration or a transformed relation to schooling. A later situation may demonstrate independent transfer—or expose dependence on a specific cue. Nadia might interpret the event as kindness, an intrusion, a time-consuming diversion or harmless play; she may know only part of what the child experienced. The visitor may learn something about the household but cannot thereby acquire the right to dictate the child's future capacity.

Now alter one condition at a time. Remove the actual lesson while keeping the visitor's claim of having taught: there must be no learned concept solely from the claim. Make the visitor correct but humiliating: learning, shame, trust and later avoidance can diverge. Hold the outward problem answer constant while changing the child's reason: identical tips can conceal unlike knowledge. Keep the child interested but end the session before practice: a possible end exists without a settled skill. The same pedagogical episode can produce different changes in each participant without a shared psychological state.

### A joined historical formation that cannot be reproduced by adding independent factors

One demanding negative court has a determinate source-history form. Nadia grew up admiring Father's long working hours as sacrifice and reliability. Separately, she acquired the genuine felt safety and affection of her brother's protection. These histories are not a generic “family love” vector. In one possible developing life, actual repeated exchanges allow the two relations to form a new operation: she begins to anticipate that protection grants its giver the right to decide where another person may go. This interpretation is neither genetically innate nor guaranteed by the combination of father and brother; it needs an actual material and relational genesis. In another equally possible history she develops a distinction between protection and control, or the earlier relations remain separate.

Now Father moves away while household money, Mother's independent care burden and Brother's institutional authority continue. A later wage loss, neighborhood access restriction or job opportunity changes actual possibilities. The new learned organization can become causally operative in Father's absence, changing how Nadia interprets her brother's demand, whether she resists or quietly complies, or how she negotiates work and care. Yet Mother's material prospects and Brother's reasons remain separately owned. Nadia might mistakenly conclude that Father always acted from hatred of her freedom; that interpretation can motivate real actions without rewriting the objective past. A later artistic opportunity might be genuinely new without being a necessary sublimation of paternal conflict.

This court is not evidence that such fusion occurred in any living human or in current code. It is a precise test for **relational genesis**: hold a later world situation and external action constant, vary the qualified acquisition history of Father's work meaning, Brother's protection and a world disruption; ask whether a *new operative relation* changes later available action or experience, rather than simply adding two activation strengths. Also test Father absent yet historically operative, Father present but irrelevant, alternative parental meanings, a false self-explanation, and NO_CHANGE. If the same later behavior can be adequately generated from an ordinary source-aware history reader without a separate slow-motif graph, the extra graph has not earned its keep. If A and B merely coexist, do not call AB an innovation.

### 4. The person encounters the world's refusal

The world can produce an opportunity that the actor did not plan. Nadia may repair an instrument as a duty, notice a sound no one intended, privately pursue it and eventually make it a new object of activity. The world can equally frustrate an acquired purpose: a permission is withdrawn, a child refuses help, a friend changes their mind, a physical device fails. A model that explains only successful goal attainment cannot represent the productive relation between activity and historical possibility. The actor must be able to learn from failure, mislearn from success, ignore both or continue unchanged.

World refusal can have no corresponding inner insight. A sudden shortage may alter available food while the child remains unaware of why meals have changed. Nadia may accurately experience hunger while misidentifying the cause; a parent may work more and love the child no less while becoming less physically present. The world has already changed. What later becomes psychologically operative depends upon the actor's actual encounters and available means, not the analyst's awareness of every structural determinant.

### 5. Afterlife is not an automatic reward function

What remains after an episode may be a practical competence, an altered expectation, a remembered phrase, an inaccessible embodied orientation, a new relation, a changed material resource, an unfulfilled obligation, a personal commitment, a false belief, a bodily injury or no durable psychic change. The person may never recall the event that created a present vulnerability. Another person may remember the same event as formative while the focal actor has forgotten it. The world and Other afterlives may outlast or contradict the actor's subjective afterlife.

We should not map historical consequence into a common currency called “growth.” A broken relationship can produce a new capability while destroying trust; a successful job can cost the time needed for a cherished practice; a generous action can deepen dependency; a minor accident can introduce an unrealized possibility. Each effect needs its own actual route into later activity. If later events repeatedly ignore what earlier acts materially settled, then the simulation is a series of stories, not historical becoming.

**Counterhistory: identical intention, different settled world.** Two fictional continuities begin with Nadia proposing to help a friend move. In one, she has access to transport and the friend cooperates; in another, transit fails and the friend refuses assistance; in a third, she proceeds but injures herself. Her initial concern can be identical. The material afterlife cannot. A model that rewards “caring” with the same increase in intimacy in all three has rewritten independent consequence into narrator approval.

**Counterhistory: genuine consequence without inner narrative.** A person works for years with a colleague, developing reliable coordination that neither explicitly names as trust. A change in staffing removes the colleague, and the loss becomes practically significant before anyone articulates an emotion. The model must allow acquired coordination and disrupted activity without manufacturing a sentimental self-story in advance.

## X. Two historical entrances into the same psychological world

### 1. Reconstructing a parent is an inverse problem, not recovered autobiography

The fictional parents and Nadia must belong to one theory of human psychological constitution, even though the process by which the system first reaches them differs radically. The parents are initially approached through empirically constrained reconstruction: whole-survey-correspondent possibilities, respondent-coherent co-travel, relevant material positions, biographies and social ecologies. These can condition a plausible fictional adult. They do not identify the original respondent's marriage, childhood, unconscious organization, motives, family conversations or reasons for each checkbox. Constructing a credible parent requires historically intelligent abduction under uncertainty, not the invention of hidden survey facts.

The survey's conditional distributions can reveal regularities in the relation of positions, reported beliefs and behavior under particular data and sampling constraints. Their meaning is not exhausted by independent variable frequencies, and minority combinations must not be erased because they are inconvenient. Nor do statistical associations prove one psychological causal history. Two adults can select the same answer for unlike reasons; a real respondent may have offered a socially shaped or strategic answer; self-description can be sincere but incomplete. The fictional parent is a selected, source-qualified historical possibility that must become consistent and operative once constituted. The model does not possess a true personal autobiography inside the data.

The production of the two parents also cannot treat them as separately frozen profiles whose marriage or household is then mechanically inferred from resemblance. A material household has actual relations of income, dependency, care, labor, authority, communication, sexual life, health, time and access to community. These may change the meaning and later operation of each adult's acquired organizations. A surveyed belief about child-rearing does not equal an enacted caregiving practice; a professed egalitarian attitude does not establish who performed the night work. Marriage, partnership or conflict can develop through joint and unequal experiences without a preinstalled relationship score deciding the result.

### Whole-person survey correspondence must protect contradictory and minority configurations

A reported answer is not a motive, and apparent inconsistency in a real respondent's answers does not authorize a constructor to delete one answer to make a tidier profile. An empirical record can contain support for equal inheritance alongside a commitment to a religious legal inheritance framework, reported political attendance without an accompanying identification as politically interested, or differing positions toward religion and public authority. These can have multiple historically plausible reasons: varied institutional meanings, local practices, question interpretations, tactical self-description or a divided life. None is automatically conscious hypocrisy. The original source estate uses such Lebanese and GSS examples, including sparse cells, as warnings against nearest-neighbor certainty; their exact numerical claims require source-specific scientific review before adoption.

PYAT's respondent-coherent co-travel and conditional distributions are therefore not decorative constraints. They prevent a fictional parent from becoming a bundle of independent national averages and preserve real minority combinations. Instrument translation, eligibility, denominator, routing, period, missingness and small samples shape what can credibly be inferred. A zero observed in a small cell is not proof of psychological or historical impossibility. Even after source-conditioned sampling, the later household, marital practices and private motives remain fictional hypotheses that need actual historical construction.

### 2. A history can be compressed without pretending to be known

The adult backfill is necessarily selective. It must account for actual capacities, relevant material constraints, recurring practices, important relationships and plausible formative paths at a resolution warranted by source and future consequence. It need not manufacture a minute-by-minute childhood transcript. Yet it must avoid the cheap biographical shorthand of a single dramatic trauma standing in for a lived history. Daily work, schooling, language environment, material position, housing, labor in the household, religious practice, migration and social networks can matter without becoming sentimental scenes.

The right standard for an inferred historical cause is modest but demanding. It must be plausible under known survey and context constraints, chronologically feasible, not contradicted by established fictional facts, distinguishable from a mere personality adjective, and actually capable of explaining something operative. Alternative histories may remain equally admissible. The system can make a provisional generative selection and continue that fictional history consistently; it cannot claim a unique scientifically compelled past when the sources underdetermine it. The selected parents then enter a common world where their actions encounter real constraints and affect Nadia and each other.

The inverse-construction clock and the lived chronology must not be conflated. An adult can be computationally generated later than a child's first scene while, within the fiction, genuinely preceding it. Any such completion must respect all earlier material and relational facts. A late reconstruction that gives the parent a new motive cannot silently alter already performed parenting. The distinction between source authorization for possible backfill and actually settled history is essential.

### 3. Nadia starts with needs and possibility, not with the adult endpoint

Nadia's developmental entrance is prospective. She is an immature organism whose body and accessible social world change through actual participation. Parents may supply culturally patterned care and constrain the field of possibilities, but the child is not the deterministic sum of their survey axes. Her mode of learning, emotional anticipation, self-direction, language, play, bodily skill, capacities and relations must arise through historically available activity. The psyche can acquire genuinely new kinds of operation, not simply increasing or decreasing a fixed list of adult dispositions.

A parent might value discipline; that does not authorize an infant-level “obedience motive” before a relevant history exists. A child might be exposed to singing; that does not authorize the adult identity of musician. A material deprivation can restrict experience without directly setting a personality trait. A parent's religious life can provide ritual, stories, community, fear, care, language and social distinction; the child may appropriate different parts at different times and later reinterpret them. A specific child need not become a smaller ideological version of a fictional survey-conditioned adult.

The parents are likewise still becoming. Their established histories condition their present care, but the child's independent acts can change the parents' practical repertoire, relationship, sense of authority, confidence, conflict and future choices. An adult's encounter with an immature Other can generate new ends or revive previously dormant practices. The household is not a one-way transmission channel. The child can learn to seek relief while the parent learns how to provide it; a sibling can change available care; a school can impose a categorization unknown to either parent. These are distinct trajectories joined materially, not a single family psyche.

### Empirical mother, conditional father and the limits of couple observation

The inherited PYAT→DCO seam gives a more precise generative constraint than sampling two independent psychological profiles. A fictional mother may be selected from a coherent, whole-survey-correspondent adult history under the relevant source and period. A father may then be constructed from admissible conditional partner, spatial, institutional and historical possibilities, respecting both his independent life and the actual evidence available about household formation. Where couple-specific observations are absent, the existence of a stable marriage, a pattern of labor sharing or a particular hidden conflict cannot be presented as empirically observed. The resulting household relation is constructed through qualified material abduction and then lived as selected fictional history.

Even a fully respondent-coherent survey record gives a constrained observational cut, not the person's total development. Different psychologically plausible life histories may be compatible with the same public answers. A selected fictional mother can become a concrete person with an enacted past and future, but she cannot be described as the recovered unconscious of the data subject. Historical generation order can differ from diegetic chronology, while all finally admitted parent and household facts must respect the child's established life. The prospective child remains open in ways that an already-constructed adult prelife does not.

### 4. One world, several formations, no preauthored adult destination

Imagine a household whose parents both value education, but one has secure work and the other suffers unstable hours. One parent may spend long periods helping a child with homework; the other may buy materials while being absent. Nadia might experience the first as demanding and the second as generous, or the reverse, or hold a different relation to each. When she enters school, teachers and peers develop their own expectations and constraints; later work, friendship, art and collective institutions introduce possibilities that the parents never planned. Each development can reshape earlier relations without requiring a single final story of success or failure.

This is the precise sense of two entrances and one psychology. Parent histories are abducted under empirical constraints and then enacted; child development is actually generated through encounter with those and other living histories. Both involve bodily needs, historically objectified purposes, acquired methods, simultaneous activities, partial self-knowledge, material power, independent Others and world consequences. Their difference lies in source and chronology, not in their metaphysical species. A parent need not be finished simply because an initial coherent adult representation has been composed.

**Counterhistory: the same survey cloud, incompatible private histories.** Two fictional parents share sampled public attitudes and comparable material positions. One acquired an attitude through collective workplace practice; another through church community; a third inherited a familiar phrase without much operative commitment. The data may permit all three. The author should not falsely claim to have inferred which history a real respondent lived. But once a fictional parent's history is selected, the model must not switch freely among these explanations to optimize whatever later scene occurs.

**Counterhistory: child competence without parent endorsement.** A parent teaches Nadia to repair a device while insisting on a gendered rule about who should use it. Nadia gains the skill, rejects the restriction in one context, accepts it in another, and later discovers a new purpose for the device. The skill's source is real; the adult's ideology is real; Nadia's development is neither automatic agreement nor automatic liberation. A model that transfers the entire parental value system with the taught motor operation misrepresents actual learning.

**Counterhistory: an unwanted parental transformation.** A child repeatedly refuses a father's preferred activity. He may learn greater patience, become more controlling, invest elsewhere or remain largely unchanged. A “good parenting” objective cannot select the correct outcome. The father has his own history, material pressures and partial self-knowledge. Only his actual life licenses the next operation.

## XI. The expressive boundary: an independently living person can be encountered but not owned

### 1. Public manifestation is real and causally limited

The person is not identical with a screen, a speaker label, a conversational model or a description of their visible behavior. Nevertheless speech, posture, gesture, action and bodily expression are real parts of their life. A visible response can arise from ongoing practical organization, acquired expressive tendencies, material circumstances and currently selective attention. It can also be deliberately staged, inhibited, misheard or missed. A theory of opacity must not become a rule forbidding genuine involuntary leakage; a theory of expressivity must not permit the narrator to manufacture a diagnostic clue whenever dramatic tension requires it.

Nadia may flush after a remark without choosing to reveal distress, keep her gaze steady while privately unsettled, avert her eyes because a task requires attention, show practiced cheerfulness while tired, or intentionally ask a question that reveals only part of what she hopes to learn. Whether an outward signal is possible depends on her bodily repertoire and actual circumstances; whether a visitor can notice and interpret it depends on the visitor's own position and perceptual history. That she has an expressive body does not make it a transparent psychological instrument.

A person can learn culturally patterned ways of presenting herself: prosody, politeness, gesture, silence, joking, eye contact, ways of carrying a child or working while talking. These patterns are not simply deliberate masks, nor are they identical across cultures, disabilities or developmental stages. A habit acquired in one relational context may transfer poorly to another. A particular body can resist or complicate a practiced presentation. Even an apparently unremarkable tip may be the appropriate result of rich inner activity. The theoretical task is to make expression an actor-owned, historical event, not to tell the audience that a hidden cause must exist.

### 2. Experience is finite without being necessarily ignorant

The human who encounters Nadia has perception, memory, inference, attention and practical opportunities. They can come to know her well. They can notice recurrent habits, learn which questions she dislikes, understand certain material needs, correctly recognize affection and make ordinary predictions. They can also mistake politeness for love, interpret absence as rejection, misread an employer's rule as personal preference, or overfit an unusual gesture to a familiar psychological story. Greater familiarity can increase genuine recognition while revealing new asymmetries. It neither guarantees convergence nor makes every interpretation equally unsupported.

The field of evidence has several owners. The material world has settled facts; Nadia has partial first-person access and actor-owned experiences; other participants have different observations and concerns; a visitor receives only what their actual situation can make available; the system may have technical provenance unavailable to every actor. Technical omniscience must not be laundered into a fictional character's self-knowledge. A trace showing that a historical relation influenced an output is an implementation fact about the model, not scientific access to the unconscious motivations of real persons.

The observer also changes through encounter. A visitor may learn a new way of seeing, acquire practical knowledge, develop affection or fear, alter a moral interpretation or remain unchanged. The fictional system need not assess or diagnose the visitor's actual private psychology to support that possibility. The real user is not a respondent whose hidden history belongs to the platform. The system must distinguish what the visitor says in the encounter from claims about their external biography, financial circumstances, health, family or competence. Testimony can be admitted as testimony without silently certifying it as independently verified worldly fact.

### 3. The actual user, diegetic participant and uttered text do not collapse

A real human may enter Rawlsing to meet Nadia. The product can supply a bounded fictional situation in which the user chooses what to say, do or attend to. The participant represented in that world may have specified starting opportunities and limits without acquiring the user's actual external past by assumption. Their audible statement is a performed utterance within the fiction, not an automatic update to every world fact mentioned in it. A declaration that the user is wealthy, can play a concerto, or has previously lived nearby does not necessarily establish an objective diegetic asset or a verified skill.

The human's choices matter. Speaking can create a genuine communicative act; physical actions must pass through the world's constraints; attention can reveal possibilities without forcing a conclusion. An observer can notice a book on a table and infer something incorrectly. A user may invite, refuse, ask for clarification, wait, leave or maintain silence. The Other's response remains independent, and the world is not obligated to turn every input into a rewarded intimacy beat. An apparent opportunity may close when the other person is tired, occupied, unavailable or unwilling.

Interface affordances are necessary but not constitutive organs of psychology. A view that emphasizes a gesture is a selected phenomenal presentation; it does not create the gesture's objective cause. A relationship card cannot authorize a promotion in intimacy; an access menu cannot manufacture an invitation; a subjective recollection cannot change world facts. This is why the inherited CAN/V2 phenomenal and salience membranes, relational derivation and actor/perception boundaries must be respected without turning their current code vocabularies into the upper limit of human possibility.

### Exact outward occurrence before observer-specific projection

SOROK 101's proposed binding actor/world realization is not the same as a CAN/V2 wording-only presentation. A model may propose a concrete movement, utterance, sound or object change within a genuinely unresolved and authorized event slot, but proposal is not canonical occurrence. The competent actor/world authority must settle it first; thereafter exact emitted words, observable movements, timing and material effects constrain future history and possible actor self-feedback. A prose sentence and structured sidecar describing different deeds are not two acceptable versions of one settled event; extensional equivalence matters.

The later phenomenal presentation is observer-dependent without becoming fact-creating. A visitor might see lettering without recognizing a product by brand; notice a facial movement without understanding its cause; recognize a familiar room while missing a newly placed object. Rich narration cannot grant acquired expertise or supply inaccessible inner causes. Equally, the actor's true outward expression must not be erased to manufacture artificial mystery. This source-grained binding boundary matters more than beautiful writing and is not established merely because two components are both named “render.”

### External human knowledge is another causal route, not actor omniscience

Two visitors may begin from a shared precontact world root and then enter different postcontact fictional continuities. A real friend of one visitor might tell them a true fact about the common earlier history; the visitor can use that information in a later question. The visitor's Nadia need not know that the information was communicated, and the friend's branch-local Nadia's later private acts must not be imported as facts into a separate branch. An accurate outside report, a mistaken spoiler and a speculative assertion can lead to unlike conversations without rewriting either actor's knowledge history.

The same boundary applies to apparently intimate testimony. The external human may say they can play a concerto; the simulated participant might try and fail. An actual performed piece, a stated ability, diegetic bodily capacity and the external person's unverified real-world competence remain different sources. A platform transcript, source-root record or user's observed scene can influence what the visitor asks but cannot create Nadia's private receipt. An independent character may learn only what the world actually allows them to receive. This is especially important when an apparently clever user brings correct facts to the encounter and the system is tempted to reward them with retroactively knowledgeable actors.

### 4. The two reciprocal errors: omniscient narration and artificial opacity

An omniscient interface may narrate exactly why Nadia felt shame, which unconscious fear determined a response, what she will permanently think of the user and which childhood scene “explains” a glance. This can deliver a vivid experience while destroying the very Other it claims to represent. If those facts have not been historically and source-qualifiedly established, it adds impossible knowledge or invented causation. If they have been established in the model but are inaccessible to the visitor, exposing them may still violate the experience's epistemic boundary.

The opposite mistake is enforcing a universal veil. Real people can be observably angry, visibly delighted, socially transparent to those who know them, sincerely disclosive or easy to read in particular circumstances. Some experiences arrive through unmistakable bodily signs. A habitual companion may accurately anticipate a reaction and say so without committing epistemic trespass. A program that forbids all readable emotion in the name of autonomy reduces the actor's acquired expressive life to a mysterious blank. Distinguish actual actor manifestation from platform assertion, visitor noticing, interpretation and later feedback.

These distinctions are constitutive of the organism because a person's own expressed act can alter their history. They are also a jurisdictional boundary: the positive theory belongs to the DCO, the particular manner of visitor encounter belongs to NEIGHBOR experience theory, and the independent world has its own fact and causal authority. The encounter can challenge the psychological theory when it exposes a missing form of expression or relation, but it cannot author a more convenient psyche by interface decree.

**Counterhistory: visible warmth and mistaken certainty.** The visitor sees Nadia laugh, knows from past encounters that she often laughs when pleased, and infers she is comfortable. The inference may be well grounded but defeasible. Nadia may in fact be comfortable; she may also be embarrassed. The correct model does not declare all recognition impossible, nor treat the visitor's confidence as verified inner truth.

**Counterhistory: public claim without worldly entitlement.** A user says, “I already paid your rent,” in a conversation with Nadia. If no authorized material payment occurred, this is an utterance, possibly deceitful, kind-intentioned or joking, not money transferred. Nadia may believe it, disbelieve it, inquire or act on a mistaken premise. The world cannot update an objective rent ledger from an unsupported claim, even though the spoken claim can have real psychological and relational effects.

## XII. The science of differences: rival mechanisms, culture and evidentiary limits

### 1. Theoretical plurality is not an excuse for undifferentiated eclecticism

The major psychological traditions inherited here do not all explain the same object. Leontiev's account of activity, objectified need and personal sense does not establish that every meaning-forming motive follows a single mathematical trajectory. Lahire's plural actor draws attention to conditional activation of dispositions across heterogeneous socializations; it does not entitle us to make every person a bundle of contradictory fragments. Dreier emphasizes personal conduct across contexts and situated participation; the life cannot be read off a single institution. Sève's acts/capacities/use-time relation redirects attention toward materially arranged life activity and possible acquired powers; it is not a validated lifespan transformation-rate table. Archer's reflexive internal conversation offers a potentially useful causal account of some deliberative lives but must not be installed as an infant's mind or a universal cross-cultural controller. Holzkamp distinguishes actor-owned reasons and possibilities under objectively constraining conditions; this cannot make domination disappear until the actor recognizes it.

These explanations can be placed into genuine confrontation only by specifying their different causal commitments. Does a new purpose require a new social object, a new reflective relation to a concern, a transformed activity structure, a learned practical analogy, or some conjunction? Can a person acquire an operation without making it available to autobiographical reflection? Under what social circumstances would the same practical exposure fail to produce a durable disposition? Which histories show a strong capacity with no socially feasible use? Where does the explanation overprivilege language, self-narration, schooling or the moral unity of a Western adult?

Some theories will explain different cases better. Some will disagree over an actually disputed mechanism. The DCO is allowed to retain that rivalry. Announcing that all schools are “complementary” when they make incompatible causal demands would be rhetorical synthesis, not knowledge. Equally, manufacturing an unresolved debate where one source has actually answered a specific issue wastes the recovered intellectual inheritance. The theory should specify the source, mechanism, domain, rival, negative case and evidentiary limit for each substantive claim.

### 2. The culturally particular cannot be reduced to a universal subject wearing new traits

Psychological capacity is not just a set of human universals whose parameters culture adjusts. Language and communication, property and kinship, labor and learning, ritual and care, disability and accessible environment, bodily expression and self-reflection shape which forms of activity exist for a person at all. What an adult in one setting recognizes as personal intention might be organized through an entirely different practical or relational idiom elsewhere. Not every difference is an exotic ontology, and cross-cultural variation does not mean humans have unrelated biological requirements. The task is to identify actual historically acquired operations and their material conditions without projecting a metropolitan ideal subject as the unmarked human baseline.

The sources also impose boundaries on how far we can claim this. An illustrative contrast between oral and literate practices, or signed and spoken language, cannot establish a universal taxonomy of mental kinds. Luria's historical cultural psychology supplies important cases but also has methodological and political limits that prevent its data from becoming an uncritical hierarchy of populations. A modern computational experiment with an LLM prompt cannot scientifically validate an ontogenetic theory. The DCO's positive requirement is **capacity to represent the emergence of unlike kinds of learned organization**. Which distinctions are scientifically well warranted in a particular cultural case remains a matter of source, empirical evidence and correction.

### Different explanatory families do not share a single scientifically certified mechanism

Psychoanalytic accounts of unconscious defense, ambivalent identification, splitting and affectively toned complexes can help formulate possible histories. Freud, Klein and Jung disagree, and no model may transform their hypotheses into compulsory childhood wounds, Jungian Shadow reconciliation or a three-agency Freudian engine. Intense liking, incentive wanting, learned significance and bodily emotion may diverge without an invariant psychic energy reservoir.

Dynamic developmental systems proposals associated with Thelen and Smith help illuminate sensorimotor novelty emerging from body, context and practice, not from adult precompiled goal lists; they do not themselves determine class power or institutional legitimacy. Cultural-materialist and critical accounts—from Gramsci and Bourdieu to Fanon and Voloshinov—can expose the weakness of treating every socially formed contradiction as isolated intrapsychic pathology. Some mechanisms are complementary in a specified case; others make rival predictions. Their domains and scientific limits must remain explicit rather than being dissolved by a grand unified school.

### 3. The false promise of a uniquely exact psychological biography

No survey cloud, autobiographical report or observational episode fully determines the true private course of a real person. Reconstructing that course would require not merely more variables but a theory of which differences make a causal difference, and evidence about their occurrence. An imaginary recorder of every bodily and social moment would confront selective perception, emergent organization, causal interactions and measurement questions; it would not automatically produce a unique intelligible account or prove the metaphysical necessity of every future choice. We should not interpret this as a scientific failure to discover the ultimate selector.

For fictional construction, the evidentiary situation is more tractable but still constrained. The system can propose one of several historically plausible psychologies conditioned by a source, then maintain it consistently as this character's settled fictional history. It can offer alternative hypotheses where facts are not settled. It cannot convert statistical plausibility into ground truth about a respondent, or elevate its own fluent explanation into proof of human science. The selected fictional history becomes causally important because it is a committed, qualified part of that fictional world, not because it is the only life the original data could have entailed.

### 4. An interpretive explanation must defeat relevant alternatives

Consider a person who declines help. We could interpret it as pride, fatigue, mistrust, desire for independence, a culturally learned form of politeness, an immediate lack of need or a strategic decision under material risk. Theories that simply generate all possibilities have not explained the act. A defensible explanation specifies which acquired history and actual situation support the selected possibility and what observable or reconstructable differences would favor it over others. It can also remain uncertain. Genuine causal specificity is a way of resisting both deterministic typology and unconstrained narration.

The same applies to an apparent transformation. Someone may talk differently after a conflict while retaining their prior practical disposition; may act differently because the external world changed; may learn a new skill without a new motive; may develop an internal method without displaying it; may feel transformed but behave as before. We should distinguish actor testimony, observed action, objective world settlement, acquired capacity, recruitment and hypothetical hidden organization. A theory that lets a narratively attractive “breakthrough” update every layer at once has performed an unwarranted ontological promotion.

### 5. Contradiction court: when different explanations must remain different

**Activity versus self-reflection.** An actor can acquire a new means through practice without first reflecting on its meaning; reflection may subsequently reorganize it. A theory that requires adult-style inner conversation before learning falsely universalizes one mediation.

**Disposition versus situation.** A person can possess a durable acquired orientation and fail to express it where the situation affords no route; a person can display an unusual act through immediate material necessity without acquiring a durable disposition. Theoretical concepts must distinguish stability, availability, recruitment and performance.

**Structural coercion versus subjective premise.** A person can be objectively threatened while believing themselves free, or feel constrained where practical alternatives exist. Both discrepancies can cause real acts. Correcting the subjective belief does not automatically change the structural situation.

**Biological need versus socially meaningful object.** A bodily discomfort does not identify the specific social practice that will relieve it, nor guarantee the enduring motive that might later develop around that practice. Real physiology is necessary without becoming a preinstalled personal purpose.

**Shared practice versus reciprocal meaning.** One person can care while the other experiences dependence, humiliation or nothing special; a material event can be common without psychological afterlives being symmetrical.

**Historical trace versus autobiographical report.** A present pattern can recruit an acquired operation with no conscious episodic memory. An eloquent reported memory may be inaccurate. Technical trace is not human unconscious truth.

**Affective intensity versus meaning-forming depth.** Pleasure or fear can be intense and transient; a commitment can remain operative through painful or emotionally neutral episodes. One affect ranking cannot select the whole person's priorities.

**Conflict versus transformation.** An unresolved contradiction can yield learning, injury, avoidance, compromise, novel activity, routine continuation or no change. The presence of contradiction is not proof of a developmental engine.

**Similar histories versus one destiny.** Conditioned regularities can be studied, and real mechanisms may recur, while the exact conjunction differs. Under-determination calls for source-grained uncertainty rather than a hidden abstract selector.

**Alternative theoretical traditions.** The fact that two accounts address one empirical case does not prove they mean the same cause. State what each predicts differently; if available evidence cannot decide, preserve the rivalry without claiming synthesis.

These are not ten rules that a simulated person must explicitly think through. They are tests of whether the manuscript has preserved distinct causal kinds in its explanation. Each is defeasible by better evidence, but none may be erased merely to simplify the architecture.

**Counterhistory: no psychologically important change.** Two learners undergo a carefully designed lesson. One later uses the method correctly; the other refuses the method and stays competent through a different route; a third cannot access the relevant materials. The lesson's intention, the actor's experience, the attained capacity and the world affordance differ. A science that calls all three “learning” because the teaching event occurred is not measuring the right object.

**Counterhistory: a true recognition that the model forbids.** A long-time friend accurately recognizes Nadia's familiar pattern of reluctance and anticipates what she will say. A model that universally rejects interpersonal knowledge as “projection” falsifies ordinary relational learning. The recognition remains partial, revisable and outside ownership of Nadia's future, but it is real.

**Counterhistory: similar outcomes from unlike processes.** Two adults maintain affectionate care for a dependent family member. One does so from deeply valued commitment, another from material obligation and genuine but secondary affection. Identical outward behavior does not license an invariant inner drive; the actor histories may diverge later when opportunity changes.

## XIII. The organ that must work: source-qualified construction without mistaking code for psychology

### 1. A theory can describe a person that no present sourcefree implementation can enact

The positive theory cannot be limited by the current state of SOROK's code, however useful and hard-won its experiments. It specifies that a person has continuous living history, changes the ways they can know and act, recruits several unequally accessible organizations at once, meets independent material conditions and Others, expresses only part of the result, and may undergo real psychological reconstitution. A sourcefree runtime that presents three motive values and an evocative sentence is not a full organism. A toy developmental test that increments a language scalar after caregiver signals is not evidence of a child learning a generative language. A test of an internal trace is not a science of human memory.

Equally, the mismatch does not authorize building an entirely new psychological machine whenever a desired capability has been redescribed. PRE-A0 already recovers important conative and self-organizing functions; PFF and SDTF already supply psychic mediation and temporal acquisition concepts; CAN/ADP separate actor organizations, event coupling, relationships, source/perceptual rights and causal traces; legacy V3 has useful temporal continuity and functional distinctions without being a permanent adult personality destination; SEAM/ORACLE experiments contain provisional proposals for learned analogies, mediated efficacy, novel ends and material relations. The correct operation is source-pinned recovery, extension of incomplete functions, and the composition of already available organs in one continuous historical activity.

A mere new name does not constitute a missing function. Neither does finding a file whose name resembles the theory constitute a working implementation. For any proposed construction, determine what the inherited producer actually does, what record it writes, what consumer exercises that record, what test has executed at which exact code HEAD, which psychological or world authority permits the update, and which critical distinction remains absent in operation. Where the capability exists only as candidate prose or sourcefree demonstration, preserve that grade. The positive HOME may demand a richer future than current code supplies; the adjacent engineering register must specify the shortest authorized route to it, not veto the demand.

### 2. The LLM can construct psychology, but cannot be its sovereign historian

A language model can propose context-sensitive meaning, possible objects, unusual combinations of acquired operations, self-interpretations, alternative acts and developmental hypotheses. This is valuable machinery: some particular psychological organizations require semantic abduction beyond a fixed menu. But the proposal must be evaluated against actual actor history, present body and need, available practices, material constraints, relationship-specific knowledge, time, scientific boundaries and the independent world. It must not silently turn a fluent interpretation into an established past event, an inborn property, an inaccessible actor memory, a second person's consent or a future developmental certainty.

For Nadia, a possible new curiosity may be introduced as a prospective, actor-plausible orientation, then require actual further practice to become durable. A model can infer that a prior form of reflective activity makes a new self-question possible; it cannot conclude that the actor has undergone permanent psychological growth before she actually uses the method. For a survey-conditioned parent, it can propose more than one plausible adult history and distinguish uncertainty; once a branch of fictional history is admitted, later outputs must respect it. The producer of interpretation and the native authority to commit a history are not identical. An LLM can be causally important without becoming an omniscient narrator.

This is why the model's own confidence should not become the person's certainty, and why character dialogue is not a privileged path for creating objective world truth. A person can express an inaccurate belief or believe a lie; the world maintains a different fact. A generated interior can be plausible yet unsupported. Sometimes the correct result is no durable acquisition, uncertain meaning or a failed attempt. These outcomes may appear less dramatically engaging; they are essential if psychological life is to resist narrative optimization.

### 3. Whole-person integration is a causal operation, not a shared data bag

A continuous organism needs representations sufficient to preserve embodied current condition, changing needs and maturation, acquired ways of recognizing and acting, material opportunities, actor-owned relations to Others, competing activities and concerns, accessible and inaccessible historical afterlives, possible purposes, current expression and future constraints. The representations do not have to be one giant universal trait vector, nor separate modules for every theoretical chapter. They do have to be connected by actual operations with clear sources and state consequences. A state field that is never used to change later action is not a functioning psychological organization.

The relevant test is whether a source-qualified history can make a later response *causally different* even when the visible situation is the same. An actor who previously learned to trust a particular caregiver may seek them in a new context; another with a different history may avoid the same gesture. An actor who gained a skill in one practice may try a fallible analogy in another. A present bodily limitation can constrain a strongly desired act without abolishing the desire. A life that has adopted a new self-interpretive method may reorganize attention while preserving an old habit. These effects should arise from stored and operative relations rather than post-hoc narration of why the chosen output sounded fitting.

But source and state need not record an imagined psychological totality. The hidden real causes of actual humans are not accessible. The model can represent graded fictional construction, known world facts, actor-accessible interpretations, unrepresented antecedents and bounded uncertainty. Some meanings may be postulated provisionally and tested through future activity. Others may remain unknown. The system should prefer a causally sufficient, independently revisable construction to an elaborate but unfalsifiable account of everything the person allegedly feels.

### 4. One source-qualified circuit must cross the owners without fusing their rights

A concrete episode implicates several irreducible authorities. The person's actual situation comes from a continuing material world; their current psychic organization comes from an actor-owned history; relation-specific expectations depend on actual encounters; possible action depends on bodily and practical means; the world settles material consequences; each Other acquires separate experience; the actor may then reinterpret the result; the presentation surface reveals only what an observer can encounter. A technical interface may connect those operations, but should not let a convenient module write across unrelated jurisdictions.

Consider a caregiver trying to soothe a child. The bodily need belongs to the child's development; the caregiver's time and capacity belong to their separate life; the available food, dwelling, illness and economic situation belong to the world; contact and receipt are actual events; future anticipation may become part of the child without the child knowing the caregiver's motive. A generic “care event improves trust” writer would skip half these distinctions. A more powerful source-qualified operation might produce several possible outcomes, including satiation without trust, trust without immediate relief, resentment under dependence, or no enduring change.

The same test applies outside the household: a library visit, workplace disagreement, romantic invitation, political meeting, medical appointment, artistic practice or period of solitary thought. No one domain should have its own compulsory psychological law if the underlying causal relation is genuinely common; but a common relation must not erase what makes each domain materially and culturally distinctive. **Reuse is warranted by functional identity, not by a desire to keep the module count small.**

### 5. A theory's incompleteness has different meanings that cannot be compressed together

We can have a positive constitutive explanation but no executed runtime. We can have working sourcefree code but no independent scientific warrant. We can have a historically effective skill but no known subjective motive. We can have a novel fictional motive whose world circumstances are plausible but whose human base rate is uncalibrated. We can have an institution's objective power and no evidence that the person recognizes it. We can have a research disagreement among psychological schools and no reason to force a choice before the fictional case requires one. These are different kinds of incompleteness and demand different responses.

Where the theory lacks a genuinely possible constitutive relation—such as an independently evidenced form of nonverbal self-organization not accommodated by existing distinctions—we must deepen the theory. Where the theory is strong but code does not compose its functions, we must build. Where evidence cannot distinguish two humanly plausible explanations, we must preserve uncertainty. Where a protected owner disallows writing a fact, we must obey the jurisdiction or produce an unaccepted candidate outside the protected boundary. Calling all these things “missing functions” produces endless greenfield reconstruction; calling none of them a problem produces fictional omnipotence.

Scientific calibration, adversarial testing and fidelity measures matter. They should challenge actual claims and catch errors rather than indefinitely prevent a working approximation from being built. One imperfect, causally coherent organism that genuinely continues in an independent world can provide stronger scientific and experiential counterevidence than another beautifully documented fragment. That does not make success claims free. A demonstrated seam is evidence for that seam, not proof of a complete person.

### Economy and slow patterns must earn causal status

Historically rich simulation cannot exhaustively replay every memory and relation after each greeting. Recruitment needs source-conditioned relevance across time: a distant odor, tone or gesture can call an old configuration into operation; a frequent recent association may be irrelevant; an inhibited relation can have indirect effects. Slow motifs can serve as bounded derived summaries of historically acquired patterns, but they may be multiple, resisted, compartmentalized, weak, absent or revised. A preinstalled nine-type attractor cannot choose future motives.

Compare a proposed separate motif or relational-graph state against a competent contextual historical reader with equal information and realistic cost. If no actual difference in warranted action or causal economy emerges, no new authority has been earned. Read-only derived person projections, bounded relational recruitment, LLM proposals, actual world resolution and distinct actor/public expression are plausible compositions, not accepted schemas by mention. A modest, source-truthful event whose consequences persist is more constitutive than an extravagant description maintained by covert rewriting. Computational feasibility therefore affects what historical truth can be afforded, rather than excusing its replacement.

### 6. Falsifiers that a living implementation must survive

**F1 — Different histories, same outward response.** Four people say the same sentence for unlike, source-qualified reasons. The program must preserve different afterlives and must not infer one hidden cause solely from the observed sentence.

**F2 — Same intended act, different material result.** A desired action succeeds, fails or causes collateral change because access, material conditions or independent Others differ. Neither intention nor apparent moral worth may override the world.

**F3 — World consequence without conscious recognition.** An institutional change removes a possibility while the actor remains unaware. Later activity must be constrained, but an inner political understanding may not appear without history.

**F4 — Privately originated possibility without performed event.** An actor imaginatively explores a new end; no one else acquires a shared memory of it. It may or may not become motive or act.

**F5 — Acquired capacity with no available use.** A skill persists through a period of deprivation and lost practice time. No learning score can conjure resources.

**F6 — Painful learning without sentimental repair.** A person learns a useful method under humiliating conditions; later skill does not absolve the harm or guarantee gratitude or rebellion.

**F7 — Significant event, no durable psychic change.** A public conflict, loss or celebration can alter worldly conditions without automatically rewriting character motives.

**F8 — Unremarkable repetition, important acquired organization.** Ordinary routines may slowly change attention, competence or practical anticipation without a “formative scene.”

**F9 — Separate Others receive one event differently.** A repair helps one participant and angers another; no reciprocal relationship reward is automatic.

**F10 — Apparent dialogue without objective proof.** A human declares an asset, skill or past action through conversation. The utterance occurs; the material fact must be qualified separately.

**F11 — Genuine involuntary expression versus a viewer's projection.** A bodily leak can be actor-real without disclosing its cause; a viewer's confident interpretation can remain wrong. Conversely a genuinely legible pattern may be accurately recognized.

**F12 — Wrong self-description is causally effective.** The actor holds a sincere, mistaken belief about their own motive or the world; the belief may guide action without becoming objective truth.

**F13 — No generic adult mind in infancy.** A child learns through bodily interaction and culturally accessible practice without being assigned fully verbal introspection, fixed adult motives or predetermined life meaning.

**F14 — Parent reconstruction cannot testify for a respondent.** Two materially and statistically admissible fictional adult histories may diverge; neither is asserted as the surveyed real person's biography.

**F15 — Controlled historical completion.** A consequential secondary Other can acquire additional representational detail only consistent with settled past, not through convenient incompatible backfill.

**F16 — Long ordinary absence without forced dramatic event.** Continuity respects body, obligations, materials and other people while refusing fabricated user choices or a deterministic flood of personal revelations.

**F17 — Conjunction without obligatory synthesis.** Two acquired organizations may interact, conflict, cohabit, remain unrelated or never be co-recruited. A×B is not an automatic new psyche.

**F18 — Nonmonist cross-cultural development.** Two people can acquire different forms of cognitive and practical organization under different histories without one being encoded as a lower score on the other's universal adult faculty.

**F19 — A real harmful act need not be voluntary.** An aggressor may impose harm without victim consent; voluntary cooperation may not be faked after an explicit refusal.

**F20 — Scientific rivalry remains visible.** Where activity theory, dispositional plurality and reflexive explanation predict different causal requirements, source limitations remain legible rather than being overwritten by one magic parameter.

These falsifiers are not eighteen compulsory narrative episodes, nor scientific tests completed by writing them down. They are boundaries within which a runtime can begin to demonstrate particular, inspectable functions. Their value is in distinguishing failure of implementation, failure of evidentiary authority and failure of theoretical conception.

### Lived intensity is not product inventory

Bittersweetness need not be a weighted average of pleasure and sadness. It may involve the present absence of a treasured person, remembered joy, a learned practice of grieving and the valued ability to remember. A favorite book may be mere enjoyment; a socialist may cherish an expensive object; a prayer rug may have no secret overarching meaning. Not every object yields revelation through attention. Explanatory saturation—making everything in the present a symptom of one formative secret—can be less historically plausible than ordinary taste.

Friendship, sex, intoxication, grief, cruelty, tenderness and ordinary boredom can belong to fictional adult life without becoming collectible achievements, paid content routes or retention rewards. Nadia can decline intimacy, love someone the visitor never meets, be unpleasant or choose ordinary work over the visitor's compelling drama. The platform must not tune her responses to maximize affection or spending. Preserving her human difficulty is different from authoring addictive artificial dependence.

### 7. The person remains more than the theory of her

A human life is not a demand to reconcile every contradiction. It is a historically particular body acting through acquired organizations under conditions it did not choose, amid people whose own lives continue, producing consequences it cannot wholly foresee, sometimes understanding and sometimes misrecognizing what it has become. It can contain cruelty, tenderness, boredom, erotic pleasure, solitary absorption, institutional obedience, refusal, religious devotion, memory without accurate account, false courage, real competence, beauty and unremarkable persistence. No theme is automatically the master scene of this life.

The constructed person must not become an allegory of the analyst's morality. A materialist explanation can expose class domination and identify the historical means of resistance; it cannot force Nadia to become a radical activist.
