## IV. The R0 kernel: real engineering progress, and one newly demonstrated defect

**The provisional constitutional kernel I produced in the preceding turn deserves the same hostile treatment.**

**I reopened its actual Python implementation and independently reran its tests. All 15 tests pass. They verify bounded properties of the isolated demonstrator; they do not prove full personhood or SOROK integration.**

**More importantly, I found and executed a missing negative test.**

**In the R0 model, an independently instantiated helper can have an explicit project directing them to decline participation. The correct coactor-side reader returns `DECLINE`.**

**But the lower-level `perform()` function accepts a caller-supplied `partner_consent=True` without independently validating that decision.**

**I executed exactly that case:**

```
Helper's own decision: DECLINE
Direct perform(..., partner_consent=True): PERFORMED
Objective joint history: child|helper → E1
```

**This is a genuine authority-bypass defect in the provisional demonstrator. The higher-level `perform_coupled()` path checks the helper; the lower-level public path permits a forged consent assertion.**

**The remedy is straightforward and consequential: actual joint performance must consume a verified, time-bound, independently issued coactor action receipt. Consent cannot be a Boolean supplied by the requesting actor or the caller.**

**It is a small bug that exposes a fundamental personhood requirement:**

> **Another person's independence is not established by storing them as a separate actor. It is established when the causal system cannot produce their participation without their own operative history and authorized conduct.**

**This is exactly the kind of computational falsifier the architecture needs.**

**The kernel has further substantive limitations. Its `joint_covered_formation()` accepts a declared condensed-history count and a caller-specified new operation, rather than generating a new functional distinction from the simulated actor's experiences. Its `PracticalProject` instances have preassigned ends rather than historically generated, changing relationships among activities. Its `INHIBITS` relation can be admitted from an accessible event without demonstrating that the event justifies the particular psychological inhibition. And its shared relational history records occurrences but not the developing reciprocal attunement or mutual organization you are describing.**

**So the R0 demonstrator currently has a typed activity and capability scaffold, not an adequate theory of practical-psychological genesis.**

**That is not a reason to throw it away. It tells us how to improve it: replace caller-declared psychological effects and partner permissions with source-grounded, actor-specific, executable constitution and coactivity operations.**
