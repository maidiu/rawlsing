## 1. PYAT has identified the actual problem. Here's the part SOROK can add.

UM-PYAT is right: PYAT's empirically conditioned **co-traveling superstructure**—including contradictory and minority bundles—must not get discarded and replaced by an SES-to-money-and-shifts gate. Neither may one move from an adult's survey checkbox to a child's attitude. The powerful candidate sequence is **same-respondent survey report → context-dependent adult repertoire → actually enacted social relations → child-accessible experience and activity → developing plural habitus → later person**.

**The irreducible SOROK addition is the *historically acquired practical premise*.** The child is not just accumulating experiences or internalizing values. Through actual participation and partial observation, they acquire fallible, context-dependent ways of anticipating **how the world works for them**—what a person may do, who can make whom do what, which costs are probable, what people seem to admire or condemn, what can be attempted, whose explanations deserve trust, what kind of life can be sought, and what *their own* prior activity has made them able to do. These are NOT nine additional scores or a universal cognitive ontology. They are a **candidate minimal explanatory state** under existing CAN/P3 actor owners, whose contents must be justified by actual experience and later use.

Klaus Holzkamp's 1992 *On Doing Psychology Critically* makes the missing distinction sharply: social possibility structures are not external stimuli directly determining human action; they become premises for a subject's reasons for acting. (Peer-reviewed abstract: https://doi.org/10.1177/0959354392022007.) Sève's acts/capacities and use-time; Leontiev's activity/motive/personal sense; Lahire's plural context-sensitive dispositions; Dreier's conduct across places; and Barbara Rogoff's guided participation (https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/sociocultural-studies-of-mind/observing-sociocultural-activity-on-three-planes-participatory-appropriation-guided-participation-and-apprenticeship/9EDBE646EE0FD4AB2375C8C8A7C5E621) suggest compatible *questions* but no ready numerical U rule. This is an explicitly synthetic research proposition, not an adopted scientific finding.

The strongest causal asymmetry is a **three-way nonidentity**:

- **Objective world:** what actually occurred, which relations of control and sanction exist, what was possible or impossible.
- **Actor's practical premise:** what THIS historically formed actor has become prepared to anticipate, attempt, justify, conceal, value, or question from their limited experience.
- **Public performance/claim:** what this actor says or does toward THIS audience; may not reflect the actor's full premise, and never automatically rewrites objective world truth.

The premise need not be *reflectively formulated*. A person can act as though a rule is self-evident, find it suddenly strange, or be unable to give a conscious account. A premise need not be *true*: observing a supervisor's punishment can lead the child to expect punishment in settings where it will not occur, without ever learning the unseen real reason for the supervisor's act. **Misrecognition must be generable from incomplete causal access**, not manually pasted into a backstory.

## 3. Two genuinely different adult enactment K hypotheses (with null and contradiction)

Each socializer has their own **historically generated** actor state; PYAT reports only a bounded empirical response-profile *proposal*, not a motive or action policy.

**K-E / expressive-norm model.** Given respondent-like normative saliences, *relevant role*, audiences, relation, and ongoing activity, a socializer may **say** or teach a principle, describe how “we” behave, correct a story, quote a sermon, or remain silent for reputational reasons. Observable output: actor-emitted words or silence, with exact witness. A Q8 selection might increase a candidate tendency to praise independent problem-solving in an eligible context, but neither ensures this utterance nor effects any sanction. The K-E hypothesis predicts more *heard articulated justifications* than objectively enforced consequences under otherwise comparable conditions. Its parameters are NOT identified by the WVS checkbox.

**K-P / practical-enactment model.** The socializer's actually available rights, time, institution and relationships condition a **performed allocation, reward, sanction, work/care practice, conspicuous compliance, refusal or protected breach**. An adult can report both obedience and independence, obey a supervisor under threat while teaching the child how to organize a different practical arrangement, or speak defiance but enact compliance. Output: actor/world occurrence with material consequences and possibly *no verbal explanation*. K-P predicts more context-indexed child observations of **costs, opportunities, and role-conditioned hypocrisy** than explicit doctrine alone. No attitude response confers the power to sanction.

**Null / refusal controls:** adult report unchanged, no enactment; adult acts while child absent; adult says one thing but institution compels another; audience sees act but not underlying motive; child has contrary practice with a peer or teacher. All legitimate. K-E and K-P are **rival limited hypotheses** as to the route, not a dogmatic dichotomy requiring exactly one mechanism for every adult.

**Potential harder hypothesis for PYAT to enrich:** adult utterance may itself be a practical act with real consequences (threat, promise, organizing call, institutional decision) rather than mere description. Distinguish *performative causal speech* from a value-statement by actual authority/action gate and transaction. Formal semantics, not superficial speech-versus-action dichotomy, decide.

## 4. Two rival child uptake U functions; no generic “internalization” mutation

**U-P / observational predictive induction.** With an age-eligible, witnessed performed action and subsequent accessible consequence, the child may acquire or revise a **conditional expected outcome**: “when this kind of person asks this supervisor for a change, a cost tends to follow.” Future behavior may become cautious **without endorsement**, skill or comprehensive knowledge of the actual causal mechanism. A single witnessed sanction supports at most a narrow sourcefree model premise, not an empirically calibrated lifelong fear. Speech only supplies “adult said X,” not automatically the validity of X.

**U-A / participatory appropriation.** The child must have **actually tried, coordinated, helped, practiced, refused, or successfully received guided participation** in a task. Recurrent accomplished activity can support a **narrow practiced competence, coordination expectation, relational confidence or project opening** under its original context. Merely seeing an adult act does not automatically create child skill; a child may imitate, but this is a different hypothesis that needs an explicitly witnessed/practiced step. This tracks Rogoff's personal/interpersonal/community planes as research lenses, not a source-valid cognitive update coefficient.

Predictions differ in a future scene: U-P may correctly anticipate a risk but lack the practical skill for an alternate path; U-A may possess a coordination competence despite not having endorsed the adult's moral claim or even inferred the risk. Both can coexist in different contexts without averaging to a personality trait. **Inability, unwillingness, nonopportunity, inaccessible cause, nonattempt and failed attempt remain different.**

**U-X / later critical objectification (not a minimum rule).** After age/experience-eligible comparative encounters, the child or adult may discover an inherited premise as *someone's claim*, not a property of reality: school practice contradicts family speech; an elder demands deference while denying care; a collective action changes an apparently unalterable institution. A previously naturalized rule may become criticizable. Outcomes include self-criticism, renewed compliance, strategic performance, cynicism, anger, active resistance, or no change; no mandatory emancipatory arc. This corresponds to the original [Governing Spirit](https://github.com/maidiu/rawlsing/blob/6a5c12f04323d74001f9632063ec907f4fc4468e/data/ddm_build_copy/vozhd_dva/20260817_downstream_completion/RAWLSING_GOVERNING_SPIRIT.md)'s `identification→internalization→naturalization→invisibility→agent-of` and later alienation/externalization possibilities, without reifying the arrow sequence as universal law.

**A key emergent paradox:** a *norm transmitted to reproduce authority can supply the criterion later used to condemn that authority*. If one learns “leaders must protect those entrusted to them” through a high-authority household, then encounters an institution that repeatedly punishes the vulnerable, the inherited premise may authorize *challenge* to its own original carrier. This is **not scripted rebellion**; it requires the person actually receive both the norm and a recognizable contradiction, preserve their own stakes, and choose action under real feasible conditions. The same person could instead blame the victim or defend the authority. **Social reproduction carries its own possible negation.**

## 7. One humanly particular history: how the user learns the person through the seam

**Early scene:** adult caretaker C is generated under the *illustrative* WVS7 joint-response potential “independence important; obedience important” and actual time/power conditions. C gives a child an ordinary opportunity to decide how to organize a task, yet at work obeys a supervisor's time-demand under threat. The child at age eight witnesses the cost but cannot see the supervisor's private reasoning. A teacher offers a contrasting practice, or peers later actually organize something together. Some socializer statements may remain unperformed; some object-level institutions operate without anybody's ideological approval.

**Sparse life-course:** at age eight, a witnessed sanction becomes an available conditional expectation under U-P, not “authoritarian personality.” At age eleven, *if* the child actually succeeds in peer coordination, U-A may form bounded competence. At adolescence, a counterexample makes a previously invisible premise thinkable as one institutional claim among others, but can produce either affiliation or alienation, withdrawal or an effort to reorganize. No predestined emancipation, obligatory trauma, or typology.

**Player encounter in adulthood:** the player tells Nadia, “I could go tell your manager this isn't fair.” The *actual* Nadia has a separate current job, another caregiver, legitimate obligations and a history of practical coordination. Her response has multiple lawful possibilities: she might refuse an intervention because she has firsthand experience of sanction; propose a concrete peer-coordination route; accept practical assistance but reject a paternalistic explanation; misunderstand the player; have already taken action without them; or remain unavailable for ordinary independent reasons. Her exact chosen performance must be conditioned by current **CAN-008 R2 uptake and existing actor situation**, not hardcoded to deliver the player's enlightenment.

**The player's possible discovery:** “she isn't just stubborn” or “she isn't just submissive”; the earlier naive reading of her conduct fails because the player discovers a life formed under contradictory claims. Yet the user may *not* discover this or may draw the wrong lesson. A game that explains the entire hidden psychology in a revelatory monologue defeats the Other's independence. PFF only mediates lawful encounter/fast carryover; V2 speaks from the already admitted actor. Later success is a practical consequential choice and partial understanding, **not a higher empathy score or coerced gratitude**.

### Eight additional prospective first-reader discriminators

1. **K intervention:** same declared adult D and world, different enacted performance, different child accessible experience; no direct D→child trait.
2. **U intervention:** same child-visible episode, rival U-P and U-A yield distinct *later situated behavior*, not simply different stored tags.
3. **Rival authority:** same family, different actual school/peer/union encounter yields plural context-bound reasons; no averaged ideological middle.
4. **Misrecognition:** child anticipates a sanction for the wrong causal reason; later counterevidence can correct or harden expectation.
5. **As-of history:** 2020 report may not author the child's 1988 household; held origin/transport properly typed.
6. **Repetition custody:** original microevents and a valid summary envelope yield the same qualified future practice; an unsupported “years of exposure” assertion must refuse.
7. **Offscreen world:** institution or coactor action can change material possibilities without adding child appraisal; previous actor history pinned.
8. **Counter-practice:** actor can cite an inherited value to challenge its original enforcer when an actual contradiction is perceived, but may equally decline.

## 9. Scientific discrimination and future expansion (not false calibration)

The WVS7 Q8/Q17 questionnaire makes an excellent **semantics test**, but *not* an identified intergenerational causal experiment. PYAT's adult same-respondent cross-sectional patterns constrain generated adult public response ensembles and can test model aggregate *observable outputs* under comparable survey selection/weights/countries/periods. They do not reveal latent true ideology, same-family marriage coupling, parent→child causal coefficients, observed household sanctions or independent institutions.

To distinguish models scientifically, look for *temporally ordered* observation/parent-child exposure/behavior when appropriately accessible; distinguish third-party norm imitation from independent spontaneous enforcement; test skill transfer and prior opportunity; compare cohort or setting changes, then inspect if models yield different prospective predictions. A real 2016 Hardecker–Tomasello study (`N=120`, journal pub. 2017) found two- and three-year-olds more likely to enforce after seeing an adult enforce, but generalization differed by age (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27653172/). A 2026 study of US children ages six to nine (`N=234`) found normative judgments sensitive to descriptive frequencies but **not consistently revised from new information** (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41619366/). These findings constrain *what variants are worth testing*; they neither identify the Rawlsing U nor license one cross-cultural developmental calendar. Rogoff warns against universalizing middle-class Euro-American guided participation as a human norm.

For a real human product study, measure separately: (a) participant perception that actor commitments and situation are independent of them; (b) their *behavioral* accuracy in anticipating later other-directed choices from partial evidence; (c) willingness to revise a mistaken model without being rewarded with disclosure; (d) whether later behavior in actual human relationships changes if that can be ethically measured. Compare to an equivalent high-quality but script-driven character; *retention/affection are not proxies for transformation*. A positive trial on perceived reality never proves empirical psychological causation, and an honest scientific hold must not erase a useful experimental software positive.
