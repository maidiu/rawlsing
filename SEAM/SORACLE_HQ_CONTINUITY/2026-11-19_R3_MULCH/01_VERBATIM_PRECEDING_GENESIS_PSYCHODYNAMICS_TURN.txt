# The missing psychology may already be hiding in the relationship between activity, motive, affect and recruitment.

I think we've found something substantial. Not simply another source of inspiration, but a way to **reformulate both supposedly unsolved problems so that they begin to answer one another**.

The two questions were:

1. **Psychological genesis:** Why does an experience produce this particular acquired organization rather than another?
2. **Psychodynamic operation:** How do simultaneous needs, desires, fears, investments, contradictions and inhibitions actually combine into situated psychological life?

We've been approaching the first as a problem of *constructing psychological contents*, and the second as a problem of *combining those contents*.

That separation may itself be misleading.

**What is recruited into activity today is what becomes available for psychological reconstitution; what that activity produces and how it is experienced can reorganize what will be recruited tomorrow.**

The two problems are different temporal aspects of one historical process.

And the most important source I recovered is Leontiev—not simply his familiar distinction among activity, action and operation, but the relationship between **motive, personal sense, emotion and the emergence of new motives**.

## 1. Leontiev gives us a missing causal relationship

In *Activity, Consciousness, and Personality*, Leontiev makes a distinction that is especially important for SOROK:

An action can succeed technically while the activity it serves fails psychologically.

His well-known *bitter candy* example concerns a child who obtains an object by violating the conditions of the task. When rewarded, the child becomes distressed. The significance of what happened emerges from the relationship between different motives implicated in the action—not simply whether the action succeeded. <Cite ref="turn142000search3"/>

Then Leontiev develops the much more important principle: emotions can act as internal signals concerning the relationship between an activity's motives and its realized or anticipated consequences. The same act can carry different, even contradictory emotional significance because it participates in several activities simultaneously. <Cite ref="turn142000search0"/>

**This is almost exactly the kind of pressure-flow machinery we've been trying to formulate.**

Consider Nadia singing.

Her technical action succeeds: she sings the correct notes.

But the action participates in several possible activities:

- maintaining a shared activity with Mother;
- seeking Mother's delight;
- enjoying the sound itself;
- sustaining a place for another child's contribution;
- demonstrating competence;
- perhaps avoiding Mother's displeasure.

These aren't necessarily six independent desires competing for control of a body.

**They are different historically constituted relations in which the same action participates.**

Its success as one action does not imply success as all six activities.

Mother may praise Nadia's technical performance while humiliating the quieter child.

Nadia may feel pleased, unsettled, protective and resentful—not because some arbitrary motivational weights have collided, but because the event has different consequences for different activities in which she is invested.

The same event can therefore produce several different kinds of psychic afterlife.

A practice is strengthened. An expectation becomes doubtful. An affective response changes. A relationship becomes strained. A previously unarticulated distinction becomes possible.

And none of those changes requires Nadia to have a mature, explicit theory of what has occurred.

### Here is the first serious breakthrough

**Psychodynamic operation is not fundamentally an arithmetic operation over motives. It is the situated organization of multiple activities and relationships whose consequences can be simultaneously compatible, contradictory, unequally accessible and differently meaningful to the person.**

That gives CAN-008's multi-stream pressure-flow a much more determinate interpretation.

The streams need not be independent trait-generated forces.

They can arise from the **different activities, relations and historical investments implicated in one present occurrence**.

This also gives us a stronger starting point for genesis.

The psychological difference produced by an event depends partly on **which already constituted activities the event entered into, how it changed their continuation, and what the person was actually capable of experiencing about those changes**.

That does not fully determine which new organization forms.

But it narrows the constructive problem dramatically.

We no longer ask an LLM, in the abstract, what psychological property the event should create.

We ask what becomes of *this person's presently implicated, historically constituted activities* through *this actual encounter*.

That is a much richer foundation for psychological construction.

## 2. Vygotsky gives us the exact missing unit between event and developmental change

This is an even more striking recovery.

In *The Problem of the Environment* (1934), Vygotsky introduces **perezhivanie**—roughly, lived-through experience—as a way of understanding why an environmental event affects different children differently.

He discusses children living through the same troubled family circumstances whose developmental responses differ profoundly according to their age, capacities, relationships and understanding of what is happening.

His point is not simply that children interpret events differently. It is that **the developmental significance of an environment cannot be determined from the environment alone**.

The child's particular way of living through it is part of the causal relationship. <Cite refs={["turn493679search3","turn493679search6"]}/>

That is almost precisely the problem we've been calling psychological genesis.

And PRE-A0 had already conserved much of it through its three-layer distinction:

**Objective event ≠ lived realization ≠ psychic afterlife.**

But we had not fully recognized the theoretical significance of the middle.

### An event does not form a psyche. A person living through an event may be changed by it.

This does not mean the objective event ceases to matter.

Nor does it mean that the child's subjective interpretation becomes sovereign.

It means that the relevant developmental unit is something like:

**This organism, with this existing constitution, participating in these activities and relationships, living through this materially specific occurrence, with these accessible consequences.**

We don't need to create a new canonical `Perezhivanie` object.

PRE-A0 already has the necessary conceptual places for the objective event, developmental semantic realization, actor experience, nondeclarative trace and subsequent psychological effects.

What changes is how we understand their relationship.

The developmental generator should not primarily ask:

*Given event E, what new psychological property does Nadia acquire?*

It should ask:

*Given the activities, needs, investments and modes of orientation through which Nadia actually encountered E, what distinctions or changes in practical organization could this lived-through occurrence make possible?*

That is a major narrowing of the arbitrary-LLM-interpretation problem.

The model may still entertain several plausible outcomes.

But those outcomes have to emerge from the **specific relationship between the child's constitution and the occurrence**, not a generic catalogue of lessons that children supposedly learn from this category of event.

### The infancy connection becomes much clearer

A preverbal infant's perezhivanie need not be a conscious proposition.

It can involve an organismic and experiential configuration: arousal, familiar sensation, interruption, expectation, distress, orientation, a contingent response and an emerging practical relation.

Later linguistic experience can reorganize how those earlier acquired relations become operative.

That brings us directly to Luria.

In *Cognitive Development: Its Cultural and Social Foundations*, Luria describes the development of higher psychological functions partly in terms of **changed relationships among psychological processes**. Language and socially acquired operations reorganize memory, classification, attention and activity; they do not simply add more information to an unchanged mental apparatus.

**This is the higher-order genesis we've been seeking.**

A child doesn't first need a fully developed faculty for reorganizing the psyche.

Through participation in socially organized activities, the child acquires practices that can subsequently help organize attention, recollection, comparison, inhibition and action.

Vygotsky's work on signs and tools goes further: practices initially directed toward coordinating with others can become means of regulating one's own conduct. That offers a historical pathway to reflective self-regulation without installing a sovereign inner controller at birth. <Cite refs={["turn268434search0","turn268434search2"]}/>

The deeper possibility is that the **relations among psychological functions are themselves acquired historical products**.

So the development of the iceberg's recruitment pathways is not a decorative extension to psychology.

It is very close to what Vygotsky and Luria regarded as the development of higher psychological organization.

## 3. Sève gives us the missing recursion between acquiring a capacity and exercising it

Lucien Sève's *Man in Marxist Theory and the Psychology of Personality* supplies another unusually direct connection.

He distinguishes analytically between activities that **develop capacities** and activities that **exercise already developed capacities**. In a concrete life, these overlap: the exercise of a capacity can also develop it, and acquired capacities alter what subsequent activity becomes possible.

This is not an exhaustive account of personality, nor a scientifically issued computational rule.

But it gives us a stronger account of the relationship between formation and exercise than a generic `learn()` function.

Consider the same evolving practice:

Sève contributes something else that matters materially.

**A capability's development is not equivalent to the person's opportunity to exercise it.**

People develop capacities they cannot use under existing class, household, institutional or political conditions.

The social organization of time determines what gets practiced, what becomes habitual, what is suppressed, what deteriorates and what becomes central to a person's life.

That links psychological genesis to ordinary-life hypercompression.

The background scheduler is not mere logistical filler between formative scenes.

**The allocation of a person's life time across different activities is itself one of the principal conditions of their psychological development.**

And the same person may acquire capacities whose exercise remains prohibited, unrewarded or materially impossible.

This is why SOROK cannot reduce activity to a momentary decision menu.

## 4. Lahire explains how the iceberg becomes recruitable—and how recruitment goes wrong

This is where the connections become unusually precise.

In *The Plural Actor*, Bernard Lahire argues that the present situation does not merely provide a trigger for an already assembled individual.

**The present activates some portions of the incorporated past while inhibiting others.**

The same person can have acquired several differently situated, even contradictory ways of acting, feeling, evaluating and interpreting. Their expression depends upon the contexts in which they become available. <Cite ref="turn142000search9"/>

That is almost a direct theoretical foundation for the **iceberg–recruitment–tip** architecture.

But Lahire contributes something more specific: **practical analogy**.

A person can encounter a new situation and recruit an old organization through a resemblance they do not consciously identify.

The resemblance may concern a social relationship, a gesture, a tone of voice, a smell, a particular object or an ordinary situational configuration.

And the resemblance can be mistaken.

### This gives us three distinct operations

<box border radius="lg" padding={3} gap={2}>
  <box gap={1}>
    **Existing recruitment**

    An actual cue activates an acquired psychological organization in an already established context.
  </box>
  <divider color="subtle"/>
  <box gap={1}>
    **Provisional analogical recruitment**

    An unfamiliar situation is sufficiently similar to a prior one that an acquired practice, expectation or defensive organization becomes a candidate for use—even though transfer has not yet succeeded.
  </box>
  <divider color="subtle"/>
  <box gap={1}>
    **Historical transformation of recruitment**

    After actual activity, the actor's acquired organization may expand, narrow, reorganize or retain its prior conditions of applicability.
  </box>
</box>

This resolves a real conceptual tension in the recent R2 code.

In [PR #1163](https://github.com/maidiu/rawlsing/pull/1163), F02 correctly repairs the mistake of letting a learned association override the target's own eligibility conditions.

But a psychologically conservative implementation of that repair could become *too* restrictive.

If the target practice can never become provisionally relevant until its context has already been expanded, **how can a person first attempt a transfer they have never made before?**

Lahire gives us the missing middle:

A practical resemblance can recruit a **tentative possibility of using an existing organization** without prematurely rewriting the organization as a generally acquired competence.

Nadia may attempt a familiar rhythm in a different social situation because something about it resembles her earlier activity.

She may succeed.

She may fail.

She may discover that the resemblance was misleading.

She may discover a difference she cannot yet explain.

The psychological change, if any, comes later.

**Transfer need not be pre-authorized as established competence in order to become a possible attempt. But an attempted analogy must not be mistaken for successful acquisition.**

That is a much stronger resolution than either unrestricted graph traversal or exact-scope imprisonment.

## 5. Dreier and Holzkamp expose the missing scale: the person is conducting an entire life

This is the next connection I would draw.

Ole Dreier's *Psychotherapy in Everyday Life* contains an extraordinarily relevant formulation: people must compose different concerns, commitments and possibilities **across the multiple contexts of their everyday lives**.

They do not simply respond to one situation and then another.

They sustain and negotiate a whole conduct of life.

A parent can remain at work while being preoccupied with a child.

A worker may sacrifice the development of one capacity to preserve another material commitment.

A person can understand what they desire and still be unable to make room for it.

Continuing an apparently unchanged life is itself an accomplishment of ongoing activity.

That means SOROK's psychological constitution cannot simply be a database of dispositions with conditional triggers.

**The person's ongoing activities form a historical arrangement, within which present recruitment acquires its significance.**

And this is where Holzkamp becomes indispensable.

The individual's objective circumstances offer a field of possibilities, but those possibilities do not translate directly into conduct. The person acts from historically formed, subjectively significant premises, under constraints upon their actual capacity to change their conditions. <Cite ref="turn493679search11"/>

So consider a mother working a shift while Nadia needs her.

She may simultaneously experience tenderness, exhaustion, resentment, fear of dismissal, a need for income, guilt about her absence and investment in her competence as a worker.

The psychologically consequential fact isn't merely that seven independent motive scores compete.

Her material and relational circumstances place **several continuing activities into an actual contradiction**.

She can be fulfilling an obligation to provide for Nadia through the very action that prevents her from being physically present with her.

That contradiction is not solved by adjusting the weights of love and employment.

It is constituted by their material relationship.

And her response to it may reproduce the contradiction.

She may work longer, suppress the longing, rationalize the arrangement, resent the employer, blame herself, criticize other mothers, or develop new practices of care.

### This is the missing Marxist mediation of psychodynamics

The organization of multiple activities is not only psychological.

It is itself materially organized through work schedules, property, care obligations, institutional power and access to resources.

**The person's psychodynamics are partly the lived organization of contradictions among real activities.**

That doesn't eliminate the deep psyche. Quite the opposite.

Repeatedly living those contradictions can form enduring mediators, expectations, defenses, affects, identifications and ways of interpreting further events.

Some become inaccessible or naturalized.

Others become objects of conscious struggle.

So the movement is not:

*Society causes a psychological trait.*

It is:

**Historically organized activities create recurring practical relations and contradictions; the person lives through them in particular ways; those histories help constitute the psychological organization recruited in later activity.**

This is exactly where Dreier, Holzkamp, Sève, Leontiev and the existing Rawlsing conception begin to converge.

Their differences remain important. But they are illuminating a common construction problem from different levels.

  <text weight="medium" textAlign="center">The same person, differently constituted for future activity</text>

The critical refinement is that **the activity between recruitment and reconstitution is not merely an output stage**.

It is the process through which the existing organization encounters its own possibilities and limits.

That is why it can become formative.

And it helps solve the original regress problem.

The infant does not need a previously acquired sophisticated psychological faculty to form a new psychological organization.

The infant already possesses organismic capacities for sensation, movement, regulation, orientation and responsiveness.

These enter actual relations.

Repeated, varied and sometimes interrupted activities can produce new practical distinctions.

Socially mediated practices can eventually organize those distinctions.

Some acquired operations become ways of organizing other operations.

And eventually the developing person may acquire reflective practices through which they themselves attempt to reorganize their conduct.

**No fully developed psychological organizer needs to be installed before development begins.**

There remains an empirical and fictional-generative question about which particular organization forms.

But the source of possible organization is no longer mysterious.

It lies in **the historical activity of an organism whose relations to the world are themselves becoming more differentiated and capable of organizing further activity**.

Thelen and Smith's developmental dynamic-systems approach offers a compatible perspective on the emergence of sensorimotor skills through interacting bodily and environmental conditions. It does not replace the social and historical explanation, but reinforces the rejection of a precompiled adult repertoire. <Cite ref="turn142000search5"/>

## 7. What this means for the R2 builder, specifically

This is where I would direct the other UM-SOROK agent's attention.

I inspected the actual R2 source, not only its report.

And several substantial pieces of the necessary machinery **are already appearing in code**.

| R2 capability | What has genuinely advanced | What the theoretical recovery now suggests |
|---|---|---|
| `activity.py` | Object, hypothesized motive, means and technical outcome are distinguished | Activity must become more than a project with a motive string; its historical relation to other continuing activities needs greater consequence |
| `constitution.compose()` | Direct, associated and trace-based recruitment produce several currents | Move toward recruitment of **interrelated activities and their distinct meanings**, not only activated forms and project priorities |
| `development.admit()` | Forms, links, practices and recruitment scope can be changed | Development should be conceived as changes in **practical organization**, including the relations among motive, object, means and other activities |
| `semantics.py` | Sourcefree developmental proposals can construct new projects | The current one-episode singing-project heuristic needs a more convincing route through impression, provisional continuation, prospective object and possible motive formation |
| `world.py` | Material production, independent Others and co-regulation are increasingly real | Their actual consequences must remain irreducible to the actor's psychological interpretation, while becoming formative through actor-specific experience |

I would not tell the builder to abandon those modules or replace them with an elaborate newly named psychological subsystem.

**The recovery tells us how to deepen the machinery they're already constructing.**

### The most immediate pressure point: `SR-P011`

R2's hostile review already identifies that its offline heuristic can install a continuing singing-oriented project from a single episode.

The correction is not to prohibit project formation from early experience.

Nor is it to require ten repetitions before any motive is allowed.

The stronger conceptual distinction is this:

**An event can make a consequence available to anticipation without yet establishing that the consequence has become a motive organizing continuing activity.**

Nadia hears the sound again.

She may attempt to reproduce it.

That attempt can be possible before she possesses a stable project of making music.

She may discover that the sound matters because Mother responds.

Or because it is enjoyable independently.

Or because another child can participate.

Or because it confers a role she can control.

These are different possible transformations.

The developmental generator can intelligently propose which becomes plausible from the existing constellation.

But it should distinguish **a newly available possible end** from **a historically established motive**.

Leontiev's account of an action becoming an independent activity is directly useful here. The transition is not a mysterious command to manufacture desire; it is a change in the role of an activity's object within the person's continuing life. <Cite ref="turn589460search0"/>

### The next pressure point: affect

R2 is right to record that technical success does not establish motive satisfaction.

But it is still missing the positive psychological operation between them.

Leontiev suggests that affective experience can be intelligible through the relationship among an act, the activities it serves, and the person's historically constituted motives.

This does not give us a mathematically complete theory of human emotion.

But it gives us a better generative question.

Instead of asking the LLM:

*What emotion does Nadia feel after this event?*

Ask, in effect:

**Which activities and relationships were implicated? What did Nadia expect? What was actually possible? What happened to the different things that mattered to her? What could she register about those consequences? And what remains unresolved?**

The model may then construct a particular affective and practical response.

It should not have to pick among twenty universal emotional categories.

Nor should it assign one positive or negative reward to the event.

And crucially, the person can experience a contradiction **before being able to explain its structure**.

That is how the iceberg remains deeper than the tip.

## 8. The strongest theory is still not a universal answer-selection algorithm

There is one important limit I would refuse to obscure.

We have now found a much better *explanation of the process through which psychological organization can emerge*.

We have **not** discovered a law that uniquely determines what every actual human would learn from a particular event.

And I don't think Rawlsing needs such a law to begin generating fictional lives.

Consider two children who hear the same sound, receive the same adult response and possess similar bodily capacities.

One may become interested in repeating the sound.

Another may be primarily interested in the adult's attention.

A third may be startled.

Those outcomes can depend on history so fine-grained that a compressed simulation does not represent it.

The generator must therefore permit genuine underdetermination.

But underdetermination is not the same as arbitrary invention.

The already acquired psyche, actual lived activity, relational history, developmental capacities, material conditions, culturally available practices and specific accessible consequences can substantially constrain the possibilities.

The LLM then supplies a **plausible, explicitly fictional generative hypothesis** within those constraints.

That is a defensible current design.

The goal is not to predict an unknowable child's unique hidden future.

It is to construct a person whose development becomes intelligible from their particular generated life.

That is a different standard.

And a much more feasible one.

## 9. The deeper connection: contradiction can generate a new level of organization without guaranteeing its resolution

This is where I'd finally bring the Marxist, psychoanalytic and dialectical dimensions back together.

A contradiction between activities is not necessarily a problem the psyche recognizes and solves.

It may instead produce repression.

Or compartmentalization.

Or a defensive interpretation.

Or a shift in the meaning of an existing practice.

Or a transformation of what the person takes to be worth pursuing.

Sometimes the contradiction becomes visible only after the person has acted.

Sometimes its consequences are registered affectively but not conceptually.

Sometimes the social world repeatedly prevents the person from recognizing it.

Sometimes the person recognizes it perfectly and lacks the material means to do anything.

And sometimes a practice acquired within one relationship becomes the means of criticizing that relationship.

This is why the movement from identification through alienation to possible differentiation should not be represented by a universal liberation operator.

It is a **historically possible reorganization of the relations among activities, meanings and claims upon the person**.

In Nadia's case, the value of another person's participation may initially be inseparable from Father's recognized authority.

Through further activity, she may come to distinguish the participation from its institutional sponsor.

She may retain the original care, lose faith in the authority, continue seeking Father's recognition and act against his decision.

Those are not mutually exclusive psychological positions.

They are precisely the differentiated relations the developed iceberg must be able to contain.

And the original history remains present—not as a compulsory command, but as part of what constitutes the newly possible activity.

## What I would now tell the UM-SOROK builder

The major finding is not that psychological genesis and psychodynamic operation have both been solved.

It is stronger in a different way:

**They should not be conceived as two independent missing engines.**

The inherited sources suggest that both are constituted through the same historical organization of activity, viewed at different timescales.

At the shorter timescale, existing psychological organization becomes operative through situated recruitment, multiple implicated activities, affect, conflict and attempted action.

At the longer timescale, the lived consequences of those activities can transform the organization that makes future recruitment possible.

Leontiev helps explain the changing relations among motive, object, action, operation and affect.

Vygotsky identifies lived-through person–environment experience as the indispensable developmental mediation.

Luria explains how acquired cultural operations can reorganize relations among psychological functions.

Sève connects capacity formation and exercise to the material organization of biographical time.

Lahire explains contextual activation, inhibition and imperfect analogical transfer.

Dreier shows why the whole conduct of life—not the isolated episode—is the relevant horizon of practical organization.

Holzkamp keeps the actor's practical reasons connected to real, unequally distributed possibilities rather than treating circumstance as a behavioral command.

And PRE-A0/PFF/CAN-008 already possesses an architecture capable of receiving much of that synthesis.

**The theoretical advance is to make the person's object-directed, relationally constituted activity the mediating substance of both present psychodynamics and later psychological genesis.**

That does not erase the iceberg.

It explains how the iceberg becomes operative, and how its operation may change what the iceberg is.

It does not erase the tip.

It explains why the tip may be so radically insufficient as an account of what produced it.

It does not make the LLM unnecessary.

It gives the LLM a vastly better psychological construction problem than inventing plausible traits or arbitrarily deciding which motive was born.

And it does not displace the R2 software.

**It tells us what the already emerging R2 organism must become more capable of doing.**

The final unsolved scientific question is narrower and more legitimate: **under what historically particular conditions will a lived activity reorganize this person's motives, affective investments and recruitment pathways in this way rather than another?**

That can remain scientifically open while the fictional computational process becomes richly productive.

The missing discovery wasn't another psychological object.

**It was recognizing that the organization which produces the present act is also among the historical conditions through which the next organization of the person becomes possible.**
