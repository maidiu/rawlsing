## 3. Cross-theory tensions that a single ontology must NOT erase

**I. 'Needs-first' versus activity constituting needs.** Biological dependency/need makes infancy possible and cannot be omitted; however Leontiev's developed motive requires object in actual activity and Sève resists translating fully developed productive life back into a homeostatic list. The coherent differential is *embodied viability and dependence conditions → actual socially organized engagement with objects/people → transformed needs and motive relations*, with feedback; never need-vector directly generating actions. The same act can count differently within different activities. A need may be genuinely present without an actor having an articulated longing; a desire may not identify its social motive.

**II. Historically coordinated motives versus conditional plurality.** Leontiev's hierarchy/system of activities explains how central motives shape the significance of success/failure; Lahire insists that concrete socialization can produce heterogeneous dispositions with different contexts of activation. These are neither logically incompatible nor interchangeable: an activity can organize a project without eliminating context-specific contradictory practices. A stable, relatively unified life is also allowed; do not require plural contradiction from birth or integrate all concerns into one ranking. Empirical discriminator: repeated same actor in several contexts, with observation of formation, triggering and absent/competing enablement.

**III. Functional change versus observed contextual switch.** Luria supports historically mediated changes in kinds of categorization/organization; Dreier demonstrates abilities situated within participation/others, and Lahire calls for context tests. The meaning of a difference between E1 and E2 remains underdetermined until prior ability, instructions, tools, access, life stage and social sanctions are controlled. Even seemingly stable new performance may be temporary external scaffolding. Strong test: same actor with/without tool, with/without instruction, and later analogous-but-not-identical event after the instructed relation remains available.

**IV. Objective political economy versus subjective immediacy.** Elder's historical household economy and SRT indicate institutions can alter caregivers' time/care; infants need not cognize wage relations. Actual co-care may substitute for missing availability; declared commitment may not become performed care; perceived affection doesn't guarantee sustenance. Later ideological language can give new meaning to an early experience but cannot backdate that language. A scientifically honest generator must preserve parents' independently constituted actions, institutional power and child's partial exposure.

**V. Event significance versus memory and durable development.** Routine/practice may matter without an iconic episode; an intense fright may produce only transient effect; consequential loss can alter external possibilities before any known internal disposition shift; language can later reframe the significance of previous history without rewriting the objective ledger. ADP016 memory formation, ADP017 accessibility, CAN008 immediate effects and CAN011 enduring state are independent targets. 'Same output after event' might be true NO_CHANGE or an untested hold; compare right owner and response grain.

**VI. Reflective project versus nonreflective development.** Archer's deliberative biography can illuminate consciously revising concerns, but cannot be made the universal channel of infant attachment, motor learning or implicit dispositions. Conversely early practical organization cannot be retroactively assigned a narrated worldview; later actor-access and ideology need independent history.
