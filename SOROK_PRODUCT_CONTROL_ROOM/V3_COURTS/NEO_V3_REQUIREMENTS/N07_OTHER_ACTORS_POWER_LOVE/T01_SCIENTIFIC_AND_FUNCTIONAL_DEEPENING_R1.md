## 1. The central distinction: what happens cannot be inferred from what somebody meant
A caregiver can sincerely love and provide skilled care, while economic/time/health/social constraints leave a dependent child's actual needs persistently under-met. Caregiver intention, provision, reachable availability, recipient's experience, objective unmet need and retrospective explanation are six separable relations. The person must be able to apprehend both reliable tenderness and recurring lack without being forced into a positive or negative evaluation of the caregiver. Nor does a later articulable accusation prove an earlier formed infant accusation.

**Love (analytic designation)**: durable other-regarding valuation/commitment as evidenced by decisions, attention, repair, sacrifice, expressed affection and conduct under constraint. It may be strongly but fallibly inferred from a *pattern*; not an observable numeric essence.
**Care**: practical work of maintenance/attention/protection/enablement, divisible among actors and institutions; can be affectionate, obligatory, purchased, coerced or collective.
**Effective availability**: a caregiver's actually accessible responsiveness at the time and in the situation a dependent needs it. Availability is neither a proxy for intention nor equivalent to total hours.
**Felt love**: recipient-side experienced relational recognition/comfort/valuedness when inferable from admissible lived cues and repeated situated responses; absence of an account does not license deficit.
**Insufficiency**: a need/available-care mismatch with specific object, situation, timing, duration and available alternatives; may coexist with felt love, trust and effective repair.
**Dependency**: need for another's participation/resources to make an action or viable life possible, shaped by developmental capacity and law/property/labor arrangements; not synonym for helplessness.
**Relational power**: differential capacity to set conditions, withhold/allocate time, command labor and resources, define norms and practical exit options, across both interpersonal and institutional relations. Care may reproduce, negotiate or resist it.
**Other actor**: separately constituted bearer of constraints, partial knowledge, practices, plans and possible refusal. A narrative prop scripted to satisfy focal psyche is not an independent other.

## 2. Historical materialism without psychological determinism
Trace the actual social reproduction circuit: employer/market and state conditions → caregiver's feasible hours, pay, fatigue, transport and childcare options → distribution of household care among real people → particular encounters/absences and child's partial reception → possible history-specific expectation, practice or attachment organization → later conceptual reinterpretation. Every arrow has conditions and may fail to produce a psychological effect. Class is an explanatory structure, not a child's inborn explanation. Institutions themselves must be allowed causally consequential alterations: childcare provision, collective bargaining, public transportation, parental leave, kin and community co-care can change the availability relation without changing how much the parent loves.

For example, equal loving intention under two leave policies may yield different effective care; equal availability under two different histories may not yield the same child reception. One parent's wage labor may support material security *and* obstruct physical presence. The contradiction cannot be solved by coding work solely as either love or abandonment.

**Power challenge:** which actor can choose to reduce work hours, delegate care, change the bedtime, secure paid leave, demand overtime, define "good parenting", or exit? Who bears the unpaid adjustment and who controls resources? Document these rather than anthropomorphize capitalism as an acting character in the child's mind.

## 3. Sources and rival explanatory lenses
- **Dreier, _Psychotherapy in Everyday Life_, ch. 2, pp. 28–38:** persons are partial participants, dependent on coparticipants, who develop distinct stances across sites. No person unilaterally controls the common practice.
- **Elder, _Children of the Great Depression_, ch. 2 and "Family Relations":** family maintenance arrangements and changes in relative family power mediate historical economic loss. Cohort-specific evidence should test, not dictate, a parent–child relation.
- **Leontiev, _Activity, Consciousness and Personality_, section on activity/personality:** relational activity is a realized relation to world; outward action cannot simply disclose motive.
- **Sève, _Man in Marxist Theory_, IV.I, especially acts/capacities/use-time:** socially consequential acts and objective temporal relations provide alternative to subjective intent as causal evidence.
- **Bhattacharya (ed.), _Social Reproduction Theory_ (2017), introduction / Fraser / Ferguson:** capitalism's social reproduction contradiction foregrounds hidden life-sustaining work and its distribution; this does not yield an individual infant causal equation.
- **Lahire, _The Plural Actor_, pp. 50–62:** the same child can manifest different competencies, expectations and responses with grandmother, school, absent parent, present parent, peer group. Conditional activation and dormancy are plausible rivals to global wound/trait updates.
- **Potential alternative scientific traditions requiring independent testing:** attachment and relational developmental research (sensitivity, responsiveness, repair, co-regulation), developmental dynamic systems and life-course linked-lives research. These may reveal patterned conditions, but no attachment category is read straight off "caregiver away at work".

## 4. Mechanism lattice and discriminators
1. **Intentional/purposeful care:** giver chooses a costly, caring act; child notices particular aspects; possible felt warmth despite objective insufficiency.
2. **Scheduling/exposure:** a missed hour matters only where it intersects dependent availability/expectation, alternatives and experienced stakes; unobserved or irrelevant absence can have NO_CHANGE.
3. **Co-care:** a second adult or institution may meet a particular need and contribute independent relationship history; never a generic "support=+10".
4. **Repair and repeated responsiveness:** reunion, acknowledgment and response may modify significance of a missed encounter, but cannot retroactively make an absence not happen.
5. **Institutionally mediated constraint:** constrained caregiver choices under employer/state rules can alter acts without changing commitment. The child need not comprehend the institution.
6. **Conflict and power:** multiple actors hold incompatible practical needs; authority, coercion, reciprocal obligation or negotiation shape real outcome; perceived safety and normative evaluation remain separate, later mediations.

**Hostile rival A:** caregiver availability actually is sufficient given child's other relationships; the initial model over-pathologizes missed hours. **Rival B:** parent proclaims love but consistently acts coercively or neglectfully; declared affection cannot override conduct. **Rival C:** child's response is situational fatigue/illness, not durable care inference. **Rival D:** later moral language reorganizes memory; it is not a transcript of infancy. **Rival E:** systemic exploitation is an objective explanatory cause but a caregiver's own contingent conduct still makes an independent difference. Every candidate must survive these.

## 7. Discriminating adversarial tests and authority
**Case P1:** loving night-shift parent, scarce bedtime time, warm reunions, reliable grandmother. Expected: objective missed presence plus realized care plus separate actor-specific histories, and possible dual child reception; no obligatory childhood belief. **Case P2:** identical parent work shift but no bedtime overlap and no child reception: objective occupancy changes, psyche can remain NO_CHANGE. **Case P3:** affectionate verbal assurances coexisting with repeatedly harmful acts: practical care must not be inferred from professed love. **Case P4:** parent present but emotionally unresponsive; time is not care. **Case P5:** change employer shift/collective care policy and keep parental intention fixed; encounter availability must change *if* that upstream edge is operative.

**Ablation:** sever caregiver roster, legal permission, second caregiver action or child sensory access one at a time. If the output is unchanged and confidently reports the identical child care event, the alleged causal source edge is decorative. Conversely, removing child exposure should not erase the objectively occurred caregiver act from the world ledger.
