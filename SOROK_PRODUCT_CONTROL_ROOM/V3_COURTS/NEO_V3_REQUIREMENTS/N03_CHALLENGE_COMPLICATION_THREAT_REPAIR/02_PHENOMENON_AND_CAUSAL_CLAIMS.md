# T02 / N03 — Phenomenon and competing causal accounts

**Status: PROPOSED, NOT ISSUED.** Primary epistemic boundary: encounter/world fact, actor access and reception, transient reaction, durable consequence, memory and retrospective account are separate.

## Three concepts and their nonidentities
**CHALLENGE**: the actor's valued purpose remains possible or attractive but demands effort, a capability not yet available, coordination, resource expenditure, new competence or adjustment. It need not imply danger. Examples: learning to navigate a new classroom; a caregiver asks a child to try fastening shoes. A threatening challenge exists when failure endangers dependency or body; the dimensions can co-occur.

**COMPLICATION**: an observed event or new account limits, qualifies or destabilizes some explanatory/practical generalization. It can introduce ambiguity, an exception or a rival account without demanding new skill or threatening a valued dependency. Examples: the normally punctual caregiver is late for a good reason; the friendly-looking adult is a stranger; a loved adult gives genuinely contradictory signals. The actor may lack concepts to interpret it; practical confusion can precede articulate contradiction.

**THREAT**: some valued dependency, bodily condition, relation, practical capability or need-satisfying possibility is endangered. Two independent claims are mandatory: (a) **objective danger** in world/actor conditions, and (b) **actor-perceived threat** given their access, embodied reaction and historically possible assessment. Actual danger can be unperceived; perceived threat can lack corresponding objective danger. Other people may identify an objective danger the focal actor does not recognize. Threat need not be existential; degree and scope are context-bound and must not be constructed from event labels.

These are not three mutually exclusive exclusive-choice event types. An encounter may produce challenge+complication, threat+challenge, all three, or none for a given actor; labels can shift as care arrives, new information appears, a real danger escalates or a practical option closes. Distinguish **description of objective encounter**, **actor-relative reception**, and **downstream durable legal change**. An "unsafe dog" label is not a proven wound, a new school task not a threat by definition, uncertainty not necessarily harm.

## Contrastive encounter courts
| Encounter | Objective condition and other actors | Candidate actor reception | Alternative | Durable change must be separate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dog barks/lunges | Animal proximity, restraint, actual action | Startle/physical threat; learning avoidance for this dog or dogs | Dog safely restrained; actor experienced noise only; parent calms vs communicates more alarm | Specific avoidance possible, not compulsory generalized phobia |
| Briefly lost in shop, reunited | Separation duration; staff/caregiver search, response speed | Danger/abandonment perception, fear, later relied-upon reunion | Child engaged in exploration; did not notice; reassurance effective | Changed separation anticipation possible OR no persistent change |
| Hugged wrong leg | Same visual/clothing cue, not caregiver; stranger's own reaction | Surprise/complication: identity cue failed | Stranger kneels kindly; angrily rejects; child laughs; no threat | Perceptual discrimination or contextual caution, not necessary attachment trauma |
| Caregiver fails, repairs | Real failed response + separate subsequent repair with agency | Complication or threat at failure; soothing/confidence from enacted repair | Repair too late, insincere, unavailable, self-serving, inaccessible | Preserve failure and repair facts, possibly coexisting expectations |
| Unmet needs due to work | Wage schedule/time, unequal access to childcare, emotional availability on return | Affection and frustration together; needs remain unmet | Grandparent/support network responds; child relates absence to work later | Scoped expectations of availability/reliability, not normative parent score |

| Unexpected exciting task | New capability requested without known jeopardy | Challenge, novelty, sometimes enjoyment | Hidden coercion means objective threat even if actor enjoys it | New practice possible, but challenge != learning guaranteed |

## Repair is not reversal or negation
An independently acting caregiver or other actor must make an actual later response: returning, explaining, feeding, comforting, protecting, admitting harm, providing alternatives, reestablishing action. The actor may access some of this response and not other parts. Repair has distinct **objective efficacy** (was dependency restored?), **expressive quality**, **timing**, **social cost**, **actor reception**, **residual unaddressed need**, and **future conduct**. A comforting apology need not undo a material deprivation; material provision need not be experienced as affection. Repair may transform a frightening disorientation into a practical lesson that someone will seek the child — but equally may fail or make conflict more complicated. Later narration ("it was fine") is not equivalent to original lack of fear.

## Causal alternatives
- **Interactive mutual regulation** (Tronick): ordinary dyadic mismatch and repair produce distinct affective states; disorganization need not become lasting wound. See doi:10.1002/anzf.1274 and doi:10.1002/cd.23219863403.
- **Direct associative learning, social observation and verbal instruction**: fear can arise from direct encounter, another person's affect or verbal threat messaging; positive counterconditioning can change it. See Askew et al., PMID 23795591; Dunne & Askew doi:10.1037/emo0000341; 2024 review/meta-analysis PMID 38789695. These do not justify one coefficient for a generated child.
- **Materialist activity account**: the practical relation between goal, obstacles, dependency, independent other, resource and actual action makes the case consequential; not simply subjective valence.
- **Appraisal theories**: classify perceived demands/resources for actors with the requisite representational capacities, but do not project adult verbal appraisal onto preverbal children.
- **Plural situational dispositions** (Lahire) and social-practice movement (Dreier): changed response can be restricted to one place/coactor/activity while another orientation endures.
- **Threat physiology/stress** (Gunnar & Quevedo doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.58.110405.085605): response varies by organism/support; physiology does not by itself supply an ideational or autobiographical conclusion.
- **Resilience/turning points** (Rutter doi:10.1111/j.1939-0025.1987.tb03541.x): protection operates through causal interventions, not a label that negates adversity.

## Discriminating counterexamples
1. Child fears a harmless bark: perceived threat true, objective serious danger absent.
2. Child approaches an objectively aggressive dog without apprehension: objective danger true, perceived threat false.
3. Complication without threat: beloved caregiver has a new hairstyle and is still reliable.
4. Challenge without complication: practiced caregiver deliberately teaches new skill with clear requirements.
5. Threat without new challenge: sudden falling object endangers child before any action is available.
6. Repair without full restoration: affectionate worker-caregiver returns but childcare during future shifts remains absent.
7. Significant relational change after one event: an adult's severe intentional violence may transform practical dependence where previously supported — never ruled out by "thick baseline".
8. **NO_CHANGE despite intensity:** startling fireworks with trusted support, no broad generalization or significant downstream difference. No-change is not "nothing happened"; transient fear may be real.

## Falsifiers and authority
A system that maps bark -> threat -> trauma, "complication" -> universal epistemic rupture, or adult intention -> child mental state without access is invalid. If a stranger's response has no causal effect on the wrong-leg scene, independent actors are decorative. If objective danger can only be inferred from child fear it confuses ontology and evidence. If the threat category is eliminated when actor does not notice actual danger, it ignores risk. If every repaired failure resets historical history, it manufactures amnesia. Conversely a model always avoiding durable changes fails serious disruption.
