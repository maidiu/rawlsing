## 1. The development to explain — not a retrospective act of ventriloquism

An infant who encounters inconsistent material availability and genuine affection need not hold contradictory *propositions*. The same person can have bodily/practical anticipations conditioned by actual hunger, warmth, timely or delayed relief, opportunities to initiate contact, the available caregiver(s), and the limits imposed by care work, money and institutional organization. Those may be differently scoped; no universal shortage→insecurity inference follows. The caregiver's objective love/intention, hours worked, actual care delivered, child's received affection, and another adult's compensatory care are distinct, and their alignment cannot be assumed.

Years later, this same person's **historically received conceptual vocabulary** can provide ways to characterize their circumstances, explain why people suffer, attribute responsibility, imagine what is possible, and commit to activity. Possible interpretations include religious providence, liberation, meritocracy, structural class explanation, responsibility toward others, personal blame, political solidarity, estrangement, art, skeptical rejection or no integrated account. The list is a contrast set, **not a generator's candidate-draw table**. Actual church, school, labor, family, peer, union, media and intellectual activity, access to words/arguments, individual counter-evidence, institutions' power and other agents' practical interventions must determine eligibility. No one childhood experience entails Islam/Christianity/atheism/socialism or a particular cosmology.

**Key direction of causation**: earlier experience can constrain or make salient later acquired categories, while later social life can reorganize retrospective attention, accessible claims, practical objectives and deliberate responses. The later operation is a **new activity at its actual later time**. It does not alter E0's original objectively occurring care nor grant E0-infant the later vocabulary. A novel concept may illuminate a prior history and yet produce no durable mediator; self-description may change without practice; action may change with little articulated autobiographical story.

## 2. Primary theory: what each supports and what it does not

- **Leontiev** (uploaded *Actividade, Consciência e Personalidade*, PDF pp.18,20,25,27): needs acquire concrete objects and human personality is constituted through hierarchies and intersections of activity. Emotion may be contradictory relative to concurrently operative motives. Supports object-mediated action, not an unobserved drive→doctrine map.
- **Sève** (uploaded *Man in Marxist Theory*, PDF pp.319–321,337,364–365): socially organized activity, acquired capacities, use-time and the relation of learning to real application; he specifically opposes reducing developed activities to an initial organic need. Religion/politics may be practice and institutional participation, not solely a compensation for primal insufficiency. No adult theology follows numerically from baby hunger.
- **Luria** (uploaded *Cognitive Development*, PDF pp.29,67–68): socially circulated words, schooling and historically organized activity can transform available categories and generalization practices. No automatic invariant 'school exposure -> correct or abstract doctrine', no social group hierarchy imported from period-specific field comparisons. A conceptual operation must become **usable by this actor in this context**.
- **Lahire** (uploaded *The Plural Actor*, PDF pp.12–14,76,79): present context filters historically acquired dispositions; plural dispositions are not universal. Religious/private, political/public and domestic/practical action may align, conflict or remain unknown. Never posit a latent contradictory creed merely because it makes a narrator interesting.
- **Dreier** (uploaded *Psychotherapy in Everyday Life*, PDF pp.49–56,198): meaning and possible action are embedded in the person's actual participations; conceptual insight in one setting need not transfer into action elsewhere. Accessible mediation is also practical access and social support.
- **Archer** (uploaded *Structure, Agency, and the Internal Conversation*, PDF pp.18–20): cultural/structural enablements become relevant through concrete projects and their reflexive encounter. Her emphasis on deliberative adult agency is a **distinct layer** from nonverbal childhood practical expectation; a baby is not an internal-conversation agent by decree.
- **Elder** (uploaded *Children of the Great Depression*, PDF pp.94,110,115): cohort, timing, household economies and consequences of economic disruption mediated through families/roles, not a class->subjective-belief bijection.
- **Nelson/Fivush** (2004, *Psychological Review*, DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.111.2.486, directly checked PubMed abstract): autobiographical remembering develops through language, memory, social narration, temporal and self/other understanding; cannot assume an adult verbally recalled interpretation is stored verbatim from infancy.
- **Conway/Pleydell-Pearce** (2000, *Psychological Review*, DOI 10.1037/0033-295X.107.2.261, directly checked abstract): autobiographical retrieval is cue and current-goal dependent. Supports a reconstruction *problem*, not invented alternative event facts.
  
These are distinct explanatory levels, not one settled causal equation. Direct readings: selected source PDF pages above; outside articles' *abstracts only*. No new empirical validation performed by this court.

**Important causal distinction:** an emancipatory interpretation can emerge *against* early deprivation and an acquiescent one may be cultivated by very different historical routes; ideology can come from new comrades, schooling, religious life, work, coercion, family authority, or chosen reading without a childhood wound. It can supply practical organization and collective projects, not merely rationalize psychological need. Conversely an ideology need not provide a beneficial meaning or stable psychic buffer; power, alienation, class contradiction and actual lived conflicts remain.

## 6. Worked original causal example and explicit countermodels

**Same childhood history:** care-giver A loves child X and must work nights; home has intermittent provision, some warm encounters, a second caregiver B intermittently meets needs, and at least one period of material deprivation. Analyst ledger records each independent circumstance and action. Child exposure is only what X actually underwent; no access to A's invisible intention, a later class theory or theological propositions.

**Branch I:** teenage X actually learns a concept of mutual dependence through a community kitchen; adult X uses this in practical mutual-aid organization, retains contradictory expectations about intimate reliability and social solidarity (only if historical evidence establishes them). Subsequent choices may differ if Rule T admits a particular practice/mediator.

**Branch II:** equally plausible X encounters a religious account of providence in an actual community; the account may help organize care work without requiring the childhood lack to 'cause' conversion. X may also dispute, reinterpret or leave that account. A competent rule must read actual concept, scope, practices, evidence and not pick doctrine by childhood needs.

**Branch III:** a stable, supportive older community or material change improves life; X develops no meaningful ideology, or uses a vocabulary without changes in conduct. This is a **genuine continuity** model, not simulation failure.

**Misrecognition variant:** adult X concludes, incorrectly, that parental absence proved lack of love. ADP017 permits an actually sourced `BELIEF` with `OBJECTIVE_CONTRADICTED` analyst relation when a concrete owner rule generated it; neither ADP016 nor V2 can insert the claim into infant memory as an objective fact. New adult testimony may also correct belief without changing the fact of missed care.
