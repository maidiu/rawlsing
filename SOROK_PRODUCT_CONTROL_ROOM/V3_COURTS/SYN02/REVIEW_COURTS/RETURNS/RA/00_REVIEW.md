## 6. Scientific identifiability and complexity court

**A decisive scientific correction:** a representation is not identifiable merely because a simulator with a new edge reproduces later behavior and severing that edge changes its own prediction. A sufficiently rich fixed-gate model can encode the same history by changing a governed eligibility predicate; a new composed procedure can simulate a binary relation; a world-dependent collective capacity may explain successful activity without any new actor-owned record. Even controlled inside-model edge severance shows that *the implementation uses that edge*, not that the individual historical edge really formed. Claim unique new functional organization only at a **domain-defined contrast**: documented actor construction/performance under specific actual conditions, qualified earlier absence/limits, explicit rival explanation tests, out-of-sample later use with source-controlled support, and an independently warranted operator semantic criterion. Where rivals remain, retain an ambiguity hypothesis rather than mint a unique hidden graph.

**Complexity decision:** indexed sparse owner refs and contextual predicate evaluation are sufficient as an initial representation *if* needed; storage can be ordinary typed records + derived ephemeral relation view, not graph database. C02's seven RelationMode values and many lifecycles are an interface-design burden; require a specific case needing each, registered operand types, bounded local neighborhood and closure, and explicit pruning without erasing factual provenance. No O(n²) all-to-all coactivation, hidden nonterminating fixed point, arbitrary global weight/trait similarity, unbounded narrative relation mining, or universal solver iteration cap. The minimal adequate representation for most early work could be FIXED_CONTEXT+SCOPED_COMPOSITE+WORLD_SUPPORT; relation store added only when an accepted target needs a separately persistent relation that cannot be faithfully expressed in existing CAN011 payload.
