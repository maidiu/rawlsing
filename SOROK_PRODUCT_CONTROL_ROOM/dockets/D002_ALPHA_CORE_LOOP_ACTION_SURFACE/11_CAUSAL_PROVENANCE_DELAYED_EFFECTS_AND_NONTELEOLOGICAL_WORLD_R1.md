# D002 — Causal Provenance, Delayed Effects and Non-Teleological World R1

Status: `PROVISIONAL_PRODUCT_LAW_INPUT__OPEN_ARCHITECTURE_QUESTIONS`

## 1. Why chronology is not enough

An event log can tell us that:
1. user stole $40;
2. Nadia later noticed money missing;
3. Nadia fought with Samir.

But Rawlsing also needs enough causal provenance to know:
- whether the theft contributed to the missing-money event;
- whether Nadia's suspicion caused or intensified the argument;
- which other causes were active;
- what each actor believed caused what.

Actual causal provenance and actor attribution are distinct.

## 2. Causal lineage

For material consequences, investigate recording relations such as:
- caused;
- contributed_to;
- enabled;
- triggered;
- mediated_by;
- constrained;
- prevented;
- amplified;
- attenuated;
- coincided_with.

The exact formalism remains open.

The goal is not philosophical omniscience.

The goal is enough provenance to support longitudinal consequence without inventing causation retrospectively.

## 3. Objective causation ≠ actor attribution

Example:
User secretly takes money.

Objective causal chain may be:
`theft → missing cash → Nadia notices → Nadia suspects Samir → argument`.

Nadia's attribution:
`Samir took it`.

Samir's attribution:
`Nadia never trusts me`.

User knowledge:
`I took the money`, perhaps without witnessing the argument.

These must coexist.

## 4. Delayed effects become new world causes

A consequence need not return to the user.

The child inspired by the user may:
- talk to a teacher;
- seek a program;
- fight with Nadia;
- save money;
- change school effort;
- later influence a sibling.

Each later action is a new event with its own causal inputs.

The user does not remain the protagonist of the causal graph.

## 5. Anti-user-credit law

Rawlsing should avoid narratively exaggerating the user's importance.

The user may be:
- central;
- marginal;
- forgotten;
- one influence among many;
- causally irrelevant to an event they think they caused.

This is necessary for a non-player-centric social world.

## 6. Non-teleological event generation

The world should not preferentially generate situations because they would:
- test the user's kindness;
- teach a moral lesson;
- expose a known weakness;
- produce relationship drama;
- create engagement.

Events should arise from:
- actor projects;
- obligations;
- institutions;
- material constraints;
- schedules;
- relationships;
- prior causal consequences;
- lawful stochasticity where applicable.

The product can select a playable window into life without turning the world into a morality playwright.

## 7. No guaranteed moral legibility

The user may never learn:
- whether an action helped;
- why Nadia withdrew;
- what happened to Samir;
- whether a private act mattered.

Some consequences may remain permanently opaque.

Retrospective product feedback, if any, must be governed separately and must not imply the live world was arranged to be legible.

## 8. Causal uncertainty

Not every long-run outcome admits a confident attribution.

The engine should permit:
- multiple plausible causes;
- weak contribution;
- unknown causal weight;
- unresolved causation.

Do not fabricate certainty merely because later product analytics want a score.

## 9. Flourishing implication

D003 must not assume every health change can be cleanly credited/debited to the user.

The user can be evaluated on:
- realized actions;
- repeated relational patterns;
- demonstrable contributions;
- avoidable harms;

without claiming sole causal ownership of another person's life.

## 10. Alpha implication

The alpha only needs enough causal provenance to support its chosen scenarios.

It does not need universal causal inference.

But the architecture must not discard causal lineage so early that later consequences become impossible to explain without retrospective LLM invention.
