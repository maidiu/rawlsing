# D002 — Non-Curatorial World Detail and Binding Reality R1

Status: `PROVISIONAL_PRODUCT_LAW_INPUT__NOT_YET_DOCKET_DECISION`

## 1. Core theorem

**The world is meaningful before the user understands its meaning.**

Rawlsing should generate ordinary objective detail because a life/world causally contains it, not because a narrator has selected it as a psychologically revealing clue.

## 2. Non-curatorial narrator

The narrator should be:
- information-responsive;
- perceptually disciplined;
- non-omniscient;
- non-interpretive;
- non-curatorial.

It should not preferentially surface a detail merely because the internal engine knows that detail is emotionally formative or narratively important.

Example:

If a Myrtle Beach magnet is meaningful because it came from Nadia's final trip with her father, the narrator may initially state only what is perceptible:

> A rectangular Myrtle Beach magnet shaped like a flip-flop.

It must not call it:
- sentimental;
- cherished;
- poignant;
- connected to her father;

unless those facts are separately perceptible or learned.

Years later Nadia may explain the history.

The persistent object then acquires new meaning for the user without the past object changing.

## 3. Ordinary objects are evidence, not authorial clues

Reject the false binary:
- meaningful clue;
- meaningless atmosphere.

Bills, menus, magnets, school schedules, food, furniture, wear, handwriting and object placement may all carry socially produced information.

Their meaning emerges from specificity and context.

A fridge containing:
- an electricity bill;
- a school lunch schedule;
- a takeout menu;
- two magnets;

may potentially support inference about:
- household administration;
- material pressure;
- family responsibility;
- school/institutional context;
- food practice;
- budget;
- taste;
- travel/history;
- memory;
- cultural belonging.

But none of those inferences is guaranteed by the object alone.

## 4. Progressive perceptual resolution

Initial observation should provide only what the user's attention and position support.

Further ATTEND actions may reveal:
- exact utility company;
- amount;
- due date;
- overdue marker;
- school identity;
- lunch items;
- handwriting;
- restaurant;
- prices;
- magnet place/name;
- physical age/wear where genuinely perceptible.

Each additional layer may cost time and social exposure.

## 5. Binding reality

User language does not create objects.

Narrated world facts do.

Internal preferred order:

`UNRESOLVED WORLD QUESTION`
→ `LAWFUL WORLD COMPLETION / RETRIEVAL`
→ `CANONICAL ADMISSION`
→ `NARRATION`

From the user's perspective:

`NARRATED FACT = BINDING PERSISTENT FACT`

If Rawlsing says a green chipped mug exists, it should remain part of the world until a causal event changes that.

## 6. Anti-suggestion law

Leading questions must not cause desired facts to appear.

User:
> Do you have Percocet?

does not imply Percocet exists.

User:
> Is that a shutoff notice?

does not transform a normal bill into a shutoff notice.

Where a detail is unresolved, completion should follow existing world/person history, constraints, seeded randomness or other governed mechanism—not conversational usefulness.

## 7. Persistence reward

Persistent objective detail permits later recognition.

The user may:
- see an object years before learning its history;
- fail to notice something that later matters;
- remember a mundane detail after its significance changes;
- misunderstand an object and later revise;
- discover that an object changed or disappeared through intervening events.

This makes world continuity itself a source of intimacy.

## 8. No “true-person clue set”

There is no complete hidden set of clues which, once collected, yields the True Nadia.

A person remains:
- temporally changing;
- partly opaque to themselves;
- multiply interpretable;
- socially embedded;
- inexhaustible by observation.

Environmental evidence can improve causal models without eliminating otherness.
