## 0. Candidate insight

One deep Rawlsing function may be:

## 1. The playable contradiction

The adaptive phenomenal system can become increasingly good at rendering the world in a way consonant with the user's demonstrated attention, recognition, vocabulary and valuation.

That creates fluency, continuity, subjective legibility and the sense that the interface has learned the user's eye.

But neighborly adequacy to an independent other can move in a different direction.

Therefore:

ADAPTIVE PHENOMENAL FIT
!=
ADEQUACY TO AN INDEPENDENT OTHER.

And:

> MORE PERSONALIZED PERCEPTION != GREATER ADEQUACY TO THE OTHER.

> THE LENS CAN DEEPEN FASTER THAN UNDERSTANDING.

The same personalization that makes the interface more phenomenologically exact for the user can recursively strengthen one partial way of seeing.

## 2. Knowledge itself can become enclosure

A user may learn the tail or trunk of the elephant in extraordinary detail.

Greater detail, recognition, local prediction, lexical sophistication and repeated evidence need not yield greater adequacy to the person as a whole.

Candidate:

> MORE DIFFERENTIATED KNOWLEDGE OF A PART != GREATER ADEQUACY TO THE PERSON AS A WHOLE.

This is stronger than generic noncompletion.

It identifies a failure mode in which knowledge is real and locally useful while still becoming a vehicle of enclosure.

## 3. Nadia as independent remainder

Without an independent other, adaptive phenomenal reflection could collapse into sophisticated self-study.

Nadia supplies the resistant remainder:
- the narrator may learn the user's eye;
- Nadia's personhood does not become fitted to that eye;
- Nadia reacts only through her own causal organization;
- the user's increasingly coherent interpretation may therefore repeatedly fail, partly succeed, or prove irrelevant.

Compact:

> THE SYSTEM MAY BECOME BETTER AT REPRESENTING THE WORLD TO THE USER WHILE THE USER REMAINS WRONG ABOUT THE PERSON IN IT.

This gap is potentially playable because it is encountered through consequence, surprise, mismatch and revision rather than merely taught as doctrine.

## 4. Ideology at the phenomenal level

Categories experienced as properties of objects can partly reflect the history of the perceiver:
- messy;
- homey;
- pretentious;
- expensive;
- tacky;
- religious;
- sad;
- normal;
- authentic;
- evidence-worthy;
- ignorable.

Rawlsing need not decide that any such characterization is false.

The point is to make available, eventually, that:
- the characterization has a history;
- another perceiver may organize the same evidence differently;
- Nadia's own relation to the object may differ from both.

Candidate:

> EVEN WHAT PRESENTS ITSELF AS OBVIOUS REALITY MAY BE PARTLY AN ACHIEVEMENT OF A HISTORICALLY PRODUCED PERCEIVER.

## 5. Reflexivity without a view from nowhere

The product should not promise:
“remove your bias and see objectively.”

Instead it can:
- disclose that the adaptive lens exists;
- show concrete behavioral evidence of what has shaped it;
- allow bounded excursions outside its habitual sampling;
- preserve that any new view remains situated.

Thus the veil task is not neutrality.

It is development of a relation to one's own partiality.

A user-invoked lens perturbation such as:
“What might my usual attention be filtering out here?”
may be useful only if it resamples lawfully available material rather than injecting corrective truth or hidden importance.

## 7. Model symmetry

Candidate only:

> NO PARTICIPANT IS EXHAUSTED BY ANOTHER PARTICIPANT'S MODEL OF THEM — INCLUDING THE PLAYER.

Possible nested structure:
- Rawlsing has a bounded model of the user;
- the user has a bounded model of Nadia;
- Nadia has a bounded model of the user;

- none becomes the person.

This may connect non-exhaustive personhood, partial knowledge, adaptive narration, actor opacity, user reflection and relation.
