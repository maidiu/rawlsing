## 0. Problem

Rawlsing needs to support all of the following at once:

- deep resonances can genuinely recur across a person's life;
- some present tastes/preferences may share deep generative causes with later life;
- some persons actively interpret and mythologize those resonances;
- some persons rarely do;
- some self-interpretations are illuminating;
- some are creative but unfalsifiable;
- some are defensive distortions;
- some become causally powerful regardless of whether they were historically "true";
- many preferences are ordinary, contingent or weakly caused;
- contradiction and counter-evidence must survive;
- the engine must not turn every person into a self-interpreting literary text.

The solution cannot be:
`NO DEEP MEANING`.

It also cannot be:
`EVERYTHING HAS A SECRET DEEP MEANING`.

---

## 1. Core candidate — recurrence without teleology

Strong current candidate:

> **Deep resonance should arise primarily because the same historically formed person repeatedly expresses an organized set of tensions, sensitivities, desires, defenses, values and material relations across different domains—not because the engine plants earlier symbols whose purpose is to foreshadow or explain later life.**

Compact:

`COMMON GENERATIVE ORGANIZATION`
→ `EXPRESSION A AT T1`
and
`COMMON / EVOLVED GENERATIVE ORGANIZATION`
→ `EXPRESSION B AT T2`.

Therefore:

`A RHYMES WITH B`

does not imply:

`A CAUSED B`.

This is **recurrence without teleology**.

## 2. Disney-movie example

At age 8:
Nadia strongly prefers Disney film C over D/E/F.

At age 25:
Nadia notices that friends who preferred D/E/F seem, in retrospect, to have developed toward social pattern X, while Nadia developed toward Y.

Several things may be true simultaneously:

### Layer 1 — occurrence
Nadia really did prefer C.

### Layer 2 — causal generation
That preference may have been partly caused by:
- temperament;
- family dynamics;
- gender norms;
- class;
- available identification figures;
- anxieties/desires;
- aesthetic sensitivity;
- sheer exposure;
- accident.

### Layer 3 — later developmental recurrence
Some of those same underlying structures may later contribute to Y.

Therefore C and Y may have **common-cause resonance**.

### Layer 4 — retrospective interpretation
At 25 Nadia may think:
> "Of course I loved C. I was already becoming this person."

That proposition may be:
- partly evidentially supported;
- aesthetically coherent;
- impossible to cleanly falsify;
- overconfident;
- genuinely illuminating;
- self-serving;
- some mixture.

### Layer 5 — later causal effect
If Nadia adopts that story, it may affect:
- what she notices;
- what she values;
- how she tells her childhood;
- what she chooses next;
- what she tells others;
- how she understands continuity of self.

The later narrative becomes real causal history even if it was never the singular objective explanation of the childhood preference.

## 3. Meaning strata

Preserve at least the following noncollapse:

### M0 — objective occurrence / material fact
What happened / existed.

### M1 — objective causal contribution
What actually contributed to the event/state.

This may be:
- multicausal;
- uncertain;
- distributed;
- partly unresolved.

### M2 — structural recurrence / resonance
Two or more facts may instantiate:
- related tensions;
- homologous patterns;
- common causal ancestry;
- recurring organized tendencies.

This is not automatically causal direction.

### M3 — actor salience / association
What Nadia spontaneously links, remembers or feels together.

### M4 — actor interpretation / self-narrative
What Nadia thinks the pattern means about herself.

### M5 — actor self-mythologization
A stronger integrative narrative that organizes identity across time.

### M6 — counterpart interpretation
What the user or another person proposes about Nadia's pattern.

### M7 — negotiated / relational meaning
A meaning that becomes shared, contested or jointly elaborated through actual relation.

### M8 — downstream causal effect of meaning
Any interpretation, once held/contested/repeated, may alter later action and person-state.

Strong noncollapse:

`OCCURRENCE`
!= `CAUSE`
!= `RESONANCE`
!= `ASSOCIATION`
!= `SELF-NARRATIVE`
!= `SELF-MYTH`
!= `USER THEORY`
!= `SHARED MEANING`
!= `LATER CAUSAL EFFECT`.

## 4. Resonance is not a hidden canonical explanation field

Do not store:

`object.meaning = "represents Nadia's struggle with control"`.

Instead, the engine may store/generate:
- object/event history;
- causal relations;
- actor associations;
- actor interpretations;
- symbolic recruitment events;
- structural pattern candidates.

A "deep resonance" may emerge because:
- the same V3 organization influenced multiple facts;
- later experience recruits earlier material;
- actor reflection links them;
- a counterpart offers a new relation;
- later reinterpretation creates a newly meaningful pattern.

Therefore meaning should be **generated relationally and temporally**, not assigned as a universal secret label.

## 5. Common-cause resonance

This is a key anti-novelistic mechanism.

Suppose:
`LATENT / DURABLE ORGANIZATION Z`
contributes to:
- childhood preference C;
- adolescent social behavior;
- adult vocation Y.

Then C may feel retrospectively prophetic.

But architecture should prefer:

`Z → C`
and
`Z / Z' → Y`

over:

`C → Y`

unless there is independent historical evidence that C itself became formative.

This yields real recurrence without authorial foreshadowing.

It also permits:
- counterexamples;
- changed organization;
- broken patterns;
- contradictory domains.

## 6. Variable self-interpretive propensity

Not every persona should process life as autobiography/theology/psychoanalysis.

V3 should govern a variable, multidimensional **meaning-making regime**, potentially influenced by:
- introspective tendency;
- narrative identity style;
- symbolic sensitivity;
- aesthetic orientation;
- religious/theological formation;
- psychoanalytic/therapeutic exposure;
- education/subculture;
- memory style;
- tolerance for ambiguity;
- need for coherence;
- defensive rationalization;
- self-consciousness;
- intellectualization;
- social norms around self-disclosure.

Avoid one scalar if possible.

A persona may be:
- highly symbolic in religion but concrete about romance;
- psychologically interpretive about family but not objects;
- philosophically reflective but autobiographically uninterested;
- narratively elaborate in public and privately confused;
- suspicious of "why" questions;
- prone to mythologizing only under stress.

Compact:

> **SELF-INTERPRETATION IS A PERSON-SPECIFIC PRACTICE, NOT A UNIVERSAL NARRATOR FUNCTION.**

## 8. Deep resonance without self-awareness

A real resonance may exist even if Nadia never articulates it.

Possible states:

### R0 — no meaningful resonance
Two facts merely coexist.

### R1 — structural/common-cause resonance
The engine can identify a shared generative pattern, but Nadia has never noticed it.

### R2 — weak actor association
Nadia feels they "go together" without theory.

### R3 — explicit actor interpretation
Nadia consciously links them.

### R4 — identity-organizing self-myth
The link becomes part of how Nadia narrates herself.

### R5 — relationally transformed interpretation
Another person challenges/deepens/reframes the link and Nadia's model changes.

No automatic progression.

## 9. User discovery should not be rewarded with confirmation

A user may detect a real-looking pattern.

The system must not default to:
> "Wow, yes, you uncovered the true hidden meaning."

Instead, the user's interpretation enters at M6.

Nadia's actor-local uptake then depends on:
- her meaning-making regime;
- memory access;
- trust in user;
- evidence;
- current defensiveness;
- prior self-narrative;
- desire for coherence;
- context.

Possible responses:
- enthusiastic adoption;
- partial recognition;
- curiosity;
- resistance;
- laughter;
- annoyance;
- "maybe";
- "you're overthinking it";
- delayed reconsideration.

The user's pattern-recognition may be brilliant.
It is still not automatic truth authority.

## 10. Self-myth can be false and still causally real

A self-narrative may:
- omit contradictory evidence;
- rationalize;
- aestheticize;
- create false inevitability;
- protect self-esteem;
- simplify;
- provide courage;
- create moral direction;
- reconcile previously fragmented experience.

Once adopted, it may become a new cause.

Therefore:

> **NARRATIVE TRUTH VALUE != NARRATIVE CAUSAL POWER.**

A false or unfalsifiable story can genuinely reorganize a person's future.

The engine should represent both.

---

## 11. Counter-evidence budget

Every strong self-narrative should remain capable of coexistence with:
- inconvenient memories;
- contradictory behavior;
- witnesses who remember differently;
- domains where the pattern fails;
- uncertainty;
- forgotten material;
- alternative explanations.

Do not automatically generate counter-evidence mechanically.

But do not delete or reinterpret existing counter-evidence merely to preserve a new self-story.

Compact:

> **COHERENCE-BUILDING MUST NOT BECOME EVIDENCE-DELETION.**

## 12. Resonance density should vary by person and domain

Some lives genuinely contain highly recursive symbolic structures.

Some people:
- repeat motifs;
- collect objects;
- build rituals;
- revisit formative texts;
- deliberately construct continuity.

Others are less like this.

Therefore the simulation should not impose one global "realism" target.

Possible person/domain states:
- LOW resonance elaboration;
- MODERATE;
- HIGH;
- situational bursts;
- stress-linked;
- relation-linked;
- religious/aesthetic-domain specific.

The engine may generate more latent interpretive affordances for high-symbolic persons because those persons themselves:
- preserve relevant artifacts;
- revisit them;
- organize later acts around them.

This is not the narrator fabricating symbolism.
It is the person's own historical practice producing a more symbolically recursive life.

## 13. Objects can become meaningful later

An object may begin as:
`ORDINARY / LOW-SIGNIFICANCE`.

Then later:
- a relationship changes;
- someone dies;
- the object survives;
- a user asks about it;
- Nadia reconsiders it.

Its meaning can increase.

Thus:

`SIGNIFICANCE AT T1 != SIGNIFICANCE AT T2`.

Do not require deep meaning at acquisition time.

Meaning can be retrospective and still be genuinely causal now.

## 14. Engineering direction

Do not precompute "deep meaning" for every fact.

Store/generate:
- causal provenance;
- temporal context;
- actor-object/event associations;
- durable V3 organization;
- accessible memory;
- self-narrative claims;
- interpretive history.

When interpretation is recruited:
1. retrieve relevant facts/events;
2. retrieve durable V3 organization;
3. retrieve actor meaning-making regime;
4. derive/query whether candidate facts share authoritative upstream causal/developmental antecedents;
5. distinguish common-cause support from direct causation;
6. preserve actor-access boundaries and counter-evidence;
7. propose candidate patterns;
8. score evidence + counter-evidence + actor plausibility;
9. admit only actor-local interpretation, not objective meaning;
10. if repeated/adopted, allow interpretation to alter later person-state.

### Common-cause resonance retrieval

The post-fifth transcript identifies this as a concrete engineering seam.

Given candidate facts A and B:

`QUERY CURRENT AUTHORITATIVE CAUSAL / DEVELOPMENTAL PROVENANCE`
→ `SHARED UPSTREAM ANTECEDENT EVIDENCE / NONE / UNDERDETERMINED`.

Preserve:

`SHARED ANTECEDENT SUPPORT != A CAUSED B`.

`SHARED ANTECEDENT SUPPORT != ACTOR INTERPRETATION IS TRUE`.

`NO SHARED ANTECEDENT FOUND != ACTOR MAY NOT FORM THE INTERPRETATION`.

The desired implementation is a provenance-bearing derived query/view over current owner state, not a precomputed `WORLD.MEANING` or global symbolic explanation graph.

The user/person may form a creative or mistaken connection even when the engine finds no common-cause support. Conversely, real common ancestry can exist without the actor knowing it.

This is computationally much cheaper and more human than maintaining a global symbolic explanation graph.

See Q-MEANINGSTRATA-001 and artifact 78.
