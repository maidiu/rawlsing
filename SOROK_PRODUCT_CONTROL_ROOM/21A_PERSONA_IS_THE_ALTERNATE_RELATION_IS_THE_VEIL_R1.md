# Sorok Court 7A — The Persona Is the Alternate; the Player Exists for Relation R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_CORE_PRODUCT_THEOREM__HOMOLOGOUS_PEER

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Core correction

The original Rawlsing 'alternate self' burden should not be placed on the player's portaled identity.

**The generated persona is the alternate human existence that Rawlsing rolled when the user crossed the veil.**

The player is not another alternate-self simulation.

The player's diegetic identity exists primarily to make ordinary life-and-relation with that generated person possible.

---

## 1. Division of labor

### PERSONA

Rawlsing answers:

> What kind of person could a different history, structure, family, field and sequence of relations actually have made?

The persona is generated developmentally through the Rawlsing machine.

They are the counterfactual human life.

### PLAYER

Sorok answers:

> What happens when the actual present user is placed close enough to that person to have a consequential relationship with them?

The player does not need a second generated developmental psyche.

They need:
- enough embodiment to be perceived;
- enough local material position to be constrained;
- enough continuity to remain the actual user;
- enough world history to acquire real consequences;
- enough access to meet the persona through ordinary relation.

---

## 2. The alternate is encountered as other

The pedagogical force should not come primarily from telling the user:

> This is who you would have been.

Instead:

> This person's existence is one of the human lives that could have been yours under another roll of the world.

But the persona remains **other**.

The user does not get access to them by imaginative identification or roleplay.

They learn them the only way persons normally become knowable:
- time;
- shared situations;
- attention;
- conflict;
- dependence;
- practical life;
- observation;
- testimony;
- misunderstanding;
- repair;
- ordinary accumulated history.

Thus:

`COUNTERFACTUAL HUMAN POSSIBILITY != COLLAPSE OF PERSONA INTO PLAYER`.

---

## 3. Veil pressure becomes relational rather than avatar-based

The user crosses the veil and Rawlsing has already rolled the consequential unknown:

> **Who is the person next to you?**

The veil's pressure is therefore not:

> Can you convincingly inhabit a fictional version of yourself?

It is:

> Can you recognize the full reality of a person whose life could have been yours, when their actual wants, judgments and history do not belong to you?

This operationalizes the veil through encounter.

---

## 4. Love-your-neighbor relation

This strengthens the 'love your neighbor as yourself' relation.

The game does not ask the user to treat the neighbor as a projection of themselves.

Instead:

- this person could have occupied the position from which the user now looks;
- the user could have occupied conditions like theirs;
- nevertheless they are not the user;
- their reality must be learned rather than assumed.

Candidate moral structure:

> **They could have been you; they are not you; therefore you must meet them as a person rather than as a hypothetical version of yourself.**

---

## 5. Consequence for homologous-peer design

Homologous-peer identity should be judged primarily by one criterion:

> Does it create a sufficiently ordinary, bounded, causally coherent position from which the actual user can genuinely come to know and affect the generated person?

Not:

> Does it provide a complete alternate biography for the user?

This lowers pressure to solve every player-side coordinate in advance.

The portaled player identity can remain intentionally thinner than a generated V3 person because its function is relational access, not counterfactual person-generation.

---

## 6. Player identity should be relation-sufficient, not biography-complete

Main-mode player embedding therefore needs enough state for:
- physical/social presence;
- local peer comparability;
- finite resources/time;
- stable identity/recognition;
- communication;
- commitments;
- relationship/event participation;
- portal translation;
- accumulated in-world history.

It does not inherently need:
- generated childhood;
- generated family psychology;
- generated personality;
- fabricated local habitus;
- complete fictional social graph;
- a full V3 developmental past.

Any additional player-side biography must justify itself by relational causality.

---

## 7. The user acquires a life here forward, not backward

The portaled player need not be given a complete invented past.

Instead, their **future from entry onward becomes real**.

After crossing:
- they meet people;
- acquire routines;
- make promises;
- gain/lose access;
- develop shared history;
- learn places;
- change expectations;
- become known;
- acquire obligations;
- leave effects.

The player becomes increasingly embedded through lived relation rather than retroactive biography.

This suggests a temporal asymmetry:

`PRE-PORTAL USER PAST = CARRIED / TRANSLATED / PARTLY OFF-STAGE`

`POST-PORTAL HISTORY = FULLY DIEGETIC AND CAUSAL`.

---

## 8. The persona carries the thick local historical burden

The generated persona is the primary vessel for showing:
- how this society produces persons;
- how structures become biography;
- how household/care/institutions become psyche;
- how relation produces durable organization;
- how material position and contingencies become a life.

The player does not need to duplicate that burden.

This prevents Sorok from building two V3 persons where only one generated V3 person is needed.

---

## 9. The player still changes

Although the user does not begin as a generated local person, they are not fixed forever.

Post-entry relation becomes actual causal history.

Therefore:

> the persona is the alternate life already formed;

while

> the player becomes a new person-in-this-world through what happens after the portal.

The user enters Rawlsing's relation-forming recursion at T0.

---

## 10. Product consequence

This reframes the main game:

Not:

> Live another life.

Not:

> Roleplay who you might have been.

But:

> **Meet a life you might have had, as the person you actually became. Then live with what happens between you.**

This appears closer to both the original Rawlsing thesis and the current Sorok relational game.

---

## 11. Court 8 consequence

Autobiographical transport becomes less existentially loaded.

The portal does not need to answer:

> What is the complete local equivalent of the user's whole life?

It needs to answer:

> What minimum coherent translations are necessary for this actual user to participate in this world without imported structural dominance or obvious causal contradiction?

This supports:
- optional Portal Profile;
- lazy coordinate binding;
- NO_LAWFUL_HOMOLOGY;
- thin pre-entry identity;
- thick post-entry history.

---

## 12. Candidate deepest theorem

> **Rawlsing generates the alternate human. Sorok gives the actual user a life beside them.**

And therefore:

> **The veil is not completed by becoming the other person. It is completed through relation with the other person.**
