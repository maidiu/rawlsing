# Sorok — Actor-Owned Disclosure, Relational Tension, and Cathexis R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_PSYCHIC_RELATIONAL_DIRECTION__EXACT_OWNER_MISSING

Date: 2026-09-30

## 0. Core rejection

Disclosure must not be modeled as:

`TRUST >= THRESHOLD -> REVEAL DEEPER FACT`.

People disclose, withhold, evade, lie, overdisclose and reinterpret for many different reasons.

---

## 1. Candidate center: event-time relational tension / cathexis

Use **cathexis** broadly as psychic/relational investment in a person, relation, image, recognition, desire, conflict or outcome.

At a given interaction, disclosure pressure may emerge from the dynamic relation among:
- current topic activation;
- relation-specific investment/cathexis;
- desire to be known / recognized;
- desire to affiliate;
- desire to impress or preserve status;
- shame / face exposure;
- fear of judgment;
- privacy/boundary organization;
- anger / protest / need to correct the other;
- need for help;
- reciprocity pressure after the other disclosed;
- self-presentation strategy;
- practical relevance;
- impulsiveness / reduced inhibition;
- fatigue / bandwidth;
- contextual privacy;
- sideways activity / reduced direct gaze;
- expected reception;
- actor's model of the user;
- current relation-specific expectation;
- self-accessibility: what Nadia can herself admit/recognize;
- defensive/metabolic organization;
- current goal/striving.

No single scalar owns the result.

---

## 2. Disclosure is an action

Possible actor-owned response moves include:
- full truthful disclosure;
- partial truthful disclosure;
- selective emphasis;
- vague answer;
- joke;
- counter-question;
- refusal;
- change of subject;
- delayed answer;
- accidental overdisclosure;
- strategic omission;
- intentional lie;
- sincere but inaccurate self-narrative;
- 'I don't know';
- saying something true but for a misleading reason.

These are not renderer flavor variants. They require causal support.

---

## 3. User inquiry is causal but not sovereign

A question from the user may:
- activate a topic;
- create face threat;
- create a chance for recognition;
- signal interest;
- expose misunderstanding;
- create reciprocal pressure;
- annoy;
- invite intimacy;
- trigger defense.

But:

`USER ASKS != ACTOR DISCLOSES`.

The same question can produce very different responses at different times because the current person×relation×situation state differs.

---

## 4. Relation-specific investment matters

Nadia may tell the same fact differently to:
- stranger;
- neighbor;
- friend;
- child;
- partner;
- coworker;
- authority figure.

Not because there is one hidden intimacy meter, but because different relations organize:
- stakes;
- expected judgment;
- desired image;
- dependency;
- obligation;
- recognition;
- risk;
- desire;
- history.

Thus disclosure must be relation-indexed.

---

## 5. Lying as one disclosure outcome

Intentional fabrication can arise when:
- desired recognition conflicts with shame;
- affiliation is valued;
- status threat is high;
- user seems to expect a prestigious peer;
- Nadia wants to sustain a particular relational image;
- direct refusal feels costlier than fabrication;
- other actor-specific pressures make lying reachable.

Example:
Nadia feels shame around underachievement. The user repeatedly offers prestige camaraderie and she likes the user / wants affiliation. She may exaggerate or fabricate an achievement.

The lie is then stored as:
- an utterance event;
- intentional presentation where lawfully known by system;
- relation-specific history;
- possible later memory/maintenance burden;
- possible downstream false belief in user;

while underlying generated history remains unchanged.

---

## 6. Inattentiveness can matter without omniscient feedback

If user misses cues, projects prestige camaraderie, and accepts a false claim:
- Rawlsing need not signal `YOU MISSED A LIE`;
- the user's model may simply become wrong;
- later contradictions may emerge;
- Nadia may have to maintain, revise, confess, forget or compound the lie;
- the relationship may reorganize around the false shared model.

This is lawful relational history, not a puzzle clue.

---

## 7. Self-narrative remains separate

A false statement is not automatically a lie.

Actor may sincerely report a self-narrative that conflicts with:
- canonical event history;
- multi-causal analysis;
- prior testimony;
- other actors' memories.

Need at least:

`OBJECTIVE GENERATED HISTORY`
`ACTOR MEMORY/BELIEF`
`ACTOR SELF-INTERPRETATION`
`COMMUNICATIVE PRESENTATION`.

---

## 8. Sideways disclosure

Shared activity can change the disclosure field.

Driving, folding laundry, cooking, walking, waiting, working beside someone, sitting in the dark, or talking around a third person can alter:
- direct face pressure;
- silence tolerance;
- attention;
- perceived intimacy;
- inhibition;
- opportunity.

This is one reason chores/tasks are valuable as relational carriers rather than core objectives.

---

## 9. No disclosure ladder

Do not encode:
- `FACT_DEPTH_LEVEL=5`;
- `TRUST_UNLOCK=70`;
- universal trauma/deep-secret tiers.

A mundane preference may remain private.
A painful fact may be told early.
A deeply intimate relation may still contain untouched domains.

Disclosure topology is irregular and person/relationship-specific.

---

## 10. Architecture question

Exact ownership must respect inherited split:
- durable relation-shaped person organization -> V3 where causal/persistent;
- event-time receivability/appraisal/recruitment/immediate response -> CAN-008;
- interaction-fast user-opacity/presentation specialization -> narrowed PFF where genuinely residual;
- outward expression -> V2.

Do not create a new 'disclosure engine' merely because disclosure is complex.

---

## 11. Product theorem

> **Disclosure is not a reward emitted by closeness. It is something a person does with another person under a particular relational pressure field.**

And:

> **To know someone is partly to learn what kinds of situations let different parts of them become sayable, unsayable, distorted, joked about, defended or offered freely.**
