## Source-based Korean caution

Jeong (정/情) here remains the user's *analogy*, not a fixed translatable universal psychometric dimension. The Academy of Korean Studies encyclopedia discusses feeling, action, social connection, ethical conduct and relations to objects/environment: https://encykorea.aks.ac.kr/Article/E0049894 .

Yu Hye-seon and Park Eun-jeong (2023) compared jeong with ojirap, finding that intimacy, permitted care, sharing and unauthorized intrusiveness condition how observed practices are understood among their surveyed Korean cohorts: https://www.kci.go.kr/kciportal/landing/article.kci?arti_id=ART003004368 . This supports attention to actual reception/permission, not automatic inference that doing a favor establishes affection.

Jea Sophia Oh (2022) examines jeong with uri and han and explicitly critiques patriarchal coercion through relational bonds; care and obligation can protect or trap: https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-94747-7_12 .

Kim and Yun (2007) document reciprocal online obligations, intimacy/autonomy tensions and relational burdens among Korean Cyworld participants: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/j.1083-6101.2007.00397.x .

**Provisional comparison, not Korean-language scientific authorization:** a temporally sedimented relation can contain care, resentment, habit, responsibility, unequal exit costs and durable practical convention simultaneously. Neither duration nor warmth maps to a unique value. Do not orientalize or canonize a JEONG primitive.

4. Repeated coexistence yields ordinary routine and NO_CHANGE in affection/trust.

11. Long undramatic practical cooperation persists without overt disclosure or romantic progression.
12. Rupture removes an actual permission while previous memory, habit and incompatible investment may remain; do not manufacture a healing arc.
