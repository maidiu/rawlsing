# Sorok Court 8F / D002 — Native Player Skill and Declared Capability Distinction R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_CORE_GAMEPLAY_THEOREM

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Core correction

Rawlsing should distinguish between:

### A. NATIVE PLAYER CAPACITIES
abilities the user actually exercises through the interface and that the game can directly observe as behavior;

and

### B. DECLARED / PORTALED CAPABILITIES
claims about embodied or external-world abilities that Rawlsing cannot verify about the external human but may admit as part of the run's player representation.

This distinction resolves much of the honesty problem without lie detection.

---

## 1. Native player capacities

These are real because the user performs them in the game itself:

- allocating attention;
- choosing what to inspect;
- noticing patterns;
- remembering prior interactions;
- forming hypotheses;
- taking educated guesses;
- revising a model after contradictory evidence;
- tolerating uncertainty;
- deciding when evidence is sufficient to act;
- asking useful questions;
- timing speech/action;
- choosing what not to say;
- interpreting another person's testimony;
- distinguishing testimony from objective fact;
- speaking persuasively / tactfully / humorously / directly;
- recognizing when one's framing failed;
- deciding whether to repair, persist, withdraw, challenge or wait;
- managing commitments and competing obligations;
- learning a particular person's habits rather than relying on stereotypes;
- tracking consequences over time.

These are not avatar statistics.

They are actual user performance in Rawlsing.

---

## 2. Declared / portaled capabilities

Examples:
- piano virtuosity;
- physical strength/endurance;
- professional skill;
- fluency in a language;
- cooking ability;
- mechanical repair skill;
- athletic ability.

Rawlsing generally cannot establish whether these are true of the external human.

If the run permits the user to declare them, they can become part of the portaled participant's represented affordance set.

But:

`DECLARED PLAYER CAPABILITY != VERIFIED EXTERNAL-HUMAN CAPABILITY`.

---

## 3. Why declaration does not solve the actual game

Even extreme declared competence does not grant:
- access to hidden actor state;
- correct inference;
- accurate memory of what another person said;
- good timing;
- relationship history;
- understanding of local norms;
- persuasive wording;
- trustworthiness;
- repair skill;
- attention to third parties;
- causal understanding.

A user can declare:
> I am a concert pianist.

They cannot declare:
> I correctly understand why Nadia stopped talking.

They must earn that model through evidence.

This is the anti-cheese center.

---

## 4. Medium-truth principle

Rawlsing should reward most strongly the capacities that are **native to its medium**.

Candidate theorem:

> **The more central a capacity is to the game's relational purpose, the less it should be representable as a stat or self-claim and the more it should be exercised directly through play.**

Examples:

`ATTENTIVENESS` should be expressed through ATTEND choices, not an attentiveness score.

`PERCEPTIVENESS` should be expressed through inference and model revision, not a perception stat.

`TACT` should be expressed through actual language/timing, not a dialogue bonus.

`RELIABILITY` should emerge from kept commitments, not a trait declaration.

`CARE` should emerge through costly choices, not a compassion stat.

---

## 5. The player's actual skill ceiling

Rawlsing can become a game in which experienced players genuinely improve at:
- reading situations without overclaiming certainty;
- asking better questions;
- noticing what matters materially;
- remembering social history;
- modeling multiple possible explanations;
- detecting when their own assumptions are doing too much work;
- speaking in ways that fit the person rather than a generic script;
- acting before certainty when delay has costs;
- withholding action when intervention would be intrusive;
- repairing after misjudgment;
- understanding network effects;
- distinguishing another person's preference from their flourishing;
- recognizing their own causal contribution accurately.

These improvements are learnable player skill.

---

## 6. Persona response to declared spectacle

If the user performs an asserted spectacular capability in-world, the persona reacts only according to:
- what they perceive;
- current agenda;
- local meaning;
- prior relationship;
- their own interests;
- practical relevance.

Possible response:
- interest;
- admiration;
- amusement;
- indifference;
- discomfort;
- request for help;
- a future practical affordance;
- no response.

But the action does not directly test the user's real-world skill.

It is therefore not a core evidentiary reward surface.

---

## 7. Why honest play can feel better

Honesty is not enforced.

But truthful self-carry can produce a uniquely valuable feedback loop:

`ACTUAL USER ATTENTION / INFERENCE / SPEECH`
→ `REAL PERSONA RESPONSE`
→ `NEW EVIDENCE`
→ `UPDATED USER MODEL`
→ `MORE PRECISE ACTION`
→ `HISTORICALLY SPECIFIC RELATIONSHIP`.

The user can feel:

> I noticed that.
> I guessed right.
> I guessed wrong and figured out why.
> I remembered something from months ago.
> I found the words that actually reached her.
> I understood that she did not need what I assumed she needed.

Those satisfactions cannot be obtained simply by declaring an external capability.

---

## 8. False or invented self-presentation remains playable

If the user invents exceptional abilities, Rawlsing does not punish them.

It may create:
- spectacle;
- narrow new affordances;
- status/reputation;
- requests/expectations;
- altered social perception.

But it does not improve the user's native game capacities.

Thus the player may roleplay a more extraordinary portaled self while still facing the same core relational problem.

---

## 9. Reflection authority

Later reflection can make strong claims about native game behavior:

> You repeatedly checked practical circumstances before asking how you were being evaluated.

> You remembered three commitments across long gaps.

> You revised your interpretation after contradictory evidence.


It cannot make strong claims about unverifiable external capacities:

> You really are a pianist.

> You lied about Arabic.


Nor should it treat a declared run capability as evidence of external-human character.

---

## 10. External assistance caveat

A user may consult notes, another person, an LLM, translation tools, or other aids.

Rawlsing may not know.

Therefore even native player performance should be reflected as:

> what occurred through this player's interaction history

rather than:

> unaided cognitive ability of the external human.

This preserves epistemic humility.

---

## 11. Product consequence

Do not spend design effort trying to authenticate user skill claims.

Spend design effort making the genuinely playable skills rich:
- finite attention;
- partial observation;
- ambiguous evidence;
- meaningful timing;
- independent actor agendas;
- durable memory/history;
- nontransparent inner states;
- real consequences;
- relationship-specific language;
- revisable models.

The richer these systems are, the less attractive pure self-aggrandizing declaration becomes as a substitute for actual play.

---

## 12. Core theorem

> **You can invent what your portaled body can do. You cannot invent understanding of another person into existence.**

And:

> **Rawlsing's deepest rewards should attach to the capacities the user must actually exercise: attention, inference, memory, judgment and speech.**
