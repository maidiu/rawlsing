# Sorok — Entry Primer and Beginning-to-Know Relational Loop R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_PRODUCT_EXPERIENCE_LAW

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Scope correction

The next product problem is not yet five-year relationship topology.

It is:

> **How does the user begin wanting to know this person at all, and what makes early attempts at being around them feel rewarding, awkward, frustrating, interesting, or consequential in ways that naturally teach the relational game?**

Recent task/flux mechanics remain substrate.

Now design the first relational layer over that substrate.

---

## 1. Core motivational theorem

The primary driver should resemble ordinary friendship formation:

> **wanting to be around this person, and gradually learning how being around them works.**

Successful presence can be rewarding because:
- conversation becomes easier;
- shared humor emerges;
- the person remembers the user;
- the user understands references that previously meant nothing;
- practical cooperation flows better;
- the persona begins initiating contact or inquiry;
- awkwardness decreases in some domains;
- candor or disagreement becomes possible;
- the user becomes more specifically known;
- the persona becomes more specifically knowable.

Unsuccessful presence can be unpleasant because:
- conversation stalls;
- questions land badly;
- the user bores or irritates the persona;
- the persona misunderstands the user;
- boundaries become explicit;
- invitations do not occur;
- prior access narrows;
- shared activity becomes tense;
- the user realizes they were inattentive or presumptuous;
- the persona prefers someone/something else.

These are causal relational consequences, not reward/punishment tokens.

---

## 2. The game should prime curiosity, not moral compliance

The entry primer should not say:
- be empathetic;
- be kind;
- respect the culture;
- make good choices;
- improve the persona;
- maximize trust.

That produces Goodharting and moral-roleplay.

It should instead prime a challenge:

> **There is a person here. You do not know them. Can you actually come to know them?**

Supporting implications:
- pay attention;
- ask when you want to know;
- remember what matters;
- expect to be wrong sometimes;
- notice what changes;
- the other person is not obliged to explain themselves;
- they are also learning you;
- life keeps moving while this happens.

---

## 3. Reciprocity is constitutive

The persona should not be a passive object of user inquiry.

Nadia may be inquisitive about the user because she is a person relating to a new neighbor/acquaintance.

She may:
- ask where the user is from in the portaled sense;
- ask what they do;
- ask why they know something;
- ask about a remark the user made earlier;
- remember a disclosed detail and follow up later;
- ask opinions;
- tease;
- challenge;
- compare experience;
- become curious about an unusual habit;
- ask for advice;
- ask a question and then decide she does not want the answer.

Her curiosity must remain actor-owned, situational and variable.

`PERSONA INQUISITIVENESS != INTERVIEW BOT`.

---

## 4. Beginning-to-know loop

Candidate early relational loop:

`CO-PRESENCE / OCCASION`
→ `NOTICE SOMETHING`
→ `TALK / ASK / ACT / SHARE`
→ `PERSONA RECEIVES AND RESPONDS OR DOES NOT`
→ `USER LEARNS SOMETHING OR BECOMES MORE UNCERTAIN`
→ `PERSONA LEARNS SOMETHING ABOUT USER`
→ `SMALL HISTORY FORMS`
→ `NEXT ENCOUNTER IS NO LONGER FIRST-CONTACT`.

The key reward is cumulative specificity.

---

## 5. Hooks should arise from life, not authorial bait

An early encounter can contain natural hooks because any real person does:
- an unfinished task;
- a joke;
- an opinion;
- a child interrupting;
- an object with unknown meaning;
- a work complaint;
- an unusual preference;
- a religious practice;
- a contradiction;
- a question directed at the user;
- a place they are going;
- a mundane problem;
- an interest;
- a disagreement with someone else.

The system should not guarantee that every hook is deep or productive.

Some are dead ends.
Some are misunderstood.
Some become important much later.

---

## 6. Presence itself is a relational skill

Early gameplay asks:
- can the user tolerate ordinary silence?
- do they monopolize the conversation?
- do they follow up?
- do they remember answers?
- do they ask questions the persona actually wants to answer?
- do they know when to stop probing?
- do they share enough of themselves to permit reciprocity?
- do they notice when the persona is busy?
- do they adapt when a topic lands badly?
- do they only appear when useful/needed?
- can they enjoy mundane company?

Do not score these.

Let the relationship causally reflect them.

---

## 7. Early reward should be relational response

The user should palpably feel success through changes like:
- Nadia asks them a question without prompting;
- she remembers something they said;
- she continues a conversation after the practical reason for contact is over;
- a joke from last time returns;
- she volunteers a detail rather than only answering;
- she asks them to stay a little longer;
- she tells them about something that happened earlier that day;
- she disagrees more directly because politeness distance has decreased;
- she asks for their opinion specifically;
- she later initiates an encounter.

None should be guaranteed or monotonic.

These are not unlocks.
They are actor actions made possible by history.

---

## 8. Early failure should remain human-scale

Failure need not be catastrophic.

It may look like:
- answer becomes short;
- topic dies;
- user asks something too personal too soon;
- persona changes subject;
- persona leaves because they really were busy;
- user forgets a detail and gets corrected;
- attempted joke does not land;
- persona misunderstands an intention;
- user pushes advice when conversation was not asking for it;
- persona declines an invitation;
- next encounter feels slightly more guarded.

The game should permit recovery and reinterpretation.

One awkward interaction is not a relationship verdict.

---

## 9. Persona curiosity creates mutual stakes

If Nadia asks the user questions, the user must decide what to reveal.

This makes the relationship reciprocal rather than investigative.

The user may:
- answer fully;
- answer partly;
- joke;
- deflect;
- be vulnerable;
- exaggerate;
- decline;
- ask why she wants to know;
- turn the question back.

Nadia then forms actor-local beliefs from what she perceives.

Thus:

`KNOWING HER` and `BECOMING KNOWN BY HER` develop together.

---

## 10. Primer function

The primer should do three things only:

1. establish the ontology: this is a person with a life, not an assistant/quest;
2. establish the challenge: you do not know them; come to know them through life;
3. establish reciprocity: they are also deciding who you are and what relationship with you is possible.

Do not explain every mechanic.

Let the interaction teach SAY/DO/ATTEND and portal realization through use.

---

## 11. Candidate primer tone

Prefer challenge / invitation over instruction manual.

Candidate conceptual shape:

> A life has been drawn.
> You did not get this one.
> Someone else did.
>
> You are about to live beside them.
> You will not be told who they are.
> Pay attention. Ask. Remember. Guess wrong. Learn.
>
> They will be doing the same with you.
>
> The world will keep moving while you get to know each other.

This is not final copy; it establishes the product direction.

---

## 12. First-session design target

The first session should not attempt to demonstrate the entire product.

It should establish:
- the person is independent and busy with something;
- there are multiple natural things the user could notice/ask/do;
- Nadia can inquire about the user;
- the user can get something wrong;
- attention/time matter;
- conversation can continue beyond the practical task or end naturally;
- one or two details become memorable enough that the next encounter can call them back;
- the user leaves with unresolved curiosity.

Desired feeling:

> **I want to see what happens the next time I run into her.**

Not:

> I completed the tutorial.

---

## 13. Anti-manipulation constraint

The system must not manufacture chemistry or cliffhangers merely to retain the user.

Curiosity should arise because the generated person is sufficiently specific and partially opaque.

No guaranteed:
- mysterious trauma hint;
- flirtation;
- crisis;
- emotionally loaded confession;
- 'she seems like she wants to tell you more';
- engagement cliffhanger.

Ordinary specificity must be capable of carrying interest.

---

## 14. Product theorem

> **The first reward of Rawlsing is not access to a secret. It is the dawning experience that there is a particular person here, that being with them has a texture, and that what happens between you changes the next time you meet.**

And:

> **The game begins when the user wants to know what this person is like, while realizing that the person is also finding out what the user is like.**
