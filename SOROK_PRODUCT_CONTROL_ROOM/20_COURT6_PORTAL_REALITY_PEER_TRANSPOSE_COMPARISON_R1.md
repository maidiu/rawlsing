# Sorok Court 6 — Portal Reality, Real-Time Friend Countermodel, and Peer-Normalized Transposition R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_COMPARATIVE_MODEL_BANK__NOT_ADOPTED

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Purpose

Do not collapse the player-embedding problem to one implementation too early.

Sorok should preserve a deliberately extreme comparative model:

**24-hours-a-day real-time friend mode**

and compare it against the bounded game we are likely to build.

The point is not that the extreme mode is likely desirable. The point is that it reveals what different reality-coupling structures train, reward, distort, intensify or destroy.

---

## 1. Countermodel A — full real-time friend / portal reality

Extreme comparative version:

- persona life runs in real time, 24/7;
- persona may send ordinary text messages outside active app sessions;
- user enters the app for embodied/physical presence;
- user's real job is their job in the game;
- user's real self is their psychic/normative self in the game;
- user's real money is the money they can spend, potentially with any actual transfer routed to real-world charity/mutual aid rather than synthetic persona ownership;
- user and persona are somehow made directly adjacent despite originating in different social worlds.

This mode is not currently preferred.

Expected risks:
- compulsive attachment;
- parasocial substitution;
- constant attentional capture;
- sleep/work intrusion;
- guilt-based retention;
- synthetic dependency;
- collapsing game and life boundaries;
- wealthy users importing overwhelming resource power;
- impossible reconciliation of real family/friends/geography with the portaled world;
- pressure to continuously simulate persona consciousness;
- high cost;
- ethical confusion over responsibility to an artificial person.

Nevertheless this mode is valuable as a benchmark because it maximizes **reality coupling**.

---

## 2. Comparative question

Do not ask only:

> What implementation is easiest?

Ask:

> What experiential habitus does each reality-coupling structure produce?

Candidate evaluation dimensions:
- attention capture;
- sense of independent personhood;
- immersion;
- inference under uncertainty;
- responsibility;
- opportunity cost;
- relational specificity;
- compulsivity;
- parasocial substitution risk;
- domination/power asymmetry;
- real-world spillover;
- interpretive distance;
- ability to pause safely;
- pedagogical transfer;
- respect for otherness;
- implementation cost.

---

## 3. Portal-reality axis

Candidate continuum:

LOW COUPLING
→ discrete authored sessions / explicit game time
→ persistent asynchronous persona life
→ real-clock synchronized persona time
→ real text-message contact
→ real-user schedule/resource coupling
→ full portal-reality mode
HIGH COUPLING.

Different modes may occupy different points on different axes.

Time coupling, resource coupling, identity coupling and communication coupling should not be treated as one toggle.

---

## 4. Countermodel B — peer-normalized transposition

New strong candidate:

**The user remains psychically/normatively themself, but their material position is translated into a peer-equivalent position inside the persona's local field.**

This differs from:
- literal real-life import;
- arbitrary fictional avatar;
- generic thin shell.

Candidate principle:

USER PSYCHE / JUDGMENT / ATTENTION / IDEOLOGY
= carried through.

USER MATERIAL POSITION
= normalized/transposed relative to the local social field.

Examples:
- comparable SES band;
- comparable access to housing;
- comparable ordinary transport;
- comparable local status/power;
- comparable institutional reach;
- no superior foreign-currency purchasing power;
- no imported elite network advantage;
- no magical outsider visa/status privilege unless intentionally part of the mode.

The user enters as a **peer**, not as a benevolent alien with a stronger wallet.

---

## 5. Peer does not mean identical

Peer-normalization should not erase every asymmetry.

The user may still differ from the persona in:
- age;
- gender;
- ethnicity/race as locally legible;
- religion;
- language;
- education;
- occupation;
- disability;
- household structure;
- social role;
- legal status;
- migration background.

But the mode should avoid letting the user's real-world wealth, passport, institutional reach or geographic privilege simply overpower the local relationship unless that asymmetry is deliberately being studied.

---

## 6. Material normalization may be relational rather than numeric

Do not define peerhood only as:

> same income percentile.

Relevant equivalence may include:
- discretionary time;
- housing security;
- debt burden;
- transport access;
- family obligations;
- labor precarity;
- social connections;
- institutional power;
- legal vulnerability;
- caregiving load.

Peer status may therefore be a **local opportunity/power envelope**, not a single SES scalar.

---

## 7. Why this may fit the veil better

Rawlsing's original alternate-self intuition says:

> You do not know in advance which social position is yours.

Sorok cannot literally transform the user's developmental history into a native alternate self without ceasing to involve the actual user.

Peer-normalized transposition offers a different operationalization:

> You bring the subjectivity history made in you, but you do not get to bring the material power that would make this other person's world easy to dominate from outside.

This makes the user foreign in consciousness but local in affordance.

---

## 8. The tension is productive, not an error

A U.S.-formed user placed beside a person in rural Iraq or barrio El Salvador may retain assumptions that are socially foreign.

That mismatch should remain visible.

Do not convert the user into a 'native version' of themself and silently rewrite:
- family history;
- ideology;
- religion;
- moral intuitions;
- social expectations;
- language competence;
- habits.

Otherwise Rawlsing is roleplaying an alternate person rather than confronting the actual user.

---

## 9. But what about user's real family, friends and work?

This remains genuinely open.

Possible models:

### Model 1 — external-life bracket
The user's real-world family/friends/job remain outside the diegetic local world.
The game does not pretend they moved too.
The user is locally embedded only for the relation simulation.

Advantage:
bounded.

Risk:
ontological weirdness if local actors ask about the user's wider life.

### Model 2 — structurally translated external ties
The system preserves broad relational facts while translating them into locally coherent material form.

Example:
user has a sibling they speak to weekly → player has a sibling-equivalent relation in the portaled world.

Advantage:
more coherent whole-life embodiment.

Risk:
rapidly becomes full alternate-self generation and stops being the actual user.

### Model 3 — dual-world portal
The user's real family/job continue in the real world while the local persona relationship exists through a portal-like adjacency.

Advantage:
maximum actual-self continuity.

Risk:
fantastical framing; hard to maintain material/social realism.

### Model 4 — bounded local self with off-stage external continuity
Only facts that become relevant are admitted.
The player may truthfully say they have family/friends/work, but those ties are not fully simulated unless they enter causal relevance.

Advantage:
bounded and flexible.

Risk:
requires careful authority/provenance rules.

Current lean:
Model 4 may best preserve actual-self continuity without turning Rawlsing into total life simulation.

---

## 10. Avoid the life-simulator trap

The purpose is not to make the player:
- earn wages every day;
- shop for groceries as a core loop;
- optimize rent;
- grind job tasks;
- micromanage a household.

Material state exists to bound care and action.

It should be simulated at the **minimum causal grain** necessary to create:
- scarcity;
- opportunity cost;
- social position;
- practical consequence;
- power constraint.

Do not turn Sorok into text-based open-world life-management RPG.

---

## 11. Resource abstraction candidate

Rather than simulating every paycheck transaction, the player may have durable resource envelopes such as:
- available discretionary money;
- committed money;
- available discretionary time;
- transport capacity;
- household access;
- social/institutional reach;
- tools/skills;
- caregiving/work obligations.

These can be updated by important events.

Example:
cooking for a grieving neighbor for a month consumes meaningful time/money capacity without requiring thirty grocery-store minigames.

---

## 12. Real-time mode as destructive-control comparison

Keep asking:

> What would the 24/7 version do here?

Examples:

### Messaging
Bounded mode: persona contacts user when lawful; delivery can wait until app/session context.
24/7 mode: user's phone buzzes at 2:13 AM because persona cannot sleep.

Question:
Does this deepen reality or train compulsive vigilance?

### Absence
Bounded mode: absence is simulated history without retention pressure.
24/7 mode: persona may experience the user's nonresponse in real clock time.

Question:
Does this make responsibility meaningful or create coercive guilt?

### Money
Bounded mode: peer-normalized simulated resources.
24/7 portal mode: real money may correspond to real charitable transfer.

Question:
Does material sacrifice improve transfer to real solidarity, or let wealthy users dominate?

### Work
Bounded mode: player's work obligations abstractly constrain time.
24/7 portal mode: actual work calendar becomes game constraint.

Question:
Does this ground opportunity cost or make Rawlsing parasitic on the user's real life?

## 13. Countermodel C — full native transmogrification

Another extreme for comparison:

> You are now locally born/formed, and your demographic/SES/family position is translated into the local society.

This would require transforming:
- family;
- class;
- education;
- social identity;
- cultural formation;
- likely ideology/religion;
- developmental history.

At that point the participant is no longer meaningfully the actual user.

This may be useful for **alternate-self simulation** or educational comparison, but probably conflicts with Sorok's core relational-practice theorem.

Keep as a separate product mode/countermodel rather than blending it into the main experience.

---

## 14. Countermodel D — full fictional avatar

Conventional roleplay character.

Useful for:
- replay;
- narrative experimentation;
- testing worlds.

Weak for:
- real-user praxis inference;
- existential bearing;
- neighbor-love exercise.

Again: potentially valuable mode, but conceptually distinct.

## 16. Current strongest main-mode candidate

**Peer-normalized transposition with bounded local embodiment and off-stage external continuity.**

Meaning:
- psychically/normatively the actual user;
- locally assigned a peer-comparable material opportunity/power envelope;
- real family/friends/history not erased, but not fully simulated unless causally needed;
- local actor/world fully thick and native to the generated field;
- no imported foreign purchasing power or super-network unless deliberately part of a mode;
- resource simulation abstract enough to preserve focus on relationship.

## 17. Open questions

- What exactly makes two positions 'peer-comparable'?
- Should peer normalization use relative SES percentile, opportunity envelope, or a multidimensional power profile?
- How much age/gender/education should be normalized versus preserved?
- How are passports/legal status handled?
- Does the user keep their actual language repertoire?
- If they do not speak the local language, is translation mediated diegetically or by interface?
- How much external-family truth can be imported without total-life simulation?
- How does real-time messaging compare experimentally with bounded asynchronous delivery?
- Could a safe experimental 'live friend' mode exist with quiet hours / delayed delivery / strict nondependency rules?
- How much real-world coupling actually improves transfer versus merely intensifying attachment?

---

## 18. Core comparative theorem

**Portal realism is not automatically moral realism.**

Making the synthetic person more continuously intrusive into the user's real life may increase felt reality while simultaneously training worse habits: vigilance, dependency, saviorism, compulsive checking, asymmetric obligation.

Therefore every reality-coupling feature should be evaluated by:

> What kind of relational subject does this mechanic rehearse?

not merely:

> Does this feel more real?
