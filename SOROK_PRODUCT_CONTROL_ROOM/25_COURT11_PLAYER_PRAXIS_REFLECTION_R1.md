# Sorok Court 11 — Player Praxis Reflection and Evidentiary Limits R1

Status: OPEN_COURT__USER_MODEL_FRONTIER__NOT_ADOPTED

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Core question

What may Rawlsing legitimately learn and later reflect back about the user's relational praxis from what the user actually does in the simulation?

The goal is not personality diagnosis.

The goal is longitudinal behavioral recognition:

> what did you repeatedly do when another person became costly, opaque, disappointing, dependent, angry, different or important?

---

## 1. Evidence hierarchy

Potential evidence classes:

### DIRECT REALIZED ACTION
What the user actually said/did/attended to in-world.

### ATTENTION ALLOCATION
What they repeatedly chose to inspect/ignore under finite time.

### COMMITMENT BEHAVIOR
What they promised, remembered, missed, renegotiated, repaired.

### RESPONSE TO FRICTION
How they behaved under disagreement, refusal, misunderstanding, criticism or unmet expectation.

### RELATIONAL DISTRIBUTION
Whom they prioritized when obligations competed.

### SELF-REPORT
What they say about their own motives/intentions.

### SYSTEM HYPOTHESIS
Any inference about pattern, which must remain explicitly uncertain.

Do not collapse these evidence classes.

---

## 2. Behavior before diagnosis

Good reflection:

> When you were uncertain whether another person was upset with you, you often spent attention looking for evidence about their evaluation of you before checking the practical situation around them.

Bad reflection:

> You are narcissistic.

Good reflection:

> After conflict, you usually tried to solve the practical problem quickly and rarely returned to the other person's interpretation of what happened.

Bad reflection:

> You have avoidant attachment.

Reflection should stay close to observed history.

---

## 3. Patterns require longitudinal support

One event should not create a user trait.

Need thresholds/confidence based on:
- repeated comparable situations;
- counterexamples;
- context;
- available alternatives;
- material constraints;
- mode/embedding;
- uncertainty.

Possible output language:
- often;
- in these situations;
- so far;
- with this person;
- when time was short;
- evidence is mixed.

---

## 4. Homologous-peer complication

The user's behavior occurs under assigned material conditions.

Therefore reflection must distinguish:

USER PRAXIS
from
BEHAVIOR CAUSED PRIMARILY BY ASSIGNED CONSTRAINT.

Example:
a user rarely gives money because the homologous envelope is extremely tight.

Do not infer stinginess.

But perhaps how they allocate scarce time/money among competing obligations is still meaningful.

---

## 5. Cross-mode comparison could be informative but dangerous

A later user might replay under different homologous positions.

Interesting possibility:
- what patterns persist across resource envelopes?
- what changes when user has more/less time, money, status?

This could reveal structure-dependent behavior.

But do not imply one replay discovers the user's true self.

Mode differences are interventions, not personality tests.

---

## 6. What should Rawlsing never infer without much stronger authority?

Candidate forbidden/very high bar:
- psychiatric diagnosis;
- trauma history;
- hidden sexual motive;
- stable moral character;
- real-world criminality;
- real political allegiance not directly stated;
- real relationship behavior outside simulation;
- unconscious cause of user action;
- immutable personality type.

Even when user self-reports, distinguish self-report from system knowledge.

---

## 7. Reflection timing

Continuous feedback risks turning play into optimization.

If every scene says:
> You interrupted again.

the user starts gaming the evaluator.

Candidate timing:
- sparse periodic retrospectives;
- major chapter/year transitions;
- optional user-invoked review;
- end-of-run history.

Reflection should not become a live morality HUD.

---

## 8. Reflection should preserve ambiguity

Possible form:

> In six situations where another person's needs conflicted with a plan you had already made, you changed your plan four times, negotiated twice, and never simply refused. There is not enough evidence to know why.

This gives the user something real to think with without pretending omniscience.

---

## 9. Self-model revision can become part of gameplay

The user may periodically state what they think is happening:
- what they think the persona wants;
- what they think the relationship is;
- what they think they themselves tend to do.

Later Rawlsing can compare:
- prior user model;
- later evidence;
- revised user model.

Reward is model revision, not being correct the first time.

---

## 10. Attention as praxis

Homologous-peer mode makes attention especially revealing.

Possible longitudinal questions:
- does user inspect status/evaluation cues before practical needs?
- do they seek cultural rules instead of learning individuals?
- do they overgeneralize from one actor?
- do they notice children/third parties?
- do they prioritize ideology over immediate circumstance?
- do they keep investigating when action is already needed?

Again: these are behavioral patterns, not diagnoses.

---

## 11. Reflection and anti-Goodharting

Never create a visible target like:
- empathy;
- humility;
- cultural competence;
- compassion.

Users will optimize for it.

Better:
- factual history;
- counterexamples;
- uncertainty;
- surprising contrasts;
- concrete patterns;
- unresolved questions.

Let the user perform the moral interpretation.

---

## 12. User rights / privacy

Need explicit design for:
- what praxis data is stored;
- how long;
- whether it leaves the device/server;
- user inspection;
- deletion;
- export;
- disabling reflection;
- separation from advertising/monetization;
- whether models are used outside the simulation.

Player praxis data is potentially sensitive even when non-diagnostic.

---

## 13. Hard tests

1. User helps constantly under one resource envelope but refuses under another.
2. User repeatedly seeks approval cues but only with one persona.
3. User changes after learning local field complexity.
4. User gives socially desirable self-reports that conflict with realized action.
5. User behaves badly in one crisis but well across years.
6. User roleplays experimentally in a replay.
7. User asks Rawlsing 'what kind of person am I?'
8. User wants all praxis data deleted.

Any reflection system that jumps from behavior to essence fails.

---

## 14. Exit condition

Produce a user-reflection constitution with:
- admissible evidence classes;
- longitudinal thresholds;
- uncertainty language;
- forbidden inference classes;
- reflection timing;
- privacy controls;
- mode-awareness;
- examples of lawful and unlawful outputs.

Core candidate law:

**Rawlsing may reflect the history of what the user repeatedly did in relation. It must not pretend that this history gives direct access to what the user essentially is.**
