# Sorok — Dual Interface, Scene Exit, Messaging Hub, and Relationship Succession R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_PRODUCT_SHELL__NOT_IMPLEMENTATION_SPEC

Date: 2026-09-30

## 0. Core proposal

Rawlsing may need two distinct user surfaces corresponding to two distinct modes of relation:

### A. PORTAL / CO-PRESENCE INTERFACE
A stylized text-based RPG surface — terminal/TI-84-like, sparse but aesthetic — used when the user is physically present inside the portaled world.

### B. MESSAGE / OUTSIDE-PORTAL INTERFACE
A familiar messaging surface — WhatsApp-like in interaction logic, not necessarily literal visual imitation — used when the user and persona are not physically co-present but have a communication channel.

These are not merely cosmetic views over the same interaction.

They correspond to different causal affordances, time semantics, perception and social stakes.

---

## 1. Portal interface

Inside the portal:
- user is embodied/situated;
- SAY / DO / ATTEND are all available;
- environment and other actors are perceptible only through lawful access;
- time advances according to realized action duration / scene causality, not wall-clock typing speed;
- interruption, movement, tasks, other actors and physical constraints matter;
- the user can be present while nothing is said;
- the persona can leave, become busy, redirect attention, or end the encounter.

Candidate aesthetic:
- dark/simple terminal-like surface;
- text blocks with strong hierarchy;
- sparse contextual state rather than game HUD;
- enough visual stylization to feel like crossing a threshold from ordinary messaging into another reality;
- no omniscient stats/meters.

The interface should communicate:

> **You are through the portal now. The world has physical and temporal depth.**

---

## 2. Message interface

Outside physical co-presence, the user may retain a contact channel with people whose relationship supports one.

Messaging supports:
- text messages;
- delayed reply;
- no reply;
- user-initiated contact;
- persona-initiated contact;
- photos/documents/links later if lawfully modeled;
- invitations to meet physically;
- practical coordination;
- jokes/check-ins;
- arguments;
- brief low-significance exchanges.

The persona remains embedded in their life while messaging.

They may:
- be at work;
- answer later;
- send one line and disappear;
- forget;
- reply at an inconvenient moment in their own simulated day;
- decide not to answer;
- ask the user something.

Messaging is not a chatbot waiting room.

---

## 3. First scene should end because life ends it

The first portal encounter should not end because:
- tutorial completed;
- turn quota reached;
- player clicked END SESSION merely for product structure;
- relationship beat was achieved.

It should end because the actual scene develops a terminating cause:
- Nadia has to leave;
- work begins;
- child needs to go somewhere;
- bus arrives;
- appointment;
- meal ends;
- another obligation becomes pressing;
- user must leave under their portaled obligations;
- encounter naturally peters out.

Thus the product teaches immediately:

`CO-PRESENCE IS FINITE`.

---

## 4. The transition itself can be a major first reward

After the first scene terminates naturally, the user returns to the outside-portal interface.

Now a new contact exists.

The relationship has crossed a threshold from:

`STRANGER ENCOUNTER`
to
`PERSON WITH WHOM A CHANNEL EXISTS`

if the actual encounter generated a lawful reason/channel for continued contact.

This should feel significant without a score or explicit unlock animation.

Possible surface:
- contact appears by name/photo/identifier known to user;
- prior messages/none;
- last encounter timestamp;
- no relationship label beyond what has actually been established.

---

## 5. The channel should not be guaranteed

Not every first encounter automatically creates messaging access.

Possible routes:
- neighbor exchange naturally establishes number/contact;
- Nadia asks for user's number;
- user asks and she agrees/refuses;
- shared local group/channel;
- practical reason creates contact;
- no contact exists yet and future physical encounters must occur another way.

This preserves actor sovereignty.

---

## 6. Time semantics

Need separate time laws for the two surfaces.

### Portal time
Simulated continuous scene-time driven by realized actions and world events.

`WALL CLOCK != PORTAL SCENE TIME`.

Typing for two minutes need not consume two simulated minutes.

### Outside-portal / message time
Open question, but should preserve asynchronous life without requiring 24/7 wall-clock coupling.

Candidate bounded model:
- simulated life advances between contacts according to world schedule/events;
- messages have diegetic timestamps;
- persona reply timing follows simulated schedule/access/motivation;
- product may deliver messages when user returns or within humane bounded notification rules;
- no obligation to continuously synchronize every simulated minute to real wall-clock.

Keep full wall-clock coupling as Court 6 countermodel, not default.

---

## 7. Messaging hub as relationship roster

The outside surface may become the user's current Rawlsing social world.

Potentially:
- one initial contact;
- later multiple persona contacts;
- group threads only if actual network relations make them lawful;
- contacts may become quiet, estranged, active, practical, warm, conflictual, intermittent.

A contact list should not become a character collection screen.

Do not display:
- rarity;
- affinity score;
- completion percentage;
- unlock level.

Each thread represents a history, not an owned unit.

---

## 8. New-person succession should be relationally earned, but not hidden moral scoring

The user's proposal has strong product force:

> a later Rawlsing person becomes available only after the first relationship has become sufficiently real/consequential that introducing another life is warranted.

This prevents:
- rerolling until user gets a desirable persona;
- treating people as collectible content;
- shallow parallelization before the user learns relational attention;
- endless novelty replacing history.

But the gate must NOT be:
- hidden goodness score;
- persona happiness threshold;
- friendship level;
- compliance test;
- flourishing score.

Better candidate basis:

**relationship maturity / historical substance**, demonstrated by objective conditions such as:
- enough elapsed shared history;
- multiple distinct encounters;
- mutual recognition/contact channel;
- some stable expectations/routines/commitments OR meaningful rupture/repair/history;
- evidence that the user and persona have become specific to one another rather than first-contact generic.

Exact gate remains open.

---

## 9. Diegetic invitation to meet another person

The next-person invitation should preferably have diegetic meaning rather than appear as:

> NEW CHARACTER UNLOCKED.

Possible product framings:

### Portal-system invitation
> Rawlsing has arranged another meeting.

### Social referral
A current persona or another person in the network becomes the causal route to a new encounter.

### Human referral / invitation chain
An actual real-world user can invite another human into Rawlsing, saying in effect:
> this person mattered in my life; I think they should meet someone here.

These mechanisms should be distinguished rather than conflated.

---

## 10. Real-world vouching premise

User proposed an opening premise roughly:

> someone in your real life has spoken well enough of the effect you had in their life that Rawlsing has arranged for you to meet someone through the portal.

This is powerful **only if it is true**.

Rawlsing should not fabricate an unnamed real person's testimonial.

Possible lawful implementation:
- invitation codes/referrals from actual users;
- invited user receives a message indicating someone they know vouched/invited them, subject to consent/privacy design;
- seed cohort uses a different explicit invitation premise.

Do not begin an epistemically serious product by falsely claiming a real human said something they did not say.

---

## 11. Cold-start problem

A referral-only system cannot create the first user.

Potential solutions:
- founders/seed cohort receive explicit origin invitations;
- research/alpha invitations;
- generic portal selection not claiming a real-life vouch;
- later social referral becomes the preferred organic entry route.

Do not fake social proof to solve cold start.

---

## 12. Restart / deletion and continuity

User may always be allowed to abandon/delete a continuity and begin again.

But a reroll must not preserve the prior relationships as if nothing happened.

Core law:

> **A relationship belongs to its history. Resetting the world destroys access to that history unless an explicit archive mode preserves it as a non-live record.**

This creates real stakes without an arbitrary penalty.

Important distinction:

`RESET COST = LOSS OF CONTINUITY`

not

`RESET COST = PUNITIVE GAME CURRENCY / SHAME`.

---

## 13. Anti-reroll effect

The loss of prior relationships naturally discourages slot-machine persona selection.

A user can reroll.

But they are choosing:
- novelty/new person;
over
- this accumulated history.

That mirrors relational scarcity better than a hard 'one persona only' rule.

---

## 14. Need humane exit semantics

Persistence must not become coercive retention.

Potential options:
- archive a relationship without deleting history;
- pause notifications/contact;
- long absence is lawful;
- end a relationship diegetically where appropriate;
- delete world/relationship permanently;
- restart a new continuity separately if product allows multiple save universes.

Do not tell user:
> Nadia will suffer if you uninstall.

No guilt-retention.

---

## 15. Multiple personas: likely scarce, not unlimited

Later multiple contacts could create exactly the care-scarcity / competing-obligation dynamics Sorok wants.

But early product should likely begin with one.

New relationships should arrive slowly enough that:
- the first person becomes specific;
- attention remains scarce;
- users cannot simply switch to a new persona when one becomes difficult;
- contacts feel like people rather than content inventory.

Exact active-person limit remains open.

---

## 16. Invitation between surfaces

Messaging can naturally create portal scenes.

Examples:
- `Want to come over?`
- `Can you help me with something?`
- `We're going to the market Saturday.`
- `Are you around?`
- user proposes meeting and Nadia agrees/refuses/counters.

Accepting a meet causes transition:

`MESSAGE SURFACE`
→ `PORTAL ENTRY / SCENE LOAD`
→ `EMBODIED CO-PRESENCE`.

Scene ends through life/time constraint and returns to messaging surface.

This creates a natural cycle without game-menu abstraction.

---

## 17. Interface as ontology

The two-interface design makes several Rawlsing noncollapses immediately intuitive:

`CONTACT != CO-PRESENCE`
`MESSAGE != ACCESS TO ENVIRONMENT`
`READ RECEIPT != UNDERSTANDING`
`PHYSICAL PRESENCE != ATTENTION`
`RELATIONSHIP != SESSION`
`LIFE CONTINUES BETWEEN SCENES`.

The UI itself teaches the ontology.

---

## 18. Candidate early user journey

1. User receives legitimate invitation / enters Rawlsing.
2. Brief primer establishes portal premise.
3. User enters first portal scene already in the middle of a real situation.
4. User and persona begin knowing one another through live SAY/DO/ATTEND.
5. Scene ends naturally because life requires it.
6. Outside-portal hub now exists.
7. If contact channel was established, persona appears there.
8. Messaging / absence / life advancement occur.
9. A later meeting is proposed by either party or caused by local encounter.
10. Portal scene resumes.

This is a coherent alpha skeleton.

---

## 19. Deep product theorem

> **The portal is where you share a world. The message thread is where the relationship persists when you do not.**

And:

> **New people should not arrive because the user consumed enough content. They should arrive because the user's relational world has become large enough to contain another person.**
