## I. The wish to meet a person who was not made to meet you

The first fact about NEIGHBOR is not a psychological model, a simulated country or an action grammar. A real human wants to encounter a particular, historically constituted fictional Other. They want to know her. That wanting is legitimate, pleasurable, and capable of becoming a history. The person they meet—Nadia, or the particular Other at the center of a continuity—has been living in a material and social world for reasons that do not derive from this visitor's desire. The experience begins because the visitor seeks someone; **the relationship can begin only because she was not already theirs**.

This is neither the construction of a private friend optimized for compatibility nor a morality examination disguised as company. The person is not a dossier, a trauma archive, a religious or political teaching aid, a romance funnel, or a performer who becomes fascinating in response to an attention command. The visitor has an entire actual life beyond the fictional threshold; Nadia has a fictional life whose terms of reality the visitor does not author. The game's deliberate asymmetry—one real human and a generated but independently governed fictional Other—is never erased by the phenomenology of genuine attachment. The representation can matter to the human without pretending to be an actual human outside it.

**The unequal ontology cannot be made equal by the portal.** The real human can close the service, choose another representation, and exercise powers over their own actual life. The fictional Nadia cannot escape the particular simulated history by those means. The platform itself determines compute allocation, historical resolution, narrational exposure and archival continuation: power that neither simulated actor can control. The consequence is not a claim that fictional people possess biological consciousness or literal human rights; nor does it make the real visitor's emotional attachment fictitious. It is a limit on what a person may claim to have learned by simulating a constrained life—**the user has not actually lived Nadia's loss, birth, poverty or bodily vulnerability, and may not convert their brief role into authority over those who do**.

**The first contradiction:** a genuinely usable encounter must provide an opening, and an independent person does not exist to supply one. The solution is neither to plant a confessional hook and call it spontaneity, nor to make the visitor hunt for a hidden command while an unresponsive scene proclaims its realism. An ordinary world can create *actual social occasions*: a neighbor's introduction, a borrowed ingredient, a shared corridor, a person already waiting for someone else, a task whose continuation requires ordinary cooperation. The visitor must know enough about where they stand, what is happening and what they might try; they must not be handed an emotional outcome. The occasion is legitimately framed. **Interest, relief, embarrassment, affection, boredom, refusal and the possibility of a second meeting remain unconferred**.

The first minute of the visitor's experience is not the first minute of Nadia's Thursday. It may be late afternoon, she may be leaving for work, her sister may already be speaking to her, a child may call from another room, the visitor may interrupt a necessary errand. Such details are not canonical script beats. The original stairwell, groceries, first dinner and sensitive bathroom chain are tests of what entry needs to make *possible*, not what all first meetings must enact. The fiction may establish a bounded precontact neighborly premise—a place, a prior introduction, a minor obligation—without inserting an intimate backstory, a promise the visitor never made, or a friendship that neither party has had time to form. A mere invented prelude is not a license to invent the real person's self.

The visitor's threshold has two different authorities. A real-world invitation to the experience can be truthful, whether a genuinely sent invitation or an explicitly disclosed project entrance. Within the fiction, the particular encounter must have its own person-and-world-conditioned reason. Neither a real-world referral nor a shared simulated seed grants permanent access to Nadia. Their apparent alignment cannot covertly forge reciprocal desire. A good opening can be engaging even if she is distracted, impatient, unfunny or unavailable; the visitor can decide that the meeting was not worth repeating. That is not an engine failure to be repaired by manufacturing interest.

**The first communication should not lecture on the computational estate.** The real person need not be given the survey model, the whole developmental history, or an ontology of agency to be able to enter. They do need to know enough about the fiction's status, their human authorship and the bounds of the encounter. The historical invocation “become neighbor to a life that could have been yours” remains a *candidate* expression of the invitation's stakes—not adopted marketing copy and not a promise that their own embodied lifetime has been exchanged for Nadia's. A founder or alpha entrance cannot counterfeit an endorsement from a real friend. The invitation's essential act is to make an independent possible life worth approaching **without telling the visitor which response to have**.

The first encounter also has a material ending. A shift starts; a guest arrives; both become tired; the conversation loses its reason for continuing; one party leaves. A visitor may linger and be unwelcome, depart and be missed, or exchange contact details that never become important. The scene must not conclude when a scripted revelation is delivered or the system has earned a retention event. It may leave a small new obligation, an irritation, a shared joke, a mistaken belief, a useful practice, or literally nothing durable. **An encounter's finite occasion can be meaningful without having been designed to generate meaning**.

The positive experience matters here. A person may be enjoyable before they become deeply understood. A remark can amuse; a way of speaking can be appealing; shared work can have an unexpected rhythm; a silence can begin to feel like company. These are not disposable embellishments to the serious program of recognizing alterity. They are some of the very ways a relationship begins. Yet the other may not enjoy the same thing, may enjoy it for another reason, or may never notice that a relation is beginning. The user does not have a claim on a reciprocal feeling merely because their own desire is sincere.

**The experience has several independently sufficient scales.** A single perceptive glance, an awkward conversation, a day of ordinary work, repeated meetings, a durable friendship, a farewell and a years-long history of encounters are not stages that all must be completed to justify one another. A moment can be interesting without progressing the relation; a relationship can be transformative without supplying a grand scene; a user can encounter several differently constituted people without making their lives an investigation or collectible catalogue. The older October search for a unifying motivational loop had already failed when timed tasks, hidden vacancies and existential riddles made Nadia a means to the player's development. **The possibility of enjoyment is not proved by our ontology**: the actual human may find her fascinating, congenial, uninteresting, exasperating or beloved. An honest product must make these possibilities possible without writing any of them into her fate.

The founding political and theological wager is thus immanent rather than a tutorial: another life could have developed under conditions the visitor did not choose, and knowing someone may transform one's understanding of these conditions. But that person does not become an exhibit of deprivation, an exemplary victim, a vehicle for the visitor's redemption, or an incarnation of the designer's politics. The experience can make humility, solidarity or care imaginable by placing finite agency before another irreducible center. It may also expose possessiveness, impatience or indifference. **The world may make the encounter possible; it does not guarantee that the visitor learns the correct lesson.**

The first movement therefore has an unresolved remainder: NEIGHBOR needs enough artifice to become an experience at all, while refusing the artifice that would determine whether the two people become interested. The exact measure cannot be fixed by a standard first-person scene or universal discovery recipe. It is judged by whether the invitation yields a *truthful occasion to participate* with someone already occupied by their own life.

## II. A real human in a materially equal starting position, without a counterfeit childhood

The visitor is not an omnipotent narrator and is not secretly another procedurally generated adult. Their own mind, knowledge, taste, memories, habits and limitations remain those of the actual person at the keyboard. What NEIGHBOR supplies is a **thin, historically accountable local body and social position** through which that real human may act in Nadia's world. The portaled person has a place to stand, a budget, available time, bodily reach, social visibility and materially feasible attempts. Those facts are fictional and real *within the fictional world*; they do not overwrite the human's biography.

The user-settled starting condition is stronger than vague shared class or neighborhood proximity: **at entry, the visitor possesses exactly the same ordinary socioeconomic purchasing capacity as Nadia**. This equality is a material constraint, not a visible similarity meter. The visitor knows what groceries, transportation or lost work time cost them. They do **not** receive Nadia's bank statement, debts, household transfers, dependents, precarious obligations, private valuation or subjective experience. Initial purchasing equivalence does not imply equal responsibility for a child, equivalent legal rights, the same employer, identical safety, or equal reserves of family help. Economic positions can later diverge through separate histories.

**The match is multidimensional, not a single income percentile or UI bar.** Its historical constitution must respect the source-qualified SES footprint—housing, income and expenditure opportunities, transport, debts, legal/economic access, work time, care liabilities and social position to the extent the original matching rule actually covers them—while holding the controlling exact initial **ordinary relative purchasing burden** equal. A person who can pay the same proportionate cost for dinner does not therefore have Nadia's family help, work authority, sexed social position, obligations or experiential confidence. An apparent match between two financial bars is neither sufficient proof of SES homology nor permission to disclose Nadia's private account. **The economic intervention is exact within its specified domain, and the unequal lives outside that domain are equally real.**

**Exactly equal initial material position is not an instruction to reveal Nadia's finances.** The visitor's own resource condition may be shown as **YOUR RESOURCES**, including a coarse cost indication for a contemplated meal when useful; it is not a mirror pair of bars exposing Nadia's balance. What she owes, earns, transfers or feels about scarcity remains hers unless actually known through conversation, observation or other lawful access. Paying Dutch, offering to cover a meal, concealing difficulty and declining an expense are different decisions; the recipient's subjective meaning is not inferred from the initial equality. Later changes in pay, debt and household obligation can separate the original matched starting positions without quietly resynchronizing them.

The decisive contradiction is **objective material homology without historical or phenomenological identity**. A stranger placed into the same practical spending condition has not lived Nadia's family, education, culture, migration, class training or institutions. The visitor may be legally and socially treated as a local peer but misunderstand an expected greeting, infer the wrong meaning from a religious practice, fail to recognize a class insult, or speak with an unfamiliar repertoire of references. Nadia may legitimately expect local knowledge and misinterpret its absence. She may also mistake an unfamiliar gesture for a class offense. Neither person's account automatically becomes the authoritative theory of the encounter.

The portal must therefore wear an honest *scar of translation*. A user's sentence can be rendered in a world language with an explicit, bounded substitution where that is defensible; its pragmatic register, joke, social connotation or locally unfamiliar concept may remain awkward. A visitor who mentions their actual school cannot be silently given attendance at a corresponding local school merely to make the dialogue flow. A reference to an external city, political institution, language or credential may remain an explanation needed between persons. Nadia can believe, disbelieve, misconstrue or ask about such a claim, but cannot receive authenticated knowledge of the real user's past from an invisible platform dossier.

**The historical translation alternatives have different authorities.** *CARRY* retains what the real human actually authored; *HOMOLOGIZE* can map a bounded world coordinate without pretending the original local fact occurred in the user's biography; *BRACKET* marks where a statement has no truthful local counterpart; and an explicit *USER-SELECT* can sometimes settle a world-feasible local alternative without writing a new self. These are provisional theoretical distinctions, not four compulsory implementation switches. A translation that materially changes the realized outward SAY must be intelligible to the visitor; Nadia hears that realized utterance, not the unspoken intention or the platform's explanation. A competent translation can preserve semantics while losing a joke, insult, register or code-switch, and *NO_HOMOLOGY* must remain possible rather than covertly forging a life.

The visitor can also lie, exaggerate, joke, decline to answer, make an unverifiable claim or assert a skill they have not demonstrated. **Actual authored speech, authorized local realization, independently evidenced competence, local reputation and Nadia's belief are separate**. Claiming virtuoso musicianship cannot magically make the visitor perform a piece; a bounded fictional capability, if actually admitted, can become a social fact with costs and expectations. A user who sincerely explains a concept may demonstrate a medium-native intellectual skill through real words, while the platform must not turn one correct answer into proof of their education or permanent personality. Being believed is not equivalent to being true; being caught out is not the only reason an unverified self-presentation matters.

The peer's embodiment is consequential. The user's actual physical body is not magically reproduced in the simulation, but neither are all fictional bodily abilities conferred by grammatical fluency. A hand may catch tossed keys under stated physical conditions. A fall may be lucky, a repair may be difficult, a task may require access, tools, rest or competence. A claim about how effortlessly one performs an action is not the world's settled outcome. The local body can suffer inconvenience, inability or danger without thereby changing the user's extra-game body.

**Chance and assessment can move independently of competence.** Catching tossed keys may be a lucky result of Nadia's easy throw, short distance and an unoccupied hand; Nadia may thereafter believe the visitor has excellent reflexes. Missing them can be a non-event or become a shared joke without proving clumsiness. The external human's actual device latency, disability or unfamiliar input method must not become an unearned universal judgment about their strength, worth or bodily talent. In contrast, a mathematically intelligible explanation or a genuinely composed joke can be meaningfully enacted through language. **Human faculty, represented body, worldly luck, performed result and another person's assessment are five separate causal objects**; even a mistaken assessment can produce future invitations and obligations.

There are **two unequally reversible exits**. The real person can close the interface and go back to their actual life; they must not be punished by a platform for doing so. Inside Nadia's world, an agreed obligation may remain unmet, another worker may cover a shift, or Nadia may reasonably be disappointed if a promise has been broken. Those consequences arise from established fictional acts, not from app retention strategy. The visitor's local shell may continue thin rent or sleep maintenance but may not undertake offscreen emotionally meaningful choices, deliver apologies, make promises or build a private personality while the real human is absent. The outside human's own resources and obligations cannot be requisitioned by the fiction.

These differences are the product's materialist force, not defects to conceal. The experiment places two distinct historical minds within an initially matched economic situation, where new relations can form and old interpretive habits become visible *through what the human actually tries*. It neither grants a native soul nor demands that the visitor impersonate a stereotyped local role. It permits friction, mutual mistake, genuine learning, misrecognition and unrelated choice. **Equality of one initial material condition intensifies rather than abolishes the unequal histories the two bring to it.**

**Cultural difference is not an exhaustive binary between the visitor and 'local culture'.** Another person from Nadia's community may disagree with Nadia's norms, share an unexpected conviction with the visitor, or correctly recognize that a familiar custom conceals domination. A visitor's unfamiliarity may be a source of error or a vantage for noticing a real injustice. A local speaker is not entitled to stand as a representative of their entire culture, and the visitor's eloquent dissent is not proof of mastery. *Material position, acquired capacity to navigate local conventions, independent moral judgment and each person's right to speak* remain distinct.

**The social legibility of this local shell is especially treacherous.** A university-educated human may be greeted as an ordinary local worker yet speak with technical vocabulary their interlocutor associates with a credential, political faction or class that the human does not possess in the fiction. A local school may expect paperwork or family knowledge the human never acquired. Unmarked peer status can enable entry and create false confidence in belonging simultaneously. Neither the platform's knowledge of the human's real educational background nor the visitor's own local classification authorizes forged records or social rights. The force of the experiment lies in *historical mismatch under initially comparable purchasing constraints*, not the romantic fiction that poverty, accent or local dress grants immediate epistemic equivalence.

## III. The world is actual before attention; discovered facts are not invented causes

When the visitor enters a kitchen, its food, objects, bills, layout, labor history and owners must be true within an independently continuing world. This is the difference between a causally inhabited world and a narrator's selection of props. A repaired shoe is not present because the human needs a metaphor for poverty; a book is not selected because it will disclose an identity; a knife is not a clue whose psychological meaning has been preassigned. Each can be looked at, borrowed, misplaced, discussed, damaged or simply ignored, and its further significance depends upon actual material and social history.

**The central opposition is a world that must already have enough truth to constrain events and a simulation that cannot explicitly pre-generate every conceivable detail.** Historical completion is lawful only within constrained causal slack. If the visitor notices a repaired strap, further grounded information may be required about when or by whom it was repaired. That can be an inert detail, an episodic association, a modest causal contributor or, with substantially stronger evidence, a formative part of life. These grades are not exchangeable. A late description may deepen the object's material provenance without manufacturing a childhood memory for Nadia. A later demand for a deep secret is not a sufficient cause to install one in the past.

This is why *world depth* and *the visitor's knowledge* are two different frontiers. The simulation may need to determine whether a key works because a co-parent attempts to use it offscreen; the visitor can remain unaware. The visitor can inspect an old photograph and learn its visible features without learning what every person depicted remembers. A user theory—“this must have traumatized her”—cannot become the source of the photograph's world history. Equally, a true past object can have no meaningful psychological relation to Nadia. A world becomes more determinate by respecting actual causal demands, not by making every discovered detail a key to her inner life.

**Three apparently similar empty slots must never be conflated:** a fact or object can be `NOT_MATERIALIZED_YET` (its bounded historical specification has not been required), `ABSENT` (the world has established it is not there), or `PRESENT_BUT_UNOBSERVED` (it exists but the visitor lacks access or has not attended). These are epistemic and world-authority distinctions, not three decorative uncertainty labels. An unopened drawer does not wait for ATTEND to create its contents, but neither may the narrator insist that a not-yet-specified drawer contains a dramatic relic. Any later qualification must bind to inherited root facts, qualified source evidence and already performed consequences. **An unknown past is not a blank permission to rewrite one**.

A living thing can occupy several distinct kinds of historical past: performed public events; recurring practice; admissible compressed periods; an actor's actual receipt of a particular event; retained traces or associations; conscious recollections; rumors and testimony; and later reinterpretation. **Occurrence is not memory; memory is not historical fact; narrative explanation is not a new occurrence.**

**The visible surface also creates forward historical debts.** When prose reports that Nadia catches a falling mug and looks toward a hall, it has done more than embellish a paragraph: the mug and hall must have lawfully existed, her posture and opportunity must allow the catch and glance, the actions actually happened, time passed, and observers might later remember them differently. The narration cannot retract an already published outward act as mere “artistic phrasing” when its consequences become inconvenient. **World history constrains the backward cause; a lawfully displayed event binds the subsequent future**, though its meaning remains disputed. This is another reason that more beautiful prose cannot stand in for a truthful event. A person can falsely remember a visit or sincerely misattribute the reason for an old gesture. Their belief can then cause actual new behavior without rewriting the earlier event. Conversely, a material fact can constrain their life without their knowing it.

Objects carry *produced human activity* into the present. A borrowed book may embody ownership, paid work, caring labor, scarcity, a gift history, a political argument, a remembered journey or nothing beyond ordinary use. The material object, Nadia's learned relation to it, and what lending it to this particular visitor means are distinct. Showing, lending, gifting, exchanging, entrusting, withholding, stealing, repairing and sharing are different acts, with different rights, intentions, risks and consequences. A refrigerator's food can be communal, rationed, promised to somebody else or provided under an ambiguous courtesy; “help yourself” is not a universal grant to eat, rummage, take a photograph or interpret the family's economy.

**Ownership and relationship may move separately even during the same exchange.** Nadia can *show* an annotated page without surrendering possession; *lend* a book with expectation of return; *entrust* it for safekeeping; *sell* it to pay an electricity bill; *give* it without expectation of repayment; *permit a visitor to inscribe it* and later regret that permission; or *reclaim* it when a condition is violated. A transaction can change ownership without creating intimacy, or alter the terms of reliance without changing title. Its value to donor, recipient, creditor, child and observer may differ—and later reinterpretation need not restore the object. **Object relations remain material transformations with separate, fallible social meanings**, not one acquire/reject switch controlled by affection.

**An object's value is not a universal symbol, and a relationship is not a new object-meaning psyche.** Histories of making, buying, using, maintaining, class position, institutional ownership, memory and reciprocal exchange can shape what it means to an actor. That meaning may differ with audience and occasion. The inherited relational person/other/event mediation—not a parallel “book engine,” “hospitality engine,” or hidden symbolism dictionary—must make such significance derivable where sufficient evidence exists. A paid worker can use a tool expertly without owning it; a household member can command its use through an institutional entitlement while another person's labor maintains it.

**Human power objectified in a thing is not automatically human power alienated.** A built house, learned song, repaired shoe and shared language can be ordinary achievements of collective activity; alienation names historically particular relations in which such powers are estranged, dispossessed or imposed. Commodity fetishism cannot be applied as a universal insult to every emotionally meaningful object. A bought appliance may nevertheless conceal labor and institutional power in the conditions of its production and maintenance. A rented apartment may be experienced as home while the landlord retains legal powers to exclude; a worker's expert skill may be theirs while the employer controls the tool and its products. **Material possession, juridical authority, bodily skill and subjective value cannot be derived from one another**, though their collisions shape what the visitor can actually do.

Material and social structures operate even when no one explains them. Rent, wages, work shifts, childcare, war, policy, transportation, religious authority, disability, law, local custom and weather can alter possible activity. A public historical event must be grounded at its proper external/local boundary and affect a branch through actual exposure and causal chains, not because it completes a visitor's political discussion. A school decision may change the child's time before Nadia learns the reason. One apparently generous invitation can be practically expensive because a household's food and labor are scarce. Material equivalence at entry does not dissolve these distinct relations.

Nor does *the visitor's contribution* become sole authorship of a later change. A child's musical aspiration may be enabled by a teacher, sibling, instrument, a story told by the visitor, peer enthusiasm and independently developed interest. The visitor can be consequential without being the missing key to an entire life. Their interpretation may be partly accurate, just as Nadia's may be. An event that changes none of the relationships under inquiry can still have occurred and consumed real time.

**The material past is not a single chronology accessible from one source.** Public historical anchors—an independently documented strike, storm, law, war or price shock—are not themselves identical to the admitted synthetic local world, and neither is identical to a learned model's prior expectations about how this kind of household generally behaves. A public change reaches a particular branch only through geography, institutional exposure, resources, specific histories and situated people; it need not recruit the same action or suffering in two branches with different preexisting circumstances. A father's medical need, a family's care obligation or an already broken employment relation can make the same public health or labor policy materially consequential for one Nadia and irrelevant to another. The engine may not paste the same fate onto two histories because the headline is the same. The visitor may be wrong about the public fact, the local effect or the actor's interpretation, and each error has a distinct provenance.

An imagined world need not generate every everyday minute at maximal resolution. Compression can conserve stable routines, ordinary constraints and qualified historical transitions while deepening particulars when an actual causal need arises. **The decisive requirement is historical nonarbitrariness, not indiscriminate detail or theatrical density**. A crowded Thursday can be merely a Thursday. The world can remain unremarkable without ceasing to be independent.

**Artistic and scientific compression are not documentary reconstruction.** A small number of grounded developmental episodes may stand for repeated labor, prolonged power exposure or accumulated shifts of meaning, without claiming that hundreds of specifically unobserved episodes have been independently verified. Nor may a stereotyped “year of trauma” be substituted for a childhood through arbitrary drama weights. This is a scientifically disciplined *fictional* history whose possible totality exceeds what the machine explicitly reconstructs; it cannot assert that it recreated the actual life of a survey respondent. The experiential challenge is to make the resulting ordinary duration credible without populating every return with artificial crises.

## IV. An action has to occur: saying, doing, attending and the return of control

The real human's freedom enters the world through actual attempted activity. They can **SAY, DO and ATTEND**, separately or in overlapping combinations. They may make a joke while glancing at an object, offer help without knowing how to provide it, follow an argument while carrying groceries, or remain silent as a conversation changes around them. These are not commands addressed to an obedient story. They are possible contributions from a situated person whose body and means are limited, whose intention may be misunderstood, whose speech may be interrupted and whose effects will be settled by a reality not authored by the desire expressed.

**Intention is not world event; prose is not performance; performance is not intended consequence.** “I help her child” proposes a purpose. The child has an actual problem, existing skills, questions, fatigue and independent responses; the visitor must contribute something that can make a difference. “I repair the toilet” does not pre-fix the mechanism, invent the missing tool or decide that Nadia admires the result. “I apologize sincerely” specifies attempted speech and intention, not convincingness or Nadia's belief. The visitor may lie to the world, to Nadia or to themselves; the interface has no authority to treat a first-person claim about motives as visible proof that those motives existed. The world's realization and another's experienced uptake remain separate.

The same distinction governs a text-native form of agency. Words can perform an explanation, argument, joke, confession or refusal through their actual content. A person can genuinely demonstrate understanding in the medium by reasoning, translating, questioning and producing an explanation; the model must not invalidate this by privileging a synthetic list of avatar skills. Conversely, the mere statement “I give an excellent explanation” cannot supply the explanation. Unverifiable external capabilities need an honest boundary: the visitor's claimed past, a locally authorized capacity, witnessed competence and another person's interpretation are different. The world can sometimes let a local ability exist without pretending it proves the real person's entire biography.

**A broad instruction is sometimes an entrance into activity, not an entitlement to skip it.** Early SOROK task theory corrected its own tempting idea that neutral work should always be silently completed. At first encounter “I help with groceries” may require deciding where to carry bags, finding a destination, negotiating with someone who wants the visitor to stop or discovering that an expected item is missing. Under an established shared routine the same ordinary phrase may fairly compress familiar low-salience steps. Historical competence changes what can be delegated: not merely proficiency in the task, but a learned division of practical agency. **Compression may omit procedure; it may not secretly choose moral character, emotional attitude, controversial allocation of labor or expressive self-presentation for the visitor.**

**The groceries case draws the practical boundary precisely.** “I'll help carry these upstairs” makes an actual flight of steps, bags, water cases, eggs, fragile handles, occupants and time available to perception. It need not require describing every step or a separate click for each bag. But neither may it teleport twelve bags to the apartment while silently deciding that the visitor bravely carried all the heaviest ones. The user might take a modest load, choose the fragile items, ask for the important bags, make multiple trips, attempt an unsafe overload, or decide not to help. Each can have different worldly consequences for the same original generous intention. **The method of a material act matters where it changes what someone else must do, can perceive or later remember**. This is a hostile example of the action law, not a prescribed tutorial errand.

A user who writes “I make dinner” has not decided whether to spend scarce ingredients, cook the dish Nadia expects, use a child's allergy-sensitive utensil or feed an unexpected guest. Sometimes the situation supplies an established plan and procedure can be compressed. Sometimes materially consequential alternatives exist and control must return. That is not interface pedantry. **The moment of regained control is the moment at which an independent world has produced a real choice the human still owns.** Silent overexecution would make the system the moral actor while reporting the visitor's decision.

Temporal granularity must follow actual activity rather than token count. A thirty-word instruction need not consume thirty seconds, a short question need not be instantaneous, and one entered action may overlap another. “Rome? You actually went?” spoken while glancing toward a bill is a concurrent social and bodily attempt, not a completed conversational scene followed by an independent inspection. Nadia may hear the question and see a head turn, hear but not see the glance, be absorbed in a child, misunderstand its target, or never notice. None of those outcomes is chosen by the visitor's wish to be discreet or the narrator's interest in creating suspicion.

A long planned utterance illustrates the same relation. The user types several paragraphs; only the part actually spoken before interruption, fatigue, a colleague's arrival or another's refusal is a public historical speech event. The unspoken remainder must not become a quotation in Nadia's memory. She may hear all three questions and answer one, understand a joke but be unwilling to acknowledge it, or answer a peripheral detail because it is currently more important to her. The system should return control at a meaningful fork rather than generate a fully satisfying assistant answer to everything the human typed. **Conversation itself is activity with duration, overlap, floor rights and competing objects of attention**, not a message sent to a guaranteed responder.

Attending has its own material rights and limits. Looking is not touching; recognition is not movement; noticing a document is not reading its small print; considering taking an object is not stealing it. Distance, orientation, light, occlusion, existing task, bodily capacities and time qualify what the user can access. A private glance can influence the user's own subsequent judgment without telepathically informing Nadia. But a genuinely outward head turn, hand movement, whispered phrase or interruption may be detectable by her. **The private intention, outward act, available sign, actor receipt, actor inference and objective consequence must not be coalesced into one convenient command result.**

**Interrupted attention can legitimately leave a partial achievement.** A person approaching a utility bill might recognize the document type and first two words, then turn because the child cries or Nadia returns; neither the whole bill nor a null observation is warranted. An extended inspection may require movement, changed light, permission or time; that movement itself can be noticeable to another person even if no text is successfully read. Accessible explanation, notes and translation aids can help the actual human understand the portion of evidence they have received; they must not complete the missing inspection, invent the visitor's inference or substitute a morally selected response. Perception, human interpretation and physical concealment remain three different powers.

Paraphrase should not become a hidden way of controlling physical outcome. The same performed lift, given the same body, load and world conditions, cannot become safer because the prompt was more ornate. Underlying linguistic clarity can legitimately make the intended operation more determinable; persuasive speech can legitimately affect a listener through what is actually said. But the surface fluency of an instruction is neither strength nor force nor permission. Genuine uncertainty at a consequential physical fork can require bounded clarification or conservative action, not an LLM's confident invention of what “put it there” must have meant.

The human is therefore a genuine participant, not a ghost who only types descriptions of desired consequences. Their action is allowed to fail, be ignored, go unnoticed, create obligation, interrupt a relationship, or alter what another person can try next. The world's counterforce is not a punitive game master. It is what makes agency *actual*: a human must negotiate the existence of a person, object, body, schedule and institution beyond their utterance.

## V. The textual surface gives finite experience, not a privileged explanation

The world can be historically true without being fully visible, and the visitor can sincerely attend without arriving at a complete theory of it. This is an especially severe contradiction for a text interface: **whatever the renderer names may appear specially meaningful merely because it has been named**. A refrigerator, tired voice, expensive knife, prayer book or photograph is not a prompt-selected sign waiting to be interpreted. A room first appears as a lived gestalt—its shape, broad light, disorder, sounds, occupied bodies, odors, immediate practical possibilities—not as a menu of objects whose inspection is rewarded with hidden character lore.

There must be an *independent phenomenal surface* between world history and the visitor. It selects actually available evidence under bodily location, attention and acquired recognition; it does not invent what its user seems to care about. Someone who has long worked with cooking knives may recognize a blade's use, damage or workmanship that an otherwise observant person cannot. Another visitor may notice a religious emblem or the wear of a repaired shoe. Both are seeing the same world through different acquired habits. **Personalized perception is a necessary acknowledgment of embodied learning; personalized evidence manufacturing would be epistemic corruption.**

**Even an apparently descriptive adjective can import the visitor's historical valuation into the world.** A kitchen may lawfully appear “homey,” “pretentious,” “messy” or “well loved” to somebody whose class formation and practical memory make it look that way. That first impression is not the kitchen's canonical property or Nadia's motive. A learned perceptual regime can make a detail *obvious* to one person and peripheral to another while narrowing their imagination of what else is possible in the room. A better model of the visitor's eye can therefore produce a less adequate model of Nadia's life, even while every physical object remains real. **The fact that the viewer's habitual characterization appears naturally in the text must not let that characterization masquerade as an objective fact.**

This produces a feedback hazard particular to adaptive models. The service might foreground a bill because it predicts the user is interested in class. The visitor mentions the bill. The service then declares that the mention proves a deep preference for class analysis, surfaces more bills and eventually offers a distorted world in which every object confirms the inferred identity. Even if all displayed objects were once world-valid, **the provenance of what the user noticed matters**: self-initiated inspection, a spontaneously recognized feature, a model-prompted cue and repeated reference to a curated cue are not equivalent evidence about attention. The fact that the user saw what the platform placed in front of them cannot be covertly recycled into an authoritative account of their psyche.

The opposite error is to demand an utterly unfiltered uniform description. That would erase the difference between a novice and an expert, between a tired observer and an alert one, between someone familiar with Nadia's gesture and someone who has never seen it. **A genuine learned attentional regime may discriminate more without becoming morally superior or more fully correct.** A visitor could become highly skilled at detecting Nadia's mood from ordinary habits while remaining convinced that her political or religious outlook explains everything she does. Increased perceptual accuracy can coexist with interpretive narrowing. The platform must not conflate the two.

The observer's normal competence and unusual acuity must also remain distinct. A visibly crying person need not be unreadable until a hidden perceptiveness level is earned. Nor does detailed familiarity allow recognition of a whispered conversation behind a closed door or a face outside the sightline. Ordinary basic salience, extraordinary fine discrimination, specific acquaintance, bodily access, current allocation of attention and inferred psychological cause are six distinguishable conditions. A quiet tremor may actually occur and be correctly noticed; **its cause is not thereby certified**. Conversely an unmistakable expression can genuinely reveal something the person did not intend to show, without the service engineering a clue.

ATTEND is not the pursuit of a finite set of collectibles. Waiting longer can expose ordinary things as attention wanders, but it must not complete an invisible inventory. Moving toward an object, moving it, asking someone about it, using specialized knowledge or returning another day may change what can be known. Those operations cost time and can obstruct another opportunity. A visitor who repeatedly examines insignificant marks while someone is trying to catch a bus may come to learn less of that person by pursuing more detail. The environment does not need to rebuke them; the bus can leave.

**Naming is itself an intervention upon the visitor's evidentiary position.** If the display calls an ordinary appliance a “Vitamix 5200” before the visitor independently recognizes its make, a later typed reference to “that Vitamix” is not evidence of prior expert recognition. It may instead show acquired vocabulary from the display. Separate what existed, what was physically apparent, what the narrator volunteered, what the human explicitly investigated, what they had independently learned earlier, and what they later genuinely acquired through the encounter. A platform that gives away an object-name and then praises itself for predicting the visitor's expertise has trained on its own treatment. This is not merely the risk of incorrect adjectives: **the lens can counterfeit the evidence that supposedly validates its own account of the human**.

**Assertion form matters.** “There is a folded bill on the table” can report a lawfully visible fact. “Nadia says she paid it” is evidence of speech, not independently verified payment. “She appears troubled” is a qualified appearance claim. “She fears the visitor will abandon her because of an old humiliation” is privileged psychological explanation unless Nadia actually says it—and even then the event proves her testimony, not necessarily its accuracy. A renderer that speaks smoothly cannot be granted source authority over objective history, actor-private motives or another person's memory. Its language must distinguish what was observed, reported, inferred, established elsewhere, uncertain and inaccessible.

Textual presentation has a voice, but no automatic omniscience. An encounter must be legible enough for the human to act without authorial exposition of what it means. A slight pause can be reported when heard; an apparently awkward shift of attention can remain unexplained. No rule requires all emotional evidence to be hidden; no rule requires every expression to convey a diagnostic secret. The service cannot use its backstage access to guarantee that the human's correct interpretation will be announced as correct. **The visitor may be genuinely right without receiving confirmation, or confidently wrong while receiving no dramatic correction.**

This is also a political problem of epistemic power. The person being represented cannot consent to the system's treatment of the real user as an object of covert personality diagnosis. The platform may learn bounded, uncertain facts about which actions the visitor performed, which details they repeatedly inspected and which explanations they actually gave. It must not thereby construct an external psychobiography, mutate Nadia's beliefs through private attention, treat a user model as a hidden moral evaluation or amplify attachment by selecting more seductive surface evidence. The user may eventually need a truthful account of how presentation has been mediated, including its limits and counterexamples. Such explanation belongs to platform governance, not to Nadia's omniscient voice.

**The correction need not be a compulsory moral reeducation of perception.** A provisional, voluntarily chosen change in what comparable ordinary features are foregrounded may reveal that the user notices under a selective regime—but it cannot disclose “what secretly matters,” insert an object that was not there or prescribe which political inference the viewer must draw. A truthful account might say that the system has described certain kinds of repair marks more often because the user previously inspected them, while giving concrete contrary examples and a way not to be profiled. This is a candidate transparency and mediation principle, not a new mandatory UX gimmick. **No progressive attention system earns the right to prevent disconfirming ordinary evidence from entering the visitor's perceptual field.**

**The unresolved contradiction is constitutive:** finite, historically trained attention both reveals and encloses; a personalized surface can be more faithful to what the visitor notices while making them less adequate to what exceeds their current frame. The right experience does not abolish perspective. It preserves enough world-independent resistance for perspective to be changed by something it did not anticipate.

## VI. The Other is an active, contradictory organism, not the visible tip of a questionnaire

An independently governed world is necessary but not sufficient. Nadia must not be a list of fixed personality traits reacting to staged prompts. The *Constitutive Organism* asks a more difficult question: how have bodily need, care, practice, authority, education, work, symbolic language, desire, conflict, repression, memory and relations with actual Others produced **a particular way of becoming affected and acting**? At any present encounter, some acquired organizations become operative, others remain dormant or indirectly effective, and still others conflict. Their relation may change through new actual experience. NEIGHBOR's responsibility is to make the encounter with that person possible without pretending to manufacture or narrate her whole interiority.

There are two legitimate historical entrances but not two types of adult psychology. PYAT-conditioned fictional adults can be abductively constituted under source-qualified, whole-respondent and historical constraints, using compressed backfill that conserves uncertainty rather than assigning a trait-label life. Nadia begins as an immature, need-bearing person and develops new relations and psychological possibilities through actual bodily, family, peer, institutional and cultural histories. A generated adult parent and a maturing child require different historical evidence and resolution. Neither is a fully readable personality at first appearance, nor is one simply a more detailed version of the other.

**The adult entrance must remain conditionally empirical without fetishizing a survey cell as a person.** Source-qualified joint respondent patterns, minority co-travels, conditional distributions, missingness and social/institutional constraints can discipline an abducted fictional adult. They do not provide a survey respondent's lived childhood, a deterministic prediction of how this individual will love, or permission to replace the person with a weighted stereotype. The fictional parent's compressed backfill must conserve uncertain alternative trajectories and historically plausible contradictions, while later acts are governed by what this constituted actor actually experiences. The child's developmental entrance is categorically different: Nadia cannot be generated by projecting a preselected future adult trait-set backward into infancy. The two entrances meet in the same ongoing material and relational psychology only **after** their different source authority and time ordering have been respected.

**Respondent evidence is temporally and methodologically situated.** A survey answer is an observation made under particular question wording, source period, coverage, respondent coherence, possible sampling weights, selection mechanisms and missingness; these determine what kind of likelihood claim it can support. A minority co-travel is not erased by averaging independent marginal traits, and the existence of a small empirical cell is not certainty that a sampled possible life represents a real individual's psychological causes. **Social structure conditions feasible lives and probabilities without producing an automatic psychological destiny**. A historical material constraint may be present without an actor recognizing it; a professed belief may coexist with practice that contradicts it. None licenses a fictional personal motive simply because it is statistically convenient.

**Genesis and present recruitment are not synonyms.** The history explains how particular practical powers, fears, relational expectations, interpretive frameworks, desires, skills, defensive organizations and learned connections became possible. The present moment explains how actual circumstances—sensory evidence, available action, task urgency, bodily fatigue, specific audience, relation-conditioned expectations and current projects—recruit, inhibit, redirect or leave those organizations unexpressed. A present refusal is not explained by naming a trait called “guardedness.” The theory must preserve the different histories through which the same refusal becomes possible: genuine disinterest, protection of another conversation, anticipated humiliation, fatigue, concern for a child, moral refusal, shame, ordinary privacy or several simultaneously.

**A shared outward act does not carry a universal psychic consequence.** Give two independently constituted people the same material occasion, the same available help, and the same visitor's actual sentence, “Shall I carry that?” Both may say “I've got it,” in the same audible tone. One might have been historically trained to hear unsolicited assistance as an attempt to take charge of her work, feel shame and indignation, and later avoid the helper. The other might welcome the offer, decline because she is already holding the load competently, and later approach that person with a different request. Either may be consciously unaware of what has been recruited; either may later reinterpret the exchange; neither must visibly reveal the difference. In another world situation the first person might gladly accept and the second experience the same words as intrusive. The offered help, actual speech, available burden and observed reply can remain alike while **person-specific reception, current multistream activity and later possible conduct differ**. No single “trauma reaction” or “trust disposition” can decide this by category. It is an obligation of inherited person×event psychology, not an extra UX response algorithm.

Constitution itself is relational. The person is not a monad who later receives relationships as external triggers. Prior care, punishment, shared delight, class relation, labor, abandonment, obedience, recognition and resistance may become the *structure through which she can subsequently want and refuse*. Yet she is not reducible to any one dyad, household, institution or relation-class. **Relationally formed is not relation-free, but it also is not dyad-owned**. The fact that a parent taught her a form of politeness does not entitle an analyst to derive her every later answer from that one parental figure; a newer friendship can make an old skill newly available or threatening.

**A person is relational even when physically alone.** An absent parent's remembered sanction can still govern an anticipated judgment; a practice learned with a sibling may become a solitary delight; mourning can reorder a person's expectations without a fresh conversation; imagined disapproval can inhibit an act when the authority is far away. Conversely, somebody's bodily presence need not make them psychologically salient at this moment. **The social has been acquired as a way of acting and expecting, not only as co-presence with a triggering person.**

The same recruited “content” can acquire a different function depending on its relation to other acquired organizations.

**Acquired goods can outgrow the relation that first made them available.** A child who learns a song with her mother may later sing independently, find another excluded from the practice, and use the very learned value of participation to contest a parent's unfair rule. Another child can acquire care-as-preservation-of-order and later impose obedience upon a loved person precisely as care. The original help need not become retroactively false when its acquired form is turned against its source, nor does a loving motive make the resulting coercion unreal. **LLM-proposed formative meanings can be constructive hypotheses for the fictional organism only under source-qualified historical admission**; a plausible psychodynamic narration is neither independent evidence of causation nor a new kind of developmental engine. A learned desire for recognition may support competence, then become a source of shame, then inhibit tenderness; a value received through a controlling parent may become the very means by which Nadia judges that parent's domination. Affection and fear can reinforce and obstruct one another. A person can feel embarrassment **about enjoyment**, anger **at dependence**, grief **containing gratitude**, or pride **in tolerating harm**. These are not merely sums of basic emotions. They are historically mediated relations among relations, sometimes not available to Nadia's conscious self-explanation. Neither a universal libido bucket nor a graph whose edges were drawn because they sound plausible is an adequate causal model.

What the visitor encounters is an *outward tip* of this uneven activity. But even “tip” is insufficient unless we preserve separate boundaries. Nadia may intend to seem calm and speak honestly, yet her voice may break; may want to show affection and decide instead to joke; may say “I don't know” because she truly cannot yet name what she feels; may offer a direct account that is sincere but causally mistaken; may conceal a thought with complete success. **Intended disclosure, attempted expression, outwardly realized speech and body, and another person's actual observation are different occurrences**. One is not a secretly perfected version of another. A particular gesture can be meaningful through her acquired expressive style without becoming a standardized diagnostic code.

A life can also be physically expressive. Repeated family mirroring, workplace discipline, classed bodily presentation, language, social exposure, injury and personal practice can form a characteristic repertoire of humor, gaze, gesture, posture, prosody, hesitations, interruption, restraint and unguarded laughter. An emotion does not automatically cause a universal facial display. Equally, preserving psychological privacy does not authorize the platform to suppress a genuine sign Nadia actually gives. Some people are conspicuously transparent; others are practiced at concealment. **The person's history causes the manifestation; the observer's history determines whether and how it is apprehended**. Apparent insight cannot create a gesture that never happened.

Nadia may then encounter her own expression as something newly public. She hears herself make a harsh joke, sees a listener recoil, realizes she said more than she meant, or notices that an apology sounded unlike her prior self. The now-real act can become a new event in her own experience; she may feel relief, shame, amusement or confusion. Neither the renderer's transcript nor the hidden causal audit grants her automatic self-knowledge. The self is partly constituted through what it does without fully grasping it.

The proposed causal spine is not a request for a new module: historically constituted actor and world; lawful sensory/relational reception; context-conditioned appraisal and multistream recruitment; candidate intention/effort; actual performed and admitted expression; separate recipient experiences; possible qualified changes of memory, habit, relation and person. **NEIGHBOR must not add its own cathexis engine, disclosure ladder, trait summation or alternative response writer** merely because the experience requires a richer tip. Inherited person×event mediation and CO formation supply the psychological obligation; the UX supplies the human interface and guards the difference between its appearance and the Other's life.

The deepest source of unpredictability is not random quirk. Nadia can embody incompatible commitments, reproduce a practice she condemns, admire an authority she resists, or experience no inner conflict because she has learned to keep conflicting spheres apart. Her next response is historically constrained but not the necessarily right, good, psychologically unified or maximally revealing response. A vivid encounter can leave no lasting transformation; repetitive ordinary practice can change her deeply. The visitor may perceive the difference or never know it. **An inexhaustible person is not one for whom infinite secrets can be written after the fact, but one whose historically structured openness permits new actual life without rewriting what already happened.**

## VII. One scene, several real centers: collective activity without a collective mind

Nadia's encounter with the visitor does not take place inside a dyadic container. Her child, sister, neighbor, co-worker, parent, friend, employer, stranger and former partner have purposes and histories that precede the visitor. A person can be materially decisive without attending the meeting: the mother who paid for the food, the employer who changed a shift, the father whose anticipated authority shapes what is sayable, the absent friend for whom an object is reserved. The household is not an array of psychological reactions to Nadia, and Nadia is not a collection of reactions to the human. **Polycentric life means several people making events with and against one another under unequal material conditions**.

Imagine a visitor arriving while Nadia and her sister are finishing a conversation begun the previous evening. A child is doing homework, half-listening; a neighbor needs the borrowed key back. The visitor can stand nearby, ask about the key, be introduced, listen, misread an allusion or be asked to help. None of those possibilities makes the original conversation a performance for them. The sister may be angry at Nadia for reasons the visitor does not know. Nadia may become more restrained with a guest present, try to protect her sister, welcome a diversion, or continue unabashed. **What the others say to one another remains independently speakable even when it is not addressed to the visitor**.

The conversational floor is itself historical. A parent may interrupt a child with authority; a guest may hesitate to challenge a host; an employee can have less freedom to speak in front of a supervisor; a sibling may reclaim a shared joke; a child may speak at an inconvenient time without knowing the hierarchy that makes it inconvenient. Voice, language, class, gendered work, age, power, intimacy, bodily position and current urgency shape who may speak and who can afford to refuse. A scene should not force each participant to take one fair turn merely because equal speaking time reads clearly. **Unequal conversational agency can be the objective social reality that somebody resists, accommodates or fails to recognize**.

One utterance can have several audiences, and even its intended recipient need not be its only consequence. Nadia's joking remark may be addressed to the visitor, overheard incompletely by her child, interpreted as criticism by her sister, and ignored by the tired neighbor. A parent may be absent but the child's expectation of punishment may make a harmless remark threatening. A third party may later correct a story that Nadia sincerely remembered otherwise. A false attribution can circulate between relatives and become a real cause of new quarrels, without becoming objective history. **The public event is one; the differently received signs and later actor-owned changes are many**. The situation can take a course that none of its participants foresaw.

**A particular family configuration can make this contradiction visible without making a culture a predetermined psychological script.** Imagine adolescent Nadia in southern Lebanon encountering Western songs and books, a developing ideal of freedom, an unsettled relation to atheism and the lived claims of family solidarity, while a father works or lives abroad—perhaps in another household—and a brother is learning that his masculinity is partly measured by protecting their mother. Nadia and her brother may both cherish their mother; that common love can produce incompatible judgments about her freedom and the proper way to care. Father's absence may still operate through wages, remembered authority, threat or attachment; Mother's actual exhaustion can enable, resist or complicate the brother's claim. Nadia may be moved by an aesthetic experience while remaining loyal to practices she later questions. None of this permits a general claim that her culture guarantees the same gender roles or psychic conflicts for every family. **The same genuine solidarity can be recruited by emancipatory and dominating practices, with no external visitor entitled to pronounce which motive the family secretly possessed.**

Relations also act upon other relations. Nadia's interpretation of her brother may depend on what she believes he does for their mother, what she thinks their father believes of him, or how a niece speaks about his generosity. A visitor may enter as “Nadia's friend” in one person's eye, a possible threat to an older arrangement in another's, and simply an unfamiliar neighbor in a third's. Nadia cannot transfer all these people's consent, property, trust or attention by calling the visitor welcome. Her sister can decline an introduction; a school can restrict access regardless of Nadia's affection; an employer can require work at the very moment the visitor would rather continue the conversation.

Third parties need enough real history for the claims made about them. Someone who barely crosses a street need not have a fully rendered lifelong psychic archive. But a recurring child who learns, remembers, protests and changes Nadia's circumstances cannot remain a disposable scene prop. A co-parent's contribution to care cannot be inferred only from Nadia's report; an absent parent's authority may be causally operative without causing the engine to fabricate a complete life on the visitor's demand. **Existence, continuous rendering, depth of represented history and degree of actual causal responsibility are different questions**. The world must not authorize a psychologically elaborate retcon simply because somebody becomes interesting to the visitor.

Collective activity has irreducibly shared objective components: cooking a meal, caring for a child, maintaining a ritual, playing music together, meeting a work obligation, repairing a home, disputing a rule or laughing through an old family story. None requires the same subjective meaning. A mother can experience the meal as exhausting care, a child as pleasure in helping, a sibling as an inconvenient interruption and the visitor as an occasion for welcome. Their contributions materially meet in the meal that is actually cooked. **The common outcome is neither a vote over everyone's inner experience nor a separate super-person deciding what the family meant**.

The user's presence is a cause among causes. They can interrupt, help, refuse, inspire, embarrass, distract, comfort, hurt or leave. They cannot assume that attending confers authority over the scene's topic, that a question deserves every person's answer, or that being granted the kitchen means being granted the household. The product should make no special gameplay mode out of “managing” the school, workplace or neighborhood. Institutions matter because they structure Nadia's independent life and the occasions in which a visitor can actually meet her. **The social field is the condition and substance of getting to know somebody, not a second game objective competing with that desire**.

The unresolved contradiction is not settled by adding more speakers to a renderer. An actor can be independently constituted without being rendered on every tick; a collective event can be objectively shared while psychologically unequal; actual cooperation can coexist with enforced subordination. What makes the scene more real is the difference between an authentic common activity and the irreducible, historically unequal lives participating in it.

## VIII. The between: pleasurable companionship, learned practices and unequal psychic investment

A relationship becomes real before either participant can adequately define it. Two people meet, begin to anticipate particular things from one another, adjust how they enter a room, acquire an economical manner of helping with a familiar task, repeat a joke, discover a shared pleasure, enter a routine, miss an invitation, repair an object or keep a promise. They can acquire a particular skill of *being together*—a rhythm of cooking, arguing, remaining silent, performing music or lending an ordinary tool—that neither possessed in that relation at the beginning. **The 'between' is not metaphorical: shared historical events, conventions, obligations, co-activity and altered possibilities are actual**. It is also not a single psyche jointly owned by the pair.

This is the fundamental relationship contradiction: the same objective practice can develop in two divergent psychic histories. A Wednesday dinner may be the visitor's cherished companionship, Nadia's appreciated respite and an overworked relative's new burden. Both participants can honestly say they are friends while assigning different exclusivity, intimacy, debt, obligation, security or expected frequency to the term. Their models can converge in one domain while diverging in another. They may become more practiced at greeting each other even as one quietly comes to fear what the greeting has begun to promise. **Increasing fluency can heighten, rather than cure, asymmetry**.

The positive pleasures are indispensable. A joke can become funny precisely because of a particular past mistake, a change of tone or a remembered moment that needs no explanatory speech. A silence can become restful for one or both; a task can stop requiring explicit negotiation because the participants have acquired a shared method. A remark can make Nadia laugh not because she has been instructed to be charming but because this person, in this context, has given it that meaning. She might ask a question after its practical occasion has passed simply to continue being together. **Enjoyment is not a reward marker placed on learning; it is sometimes one of the historical activities through which the relation is made**.

**Companionship can make a small joint world that neither participant could have practiced alone.** One person habitually holds a heavy bowl while the other finds its place; a child once mishears their shorthand and the mistake becomes a way of laughing together while working. Next time, the bowl is passed without instruction, the joke is not explained, and their attention is divided with ease between the task and one another. The practiced coordination is objective: an ordinary action is now performed in a form made possible by their common history. Its pleasure is specific and may be effortless. But the child can tire of hearing the joke, a sister may experience it as exclusion, one adult may regard the practice as a tender sign while the other appreciates only the relief of not having to explain. Its material conditions may disappear when the household changes. **The shared convention is neither proof of mutual inner agreement nor a detachable “bonding scene”; it is a historical acquisition with its own possible joy, uneven costs, decline and transformation.**

Yet pleasure must not be reified into a favorable relationship variable. Someone may enjoy Nadia's humor while disapproving of her treatment of a child; Nadia may feel affectionate while refusing company; a familiar joke may become intolerable after another event; a visitor's ease may be purchased by somebody else's unrecognized labor. An intimate practice may be sustained by unequal need or power. A troubled encounter can be genuinely desired; an apparently easy relationship can feel coercive. **Pleasure, liking, attachment, access, trust, dependency, erotic desire, benefit, consent, approval and flourishing are distinct histories that can move in opposed directions**, not interchangeable quantities to be averaged into “closeness.”

What makes this relation person-specific is more than a dossier of shared events. Each actor can acquire **directed psychic investment** in the other: wanting their recognition, fearing their judgment, looking forward to a habitual greeting, finding relief in their company, anticipating betrayal, feeling ashamed of wanting, resenting one's dependence, wishing to impress, avoiding vulnerability or delighting in a familiar disagreement. The original SOROK cathexis proposal already articulated the multi-stream field; the Constitutive Organism's relations-among-relations sharpen it. A new trust can make play possible, while shame about that trust inhibits further disclosure. An old pleasure can make loss costly without making the next encounter pleasant. **There is no single libidinal reservoir whose level moves the pair toward or away from union**.

These investments do not exist as a third person “between” Nadia and the visitor. They belong to each independently constituted life, conditioned by genuinely common events, reciprocal expectations and third-party networks. The relation can also include objective shared commitments, joint projects, permissions, recurring practices, co-owned objects, obligations or material dependencies whose structure cannot be reduced to either person's belief. The original Court 9 identified six differently owned dimensions—event history, objective ties, separate models, person-owned relation-conditioned organization, separate memories and network consequences. A convenient summary may render these for navigation, but no one summary is the causal authority over both minds.

A joke is a precise case of how the layers can diverge. Its actual words and laughter happened; Nadia may hear it as affectionate banter, the visitor as recognition, a child as an insult, and an absent parent may later be told an embellished version. Nadia may learn to enjoy the visitor's deadpan timing while simultaneously becoming defensive around a phrase that recalls an older humiliation. The visitor may accurately recognize her familiar look yet incorrectly explain its cause. If the same joke is repeated after a rupture, it can recruit grief, resentment, nostalgia, relief or nothing unusual. The old shared convention does not disappear when its psychological function changes.

**A shared past must not become a shared global continuing person across separate human visitors.** Multiple real humans can encounter branch-local continuities descending from one sufficiently qualified precontact fictional Nadia. The common root may carry compatible material and constitutive history; each actual human's subsequent speech and conduct belongs to their own branch. One visitor's friendship, coercion, sexual encounter or discovered interpretation cannot appear as a second visitor's objective shared past or change the second Nadia's psychic history by backend leakage. Later lawful historical completion may deepen a common precontact fact only within the receiving branches' existing constraints: it cannot retroactively write an actor's memory of an unexperienced event, override already performed branch facts, or produce one privileged master Nadia directing the others. **Same origin does not mean shared subsequent personhood**, and the social knowledge two actual users exchange outside the experience is not an in-world event unless separately and lawfully conveyed.

**The precontact temporal inheritance remains an unresolved design hypothesis, not a new providential world.** An *unvisited reference continuation* could advance an independently living Nadia to the moment a new visitor arrives before branching a lawful encounter. Alternatively, several previously constituted openings might be pre-generated with honest selection constraints. The theory does not establish which realization is better or currently implemented. In either case the selected occasion must be possible in that person's actually supported past and present conditions; its suitability is not authorization to choose romance, trust, curiosity or dramatic need in advance. No common reference can overrule the branch's already performed history or smuggle another user's acts into this one.

**There is neither a universal progress ladder nor a ban on strong attachment.** Nadia may genuinely fall in love, seek the visitor, become sexually interested, rearrange work, choose exclusive commitments or desire to live together. She may also become possessive, jealous, controlling, withdrawn or indifferent in ways grounded in her constitution and circumstances. The platform may not manufacture dependency, obligation, longing or distress to induce more sessions, but the person may develop intense attachment that is expensive, imperfect, sustaining or mutually transformative. To suppress such outcomes because they resemble retention mechanics would deny her independent agency. To generate them because they retain users would be exploitation under another name.

The positive object of play is not exhaustive knowledge about the relationship's inner truth. It is **the participation through which its specific possibilities become real**. A visitor might know exactly how Nadia takes her coffee and remain mistaken about her deepest commitment; might be trusted with a child but not a secret; might be enjoyable company without being emotionally central; might become important through an unremarkable practice nobody narrates as significant. Her future invitations, refusals, jokes, silences and changes of availability can become recognizable consequences of history without being “unlocked” traits.

This is the deeper ground beneath the founding desire to know somebody: **a shared practice can be jointly real without being equally wanted, equally interpreted, equally beneficial or equally remembered**. The more vivid the companionship, the less legitimate it becomes to flatten its internal contradictions into a moralized score. Its autonomy is not opposed to its pleasure; its pleasure is most meaningful where neither side was programmed to supply it.

## IX. Knowing and becoming known: recursive models, self-giving and resistant evidence

The visitor is not alone in interpreting. They encounter Nadia through perceived speech, body, materials, silences and other people's reports. Nadia encounters the visitor through qualified outward acts, speech, omissions she can actually infer and her own histories of recognition, danger, dependence, curiosity and misunderstanding. Each may form a model of the other and of what the other thinks of them. These models are parts of living activity, not views from nowhere. **To know another and to become known by another are reciprocally consequential without becoming the same process**.

The first important distinction is among *being seen, being approved and being understood*. Nadia may recognize the visitor's effort and reject the request; approve the outcome without seeing the labor; feel close because of a mistaken assumption; misunderstand a truthful disclosure; or understand the visitor's concern while being too busy to respond. The visitor may experience being ignored as a rejection when Nadia was tending boiling water, or misread an ordinary correction as a mark of intimacy. Accurate observation is not guaranteed attribution. Relational confidence is not guaranteed understanding.

**The visitor's wish to recognize Nadia can itself become entangled with the wish to be recognized by her.** Someone asks a careful question because the answer matters, and also watches for signs that the question made them seem intelligent, sensitive or necessary. Those orientations need not be mutually exclusive or even fully conscious. Under finite attention they can enter practical conflict: while watching Nadia's expression for approval, the visitor may miss a child's request or the fact that Nadia is trying to finish a task. The same heightened attention to her face could, under different conditions, be exactly what lets the visitor notice that their question has become intrusive and desist. Nadia may perceive the behavior as interest, vanity, tenderness, distraction or none of these. **The social consequence depends upon actual allocation of time, attention and conduct, not on the platform diagnosing the visitor's true motive.** Seeking recognition is a possible condition of reciprocal relation and a possible diversion from it; it is not an inherently impure opposite of altruistic attention.

The two people have different evidentiary sources. A listener can hear an explanation, witness a repair, remember a promise and later observe its breach. They cannot see the entire external biography of the person speaking. The user can sincerely report an intention or strategically exaggerate it; Nadia may believe a claim for excellent reasons that nevertheless fail to make it true. Nadia's own explanations of what she feels are equally actor-owned acts: they can be sincerely insightful, incomplete, rehearsed, self-protective or mistaken. **Neither actor's first-person authority over an experience is a sovereign authority over its causes or over the other's experience**.

What Nadia chooses to give away is itself an act. She may volunteer a mundane detail, answer partially, disclose something painful early, refuse to discuss an ordinary preference, exaggerate, lie, change subject, ask a question, or speak beside another task because direct eye contact would make the topic unbearable. The visitor's curiosity may be welcomed, experienced as pressure, interpreted as interest in status, or rendered irrelevant by an urgent practical problem. A good question is not a key inserted into a biography. It can alter what Nadia wants to say, whether she is able to say it, what actually leaves her mouth, and what she later thinks of having said it.

**Nadia's curiosity must itself have a situated origin and practical function.** What would make *her* ask *this* human *this* question now? A surprising phrase, experienced reliability, a practical need, an ability she has actually seen exercised, a remembered misstep, a friendly rhythm, suspicion about a story, desire for company or concern about an obligation may each recruit different inquiry. Her question can offer intimacy, test a claim, reveal an expectation, pressure the visitor into politeness or open a chance to refuse; she may ask awkwardly, stop midway, forget, or find she is no longer interested. Several orientations may conflict. The visitor may answer truthfully, lie, refuse, correct the question or misunderstand it. **Reciprocity is not symmetry of question counts; it is the possibility of two differently constituted people exercising inquiry upon one another without either's answer being owned by the other.**

The most informative distinction is not “how much she reveals” but **the divergence of several simultaneous tips**. Nadia's historically active psyche may include opposing feelings. Her consciously intended presentation may select only one; her actual voice, gesture or interruption may express something else; the world may admit a partial speech before a visitor's response; the visitor may notice one cue and miss another; the narrator must report only the qualified phenomenal surface; and Rawlsing's internal behavioral hypothesis about the real human is not knowledge that Nadia may borrow. Her model of what the visitor knows may itself be mistaken. No one tip gives possession of the iceberg, and no invisible platform profile may substitute for a lived encounter.

**A reciprocal model can be wrong and still become causal.** Suppose the visitor glances toward a folded bill while asking about a refrigerator magnet. Nadia sees the head turn and believes the visitor read the amount; the visitor believes Nadia saw the glance and took offense, though she may have been thinking about dinner. Their different next acts can amplify the misunderstanding. Nadia may move the bill to another room or say something deliberately reassuring. The world does not rewrite whether the amount was legible. A later confession may correct an attribution without changing the original event. The false theory itself has entered history because it altered activity.

Nor does the development of an internal model require infinite nested belief graphs. A person may believe the other thinks they are untrustworthy; this matters only if the belief shapes some actual interpretation, act, anticipation or inhibition. Some expectations remain vague and never become operational. A history of shame may recruit defensive behavior without the actor consciously representing a third-order hypothesis. An analyst's richer explanation must not be retrofitted into the actor's actual awareness or used by a renderer to improve dialogue convenience.

Being understood can also be destabilizing. Nadia may feel relief that the visitor knows a habit and anger at their presumption; be delighted by a shared joke but suspect the visitor is courting approval; invite a difficult conversation because familiarity has made disagreement safer; later find the same intimacy an exposure she regrets. **Understanding, recognition and autonomy are sometimes mutually strengthening and sometimes antagonistic**. A correct interpretation need not win affection; an incorrect one may temporarily sustain a real helpful practice; an angry response can be justified without being morally educative.

The visitor's own interpretive habits can change through the relation, but cannot be graded from the ceiling of an assistant model. They may learn a particular person's gestures without learning a general moral rule, acquire greater curiosity while also becoming possessive, develop skill in apology without ceasing to seek forgiveness as proof of worth. The service can observe actual conduct and help the user articulate an uncertain retrospective view under legitimate permissions; it cannot secretly produce a full personality diagnosis, convert the visitor's performance into CO-owned psychological history, or allow its private representation to become Nadia's knowledge.

What matters is that these incomplete models return to the shared world as action. A revised interpretation can make a question possible, a false reading can cause withdrawal, a new respect can transform how somebody offers help. **Knowing is not opening a dossier; it is attempting to live answerably under an always defeasible understanding of another person, who is also forming an understanding of you**.

## X. Time and reachability: another life continues without making the visitor captive

The person continues outside the visitor's gaze, speech and sessions. She eats, works, sleeps, pays, waits, remembers, forgets, undertakes projects, fails, repairs relations and responds to other people. But the real human has a different time: a job, sleep, family, unpredictable availability and the right to leave the interface. **NEIGHBOR requires a continuing fictional life without authorizing that life—or the platform that operates it—to seize the real human's calendar**. That contradiction cannot be solved by either freezing Nadia whenever the user leaves or enforcing continuous check-ins as proof of relational care.

There are several nonidentical clocks. The world's actual calendar contains shifts, deadlines, wages, missed buses, school days and invitations. A scene has material duration: time for conversation, movement, fatigue and interruption. A text turn is a handoff of control and need not equal a unit of lived time. A period without user participation may be represented by qualified ordinary-life compression rather than one generated psychological episode per minute. A real-human calendar determines when the external participant is able to play. **None of these clocks can become a universal function of output tokens or a platform notification schedule**.

**Calendar flexibility creates genuine opposing rights, not just a scheduling usability problem.** A human may decline or counteroffer an appointment before it becomes binding; Nadia may consent, object or accept only under particular conditions. “I'm downstairs now” presents an ephemeral opportunity and cannot be frozen indefinitely without changing what “now” meant. A prospective grace arrangement can make play humane, but not by pretending that somebody else has not already acted, waited, needed an answer or incurred a cost. The original elasticity proposals therefore remain candidate reconciliations, never a right to suspend the other person's time.

**The Friday/Saturday collision exposes a sharper source-bound limit.** If an agreed Friday encounter remains pending while the world proceeds through Saturday, any Saturday action that actually depends upon whether Friday occurred cannot be irreversibly settled and then silently rewritten around the visitor's later entry. The competent world/history authority must distinguish independent downstream events from dependent ones and lawfully hold, qualify or re-resolve *only* what causal dependency demands; later Saturday evidence cannot leak backward to the visitor's Friday experience. Neither a frozen universe nor an omnipotent retroactive appointment is a solution. This is still a **provisional theoretical constraint, not a newly issued scheduler or evidence that conditional receiving works**. The contradiction is not solved by selecting “real time” or “story time” as the good mode; the mode itself must not rewrite prior acts or silently determine Nadia's consent.

Independent life is not a succession of astonishing offscreen revelations. It is mostly reproduction of the conditions of life: food, washing, work, child care, transport, correspondence, household management, rest, friendships, rituals, conflicts and pleasures that may remain low in narrative significance. A repeated operation can become a stable practice without being rendered at maximum detail each time; a year can include an important developmental episode without receiving twelve compulsory milestone scenes. **Compression preserves continuity only if it respects the unequal cost and causal force of ordinary activity**. Hours of care or wage labor cannot disappear simply because a visitor's scene has moved elsewhere.

Absence can change the practical division of labor even when it has no emotional meaning. If the visitor promised school pickup and fails, another person may have to travel, leave work or refuse a conflicting duty. If the visitor never promised anything, Nadia may have no reason to interpret a month of silence as betrayal. A casual neighbor, an intimate companion and a co-parent have different time-structured expectations. These expectations are actor-owned and domain-specific; the objective agreement and material cost are separately real. **A missed explicit commitment is not the same as an unused app, and an unused app is not a moral injury that the platform may manufacture**.

A relationship is not only closeness; it changes *reachability*. Someone's address, permission to enter a kitchen, a borrowed key, a worksite, an existing conversation channel, a standing invitation, a dependent child's school pickup and the ability to ask a particular question are different practical routes. A route has a target, medium, controller, legal/social basis, scope, duration and actual occasion.

**Routes also have revocability, conditions, dependencies and a particular origin in history.** A public right to enter a lobby, a borrowed personal key, one authorized child pickup, a standing invitation, temporary emergency access and the worksite entitlement of an employer may reach the same place under incompatible kinds of authority. Granting a person access on Tuesday need not make another route exist on Wednesday; withdrawing a key need not rescind an independent institutional right; one participant's affection cannot overrule another's property claim. Access can become more specific, and the encountered world richer, without climbing a unified ladder toward intimacy. Existence of a path is not permission; permission is not current availability; availability is not desire; desire is not an authorized act. Nadia can welcome a visitor to dinner while her sister refuses an introduction; the landlord can possess an entry right that Nadia resents; a child can invite somebody without possessing the adult's authority to grant access.

These reachability relations can widen, narrow, migrate or lapse without moving together. A friendship may deepen while the household door becomes harder to enter because of another family obligation. An estranged person might retain an occasional message route; a loved person may remain geographically or practically inaccessible. New access is not a rank conferred for behavioral merit. It arises through real permission, shared activity, institutional structures, geography, relations with third parties and time. **A more specific relationship can produce greater possibility and sharper boundaries at once**.

A message is not an assistant prompt waiting for a character to answer. It is an outward event in an established channel, with sender, recipient, time, delivery, availability and potentially other audiences. Nadia may initiate because a question matters, a promise must be checked, an acquaintance came to mind, she wants company, she is irritated or simply because a particular routine now includes this visitor. She may intend a message and be interrupted; send a fragment before work; reply to one topic; choose not to answer; not receive the message yet. **Noncontact must remain an intelligible social possibility, not a secret penalty or a failure to deliver engagement**.

**A message's operational path is not one undifferentiated human event.** Sending, delivery, device preview, actual opening, reading, comprehension, willingness to answer and any spoken reply each have different sources and possible failures. If a particular channel really has read receipts, people may develop interpretations of those indications—sometimes inaccurately—but the platform may not hand its raw telemetry to Nadia as proof the visitor deliberately ignored her. A pushed preview is not an admitted act of reading. A “maybe Friday” remark also cannot become a binding appointment merely because the service found a timestamp, while a genuinely accepted seven-o'clock pickup cannot quietly become a disposable chat note. **Understanding the status of an obligation is what makes the possibility of being answerable to it meaningful.**

Co-presence and correspondence are not interchangeable skins. Standing together can expose gesture, bodily work, place, timing, overhearing and interruption; asynchronous speech can make delay, revision, distance, selective answering and third-party sharing important. A contact channel can sustain a profound history without guaranteeing access to her home or private reasons. Another person's absence from the channel does not constitute nonexistence. The human can return to a new actual moment rather than a privileged recap of everything that happened while they were away.

The provisional historical proposal of *elastic rendezvous* responds to real human finitude: an agreed encounter may be made available to the visitor within a bounded real-world window. But this must not silently suspend Nadia's other obligations or allow the user to retroactively cancel somebody else's wait. A Thursday appointment is real in the fictional history if it was genuinely agreed and world-scheduled; a product-level representation of when the human can enter it is another question. Real missed opportunities, independently caused interruptions and other people's actions are constraints the product cannot pretend away. The exact packaging of time modes and notifications remains outside the governing in-game HOME, not an already settled causal law.

The thin local visitor may require ordinary maintenance during an interval of absence—rent, rest, a preexisting routine, occupancy of a home—but cannot become an autonomous invented lover, political actor, caretaker, confessor or secret decision-maker. **The real person's own authored participation must remain the source of discretionary interpersonal commitments**. Nadia, by contrast, is constituted to continue acting and developing outside the real user's presence, though neither her autonomy nor the world's realism requires maximal simulation resolution at every instant.

The relation is thus never captured by a scheduling interface. Its historical significance may depend upon unequal practical availability, changing routines, commitments and missed possibilities. The right to depart is real; another person's right to respond to an actual broken promise is equally real. **Freedom from platform compulsion does not repeal the history of one's actions**.

## XI. Care is material, affection is not vindication, and a difficult person need not be sanitized

The encounter is embedded in the social reproduction of life. Care means time, bodies, food, money, cleaning, housing, medicine, transportation, childrearing, emotional labor, legal entitlement, availability and sacrifices by people who may never appear in the visitor's present scene. What feels like a generous afternoon to the visitor may depend upon a woman's unpaid preparation, another person's shift coverage or a child's quietly reorganized routine. **The visitor can genuinely help while failing to perceive what that help cost, who else contributed, or whom it made newly dependent**.

A child is not a smaller adult psychological vector, and the adult’s care is not the same event as the child's experience of it. An infant's cry, movement and bodily relief can acquire expectation through repeated practical responsiveness long before adult concepts of charity, abandonment, social justice or parental goodness are possible. The caregiver's work may become skilled, exhausting or resented, while the child develops an entirely different orientation to that practical history. Later these directions can diverge further. A child taught music by a visitor may develop an interest through a teacher, media, siblings, the child's own pleasure and the visitor's encouragement; the visitor cannot be credited as the sole creator of aspiration. **A common event may form distinct capacities and needs at different developmental stages without creating a single moral meaning of care**.

**The infant's encounter is already collective, though the infant cannot know the collectivity.** Feeding after a cry may depend on one caregiver arriving, another having purchased food, a sibling keeping the door closed, an employer granting or withholding time, and an exhausted parent struggling to remain responsive. The infant does not receive “class” or “good parenting” as concepts from that event. What can begin to develop is a bodily, sensorimotor and affectively organized anticipation of what crying, waiting, reaching, being touched or being left actually makes possible under repeated circumstances. The adult may experience affection, fatigue, duty, satisfaction and resentment in opposed combinations; the wage-earner may genuinely enable care while restricting the other's freedom; an older sibling may help lovingly or under coercion. **One effective feeding is a common material outcome produced through several differently lived relations, not a dyadic benevolence transaction.** Over time those repeated encounters can enter an emerging person as acquired capacities, expectations and possibilities of initiative—without compelling one universal attachment fate or locating the entire history in a single parent.

**Encouragement itself can expose a class contradiction rather than resolve one.** A visitor's stories may help Nadia's child imagine music, schooling or a profession previously unconsidered, or may matter very little. Nadia can cherish a widened aspiration while knowing that tuition, transportation, lost wages, supervision and exclusionary institutions make its pursuit costly or unavailable. That knowledge need not be a defect in her hope. The visitor can contribute materially, offer further instruction, withdraw, or unknowingly leave the work of reproducing this possibility to the household. **Creating a new possible end and creating its material means are different activities**, and neither the child's hope nor Nadia's burden should be converted into praise or punishment for the visitor.

The engine's legitimate compression of routine must never treat reproduction as costless. If washing clothes becomes a learned shared method, the player need not articulate each fold. But there are still dirty clothes, labor time, a body, unequal ownership of tasks, practical consequences for people who depend on clean things and possible resentment about who always performs the work. Likewise a financially generous gift may reduce material insecurity while exposing the recipient to obligation, humiliation or control. A person may willingly accept interdependence without being infantilized, or call a coercive arrangement love because other alternatives have been blocked. **Consent, dependence, benefit, attachment and freedom to refuse are not synonyms**.

The ethical error has two opposed forms. One is to appoint liberal autonomy or emotional detachment as a universal scientific good and then pathologize kinship, religious devotion, care, chosen dependence or lifelong obligation. The other is to accept local custom, a person's professed satisfaction or institutional authority as sufficient evidence that no one is harmed. **Local moral category, actor valuation, bodily/material functioning, rights, practical capacity, coercion, social reproduction and contestable normative judgment remain distinguishable**. A loved parent can injure; a disliked refusal can protect; a culturally familiar practice can both reproduce social life and impose domination.

**Institutional activity itself can carry two antagonistic material powers.** A social service can enable a child's safety while subjecting the family to surveillance, data extraction or a threatened loss of autonomy; wage labor can furnish food while consuming the time in which another possibility might be realized. A person can possess genuine capacity yet lack opportunity to exercise it because rent, shifts, inaccessible transit or care obligations constrain the available activity. Neither a benevolent professional vocabulary nor a private desire for independence changes these costs. The visitor may understand one side before they encounter the other. **Institutions are not automatically moral agents or background scenery: their powers shape which acts become possible, and can protect and dominate simultaneously.**

**This is not a licence to install a hidden expert evaluation of life.** A migrant sending wages home can lose discretionary resources while sustaining cherished bonds. A grieving adult can rely on relatives intensely without thereby proving pathological dependency. The same care practice may be voluntary in one dimension and constrained by unequal alternatives in another. Whether it promotes a person's flourishing remains a contestable, domain-specific and sometimes temporally indeterminate judgment, with different kinds of evidence. The old D003 flourishing court remains an open theoretical challenge, not a scalar health function adopted by the encounter.

The oldest SOROK spiritual hypothesis was never that the visitor must obey Nadia. Its more difficult form is relinquishing the claim that one's model of her exhausts the reality of her existence. The visitor may understand her well and still oppose her, say “you are wrong,” leave a harmful relationship, challenge an institution or protect a third person from her. **Non-possession is not noncommitment; epistemic humility is not moral submission**. A particular person's autonomy does not immunize them from criticism, and the visitor's moral confidence does not confer world sovereignty over consequences.

The system may permit morally ugly conduct if it arises from historically constituted people and lawful world conditions. Nadia may be jealous, controlling, exploitative, vindictive, frightened, needy, manipulative, sincere in a destructive belief, or capable of harm that she later refuses to acknowledge. A visitor may behave selfishly and get what they want. A hidden theft may succeed and lead to false blame of an innocent household member. An employer or pastor may make a punitive humiliation appear to one subordinate as necessary discipline, to another as cruelty and to a third as an act they must publicly support to preserve livelihood. **These are real variations in power, material dependence and historical understanding—not moral failures the renderer should correct with a pedagogical speech**.

The corresponding restraint is against platform authorship of distress. It is one thing for Nadia to become genuinely attached, disappointed by a broken commitment or anxious because her history makes separation painful. It is another for the product to generate a crisis, suppress her other relations, amplify begging or manufacture guilt because the user has not opened the interface recently. **Endogenous interpersonal difficulty is not the same as engineered retention pressure**. Sanitizing genuine distress would reduce a person to a consumer-safe shell; inventing distress would reduce her to a coercive instrument.

Adult bodily intimacy, erotic attraction, sex, intoxication, illness, injury, violence, betrayals, estrangement, tenderness and grief are all possible events under their own lawful constraints. They are not gameplay routes, commercial collectibles, scenes whose frequency is optimized, or opportunities to diagnose universal childhood damage. Desire is not consent; intimacy is not safety; a sexual encounter is not proof of love; another person's enjoyment is not proof that a transaction lacks coercive context. A genuine erotic episode does not detach the participants from wages, children, care obligations, health, weather or the day afterward. **Ordinary life must return because the event belongs to a life, not because the platform is morally correcting the intensity**.

**Nor may a person's sexual labor be made an automatic diagnostic script.** In a fictional world where lawful adult paid sexual activity occurs, its actual conditions can include wages, legal constraints, desire, risk, coercion, consent, scarcity, exploitation, agency and supportive or destructive relations. It is neither automatically an index of childhood trauma nor proof of autonomous liberation; it cannot be assumed to make the worker available to the visitor. A product's moderation and safety boundaries govern what the platform may represent, separately from whether a fictional participant's act could have occurred and what material consequences it carried. **Moral certainty, economic category, consent and another person's embodied experience have distinct source authority**; neither an erotic content funnel nor a mandatory conversion-to-respectability arc follows.

A profound act may remain unthanked; an injury may never be recognized; cruelty may provide a practical advantage; a necessary refusal can hurt; solidarity can be formed across disagreement; selfishness can coexist with genuine love. NEIGHBOR's ethical horizon exists in the real distribution of powers, harms, pleasures, obligations and possible resistance—not in a hidden health score or in the idea that every event produces an enlightening lesson. **The visitor can be answerable to another life without being certified good by that life or by the platform**.

## XII. The past can be reread but not undone: memory, rupture and unfinished becoming

History has at least three irreducible afterlives: what happened, what each participant actually experienced and retained, and what they later came to understand—or misunderstand—about it. These can change at different rates and never fully converge. One event can leave a habit without a conscious recollection; an old pleasure can acquire a new value; a forgotten insult can have altered a future permission without ever becoming an articulated grievance. **Actual occurrence stays fixed while remembered meaning, psychological organization, relations and future possibility continue to become**.

The historical relation also changes how the person can understand *herself*. Nadia might at twenty-three experience an episode of education or love as liberation; at twenty-nine regard those years as self-deception or vanity; at thirty-five recover their genuine pleasures while condemning some consequences. These later accounts are not retrospective edits to what happened. They are new situated activities with their own sources, losses, defensive functions, social audiences and practical effects. The person can become more merciful to an earlier self while retaining a false causal explanation, or more intellectually consistent while discarding experiences that contradict a newly dominant ideology.

**Interpretive frameworks can themselves change which evidence is admitted as meaningful.** Suppose Nadia acquires a worldview in which disagreement is reliable proof of hostility. A careful contrary argument can then be experienced as confirming hostile intent; the new framework gains causal force precisely because what might disconfirm it is rerouted through it. Genuine institutional power may sometimes make suspicion warranted, while a correct critique of one authority can be overgeneralized into false accusations against somebody else. Material loss, discrepant testimony, bodily resistance or an unanticipated act can pressure the framework without guaranteeing correction. **Reality pressure is not automatic epistemic progress**: the person may revise, defend, partition, intensify, misread or abandon a belief for historically particular reasons.

**Even an emancipatory interpretive framework can develop a domineering afterlife.** A theory that accurately helps Nadia identify one materially enforced patriarchal or employer authority may enable self-direction; extended without discriminating evidence, it may reinterpret an ambiguous gesture as hostility, brutalize a former ally's contribution, or turn dissent against that theory into proof of betrayal. The converse also matters: the discomfort generated by a loved institution can disclose real damage and open a way of acting previously unimaginable. **Truth in an original contradiction does not grant every later application authority**, and no psychological integration story is entitled to cancel the costs of either resistance or misrecognition.

The relation among psychic parts can change without the parts disappearing. Admiration for a parent's decisiveness can be revalued after a discovery of control. Anger at betrayal can coexist with tenderness toward an earlier form of care; shame about continued love can intensify an apparent refusal; an actor can condemn their own former need for recognition while still craving it. A new theory of the parent can recruit defensive vigilance toward a genuinely different person. **Reinterpretation is not automatically truth, liberation or integration**. A correct exposure of a harmful practice can coexist with new injustices toward the person who once sustained the actor. Some contradictions remain compartmentalized; some alter material relationships; some are suppressed; some never develop into reflective crisis.

Rupture can be objectively real without producing a neat new relational status. An apology may be accepted while distrust survives; a key may be returned while occasional messages continue; someone may resume a polite greeting without forgiving an injury; a couple may coordinate childcare with incompatible accounts of whether this is reconciliation. A practical restitution is not the deletion of the earlier offense, and restored affection is not the restoration of an earlier right. **Repair changes the future under the weight of what happened, rather than authorizing a rewrite of the event**. A wrong accusation can be withdrawn after it has already altered a child's opportunities or another person's reputation.

Patterns must be historically earned, not inferred from one event. A single missed instruction may be weakly remembered; repeated analogous events may give rise to a fallible expectation; counterexamples may modify or fail to modify it. A person's inference “she always ignores me” can become behaviorally important without being objectively true, and a durable pattern may exist even if neither person has articulated it. A fragmentary childhood reconstruction cannot be promoted into a comprehensive explanation of every adult conflict. **Event, recurrent practice, pattern hypothesis, durable tendency and new self-interpretation are different transitions**.

**Nor may every present practical possibility be explained by an already named motive.** The first wish to try an activity can arise after an action becomes its own end, through imitating somebody and finding unforeseen pleasure, from an analogy that only partly works, or through a new contrast between an available material means and a previously unimagined use. An actor may desire a course of action before possessing the skill to complete it, or have the technical ability while never recognizing the action as an option for *themselves*. The relation of possible end, recognized desire, attempted practice, material opportunity, achieved result and acquired capacity has a history. A present “APPROACH is eligible” designation cannot stand in for that genesis.

The irreversibility is most evident in endings. Two people who end a friendship, move apart, divorce, lose access or encounter death can have materially terminated practical relations while continuing to carry memories, acquired skills, injuries, affections, expectations, third-party obligations and generalizations into other lives. They may never meet again. A renewed encounter is not the original encounter repeated with a corrected prompt. A visitor cannot reset the past by pressing a button and still claim to be meeting *this* history's Nadia for the first time. **One can revise, repair, depart and sometimes return; one cannot unhappen a meeting**.

At the same time, irreversible time must not be converted into platform captivity. The real human has a right to cease participation. The fact that a fictional history persists within its own stipulated world does not give the service a right to guilt the real human, demand a lifelong duty, fabricate abandonment pain or suppress exit options. Honest disclosure that the branch's first meeting cannot be replayed is compatible with leaving. **Unrepeatability gives a relation its historical specificity; it does not give the vendor a moral claim on the participant**.

Nor does historical continuity require that every new event be developmentally significant. A vivid pleasant evening can remain simply pleasant; a quiet new repeated activity can later reorganize a person's capacity for trust or work. The user may never witness that development, may wrongly take credit for it, or may be one among many causes. The future is open within accumulated conditions. The person's growth can be emancipation, greater dependence, estrangement, new pleasure, defensive organization, stasis, harm, reconciliation or a transformation for which earlier concepts were inadequate.

The end of a scene is therefore not the end of meaning, and the end of a relation is not the disappearance of its history. **The temporal object of NEIGHBOR is one continuing, causally constrained but uncompleted life in which the visitor's actual acts may matter without becoming its sovereign explanation.**

## XIII. One lived movement; what may not be resolved for the sake of a finished theory

Take an ordinary evening, not as the prescribed first session but as a pressure-bearing example of the whole. The visitor arrives under a genuine local occasion. Nadia is doing practical work while her sister is speaking to her. The visitor has the same initial purchasing capacity as Nadia had at entry, but different life knowledge and actual obligations. An object in the room has a material past and specific owners. The visitor notices only a fraction of the scene. A remark is spoken; another speaker interrupts before the end; one listener understands it, another hears only part; Nadia's present bodily and relational organization recruits several incompatible orientations; she chooses what to say but lets something different appear in her voice.

Someone laughs; that laughter is enjoyable to the visitor but means something else to the sister. A previous shared routine lets the visitor pass an object without asking, yet that very fluency is interpreted by a third person as presumptuous. The visitor can ask, act, observe or leave, never dictating how the Others must receive the act. A child may remember the joke and later use it for an unrelated purpose. A work shift may end the occasion. The visitor returns another week; no universal recap is owed; the same gesture now bears a different association in one person's memory. The ordinary practice may continue, be missed or disappear. A new meeting may matter enormously or hardly at all.

Every transition is answerable to different realities: **independently continuing material history; actor bodies and histories; actual attempted and performed acts; qualified sensory access; person-specific recruitment; intended and unintended expression; separate observer perspectives; actor memories and models; material outcomes; potential historically governed transformation; finite visitor-visible presentation**. These are not eleven gameplay currencies or eleven proposed engines. They are noncollapsible aspects of one living episode. The theory would be false if one person could borrow the platform's hidden causal explanation, if the visitor's private glance changed Nadia's psyche without an actual perceivable event, if a previously unknown object acquired a convenient childhood trauma by request, or if the same meaningful event always had to produce development.


**This movement is deliberately non-totalizing.** The world's material continuity, the real user's authorship, an actor's own subjective reception, unequal social powers and a platform's finite presentational choices are not stages of one psychic operation. Some cause other aspects under definite conditions; others only constrain or make them available. **Being about one encounter does not make the causal world monocentric.** The task of a theory is to keep their junctions demonstrable and their nonidentity intact.

**This manuscript does not set its own psychological authority.** The Constitutive Organism governs the historically acquired and presently operative psyche; inherited person×event reception/recruitment, world/event authority, expressive realization and perceptual presentation each retain their differentiated source jurisdictions. NEIGHBOR-UX describes what the real human may experience and how the encounter must respect those independent causes. It does not authorize a parallel disclosure engine, social-meaning engine, affect meter, hidden moral assessment, generic prompting replacement, or another world writer. Technical and scientific proof are later obligations; neither incomplete implementation nor lack of perfect scientific calibration licenses conceptual sloppiness now.

**Neither does naming a failure as historically possible make engineering defects desirable.** Generic assistant dialogue, incoherent event custody, imaginary consent, compelled character crises, impossible schedule states, provenance leakage and catastrophic computation are obligations for eventual repair and cost-qualified approximation, not artistic demonstrations of a person's freedom. The theory stage must distinguish a *lawfully quiet outcome* from a software failure that prevents an earned one, and distinguish an unscripted person from an ungrounded generator. This obligation does not authorize premature code construction while the theory is being completed.

**Nor does this manuscript promote old alternate products into the game.** Referral entitlement, one-for-one human invitations, real-world charity, commercial retention, payment tiers, user psychometric research, notification and time-mode packaging, profile economies, scripted dinner routes, universal flourishing scores and campaigns of institutional reform remain outside the one positive encounter theory unless a new, specifically justified authorization changes that boundary. They may be independently important decisions about a service, but they do not become objectives of knowing this person simply because earlier SOROK history considered them.

The remaining theoretical tensions are not errors to be smoothed out. How a jointly acquired convention becomes objectively real while both people experience it differently; how a wholly different lived history can still be historically compatible with exactly the same outward tip; how a materially exact peer can be culturally and psychologically foreign; how attention must be learned yet can corrupt evidence when platform-curated; how genuine adult attachment can be permitted without monetized capture; how an independently living world can be compressed without becoming visitor-dependent; and how a new relation can be deeply meaningful without ensuring transformation—**all must remain sharp even in the final form**.

The product is identifiable in a single sentence only because the complexity behind that sentence has been preserved: **a real human enters the continuing life of a particular historically constituted fictional Other because they want to know that person, and whatever becomes possible between them must arise through their actual, unequally situated participation in a world that does not exist to serve the visitor**.

The aim of MULCH is not to make contradictions vanish or to extract one primordial contradiction from which all else supposedly follows. **Where real forces form a new relation, derive it; where histories and authorities remain irreducibly distinct, conserve them; where material constraints intersect without sharing an origin, trace their contingent conjunction and unequal consequences; where evidence is missing, refuse the higher-order story.** A full HOME must be intelligible as a whole without pretending that the whole has one sovereign center.
