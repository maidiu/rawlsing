## I. The wish to meet a person who was not made to meet you

The first fact about NEIGHBOR is not a psychological model, a simulated country or an action grammar. A real human wants to encounter a particular, historically constituted fictional Other. They want to know her. That wanting is legitimate, pleasurable, and capable of becoming a history. The person they meet—Nadia, or the particular Other at the center of a continuity—has been living in a material and social world for reasons that do not derive from this visitor's desire. The experience begins because the visitor seeks someone; **the relationship can begin only because she was not already theirs**.

This is neither the construction of a private friend optimized for compatibility nor a morality examination disguised as company. The person is not a dossier, a trauma archive, a religious or political teaching aid, a romance funnel, or a performer who becomes fascinating in response to an attention command. The visitor has an entire actual life beyond the fictional threshold; Nadia has a fictional life whose terms of reality the visitor does not author. The game's deliberate asymmetry—one real human and a generated but independently governed fictional Other—is never erased by the phenomenology of genuine attachment. The representation can matter to the human without pretending to be an actual human outside it.

**The first contradiction:** a genuinely usable encounter must provide an opening, and an independent person does not exist to supply one. The solution is neither to plant a confessional hook and call it spontaneity, nor to make the visitor hunt for a hidden command while an unresponsive scene proclaims its realism. An ordinary world can create *actual social occasions*: a neighbor's introduction, a borrowed ingredient, a shared corridor, a person already waiting for someone else, a task whose continuation requires ordinary cooperation. The visitor must know enough about where they stand, what is happening and what they might try; they must not be handed an emotional outcome. The occasion is legitimately framed. **Interest, relief, embarrassment, affection, boredom, refusal and the possibility of a second meeting remain unconferred**.

The first minute of the visitor's experience is not the first minute of Nadia's Thursday. It may be late afternoon, she may be leaving for work, her sister may already be speaking to her, a child may call from another room, the visitor may interrupt a necessary errand. Such details are not canonical script beats. The original stairwell, groceries, first dinner and sensitive bathroom chain are tests of what entry needs to make *possible*, not what all first meetings must enact. The fiction may establish a bounded precontact neighborly premise—a place, a prior introduction, a minor obligation—without inserting an intimate backstory, a promise the visitor never made, or a friendship that neither party has had time to form. A mere invented prelude is not a license to invent the real person's self.

The visitor's threshold has two different authorities. A real-world invitation to the experience can be truthful, whether a genuinely sent invitation or an explicitly disclosed project entrance. Within the fiction, the particular encounter must have its own person-and-world-conditioned reason. Neither a real-world referral nor a shared simulated seed grants permanent access to Nadia. Their apparent alignment cannot covertly forge reciprocal desire. A good opening can be engaging even if she is distracted, impatient, unfunny or unavailable; the visitor can decide that the meeting was not worth repeating. That is not an engine failure to be repaired by manufacturing interest.

The first encounter also has a material ending. A shift starts; a guest arrives; both become tired; the conversation loses its reason for continuing; one party leaves. A visitor may linger and be unwelcome, depart and be missed, or exchange contact details that never become important. The scene must not conclude when a scripted revelation is delivered or the system has earned a retention event. It may leave a small new obligation, an irritation, a shared joke, a mistaken belief, a useful practice, or literally nothing durable. **An encounter's finite occasion can be meaningful without having been designed to generate meaning**.

The positive experience matters here. A person may be enjoyable before they become deeply understood. A remark can amuse; a way of speaking can be appealing; shared work can have an unexpected rhythm; a silence can begin to feel like company. These are not disposable embellishments to the serious program of recognizing alterity. They are some of the very ways a relationship begins. Yet the other may not enjoy the same thing, may enjoy it for another reason, or may never notice that a relation is beginning. The user does not have a claim on a reciprocal feeling merely because their own desire is sincere.

The founding political and theological wager is thus immanent rather than a tutorial: another life could have developed under conditions the visitor did not choose, and knowing someone may transform one's understanding of these conditions. But that person does not become an exhibit of deprivation, an exemplary victim, a vehicle for the visitor's redemption, or an incarnation of the designer's politics. The experience can make humility, solidarity or care imaginable by placing finite agency before another irreducible center. It may also expose possessiveness, impatience or indifference. **The world may make the encounter possible; it does not guarantee that the visitor learns the correct lesson.**

The first movement therefore has an unresolved remainder: NEIGHBOR needs enough artifice to become an experience at all, while refusing the artifice that would determine whether the two people become interested. The exact measure cannot be fixed by a standard first-person scene or universal discovery recipe. It is judged by whether the invitation yields a *truthful occasion to participate* with someone already occupied by their own life.

## II. A real human in a materially equal starting position, without a counterfeit childhood

The visitor is not an omnipotent narrator and is not secretly another procedurally generated adult. Their own mind, knowledge, taste, memories, habits and limitations remain those of the actual person at the keyboard. What NEIGHBOR supplies is a **thin, historically accountable local body and social position** through which that real human may act in Nadia's world. The portaled person has a place to stand, a budget, available time, bodily reach, social visibility and materially feasible attempts. Those facts are fictional and real *within the fictional world*; they do not overwrite the human's biography.

The user-settled starting condition is stronger than vague shared class or neighborhood proximity: **at entry, the visitor possesses exactly the same ordinary socioeconomic purchasing capacity as Nadia**. This equality is a material constraint, not a visible similarity meter. The visitor knows what groceries, transportation or lost work time cost them. They do **not** receive Nadia's bank statement, debts, household transfers, dependents, precarious obligations, private valuation or subjective experience. Initial purchasing equivalence does not imply equal responsibility for a child, equivalent legal rights, the same employer, identical safety, or equal reserves of family help. Economic positions can later diverge through separate histories.

The decisive contradiction is **objective material homology without historical or phenomenological identity**. A stranger placed into the same practical spending condition has not lived Nadia's family, education, culture, migration, class training or institutions. The visitor may be legally and socially treated as a local peer but misunderstand an expected greeting, infer the wrong meaning from a religious practice, fail to recognize a class insult, or speak with an unfamiliar repertoire of references. Nadia may legitimately expect local knowledge and misinterpret its absence. She may also mistake an unfamiliar gesture for a class offense. Neither person's account automatically becomes the authoritative theory of the encounter.

The portal must therefore wear an honest *scar of translation*. A user's sentence can be rendered in a world language with an explicit, bounded substitution where that is defensible; its pragmatic register, joke, social connotation or locally unfamiliar concept may remain awkward. A visitor who mentions their actual school cannot be silently given attendance at a corresponding local school merely to make the dialogue flow. A reference to an external city, political institution, language or credential may remain an explanation needed between persons. Nadia can believe, disbelieve, misconstrue or ask about such a claim, but cannot receive authenticated knowledge of the real user's past from an invisible platform dossier.

The visitor can also lie, exaggerate, joke, decline to answer, make an unverifiable claim or assert a skill they have not demonstrated. **Actual authored speech, authorized local realization, independently evidenced competence, local reputation and Nadia's belief are separate**. Claiming virtuoso musicianship cannot magically make the visitor perform a piece; a bounded fictional capability, if actually admitted, can become a social fact with costs and expectations. A user who sincerely explains a concept may demonstrate a medium-native intellectual skill through real words, while the platform must not turn one correct answer into proof of their education or permanent personality. Being believed is not equivalent to being true; being caught out is not the only reason an unverified self-presentation matters.

The peer's embodiment is consequential. The user's actual physical body is not magically reproduced in the simulation, but neither are all fictional bodily abilities conferred by grammatical fluency. A hand may catch tossed keys under stated physical conditions. A fall may be lucky, a repair may be difficult, a task may require access, tools, rest or competence. A claim about how effortlessly one performs an action is not the world's settled outcome. The local body can suffer inconvenience, inability or danger without thereby changing the user's extra-game body.

There are **two unequally reversible exits**. The real person can close the interface and go back to their actual life; they must not be punished by a platform for doing so. Inside Nadia's world, an agreed obligation may remain unmet, another worker may cover a shift, or Nadia may reasonably be disappointed if a promise has been broken. Those consequences arise from established fictional acts, not from app retention strategy. The visitor's local shell may continue thin rent or sleep maintenance but may not undertake offscreen emotionally meaningful choices, deliver apologies, make promises or build a private personality while the real human is absent. The outside human's own resources and obligations cannot be requisitioned by the fiction.

These differences are the product's materialist force, not defects to conceal. The experiment places two distinct historical minds within an initially matched economic situation, where new relations can form and old interpretive habits become visible *through what the human actually tries*. It neither grants a native soul nor demands that the visitor impersonate a stereotyped local role. It permits friction, mutual mistake, genuine learning, misrecognition and unrelated choice. **Equality of one initial material condition intensifies rather than abolishes the unequal histories the two bring to it.**

## III. The world is actual before attention; discovered facts are not invented causes

When the visitor enters a kitchen, its food, objects, bills, layout, labor history and owners must be true within an independently continuing world. This is the difference between a causally inhabited world and a narrator's selection of props. A repaired shoe is not present because the human needs a metaphor for poverty; a book is not selected because it will disclose an identity; a knife is not a clue whose psychological meaning has been preassigned. Each can be looked at, borrowed, misplaced, discussed, damaged or simply ignored, and its further significance depends upon actual material and social history.

**The central opposition is a world that must already have enough truth to constrain events and a simulation that cannot explicitly pre-generate every conceivable detail.** Historical completion is lawful only within constrained causal slack. If the visitor notices a repaired strap, further grounded information may be required about when or by whom it was repaired. That can be an inert detail, an episodic association, a modest causal contributor or, with substantially stronger evidence, a formative part of life. These grades are not exchangeable. A late description may deepen the object's material provenance without manufacturing a childhood memory for Nadia. A later demand for a deep secret is not a sufficient cause to install one in the past.

This is why *world depth* and *the visitor's knowledge* are two different frontiers. The simulation may need to determine whether a key works because a co-parent attempts to use it offscreen; the visitor can remain unaware. The visitor can inspect an old photograph and learn its visible features without learning what every person depicted remembers. A user theory—“this must have traumatized her”—cannot become the source of the photograph's world history. Equally, a true past object can have no meaningful psychological relation to Nadia. A world becomes more determinate by respecting actual causal demands, not by making every discovered detail a key to her inner life.

A living thing can occupy several distinct kinds of historical past: performed public events; recurring practice; admissible compressed periods; an actor's actual receipt of a particular event; retained traces or associations; conscious recollections; rumors and testimony; and later reinterpretation. **Occurrence is not memory; memory is not historical fact; narrative explanation is not a new occurrence.** A person can falsely remember a visit or sincerely misattribute the reason for an old gesture. Their belief can then cause actual new behavior without rewriting the earlier event. Conversely, a material fact can constrain their life without their knowing it.

Objects carry *produced human activity* into the present. A borrowed book may embody ownership, paid work, caring labor, scarcity, a gift history, a political argument, a remembered journey or nothing beyond ordinary use. The material object, Nadia's learned relation to it, and what lending it to this particular visitor means are distinct. Showing, lending, gifting, exchanging, entrusting, withholding, stealing, repairing and sharing are different acts, with different rights, intentions, risks and consequences. A refrigerator's food can be communal, rationed, promised to somebody else or provided under an ambiguous courtesy; “help yourself” is not a universal grant to eat, rummage, take a photograph or interpret the family's economy.

**An object's value is not a universal symbol, and a relationship is not a new object-meaning psyche.** Histories of making, buying, using, maintaining, class position, institutional ownership, memory and reciprocal exchange can shape what it means to an actor. That meaning may differ with audience and occasion. The inherited relational person/other/event mediation—not a parallel “book engine,” “hospitality engine,” or hidden symbolism dictionary—must make such significance derivable where sufficient evidence exists. A paid worker can use a tool expertly without owning it; a household member can command its use through an institutional entitlement while another person's labor maintains it.

Material and social structures operate even when no one explains them. Rent, wages, work shifts, childcare, war, policy, transportation, religious authority, disability, law, local custom and weather can alter possible activity. A public historical event must be grounded at its proper external/local boundary and affect a branch through actual exposure and causal chains, not because it completes a visitor's political discussion. A school decision may change the child's time before Nadia learns the reason. One apparently generous invitation can be practically expensive because a household's food and labor are scarce. Material equivalence at entry does not dissolve these distinct relations.

Nor does *the visitor's contribution* become sole authorship of a later change. A child's musical aspiration may be enabled by a teacher, sibling, instrument, a story told by the visitor, peer enthusiasm and independently developed interest. The visitor can be consequential without being the missing key to an entire life. Their interpretation may be partly accurate, just as Nadia's may be. An event that changes none of the relationships under inquiry can still have occurred and consumed real time.

An imagined world need not generate every everyday minute at maximal resolution. Compression can conserve stable routines, ordinary constraints and qualified historical transitions while deepening particulars when an actual causal need arises. **The decisive requirement is historical nonarbitrariness, not indiscriminate detail or theatrical density**. A crowded Thursday can be merely a Thursday. The world can remain unremarkable without ceasing to be independent.
