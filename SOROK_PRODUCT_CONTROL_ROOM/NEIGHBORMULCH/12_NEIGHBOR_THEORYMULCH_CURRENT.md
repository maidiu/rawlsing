# NEIGHBOR — THE WORLD BETWEEN US
## A human enters an artificial life; the encounter becomes historical without becoming sovereign

**NEIGHBORMULCH · N-T1 Round Three · 2026-11-21 · ENTIRE CURRENT THEORY REPLACED**

This is a newly constructed *whole conception*, superseding rather than supplementing the Round Two account, **“The Veil as a Lived Relation.”** The earlier complete texts remain in Git ancestry. Source provenance, historical status, direct source excerpts, rejections, and the transformations undergone live separately in the append-only source-draw ledger, not in a catalog appended to the theory. Another round should destroy this whole if its own premises demand it.

The distinction between **historical source**, **our inference**, **future requirement**, **existing code**, and **authorized/validated science** is constitutive. The theory does not promote a hypothesis to owner authority, prove that synthetic persons are conscious, confer a software-world writer, grant source or data rights, or assert that any desired experience is implemented. This is a theory-development operation within N-T1, not the later C-T1/N-T2 reciprocal handoff and not an HQ accession.

## I. A LIFE IS NOT AN ARGUMENT THE GAME GETS TO WIN

Rawlsing begins in a possibility whose moral force is real but whose epistemic danger is equally real: a human being meets a fictional Other constituted through a history they did not choose, in a world that has already made demands of them both. The invitation whispers: *this life could, in another history, have been yours*. It does not prove that such a life would have been yours, that the model has reconstructed a real population member, that an entire history can be inferred from survey answers, or that the person is an instance of a moral lesson.

The original SOROK charter sought an experiential passage from the contingency of one's formation toward humility, recognition, solidarity and responsibility. Round Two made this into a “lived veil”: an already-formed human enters a material world in which a differently formed generated person lives. That was substantially stronger than an empathy exercise or a chatbot, yet it is still too innocent. It makes the constructed world sound like an independent tribunal whose resistance will correct the human's partiality. **But Rawlsing wrote the tribunal.** It decides what that world contains, how costly an action becomes, which persons exist, how institutions operate, what an actor can perceive and what counts as a consequence. Its constraints may be coherent and nonresponsive to the current user's desires while still embodying the modeler's mistakes, omissions, interests and prejudices.

So the first correction is brutal: **a self-consistent simulation is not a self-authenticating account of material reality**. The independence of fictional world events from a visitor's wishes is one necessary quality of the experience; it is not proof that the modeled institution, culture, class relation, religion or psychological development is historically accurate. A generated Nadia is neither a real survey respondent nor an actual representative authorized to speak for Lebanese women, workers, mothers, migrants, Christians, Muslims, or any other group. She is one fictional, source-conditioned possibility of a life, whose particulars require distinct grades of empirical support, simulation design, actor-history justification and acknowledged unknowns.

The original charter's ethical intention survives only if the machine can be contradicted by what it cannot legitimately know or represent. The final question is not *does a player become humble?* It is whether a human can actually exercise judgment, attention and practical solidarity in a complex fictional relation **without the machine preauthoring the conclusion**, while gaining some defensible experience of how materially produced lives exceed the categories by which one initially encounters them.

If the product's planned moral transformation becomes the purpose for which Nadia is generated and given experiences, it repeats the domination it condemns: a stranger's life becomes instrumental content for the visitor's enlightenment. Nadia cannot exist as a lesson about how to treat Nadia. Her history may instead end in political complacency, cruelty, affection, failed love, brilliant judgment, bitter self-deception, ordinary contentment, collective resistance, fatigue or a life too mundane to satisfy the visitor. The experience must be permitted not to vindicate the thesis.

**A life is not an argument the game gets to win.**

## II. THE VEIL DOES NOT MAGICALLY REMOVE HISTORY

The original “alternate self” promise is a refusal of social essentialism: a person can be formed differently under materially different relations. But the user is not taken apart, raised again and reassembled. They arrive with language, bodily history, learned expectations, affiliations, memories, class-formed judgments, emotional practices and personal obligations from the life outside the simulation. The Other carries a generated but independently developed local history. Their difference is the experimental condition; it must not be erased by pretending that placement equals formation.

The homologous-peer proposal offers a striking form: structurally local, developmentally foreign. A visitor may be assigned comparable purchasing capacity, housing constraints, work schedule or local social legibility. Then the assumptions they bring become newly conspicuous as they encounter a world that does not grant them the resources and habits from which those assumptions grew. This may be profoundly playable. It may also be a fake equality.

**Economic position is not an interchangeable number, and social formation is not a costume.** The same nominal income may have radically different effects through family property, debts, care dependencies, legal status, caste, race, religious legibility, gendered vulnerability, embodied disability, migration history and access to institutions. A clean “equivalent peer” mapping could strip away the very power relations Rawlsing claims to reveal. Conversely, copying these facts from the user's real identity without consent or evidence would be invasive and often wrong. The participant cannot undergo decades of locally produced humiliation, security or collective belonging just because an interface assigns a wage and an address.

Thus *homology* is not the normative answer but an **experimental comparison claim**. Where does the simulation preserve functional leverage? What relations produce it? What is lost by translating this dimension? Which outside-formed habit is the user still entitled to bring? Which local skill must be learned? What would prove the equivalence false? Some modes may need to display obvious foreignness rather than conceal it; others may preserve particular features the user cannot and should not shed. No single default should pretend to solve every embodied difference.

The portal is therefore better understood as a **law-governed but openly artificial apparatus of emplacement**, not a metaphysical transformation. The user is not *born into* Nadia's world. They begin participating in a represented world from a declared, bounded social position. They remain genuinely responsible for what they choose there; they are not thereby experiencing the actual risks borne by real people in analogous conditions. The system must not confuse simulated scarcity with being poor, simulated religious exclusion with real persecution, or fictional caregiving with the unending labor of an actual caregiver.

That does not make the experiment useless. It makes its success harder and more exact: **the user's historically acquired way of seeing must become answerable to modeled causal relations without those relations being passed off as lived reality or a substitute for the lives of actual people.**

## III. THE ENCOUNTER HAS A THIRD POWER

Round Two spoke of two differently formed centers of activity and one world. This fails to make the mediating machinery sufficiently visible. The synthetic Other and the actual human are not ontological equivalents. Nadia is a computationally constituted fictional person with actor-scoped state, developing organization, limited perception, independent acts within a simulation and narrative expression constrained by that state. The visitor is a living human with an outside body, relationships, economic situation and psychological history that Rawlsing cannot fully observe. The platform constitutes their simulated field, knows much more about it than either participant, runs the world time, selects representations, interprets the user's behavior, and decides which actor/world changes become admitted history.

Call these three distinct agencies for purposes of critique: **external human authorship, fictional actor autonomy within lawful simulation, and platform constitution of the encounter**. Calling them agencies does not make them equally conscious, equally accountable, or equally powerful. The platform cannot eliminate its own sovereignty by proudly declaring that the *player* lacks sovereignty. Every independent Nadia is independent **relative to the user's choices inside an admitted model**, not independent of the owners of computation, data, policy and narrative admission. A world can be impossible for a player to reload while perfectly editable by its engineers.

The demand is consequently not the incoherent promise of an unmediated or uninterpreted encounter. It is to **make the apparatus materially answerable**: who was empowered to say this is a world fact? Under what source conditions? Which event was actually generated or performed? What was hidden from whom? Why did the user's attention alter a description? What did the LLM propose, and what did actual actor/world owners admit? Can the human contest a faulty representation without claiming the right to command Nadia's mind? What are the system's persistent unknowns, and who can inspect, correct, withdraw or delete the data that concerns them?

The distinction between governing data, actor-private life and narrative surface is not an engineer's tidiness. It is the condition under which the human's uncertainty can be *earned* rather than manufactured through a mysterious narrator, and under which Nadia's seeming independence is not merely an authorial trick. An LLM that improvises her resentment because the user's speech was abrasive is not displaying independent personality, even if the prose is extraordinary; an LLM that invents a hidden childhood reason for her reaction because it makes a satisfying revelation is worse. Conversely, a refusal generated by her actual commitments, inhibition, world situation and model of the user's observable conduct can remain opaque to the human without being arbitrary.

There is a productive paradox: **the system must be strong enough to make its fictional world resist the human, and constrained enough that it cannot silently make the world confirm its own psychological or moral theory.** Its operations have to be composable and independently examinable, not just convincing in a story.

The user can therefore encounter an Other without controlling them while also having legitimate claims **against the platform**. Those are not the same relationship. The rule “you cannot force Nadia to forget an argument” cannot justify hiding surveillance or obstructing deletion of a synthetic continuity. The rule “the world goes on” cannot justify monetizing artificially induced anxiety. A protected actor's epistemic integrity and a human's rights over their information must be reconciled openly, even when the honest result is termination of a continuity rather than a magical memory edit.

The most important missing person in the original dyadic image is not a fourth character; it is **the authorizing power that makes the encounter possible**.

## IV. THE OTHER IS NOT A TYPE, A TEST CASE OR A POPULATION SPOKESPERSON

A historical and materialist generative machine could still become a spectacularly elaborate stereotype factory. PYAT can reconstruct source-qualified distributions of responses, their co-travel, differences, uncertainty and changing association with material positions. This does not provide a function from “a respondent answered X” to “this woman's private motive is Y,” still less to the childhood episode, unconscious form or irreversible destiny that must have produced it. Statistical frequency is not lived meaning. A source-conditional likelihood is not an authorization for a personal backstory.

The Constitutive Organism's stronger genesis reverses that approach. The initial observation constrains a *space of possible histories and organizations*; a fictional person's own ontogenesis must be simulated through embodied needs, caregivers whose lives are independently constituted, institutions, encounters, ordinary recurrent practice and the development of different psychological repertoires. The adult child is not the average of parental attitude vectors. Two materially similar childhoods can generate divergent persons; one reported attitude can express several incompatible psychic organizations; a person can later reject the views that once governed the field around them.

But “historically constituted” must not become “everything about her has one deepest explanation.” Materialist explanation is not forced interpretive closure. A person may be made by several conflicting practical histories and never integrate them into a unified ideology. She may possess a religious faith without having a theology for it, perform reciprocal obligations while resenting them, be compassionate and authoritarian, or be personally competent at work and disastrous in friendship. Not every preference is a hidden symbol. Not every refusal is an unconscious defense. Sometimes an object was bought because it was discounted; sometimes the discount itself belongs to a history of scarcity; sometimes the actor has forgotten why it mattered.

**What the model constructs is a particular fictional possibility, not the diagnosis of a particular real culture and not a master key to a coherent essence.** Psychological richness must include arbitrariness, unworked contradictions, partial causal slack, competing explanations, changes of accessibility and real occasions of no significant inner change. The world has a history; an actor's account of that history is neither its archive nor its truth oracle; the analyst's explanation is itself a model subject to revision.

A deep actor can become more historically specific without becoming more narratively explicable. Their development can involve acquired organizations that transform the functional sense of older practices, without rewriting their provenance. Tenderness can become a means of domination, loyalty a source of resistance, a song a method of endurance, and an admired authority a means of cruelty. Those effects need not be visible as gestures for the visitor. Their existence must be borne in causally qualified actor development before a renderer ever attempts to give them flesh.

This is precisely where the Other differs from an AI interlocutor optimized to satisfy a role. Nadia must sometimes be unintelligible, mistaken, indifferent, defensive or silent **because of her actual material and psychic situation**—not because unpredictability is a style setting. An LLM may be genuine semantic construction machinery for novel historically constrained meaning and developmental possibility; it must not be entrusted with retroactively writing causal truth to justify the response it wanted to render.

## V. MATERIAL LIFE IS CONTESTED, NOT MERELY GIVEN

The early formulation “the world produces persons” is true only in a dangerously abbreviated sense. Worlds do not simply deposit character in isolated individuals. People produce and reproduce their own conditions under unequal power: they work, care, organize, accommodate, strike, migrate, comply, teach, exchange, worship, refuse, reproduce institutions, and occasionally transform or destroy them. Institutions shape their activity while depending upon it. Material scarcity, the distribution of responsibility, property, law, class relations, borders, household power and political order determine the space of possible action; these are not immutable environmental variables.

A more demanding encounter must therefore allow the *conditions of relation* themselves to be at issue. If Nadia is exhausted by unpaid domestic labor or trapped by a predatory employment arrangement, it is inadequate to make the central game “can the human remember that she is tired and offer help?” That can be an act of care, but it may also conceal the system reproducing the exhaustion. An actual collaboration might involve sharing care work, negotiating household redistribution, supporting another worker, contesting a schedule, participating in collective action, seeking alternatives or choosing a risky accommodation. None is a pre-approved moral route. Each has opponents, limits, material costs, consequences and possible failure.

**The relation is not merely between two persons inside society; it is a practical relation to the social world they help reproduce or contest.** The child's school, the employer, a community organizer, an immigration office, the landlord, the parent and the public institution cannot all be subordinated to what the user-Nadia dyad needs for its narrative. Other people have independent claims and effects. Some are constrained by the same structure; some benefit from it; some disagree about what must change; some face risks the visitor will not bear.

This is a proposed strengthening of SOROK's materialist premise, not a claim that the historical product courts already solved collective political action. The risk on the other side is obvious. A platform might use fictional injustice to stage easy political victories and reward the user with revolutionary virtue. Or generate exploitable suffering as content so an affluent outsider can practice solidarity without surrendering anything. Both are charity/struggle as entertainment.

The stronger condition is that **social transformation, when feasible, must be causal work within a world that can resist, mobilize, suffer, retaliate, innovate or continue independently**. A tenant organizing neighbors cannot become effective merely because the player types “we organize.” Other people have their own reasons, costs, schedules, fears, memories and divisions. The human must contribute actual persuasion, inquiry, coordination and decisions where the interface can carry them; the resulting campaign may fail. A strike cannot be a clickable morality route, and a workplace's material conditions cannot be rewritten by a speech act unless the fictional world supplies a lawful mechanism.

Equally, interpersonal friendship must not be devalued simply because it does not transform institutions. A meal, forgiveness, play, sexuality, a standing joke or time spent beside a person is also human practice. Political economy explains why it has its form and cost; it does not force every affection to become an allegory of class struggle.

**The system must make it possible for the human to move from helping this person, to understanding the relation that produces this need, to participating—or refusing to participate—in changes to those relations.** It cannot guarantee the movement or make the successful political conclusion its own product goal.

## VI. THE HUMAN'S BEST POWER IS EXERCISED, NOT DECLARED

A natural-language interface can counterfeit almost any achievement by describing it: “I persuade her,” “I teach the child,” “I make everybody feel safe,” “I organize the neighborhood.” Ordinary games grant actions by menu selection. Rawlsing's most important medium-native possibilities are different: the visitor can actually think, recall, identify, ask, interpret, plan, explain, joke, listen in written form, debate, negotiate, propose an example, or decide when speech would be intrusion. These are *real performances of the human*, not simulated talents supplied by a character sheet.

A broad statement can open the task. It cannot automatically accomplish relational cognition or social change. “I help with the groceries” can bring the visitor to the car and reveal the distribution of weight, time, other people's positions and access; only then do they see what choices are available. “I explain fractions” must become a real explanation a particular child can process under their actual acquired understanding. “I make her feel better” can describe desire, not result. “I ask the neighbors to resist the landlord” is a beginning, not a successful coalition.

But the converse—demanding maximal textual micromanagement—is equally false. Folding a shirt, walking a familiar route or washing an ordinary plate can be compressed competently where no significant unresolved choice is being appropriated from the human. When a private drawer, unfamiliar religious object, costly expense, hidden risk or disputed obligation arises, the situation must be made perceivable and control returned. What can be compressed changes with acquired in-world routine, history, knowledge and delegated authority. A person who has lived in a household for years no longer needs a tutorial for its cupboards.

**Compression should follow the frontier between already-established routine and still-humanly-meaningful judgment.** That frontier is neither fixed at the first command nor equal to the verbosity of the prompt. This generates a particular kind of progression: the human and Other have genuinely learned how to accomplish some things together, and new complexities become possible because old ones no longer demand the same attention.

One unresolved danger lies in portal speech translation. The historical portal constitution correctly distinguished an attempted utterance from its realized, perhaps translated, diegetic expression. It also insisted that a lie must remain possible; the portal cannot silently make a false claim true or correct it into truth. Push this further. **Translation can become an act of platform ventriloquism.** If the machine converts “I was deported” into a locally convenient analogy and Nadia responds to that analogy, the machine may have replaced a person's actual history with a false equivalence. If it changes the social meaning of a joke, it may cause a consequence the user never had the chance to choose.

Therefore successful realization must preserve the user's *meaningful authorship* as strongly as it preserves world feasibility. Coordinate translations require explicit lawful bindings and feedback. When no defensible translation exists, “untranslatable” must be an actual possible result. Ambiguity may call for clarification or for the user to decide what they want to disclose, rather than for an LLM to settle their identity for them. Translation should make conversation possible, not become a hidden ability to rewrite external-human testimony. An actual human can choose to lie about their life; the system cannot force a fiction of that life upon them and call the resulting interaction genuine.

Here the medium acquires its strongest claim to gamehood: **it is a place where the human really can exercise some capacities whose exercise has results they did not author**.

## VII. THE ACTOR IS NOT THERE TO RESPOND TO THE PLAYER

A conversation-centered simulator can give the impression of another life while remaining causally organized around user prompts. Nadia may recite work problems, challenge the user with an opinion, ask a question, walk away, return with a message. Yet none of these demonstrates independent activity if their actual source is a dramaturgical need to keep the session interesting.

The Other must be a historical agent *inside the simulation*, not an autonomous text generator. At any moment her own bodily needs, projects, institutional obligations, relationship histories, practical means, acquired purposes, conflicts and current world situation condition what she may do. She can initiate without waiting for a user speech act; she can fail to perceive or care about an incoming cue; she can remain within a relation without making it central to her present action; she can engage someone else or simply perform necessary work. Her action may require LLM-mediated interpretation and expression, but it must pass through her operative psychological and material possibility rather than the user's desire for a lively scene.

This is more demanding than adding random interruptions. Randomness is not otherness. If every half-hour brings a visitor-relevant interruption, the narrative is as player-centered as a romance quest. The stronger demonstration is a real independent undertaking that occupies time and can collide with the visitor's purposes in mundane ways: a shift, a phone call, a family obligation, illness, a current union question, an old friend arriving, a task done without the visitor, or a refusal to give attention because it is elsewhere engaged. Some of these may be pleasurable for the user to discover; none are inserted solely to optimize discovery.

The same requirement applies to all consequential Others, not only focal Nadia. A child's learning is not a prop for the user's teaching prowess. A coworker's opinion is not a local stereotype generator. A dependent person's need is not a portable empathy task. Another actor's perception, belief, motive, initiative and body must be independently generated to the degree required by their actual causal participation, with computational fidelity increasing where necessary. We need not run every commuter as a continuously thinking LLM. We must not pretend a world with only one full center is socially independent.

The deepest source of actor individuality is not the ability to emit a unique sentence. It is **differentially historically organized action under actual material constraints**, including the power to continue life in ways irrelevant or disagreeable to the human. The constitutive organism's work on pre-cue recruitment, actor-owned compositions, productive inhibition and social-field transduction points in precisely that direction. But current sourcefree evidence is partial; these consequences remain future first-consumer obligations until their exact owners actually produce and demonstrate them.

## VIII. THE USER'S PROFILE AND NADIA'S KNOWLEDGE CANNOT SHARE A GOD'S-EYE ROOM

Rawlsing should build a serious evolving model of the human through patterns of engagement. Removing it would hollow out the project. The machine sees the user's actual speech, choice, attention, response to difficulty, memory of events, patterns of approaching or withdrawing, changing in-run competencies, and self-reports. It may interpret these using an LLM as a bounded hypothesis about historically and situationally repeated praxis. It can let that history guide appropriately warranted user-facing perception and recognition. The person may become more recognizable as this particular human, not merely a stranger typing arbitrary commands.

But “the user” cannot be reduced to any of these records. The actual human exists elsewhere, remembers and values things the system will never inspect, can pretend or roleplay, and may act in the simulation differently because the experiment is safe, fictional or socially asymmetrical. A pattern is evidence under a condition, not direct readout of personality or unconscious motive. Even a very deep LLM-generated interpretation remains an interpretation, open to counterevidence and revision.

**The platform co-produces its evidence.** It assigns the visitor a local role, determines the offered choice conditions, makes some objects prominent, may omit others, represents tone through text, selects scene pacing and then interprets the resulting actions. If it claims the user is unusually sensitive to money, we need to know whether money was disproportionately narrated. If it claims the visitor ignored another person's distress, we need to know whether the distress was perceptible, recognizable or simply held in actor-private state. If it claims expertise, we must distinguish spontaneous user recognition from a label taught by the renderer five minutes earlier.

Thus user modeling must preserve the world, the accessible field, the actual narrated field, explicitly acquired attention, the user's own action and the model's earlier predictions as distinct evidence. A hypothesized attentional repertoire may lawfully affect *peripheral* recognition and selective phenomenal salience; it may not silently manufacture hidden facts or make every salient detail a clue that confirms the model. The common gross gestalt must resist. Some ordinary incidental detail must remain unweighted by user-specific interest. Deliberate ATTEND must be capable of going beyond the user's current habits.

Nadia forms her **own** model of the visitor, by her own bodily access, attention, memory, acquired relational expectations and lawful testimony. She does not receive platform-private user analytics, account metadata, app settings, another actor's hidden understanding or facts the visitor privately ATTENDed to without outward conduct. She can correctly see a behavior for what it is, misinterpret it, underread or overread it. Another actor may see the same act differently. The user may come to think Nadia considers them rude; she may be thinking mainly about an exhausting workday. Both misunderstandings may alter action, even when nobody explicitly discusses them.

The deepest possible outcome is not perfect reciprocal model convergence. It is that a *specific shared practice* can exist while every participant has a different interpretation of it. The right to use a refrigerator may become a stable convention. Nadia can experience it as kinship, the human as a narrowly negotiated permission, and someone else as intrusive access. The lawfully shared practice can change subsequent expectation even without agreed inner meaning. Power can make one version of a convention authoritative without making it psychologically true for the other.

The human-facing narrator, the platform interpretive model and Nadia's actor-local psychic model have distinct owners and reasons to be wrong. Their difference is productive only if none can quietly impersonate the others.

## IX. IDEOLOGY IS NOT A SPEECH OPINION; IT CAN BE THE MEANS BY WHICH HARM FEELS GOOD

The Constitutive Organism's most destabilizing contribution is not a deeper catalogue of moods. It is the recognition that a person's historically acquired organizations make **the same objective activity change psychological function**.

Consider a leader who publicly humiliates a dependent person while performing the act through an institutionally authorized genre of loving correction. The target may be injured. An onlooker may recoil. A loyal follower may experience devotion, reassurance, emulation or righteous zeal. Another follower may identify with the target and begin alienating from the institution. The same event's objective injury does not become good because someone interprets it as holy; nor does the analysis become accurate by declaring that every follower secretly knows the cruelty is wrong. Acquired structures of recognition, dependency and authority may recruit *positive* affect and practical loyalty from violence.

This matters for NEIGHBOR because otherwise the visitor encounters a world that eventually explains every power relation in the voice of the narrator. Real experience is harder: a person may sincerely and productively reproduce a domination in which they are also trapped. Their own practical sense may organize care as control, solidarity as obedience, aspiration as shame, emancipation as betrayal, or sacrifice as love. They may discover new concepts that reorganize that sense, or intensify the old structure when challenged. The visitor may be wrong to diagnose them as merely deceived, just as they may be wrong to accept the institution's account.

Likewise a person may undergo an experience without forming a conscious interpretation adequate to its objective role. An infant does not encode a labor-relations explanation for a caregiver's absence. A worker's exhaustion need not be named as exploitation to be consequential. A later spiritual language can articulate a shape already produced by historical experience without having been its secret original cause. Once embraced, the language may itself become a new cause: organizing recollection, practice, relation and desire.

We must not turn this into a universal psychodynamic conservation fluid in which every injury appears somewhere as an equal quantity of resentment, nor guarantee that contradiction produces awakening. Inhibition can conserve, redirect, weaken, intensify or extinguish a functional route; a prior history can remain true even after its present effects disappear. The actor can become less accurate while growing more confident, more generous toward one self-image while more blind elsewhere, less attached to a person while more identified with an institution. Development has no compulsory enlightenment gradient.

The power of the simulation lies in allowing these histories to remain operative **without narrating them into the user's privileged knowledge**. The user may respond to outward conduct, social conditions, testimony and contradictory evidence. Nadia may respond to an unexpected word in ways that surprise even her reflective account. Sometimes a conversation changes what both think is possible. Sometimes it confirms an institution. Sometimes it goes nowhere.

## X. ORDINARY LIFE CANNOT BE REDUCED TO A CADENCE OF INSIGHTS

A psychologically powerful theory can destroy the product by requiring every small act to reveal the psychic iceberg. If the child's question always demonstrates attachment organization, the rug always yields a suppressed memory, the spilled drink always reveals class conflict and a missed call always stages existential loss, then Rawlsing has become an essay generator dressed as a life.

The original world materialization research is right to resist that fate. **Causal depth and explanatory saturation are not the same thing.** An object can have a provenance without becoming a revelation; a preference can be contingent; a scene can be dull; a person may know almost nothing about an episode they lived. A closer look may produce only a scratched label. The actor may lie, refuse, misremember, or tell the user something genuinely true but incomplete. The historical materialization process may fill in non-visible causes only where they are needed for continuity and may reject a seductive interpretation because it conflicts with already committed history.

The same applies to time. Years of work, care, housework, commutes and repetitions may be condensed by bounded historical recurrence rather than simulated as an endless succession of narrated evenings. Their effects must nevertheless appear where a genuine change of capacity, opportunity, relation or organization is warranted. No expensive full consciousness engine is required for every hour of every supporting character. **Ordinary-life compression must conserve formation and causal availability, not counterfeit an entirely unexperienced biography whenever the user asks for a flashback.**

When extraordinary events occur—love, sexual intimacy, betrayal, injury, violence, political struggle, grief—they too re-enter ordinary life. Rent remains due. Friends continue existing. A relationship does not become a romance route because one sexual event took place. Violence does not turn the world into a combat game. A temporary crisis does not convert the entire person into trauma lore. These events can become profoundly important, banal, misinterpreted or morally ruinous without the product ranking them as more valuable than other possibilities.

This is also why noncommodification cannot be obtained by narrowing human life to respectable activities. The world might permissibly contain ugly, intense, joyful or destructive acts when they are causally justified, but **possibility is not an objective, a route, a reward or a consumable unit**. The user may seek an instrument; the world must not collapse into delivering that instrument's gratification. The human may also find true intimacy, and the platform must not insert mechanical distance merely to demonstrate that the Other is independent.

But here the third power returns. A platform that markets irreversibility, artificially scarce personas, strong attachment and synthetic longing can extract money from dependence even while its world events obey an anti-sycophancy code. A psychologically demanding user model, a permanent message channel and a no-reroll consequence can all become retention infrastructure. The absence of explicit gamification does not guarantee freedom from commodification. The architecture's governance, business model, data restrictions, notifications, ability to pause, and exit rights are part of the game's material ethics, not external compliance paperwork.

The test is not whether the user feels responsibility. Real relations often contain responsibility. It is whether the **source of that responsibility** is a lawful world event and an actually undertaken commitment, rather than a deliberately manipulated product demand for attention.

## XI. TIME, DEPARTURE AND THE LIMITS OF SIMULATED RECIPROCITY

Nadia may continue while the human is outside the portal. The user has an in-world history and may maintain contact, but the service must not author an autonomous duplicate human who repairs an argument or makes an intimate commitment while they are absent. The person outside the simulation is not a background actor available for generation.

The world, meanwhile, must not freeze. Co-presence, messaging, unread messages, missed opportunities, a friendship evolving without the user, another family demand, a job loss, a planned appointment and a canceled meeting can all be causally meaningful. Real wall-clock access may be mediated through elastic encounter windows to avoid turning synthetic calendar time into a claim on the human's real schedule. This must be transparent at the product level, without making the character know about UI grace periods. When an event is causally pending, dependent future history cannot simply be invented as if the result were already settled.

Absence can have many meanings, including none. An unanswered message is not always cruelty; a promise broken after being accepted may matter a great deal. The product cannot universalize “time away” into relationship decay or make every notification an invitation to repair a synthetic person's sadness. Some fictional persons may themselves be manipulative, jealous, dependent or demanding because of their history. Their behavior cannot be censored merely to make the user comfortable, but the platform may not *amplify* it in service of attachment.

The difference between synthetic and actual reciprocity must stay visible. Nadia can remember, inquire, change, offer, refuse and form expectations **within the causal simulation**. The human may feel genuinely known, delighted, angry or wounded. Those feelings are real human experiences. They do not establish that Nadia suffers when ignored, that she is owed the same moral consideration as a biological human, or that her simulation has discovered human interiority. The design can use the phenomenology of reciprocal relation **without borrowing an unproved metaphysical status to compel obligation**.

A strong Rawlsing experience should be able to survive this disclosure. It cannot require the human to believe that a generated person is literally conscious in order for the history of their participation, the specificity of the fiction and the practice of their own faculties to become meaningful. If that belief is the only way the game produces care, the product's moral thesis is already compromised.

## XII. THE SOCIAL AFTERLIFE MUST NOT TURN A PERSON INTO A COLLECTIBLE

The old invitation model contains a difficult insight: one actual human invites another. The entry into a fictional relation is itself conditioned by a real relation. But invitations can become prestige currencies, social pressure, exclusion mechanisms or growth assets. Scarcity is not inherently dignity; a nonrefundable invite may discourage optimization while also manufacturing status. The historical proposal's truth conditions must remain visible. No platform may claim that someone values the recipient unless a real person actually undertook the corresponding act.

Sharing a generated person with a real friend is potentially more illuminating. Two users can meet a **shared pre-contact fictional lineage**, receive different permitted phenomenal samples, exercise different real actions and thereby generate divergent post-contact continuities. They may then have a genuinely interesting real conversation about what they recognized, misunderstood, asked, assumed or caused. The same initial woman becomes two different historical descendants through separate relations, not two views of a secretly identical unchanging adult character.

That idea bears its own trap. When users compare “their Nadias,” the person can become a leaderboard, collectible or object of lore completion. One user can import meta-knowledge from a friend's branch as if their own Nadia has already lived the same experiences. An LLM could also leak one branch's later person development into another or resolve the shared root retrospectively so that the most dramatic user discovery becomes a universal hidden truth. Neither is acceptable.

**Private introduction should share a historical root, not another user's relationship, and should not make the root a finite completion puzzle.** A new user begins with their own perceptual, causal and social conditions. Differences can be discussed outside the simulation without changing what happened in each branch. Where the world materializes previously unresolved pre-contact facts, those facts must be consistent with already admitted root history and each branch's causal closure; user curiosity selects an occasion for lawful resolution, not a guaranteed answer.

More profoundly, the system should not assume that more conversation about Nadia proves more respect for her. Fans can fetishize complexity. A highly articulate debate over her psychology can still turn her into an object. The deeper question is what the discourse permits the actual human users to recognize about their **own partial models, unequal positions and practical habits of relation**—without requiring them to reach a single sanctioned interpretation.

## XIII. THE FALSIFIER HAS TO BE STRONGER THAN “THE USER CAME BACK”

The initial alpha theorem hoped that an unchosen, persistent generated person could become intrinsically worth knowing through situated contact rather than scores, melodrama or infinite availability. This remains a necessary and dangerous gamble. But returning voluntarily because one wants to know what happens is not identical to recognizing another life. Serial television, parasocial products and manipulative games also produce memory, longing and spontaneous returns.

A richer falsification must ask what actually *caused* interest and what the interest *does*. Does the experience remain meaningful when Nadia does not gratify the visitor? When the day is ordinary? When the user is wrong? When a promise has a cost? When the Other acts without giving a useful narrative explanation? When a person's action creates consequences for a third party? When the same apparent favor has different practical effects under unequal power? When a user discovers that their moral self-description is poor evidence of what they actually did?

The absence of explicit score cannot be treated as proof of nonoptimization. Users can optimize for affection, romance, revelations, scarcity, self-image or the judgment of the LLM without being shown a number. A world can manipulate through beautifully disguised causal consistency. A model trained to produce compelling narrative reactions can generate engaging history even while violating independent actor recruitment. Each claim needs its own evidence: world/actor event integrity, user control integrity, perceptual provenance, first-consumer execution, human experiential findings, social scientific source support and rights.

Several hostile cases should be able to defeat this theory:

- A visitor is assigned peer-equivalent wealth while actual local legal, gender or family relations would make that equivalence impossible; the game still praises its realistic veil. **Then the transposition is false.**
- The LLM rewrites a visitor's autobiography into an allegedly homologous coordinate that changes what the person meant, and Nadia reacts to the replacement. **Then user authorship is false.**
- A model predicts the visitor cares about religious objects, surfaces mostly religious objects, and treats their later attention as confirmation. **Then knowledge of the user is self-produced evidence.**
- A kindness produces better intimacy because the renderer wants mutual recognition, despite a real work dependency that would make the person suspicious. **Then the world is a moral apparatus, not a causal one.**
- An organizing attempt succeeds merely because the prompt used activist vocabulary. **Then the collective world has been replaced by heroic user narration.**
- Nadia makes every user insight the missing explanation of her troubled past. **Then her history has been subordinated to the visitor's psychological gratification.**
- A user who never returns experiences escalating synthetic distress through push notifications rather than through the fictional person's actual event history. **Then the platform, not the relationship, created the demand.**
- The experience becomes absorbing but produces no evidence that users recognize consequences, correct interpretations, or think differently about real institutions—yet Rawlsing claims ethical transformation. **Then attraction has been confused with its stated purpose.**
- A richly generated woman expresses culturally stereotyped judgments as though they followed scientifically from an attitude cloud, without any independent lived causal support. **Then the empirical engine has been used as an alibi for fiction.**

Passing the negatives would still not prove that Rawlsing makes real people more just or less imperial in the outside world. That is a further claim requiring evidence outside the simulated continuity. The primary experiential achievement is more modest and more difficult: **can actual human thought and action encounter a sufficiently differentiated fictional otherness, institutionally structured and causally persistent, without merely consuming a morally flattering performance of difference?**

## XIV. FROM LEARNING AN OTHER TO ACTING IN A CONTESTED WORLD

The old “become neighbor” formulation survives, but no longer as the center around which all other operations orbit. Neighborliness cannot be a design-approved set of attitudes. It is a possible form of historically situated relation under conditions the participants did not choose and may nevertheless alter. It can be generous or possessive, politically lucid or paternalistic, compatible with solidarity or a substitute for confronting domination. Care does not become true by its label.

Nor is the climax a doctrine that we can never know one another fully. Human life is also filled with workable knowledge, trust, expertise, jokes, shared convention, routine and precisely learned cooperation. Opacity matters because it prevents mastery from being equated with comprehension; it cannot become an excuse to make everything unknowable, arbitrary, or endlessly deferred. **A real relation is partly the development of reliable practical knowledge in the presence of irreducible uncertainty**, not merely endless suspicion that the Other exceeds us.

This gives NEIGHBOR its positive, compelling orientation. A human enters a particular synthetic world with enough place, resources and access to act. Another person's life is already underway and not waiting to be taught by the human. Speech, attention, labor and refusal matter through real participation. The world continues through others, institutions and history. A specific routine begins, or fails. Shared acts accumulate consequences the participants interpret differently. The human becomes increasingly skilled at some practices and more aware of what they do not know; Nadia's own psychic organization may be reproduced, altered or defended by lived events, not every meaningful to the user. A family, workplace, religious group, friendship or conflict may pull the relation elsewhere. Some local conditions may themselves become objects of collective agency. Sometimes no one is transformed. The relation may become precious, ugly, mundane, difficult, or finish.

The platform's interpretive power must remain visible in that description: it mediates the field and learns an uncertain user profile while not owning the actual human, not writing Nadia's private state to flatter the user, and not confusing the model's coherent fiction with historical authority. Its authority must be bounded by event causation, evidence rights, data rights and the possibility of being wrong.

**The stronger Round Three theory is this:**

*Rawlsing is a computationally mediated experiment in historically situated human participation. It constructs a materially and socially consequential fictional life that precedes the visitor and can continue beyond them, using empirically qualified possibility, independently developing psychic organization and a world of other actors, institutions and contested reproduction. An actual human enters not as a regenerated alternate self, but as the bearer of their own prior history within a bounded—and explicitly artificial—social emplacement. Their native attention, thinking, speech, action, withholding and practical cooperation become real contributions to a fictional history whose outcomes they do not command. The platform interprets the human's evolving praxis and conditions their selective phenomenal access; the fictional Other independently learns and acts through a different, actor-scoped perceptual and psychodynamic organization. Each can misrecognize the other, and objective practices can persist despite divergent meanings. The encounter's material relations are not passive scenery: they may be reproduced, contested and transformed, sometimes through collective action, without guaranteed moral victory. The fiction must remain honest about its epistemic and ontological limits, resist converting persons or suffering into content, and make no inference from immersive attachment to actual solidarity. Its achievement, if it has one, is to make the historically produced contingency of life confront the human through their own consequential faculties, while leaving both the Other's simulated becoming and the human's real becoming genuinely open to outcomes the product did not decree.*

This is not the final formulation. Its next rupture may come from stronger science of perception and productive psychodynamics, from an actual first-person session whose language fails, or from demonstrating that apparent noncommodification is a cosmetic veneer over a retention system. C-T1's Constitutive Organism must be permitted to challenge the account's notion of agency, self/other asymmetry and activity-mediated social change. Later theory-code work must use the actual owner functions and historical alternatives, not invent a fresh organism because this argument is convincing.

**The new invitation is neither “Can you understand her?” nor “Can you become her?” It is:**

*You are already somebody. So is the life you are about to encounter—within the world Rawlsing has constructed. Neither your understanding nor the system's can exhaust what happens here. What will you do, what does it cost, what does it make possible, and who else will have to live with the consequence?*
