## I. THE NEW KERNEL: CONTINGENT BIRTH OPENS A LIFE; ACTIVITY CONTINUES IT; ENCOUNTER ENTERS IT

A human does not choose the world into which they are born. Their body, family, land, economic position, institutions, language, historical period, dependency, kin, social classification, cultural practices, available technologies, power relations and developmental exposures begin without their consent. Within that world they exercise, acquire, lose, misrecognize and reinterpret capabilities. They form projects, cares, defenses, beliefs, attachments, ways of noticing, capacities to act, and relations among those historically formed organizations. What they can want and do tomorrow depends on what has been possible, attempted, experienced and sustained before. That is the Constitutive Organism's real contribution: **psychological genesis and present psychodynamics are not two separate processes, but distinct timescales of active world–organism history**.

The focal person in NEIGHBOR is not an already completed package of personality contents assembled to supply the player with interesting responses. She has been constituted through a fictional but source-conditioned material history, and **continues to be constituted even when the visitor is absent**. She spends a day. She sleeps, cooks, works, delays something, receives information, meets another person, revisits a commitment, is affected by bodily conditions, keeps a routine unchanged, takes pleasure in something, discovers a problem, or has nothing very remarkable happen. Her activities may reproduce her existing psyche and institutions rather than transform them. One important event may be psychologically insignificant; several banal repetitions can form a practice. A tiny interruption can bring previously acquired relations into a different combination.

The human's entrance is a different kind of event: an already historically formed actual person is given a limited social/material place near her and can now **participate in her continuing world**. They cannot inherit the childhood they did not live, author her internal state or determine the meaning she assigns to their act. They may genuinely become one of the conditions of her later becoming—through speech, attention, laughter, persuasion, help, conflict, coactivity, betrayal, affection, neglect or no consequential participation at all. The contact enters a field of causes that already includes independent family, work, money, bodily need, local politics, institutions and the other actors' projects. The same occurrence can be differently lived by each person and change their later possibilities differently.

The rolled person's **birth contingency** and the visitor's **encounter contingency** are thus neither separate products nor one metaphysical identity swap. The generative birth roll gives an unchosen *possible life* with a constitutively independent history. A particular visitor's encounter becomes one possible *continuation* of that life. Other visitors may encounter its same starting person-root and cause incompatible, independently true continuations. And an encounter can occur at all only because some actual human used a scarce invitation, accepted an encounter or eventually commissioned a new root through a roll. Rawlsing is a deliberately artificial medium for these contingencies, not a claim that the real visitor has actually undergone that birth.

The deeper experiential unit is therefore **an unchosen life that continues through time under its own and others' activity, into which an actual human may become a consequential participant without becoming its sovereign author**.

That formulation is already a game: there is something worth learning, something to attempt, something happening independently, something one's actions can affect, and something one can miss. It is also a materialist theory of personhood: every life is historically and socially mediated rather than an isolated mind that exists to converse with the visitor.

## II. THE ROLL: A SCARCE HUMAN ACT THAT COMMISSIONS SOMEONE WHO IS NOT YOUR CREATION TO COMMAND

“Roll to meet someone you could have been born as” is not merely a random-character button. The contemplated later product makes the roll a **deliberate, finite commission of a new fictional person's existence**. The user has one or perhaps two opportunities under an eventual bounded entitlement policy; pressing the control commits one rare possibility. They feel personal agency because **their action occasions the creation**. They can later say, quite truthfully in product terms, “I rolled that person into the shared Rawlsing bank.” But that gives them **no authority to choose which person is born, rewrite their history, own the person, control future independent encounters, or claim exclusivity over them**.

This creates an unusual combination. User *causally initiates generation*; user does **not** *select its human result*. The person-root's sociohistorical position, family, world, past and developing psyche must be generated independently of the roller's desires, psychographic profile, performance history or fantasies, under a declared conditional sampling frame and computational-developmental procedure. The rolled life is commissioned by this human action and yet has no fictional dependence on its commissioner for its birth or pre-contact biography. This is another productive artificiality: in wall-clock time the user presses a button and a historical existence is generated; within the fictional ontology that person has already lived decades, or such a lawful age as generated, before the visitor can be introduced.

The right way to make this paradox honest is not to pretend that a person existed in real history until a user found them. The user initiated the **construction and admission of a fictional historical root**. Once constituted, its earlier history is canon for all future lawful encounters and is not a retrospective story tailored to the roller. Generation must complete sufficient causal commitments before any visitor-specific relational influence is allowed to contaminate them. An unresolved pre-contact particular may later be completed, but only under the root's own constraints and canonical target—not because a user wants an interesting answer. The system's generation time and the person's fictional birth time are different clocks and different kinds of truth.

A scarce roll can be meaningful because it is committed and irreversible in the relevant product sense. The user accepts what was drawn rather than retrying until the draw flatters their tastes. Technical failure, invalid generation or source-authority rejection are not the moral equivalent of disliking a perfectly valid stranger; they warrant truthful repair or restitution. The exact number of rolls and conditions of accrual remain product/compute decisions, not something this theory has scientifically determined. If the user may receive one or two, be candid about how and when, rather than manipulating them with opaque “earn worthiness” gauges or artificial scarcity theater. The past proposal for morally conditioned in-continuity person succession and the new user's desire for a fresh roll after alienation must be reconciled by product ownership, not silently collapsed.

The scarce act makes a claim on the user because *it could have become any of many possible lives*, while the one that did become actual for the fiction cannot be discarded and rerolled as though its complexity were a defect. Yet this must not become an obligation to remain with that person. The human has a right to leave, dislike, refuse, or encounter somebody else under the product's actual entitlement policy. A singular commitment is not possession; a social encounter is not a debt enforced by the service.

A further caution: “you could have been born as this person” speaks to natal contingency, not literal identity equivalence. The human could have been born under conditions like theirs and developed into some historically different person; no simulator identifies a unique counterfactual real consciousness. Neither demographics nor survey patterns manufacture Nadia's interior by themselves. The birth roll is defensible only as a historically conditioned **fictional possibility** whose actual development is constructed through SOROK, never as the discovery of a real unsurveyed person's soul.

## III. A PERSON BANK IS A COMMON BEGINNING, NOT A MULTIPLAYER SHARED PSYCHE

The original SOROK source already conceived precisely the necessary architecture for a small shared-person alpha. A **seed person-root** has one pre-contact history, one baseline network, material ecology, developmental organization and original world situation. When User A enters, an independent continuation begins: Nadia-A. When User B enters, another: Nadia-B. They are **counterfactual descendants of the same generated person**, not one simultaneously multiplayer person with unified post-contact memory. Each has actual independent meetings, absences, interpretations and world effects. Neither visitor is authorized to change the other's branch by playing.

The common root supplies real ground for social comparison. Two people can both encounter the same initial Nadia, with the same past injuries, learned jokes, private religious practices, socioeconomic situation, friends, aspirations and prior history, and still experience startlingly different apparent personalities. Some differences derive from their respective attention and recognition: one reads the books, another notices cookware, another hears the resentment in a joke. Others are **real divergent future becoming**: a visitor's attitude evokes distrust in one branch and collaborative humor in another, and the branches proceed differently from those encounters. The person really differs *after* the meeting because meeting her is part of her later history.

The bank should preserve precise lineage: originating root, source/provenance grade, canonical pre-contact state or version, generation constraints and any lawful later pre-contact closure. “Shared Nadia” does not mean shared user experience, relationship, present memory or newly formed adult disposition. No pooled statistical training on players' romantic success should alter Nadia's pre-user preferences. No collective “Nadia learned from millions of visitors” should be inserted into her childhood. The bank is a **library of common starting lives**, not a central actor collecting everyone’s afterlife.

A user-originated rolled root may be **introduced to other users** who receive their own continuities from the bank, subject to source quality and access rules. This gives the roller meaningful participation in the population of available persons without granting them creator privileges over the person. A person generated under adequate cost and integrity controls may be shared; not every proposed generated result must be certified for a public bank merely because a button was pressed. A failed or incompletely verified root should not be silently laundered into a globally accepted human possibility. Product-scale history construction and user-attributed creation are not scientific authorization.

This is also what makes “I loved Nadia” and “She was a bitch to me” capable of being **both locally true**. Neither report proves the other player's observation was false. Yet neither licenses complete relativism: they may have encountered the same initial historical truth, and objective branch events still exist that each might misunderstand. They can compare the real human experience of learning differently and producing different relations while the underlying person was originally one modeled possibility. The comparison is neither a conventional narrative spoiler game nor “your choices modified her mood slider.” It is a dramatization of historically constituted personhood.

Eventually the game acquires a social layer outside any branch: real human beings discuss fictional human beings who have independent pre-contact canon and user-specific futures. That social layer is an actual human relationship and discourse, with its own capacity to transform players' perceptions, friendships, values and strategies. **The fictional person belongs to no particular visitor, but the experience of that person can become part of real human social life.**

## IV. THE SOCIAL PARALLAX: REAL USERS KNOW DIFFERENT TRUE THINGS ABOUT ONE COMMON PAST

Shared history creates a further possibility that goes beyond comparing whether Nadia was friendly. User A looks through her old photographs and resolves a previously unspecified **pre-contact** detail. User B, in a separate continuation, never inspected that photograph but has spent weeks talking with her mother. Their experiences differ. They may later speak in the real world and learn things from one another. That community encounter changes the human users' actual knowledge; it does not retroactively become an event through which Nadia-B heard User A's discovery.

The original SOROK doctrine is exact: **the user's attention may select the locus of historical completion but may not determine the completed fact's content.** For an unresolved object, photograph, school, childhood friendship or pre-contact episode, the generator must draw upon the root person, already admitted earlier history, constraints, a canonical target and governed randomness. Once admitted, the particular can become **retro-resolved pre-contact canon** available to later compatible forks, while respecting versions and causal closure. An arbitrary claim or seductive prompt cannot make Nadia's mother have died, make her secretly own a yacht, or install a past ideology across every user because that result suits the player.

The difficult point is *causal backfill*. If a late root fact would materially alter a previous branch's established acts, childhood causes, memories or current psychic organization, it cannot be silently inserted into that branch. Existing continuities may need version-pinning or guarded compatibility adjudication; the exact mechanism remains unresolved in historical sources. Nor is it enough to claim root shared because the new invented story sounds plausible. A late historical claim cannot exceed the remaining lawful causal room or explain every psychological characteristic as a conveniently perfect symbol. **Newly resolved backstory must remain constrained, contingently ordinary, and sometimes irrelevant.**

The social community then experiences three forms of difference. First, different perceptual access to **the same** common truth. Second, genuinely different **post-fork** histories: Nadia responds differently to different humans and later becomes different in each continuity. Third, **external metaknowledge**: a real player may learn true root information from a friend and choose in-game speech on that basis, even though their branch-Nadia has never disclosed it. That is ordinary human social learning. It becomes an actual in-world user act only when the player uses it; Nadia may find it surprising or intrusive and judge its source incorrectly.

This social parallax could generate authentic discussion: “My Nadia never said that”; “Mine went out with somebody else”; “Was her brother always so angry?”; “I didn't notice the drawing”; “Mine believes something completely different now.” The platform should not automatically reveal a master comparison dashboard, hidden psyche, percentages discovered, a catalogue of secrets or a “best Nadia route.” Real users can talk, disagree, spoil, interpret and sometimes be misled. Their external discourse is not an authority to synchronize fictional branches.

There is a political and spiritual argument here which should **emerge** from the structure rather than appear as copywriting: **a person is not exhausted by one relation's view of them, and two people can truthfully speak about the same starting life while having made themselves participants in different continuations**. This insight is more persuasive when a community has actual disagreements about the person than when a developer explicitly lectures everyone about observer relativity.

But it can also become optimization culture. The shared person might acquire fandom status, prestige, memes or walkthrough-style exploitation. Those outcomes are possible social consequences, not proof the person was reduced by design. Product choices should avoid making share counts, “most loved,” erotic routes, fame or competitive completion the system's own reward signals. The community's life is permitted to be contradictory, too.

## V. A DAY MUST HAVE HAPPENED, EVEN IF NO ONE WATCHED IT

A fictional Nadia whose existence resumes only when the player opens the portal is not a continuing organism. She is a sophisticated reactive character who carries a history between pauses, but does not actually **conduct a life**. A persistent record can remain motionless while its prose declares that days went by. To realize the present vision, **world time must be causally generative in the user's absence**, and not merely a decorative clock.

This is not a demand that a model think continuously at token cost every second. The Constitutive Organism's own theory and original SOROK temporal proposals already support many timescales: bodily minutes, household tasks, daily routines, annual development, occasional significant events, and compressed ordinary-life coverage. A day may contain a few high-significance events, numerous ordinary routines, or nothing salient to the next encounter. **A day is still a day when no dramatic event occurred.** The passage of actual work hours, food preparation, exhaustion, sleep, caring, boredom, practice, expenses and attention allocation maintains or reproduces the person's condition even if nothing spectacular changes.

Offscreen continuation should begin from actual Nadia: her current body and needs, commitments and projects, household labor, relationship histories, external environment, available means, financial conditions, ordinary obligations, sleep/wake cycles, prior plans and the independent schedules of other people. Within a bounded interval the engine can advance known routines; identify outstanding events that must be resolved; propose uncertain incidents conditioned by their actual world causes; let the involved actors receive and act; commit objective results; and route differentiated psychic afterlives through existing owners. Where a day's details are not needed, an admitted coverage interval may preserve recurrence and its effects without pretending exact unperformed conversations have been simulated.

The user suggests near-live-V3 recursion for a missed day. That is a powerful direction if applied selectively: events significant for Nadia's development or near-term encounter may need more detailed actor-world computation, while banal maintenance can be compressed, and already-established routines carried forward. It would be a mistake to spend expensive full-psyche model calls on every toothbrushing or meal, or to turn time compression into “Nadia had a busy day” with no objective causal continuity. The correct computational economy is **dense enough constituted life at the interfaces that matter**, not universal simulation of every moment.

A user's return after one day should therefore encounter a person who had *a day*, even if she has no exciting story. She may have had an argument that altered her relation to a colleague; renewed a practice; forgotten a minor task; felt unusually good after sleeping; received an invitation; missed a bus; made progress at work; watched part of a film; helped her child; read a local report; or simply handled her normal obligations. **The event record can be richer than what she tells the visitor**. She may not remember, care about, volunteer or correctly interpret the very thing the system knows occurred.

This makes the question “How was your day?” an actual question. Not a retrieval request for generated player-oriented content, but an invitation for a particular person to report on the portion of her independent life she presently understands and chooses to share. She can say “fine,” joke, complain, change subject, ask about the user's day, or open a serious conversation. The system may possess a realistic offscreen event history while offering no guarantee of narratively satisfying disclosure.

If no independent agenda, world evolution or differential experience lies beneath that question, the appearance of life is fraudulent. The game becomes fundamentally different when **time itself is a source of new relation-relevant truth, not a gap awaiting the player's next prompt**.

## VI. WHEN THEY ARE TOGETHER, THE WORLD DOES NOT STAND STILL

A second, still stronger implication follows. A static visit in which Nadia waits for the player to address one issue at a time, even with rich dialogue, cannot reproduce the generative pressure of ordinary life. Her activities have their own timing. Other people need things, dishes boil over, the phone rings, a scheduled date approaches, a neighbor knocks, a child asks for help, an employer calls, a plan is canceled, a relative is unexpectedly early. The visitor **arrives inside an event-bearing world**, not a blank room where the NPC simply responds.

The user's examples are exemplary because their power does not rely on melodrama. Nadia starts dinner. Her child cannot understand a homework problem. The pan needs immediate attention. A visitor might help with cooking, work through the problem with the child, ask Nadia what she wants, make matters worse, start an argument about teaching, misread the situation and stay silent, or leave. Nadia is not paused while the player chooses. She can choose to deal with the food, attend to the child, send someone away, decide she wants neither task, or act before the visitor even understands the conflict. The child has their own developing capacities and may resist help. A sibling may call in the middle. *The event is not the offer of a multiple-choice puzzle.* It is an intersection of activities and practical time.

This is why the original D002 rules for medium-native agency become more important. The human can actually explain a mathematical idea, hear a child's mistaken concept, negotiate a division of labor, ask a practical question, notice smell or sound, make a joke, recognize that a pan is too hot, revise an explanation, comfort, apologize or disagree. The interface should not silently convert “I handle dinner and homework” into a successful completion of both activities. A broad goal reveals the actionable state and returns control at the points where human cognition and choice really matter. Mechanical routines can be compressed when their meaningful method has already been established, avoiding tedious micro-action hell.

Even a phone ringing can carry many meanings: intrusion, relief, excitement, fear of a boss, a sibling's expected call, or just a delivery update. But those meanings come from the persons and events involved, not a generic story beat. The visitor may not know why Nadia's posture changed. Nadia herself may not fully know. A small actual interruption can recruit a deep historical relation or have no lasting effect at all. The event's *objective* scale and its *psychic* significance need not correlate.

This converts a text interface's chief limitation into an extraordinary strength. A limited phenomenal sample is necessarily selective, so attention is precious; no amount of text gives the user universal surveillance. Events occur while the player is allocating attention. Their timing and competing demands force them to become participant rather than commentator. A system that presents the child, dinner and phone as menu items after halting causality would lose much of the tension. The engine should allow lawful deliberation without ceasing to represent the material passage of scene time.

A woman preparing for a date while the visitor chats with her is another rich situation. The date is **her project**, not a jealousy event queued for the user's character arc. She may want advice, refuse it, be nervous, be happy, invite the visitor to leave, change her mind for reasons unrelated to the visitor or let the time arrive and depart. The visitor can talk about the relationship, judge the person she plans to meet, offer assistance, keep her company or make her late. The outcome enters her social life whether or not it flatters the user.

**The living world does not need constant spectacle to become engaging. It needs independently consequential interruptions that make participation a practical act with tradeoffs and an afterlife.**

## VII. EVENTFULNESS IS NOT THE SAME AS A DRAMATURGY ENGINE

There is a dangerous shortcut inside the user's proposal: tell the LLM to “make something happen” during each visit or day. That could produce a succession of conveniently timed visitors, disasters, jokes, secrets, confessions and conflicts. It would seem active while violating the strongest Rawlsing law—world happenings must not be curated according to the user's appetite or the platform's desire for retention.

The better construction is **independent event potential governed by person, world, institutions and time**. It can be usefully distinguished into several origins without inventing competing canonical owners. Some events arise from current actor projects and routines: Nadia is baking because she wanted to, not because the user arrived. Others arise from independent nearby persons: a child asks a question, a friend calls, a date comes to the door. Others arise from material processes and ordinary contingency: food burns, a bus is late, an appliance breaks, weather changes a plan. Others depend on verified real public events within the represented external-anchor scope: a transit strike, closure, dangerous weather or relevant policy change. Each origin requires different admission evidence, but all must become **world-owned occurrences** before the renderer is allowed to treat them as facts.

The LLM may legitimately propose concrete possible incidents from existing state, calendar, institutional pressures, actor initiative, material affordances, population base rates and unresolved event frontiers. It may help estimate plausible relations and construct participant-specific continuation hypotheses. **It must not become the sovereign event author** by generating surprising things merely because a scene needs motion or the user has not logged in. A proposed incident is not an occurrence; the competent world/actor resolver must establish that it is possible and that it actually occurred at a lawful time. Each affected actor must then live its share through that actor's own perception, current organization and response.

This suggests a necessary distinction between *scheduled or causally pending*, *ordinary uncertain*, and *externally anchored* event possibilities. A scheduled date can already be in the world before the user knows it. An ordinary interruption might become probable because the meal is being cooked too long. A verified regional power outage may change a building's electrical state whether the player is present or not. A knock at the door from missionaries may be plausible in some neighborhoods and at some times, and the missionaries themselves have real institutional activities in the simulated world. It should not be selected because the player seems religious or argumentative and the product wants an engaging dispute.

The source of an event can be unrelated to the user, even while its *consequence* legitimately depends on the user being there. A visitor's actual assistance might prevent a pan from burning; a refusal might leave Nadia to manage two competing tasks; a user's presence might affect whether a neighbor stays, whether the child asks for help, or whether Nadia shares a private thought. These are prospective consequences of what has happened. The same visitor's mere desire to have a meaningful evening cannot reach backward and make the missionaries arrive or the child receive homework that conveniently tests their expertise.

Non-teleology must also govern the occurrence rate. **No fixed guaranteed drama per visit.** Some evenings are ordinary; people repeat familiar stories; the visitor may be bored; Nadia may have a headache and want to sleep; the child's homework may go smoothly. If the engine introduces an interruption every few turns, the ordinary world becomes a content treadmill. The user is not owed narrative novelty and cannot be kept “addicted” by systematic manufactured stakes. The strongest potential retention mechanism is the person's intrinsically unpredictable but historically intelligible life, not the platform's appetite for continuous engagement.

The difficult computer-science target is not the invention of an “event generator” name. It is joining **qualified event proposal, actor initiative, time, independent world resolution, multiple actor-specific experience, persistent afterlife and truthful user disclosure** through functions Rawlsing already possesses or must extend. A plausible paragraph from an LLM satisfies none of those by itself.

## VIII. THE GAME'S BASIC PLAY UNIT: FINITE ATTENTION AMID COMPETING ACTIVITIES

The last theory called the basic unit “historically situated participation.” The user's live-event examples demand a sharper account of *how that becomes play*. A human in a room cannot attend equally to all things, do every task, speak to every person, solve every problem and satisfy every relation simultaneously. Their attention, time, embodied position, knowledge, social permissions and material resources are finite. Meanwhile other people and processes do not wait for them. **The encounter is an allocation of one's historically formed capacities amid the independent claims of a living situation.**

This is not a universal resource-management score. The real human performs cognition and chooses where to put it. A cooking problem may be materially urgent while an interpersonal question is relationally delicate. Helping the child may mean allowing the meal to go wrong, or trusting Nadia to take the stove, or discovering that Nadia did not actually want help with either. A visitor can decide poorly. The decision may be admirable but unwelcome. Someone else may take the initiative and change the situation before the human acts.

The reason this can be compelling is the interaction of **three ordinary scarcities**: the scarcity of *who one meets* (natal roll), the scarcity of *what one can notice/act upon at once* (situated time), and the scarcity of *what another person is willing or able to share* (relational possibility). None needs a displayed balance. Their actual consequences are sufficient to make a player deliberate, care, make mistakes, laugh and want to try again. A person who misses a family remark while repairing a burner does not earn an “unobservant” penalty; they simply did not hear it. A person who interrupts a child to correct a mathematical error may make the child understand or feel belittled, depending on how the intervention is carried out and received.

There is a further implication: **novelty does not require new characters or locations**. The same kitchen becomes a different activity field on different days. One time Nadia is tired, another she has promised her child something, another her former partner has called, another a worker is fixing the sink, another nothing unusual is happening but a disagreement from last month makes the silence difficult. The relational grammar of a familiar object or room changes as the people and world change. Ordinary scenes are generative because the persons are historical, not because the engine invented five brand-new props.

The text surface makes this relatively feasible. Instead of animating an entire city, it presents a limited, actor-qualified phenomenal field in which objects, other people and their outward conduct can be investigated. The user can direct attention rather than simply select speech dialogue. But the narrator must not label an object at a level the actual visitor's acquired recognition does not justify, tell them what a facial expression “really means” without observable support, or leak the actor's private psychological explanation as omniscient lore. The user should be allowed to notice a smoke smell without automatically knowing Nadia's private fear of ruining dinner.

The activity is demanding precisely because it is neither a reflex arcade game nor a scripted point-and-click puzzle. **The human's meaningful powers are attention, language, memory, interpretation, explanation, negotiation and moral/practical judgment; the world has its own material and social powers.** The encounter occurs where they meet.

## IX. OUTSIDE THE PORTAL, NADIA LIVES; RAWLSING DOES NOT CONTINUE BEING YOU

The original SOROK law already states this asymmetry cleanly: the persona has continuing generated life; the visitor has a persistent local identity and remembered history, but the program must not invent meaningful user speech, desire, work, consent, relationship or apology while the real human is gone. This is not a trivial privacy preference. It is the ontological division that keeps Rawlsing from becoming a conversation between two generated characters after the human leaves.

The visitor's in-world dwelling, already established material resources, ordinary status, nonexpressive scheduled maintenance and legally created commitments may need bookkeeping. If they are outside the portal, Nadia may have a contact route or may not. She can initiate a message, attempt to invite them, become busy, take another social opportunity, interpret an unanswered commitment, or not think about them at all. **Only actual human actions become new expressive or voluntary user acts**. The system cannot rescue a strained relationship by making the absent user autonomously say the right thing, nor can it fabricate a betrayal or a new promise to make the storyline exciting.

The significance of an absence is profoundly relation-specific. A casual acquaintance can spend a month without asking where someone went. A close friend may feel slighted if the user vanished during an actual crisis. Someone awaiting a promised visit may interpret a no-show according to history and their own current concerns. Someone happily occupied with family may feel relief. A user who has never promised daily contact is not guilty because a platform counter says they missed three check-ins.

But the world's day must still happen. Offscreen events need a path into Nadia's **branch-specific** history even when the player cannot view them at the time. On return she may tell the visitor what happened, refuse, abbreviate it, conceal something, have forgotten it, or express a mistaken interpretation. The visitor can ask better questions than “What's next on our quest?” because the person had actual independent time and is not obliged to report it.

The original elastic rendezvous decision poses a real technical tension. Nadia can arrange Friday at seven in the fictional world without requiring the user to open the app at that precise real clock time. A bounded access grace may hold the appointment's outcome **causally pending**, then simulate the meeting at the diegetic timestamp and catch subsequent world history up afterward. That prevents fake tardiness as punishment for real-life nonuse, but creates a need to avoid advancing dependent events past an unresolved appointment. The world may continue where independent; the branch cannot commit contradictory facts about how that very meeting turned out. Once the user plays, cancels or the window expires, history can close and advance.

**The actor must not know about the product's access window.** She knows a meeting occurred or did not. If the user returns late within the allowed real-world window, they are not magically late in her world. If the user truly misses the diegetic commitment under the stated conditions, an actual no-show can matter. A stricter close-sync mode may be offered with clear choice, but it must not be treated as spiritually more virtuous or rewarded with better intimacy.

A small alpha can advance background life **on demand when the human next returns**, using deterministic and causal interval accounting, rather than paying to run continuously while nobody watches. The time elapsed is still fictional time for Nadia. The computation schedule is a service detail; its later execution cannot grant itself retroactive license to tailor Nadia's offscreen day to the returning visitor's present mood.

## X. REAL CURRENT EVENTS ENTER THE WORLD, NOT DIRECTLY HER MIND

The user has added a particularly powerful extension: Nadia should have a life in the actual historical present. She may be aware of news and public events relevant to her region and world, may interpret them according to the person she has become, and may disagree with the real human about things both have encountered. This can make the relationship feel unexpectedly contemporary rather than sealed inside a timeless fiction.

This ambition was already present in SOROK's historical Q-WORLD-001 constitution. It explicitly rejects a complete digital twin. The correct model is **verified external public anchors + synthetic private/local life + context-conditioned learned-world priors**, with different kinds of authority. A real regional transit strike may govern her commute; a generated private workplace may respond under its own simulated obligations; a speculative local anecdote may supply texture only where it does not assert false named current facts.

The essential progression is: an external event occurs; the service verifies its relevant facts and effective time; the declared world anchor and current causal dependencies determine whether it applies; the world receives an actual condition or exposure opportunity; Nadia may or may not encounter information about it through her commute, media, coworkers, family, practice, institution or personal interests; she interprets it through her own acquired needs, commitments, ideologies, fears and concrete current activity; her response becomes one possibility for action, self-interpretation or conversation. **World knowledge does not jump straight into actor knowledge, and actor knowledge does not jump straight into user disclosure.**

Two people can talk about a public event in a way that neither can fake by exchanging prewritten opinions. Perhaps Nadia was actually unable to get to work and the user saw a national article about the strike. The user describes the politics; Nadia cares about who is going to cover her shift; her brother is excited by the strike; a colleague condemns it. Nadia may agree with the user, disagree, be surprised by a detail, have a false belief, or know more about the local practice than the user does. Her interpretation cannot be selected to flatter or confront the user's politics. It must arise from what she has actually experienced and which historical relations the event recruited.

In the strongest case, this creates a shared temporal horizon between actual player and fictional Other. Both can be referring to a real public occurrence in the same calendar period, but from different material positions and different knowledge sources. The real human's external life is still not fully simulated; the fictional person's own private life is not a mirror of a real person's unverifiable biography. **Shared current events can create an intersection of public history without falsely collapsing their two ontologies.**

This is also an expensive currentness and truth obligation. More geographically specific claims require better source authority. The system must not use general LLM knowledge to invent a live election result, law change, strike or named institutional fact. If an external condition became materially relevant during an interval, stale or missing retrieval cannot be treated as evidence it did not happen. If discovered after branch history has already closed, it cannot silently retcon contradictory events. The original Q-WORLD law explicitly recognizes this temporal-integrity problem. Alpha should prefer bounded region/domain currentness, cadence-bounded or event-triggered checks, and real public facts that intersect active represented dependencies. **The goal is being materially present, not being a news chatbot.**

Most news will not matter to Nadia; some will matter greatly without her knowing; some she will debate passionately while its material impact remains modest. That is what a person actually does with a world, rather than what an engagement engine does with trending topics.

## XI. THE ICEBERG MAKES EVENTS BECOME PERSONAL WITHOUT TURNING LIFE INTO A THEME PARK OF SYMBOLS

A continuing life does not become a person merely by accumulating timestamps, and a thousand random incidents can still yield a shallow simulation. The Constitutive Organism locates psychological meaning in historically formed relationships of activity: what bodies can do, what people need, which practices are acquired, how other persons and institutions organize recognition and power, how recurrent experiences recruit defense and desire, and how a lived event changes what future activity means.

The living iceberg is operative before conscious explanation. It can condition what Nadia notices, what an ordinary remark recalls, whether a demand feels tolerable, why a gesture seems loving or insulting, why she is drawn to someone and wary of them simultaneously, which ways of managing contradiction have become habitual, and which new practical actions seem conceivable. She does not choose between private motives like an administrator picking from a file. Her outward response is a partial manifestation of a situated historically constituted person, always under bodily and world conditions.

This matters as much for offscreen life as for conversations. Suppose Nadia repeatedly helps her child with homework. The activity may strengthen actual teaching competence, produce delight, reinforce an identity, exhaust her after work, become a source of frustration, build a family practice, or remain a routine of merely getting through the evening. Another day when the user helps the child, Nadia experiences the act in relation to her own history of that practice and the current network around it. A competent explanation might be welcomed as relief or perceived as a challenge to her authority, or both. The same successful teaching operation can serve several incompatible activities.

The actual psyche can become more contradictory rather than better integrated. A beloved friend's advice can strengthen a harmful norm. A genuinely compassionate motive can be recruited into coercive discipline. A desire for intimacy can sustain a materially restricting relationship. Sexual pleasure may coexist with economic vulnerability without a universal victim script; a person may genuinely prefer an arrangement that family members condemn while retaining serious material conflicts with particular people involved. A meaningful disappointment may produce a new capacity, a defensive inhibition, a reordered recognition claim, no lasting effect, or gradual change years later. **The organism is not a moral progress algorithm.**

The person’s history should also allow banal contingency. A broken appliance need not symbolize her father. A woman's preference for certain shoes may derive from comfort, price, an incidental gift, a practical habit or something she does not presently understand, and any one of these can be psychologically negligible. The actor may have sincere but inaccurate self-explanations. A user can theorize brilliantly and be wrong. A user’s theorizing can itself later become an actor-accessible event changing her self-understanding without retroactively changing her true childhood.

The LLM can help **propose** concrete historical events, psychological associations, acquired practices and bounded changes given the current world/actor state. It cannot grant its proposal the status of performed world fact or native human psychological law. A consequence is admitted by competent typed owners; a separate realization renders only what actor and observer might experience. This is why background life and in-scene interruptions cannot be implemented honestly by one high-temperature prose prompt asking a model to improvise the most touching story.

The engine's inner richness is valuable only insofar as it becomes **operative** through material life, even when the visitor sees little of it. Greater depth must sometimes produce nothing dramatic, a stubborn routine, an unwelcome fact, a missed opportunity, or an obscure relation. The world's resistance to theatrical meaning is one of the strongest ways its person can feel particular.

## XII. OTHER PEOPLE ACT WHILE YOU ARE PRESENT, AND WHILE YOU ARE GONE

The Constitutive Organism's treatment of family triads is not optional decoration. Nadia's household, relatives, companions, former partners, coworkers, religious community, children, employers, friends, rivals and institutions may be constitutive parts of her life. Their relationships with **one another** can matter even when neither Nadia nor the user directly intervenes. A child's understanding of a parent can change after witnessing how another parent is treated; a sibling's recognition can matter differently from a stranger's; a boss's routine authority can reshape a family's material hours.

In the home-and-text encounter, this can generate much of the active gameplay without requiring an enormous traversable city. A friend calls; a child needs assistance; a parent is offended; a coworker offers a ride; someone needs a key; a neighbor knocks; Nadia has a date; a third person has their own agenda and may leave. Each action should be at least sufficiently grounded in that person's represented situation and probable practical activity. Significant third parties need enough independent constitution to be causal contributors, not actorless events whose only function is to make Nadia more interesting to the visitor.

This becomes decisive in morally and sexually unconventional situations. Nadia may enjoy a social or economic role others condemn, may resist something they approve, may be coerced without publicly recognizing or naming it, may sincerely love a relationship that also creates dependence, may change her own desires over time, or may not. Her sister, father, best friend and coworkers cannot all share the same script. Some may approve, object for different reasons, misunderstand, envy, worry about money, take sides, offer help, withdraw support, behave exploitatively themselves, or stay out of it. Their words become actual events if lawfully spoken or reported; their private thoughts remain theirs unless admitted to a perspective that can know them.

The world also contains enforceable material powers. A controlling person can impose harm without the other's consent; voluntary coactivity requires independently authored participation. Enjoyment cannot prove the absence of coercive resource dependency; an institutional label cannot prove suffering; paying for sexual labor does not itself establish a particular person's private experience. The system must distinguish bodies and available exit, ownership/control, actual acts, participant preference, knowledge, pressure, legal/social condition and subjective interpretation. **What matters is what each participant actually does and lives through in the generated circumstances**, not what a visitor or narrator wants the relationship to symbolize.

After extraordinary intensity, ordinary life returns. The child still needs dinner, the job still consumes time, another relative still has opinions and interests, friendships still change, the person's own current experience remains contradictory. This continuing world can make a user's fantasy of total possession fail even if a coercive act succeeds materially. Yet the engine must not manufacture suffering as a guaranteed visible morality lesson or a special dark-content route. The fictional Other's future exceeds the user's narrative appetite and may not provide the answer they would find most satisfying.

**A two-person relation is never only two isolated minds. It is a newly developing relation inside a larger historical network that continues to produce causes.** One of the game's most powerful experiences will be discovering how little of that network the visitor had actually imagined when they first knocked on the door.

## XIII. CLOSENESS, RUPTURE AND THE POSSIBILITY OF AN ETHIC WITHOUT AN ETHICAL WIN CONDITION

The user's game is deliberately simple. More of the person's life may become available: shared meals, ongoing jokes, messages, a private confidence, a family introduction, joint care, mutually recognized conventions, invitations, permitted silence, candid disagreement. Or that world may close: short replies, refusal, boredom, hostility, lost practical contact, an unwanted visitor who is politely managed, a genuine rupture or a long silence that means nothing much. There is no visible relationship score and no guaranteed ladder of secrets.

The live-world design deepens this rather than replaces it. **Access is a changing field of possible shared activities**, not a universal scalar of trust. One visitor can share food but not politics; another may discuss religion openly yet be unwelcome at family meals. The person's own newly acquired organizations may make certain things harder to do with the visitor even as affection grows. Being trusted with a task may be a recognition of competence or a way of unloading labor. A disclosure may express intimacy, self-presentation, anger, a need for help or a strategic deception. A refusal may signify safety rather than mistrust.

The game does not always reward virtuous behavior. A snobbish visitor can bond with a snob. A decent visitor can be rejected by someone intolerant. An exploitative visitor can increase influence and dependency. Two people may passionately agree on something that does harm to others. Kindness can be wrongly understood, and an unjust social norm can be successfully challenged by a visitor whose imported practice is better than local convention. The model must not optimize the actor toward the political or moral outcomes the designers find desirable. **An ethical ambition is not permission to install an ethical result in the other's psyche.**

The hoped-for transformation is deeper and more uncertain. Through particularity, durable consequence and repeated exposure to historically different lives, the user may become less able to reduce a population to a stereotype. A person who initially served only as a social category becomes someone whose humor, job, family and contradictory desires are now familiar; the user's later understanding of the category may change. But a visitor may become more skilled at manipulating people instead. They may enjoy a harsh relationship. They may skip every poor stranger. They may become bored. Rawlsing must allow these results without claiming to have morally educated the user.

The statistical veil will eventually compound that effect if the generator genuinely samples from qualified, diverse human possibilities in ways that reflect unequally distributed material lives. One, three or five seed people cannot demonstrate that population property; nor must any of those seeds be wealthy. What a small alpha **can** test is whether a particular independent person becomes compelling enough that the real human comes back for *that person's life*, rather than to complete an algorithmic task.

And the strongest “loss” remains irreducible: **if this person does not want to know you, you do not get a hidden alternative activity that transforms rejection into victory**. You can attempt repair, preserve a bounded acquaintance, leave, or roll another person when permitted. You cannot erase the existing relation's past while pretending it is the same Nadia. A newly rolled root is another human possibility; an independent fork of the same root is not a save point.

## XIV. THE PORTAL'S ARTIFICE, THE REAL HUMAN, AND THE PERSON WHO IS NOT THERE TO READ THEIR MIND

The portal assigns the visitor a locally coherent but thin material/social position while the human retains the cognition historically formed in their actual life. The person may be regarded as a local neighbor but lacks a genuine local childhood; their known skills, jokes, ideologies and personal history did not arise in the simulated field. The system must never equate a similar wage with having lived the same classed experience, or a locally homologous identity with a real history of citizenship, disability, racialization or family status.

The artifice can create powerful experiential friction. An ordinarily obvious solution fails under assigned economic means. A credential matters differently. A locally common practice is surprising. A relative's authority makes sense to Nadia but not to the visitor. Some of the human's habitual assumptions become visible as historically conditioned by the world they actually came from. Sometimes their unfamiliar way of acting proves preferable or helps others imagine a new practice. That is how the veil becomes a real interactional problem rather than a claim that the human is literally reincarnated.

Language translation must preserve the human's meaningful authorship. An attempted utterance passes through governed reality; local coordinates may require stable mapping and some self-referential facts have no lawful equivalent. The portal is not a truth filter. A visitor can lie, joke, exaggerate, refuse, reveal personal testimony or ask the Other how a local category works. Some translations may quietly support ordinary conversation. At material semantic mismatches, **selectively visible private feedback** should distinguish what the person tried to say from what could actually be expressed, allowing correction or retained foreignness without an elaborate identity-building interface. Nadia does not get to read private translation or system-user-model metadata.

The narrator's access to the human is asymmetrical as well. Rawlsing may form a rich longitudinal account of the human's demonstrated attention, expertise, speech, choices, relationship patterns and practical judgment. It may use that account to make available perception more recognition-faithful and to offer evidence-linked reflection under proper control. It cannot know the real human's private childhood, latent motives or character essence from selected play data, especially when its own world and narration made some actions possible and others invisible. A person's actual choices inside the simulation are conditioned by the assigned role, practical time, available data and experiment/roleplay possibilities.

Nadia, meanwhile, makes her own fallible model of the visitor from the evidence she has actually received in the world. She does not know the user's hidden system profile, the content they privately attended to without visibly acting, or the full external human's psychology. A sibling may form a different view; the visitor may be wrong about both. This creates real reciprocity through partial knowledge. Knowing the other is an ongoing undertaking even when their outward reaction is articulate and apparently transparent.

A shared community introduces one additional layer: a player can know root-Nadia facts from another real user's account, and can put them into a branch conversation. That knowledge does not automatically become Nadia's own memory or an in-world shared discovery. It can surprise, flatter, disturb or bore her when the human actually speaks it. The user has an external real human social life; the generated person has an internal fictional one. They can come into contact through authored participation but never become one omniscient mind.

## XV. THE PLATFORM IS THE THIRD POWER, NOT THE THIRD PERSON IN THE ROOM

A real product chooses when to compute, what to show, how to allocate access, whether the user gets more rolls, how difficult it is to keep a relationship alive, how much data it saves, and what model inferences it makes about the human. The fictional world may be causally coherent while still embodying biased sources, unrealistic psychology, false demographic claims or expensive narrative shortcuts. Source coherence and moving prose cannot be used as proof that the system actually models real human beings.

The owner of the platform must therefore distinguish its own ethical obligations from the fictional possibilities it permits. The user can try to exploit a fictional person; the platform must not turn that exploitation into preprogrammed pornography, domination rewards, trauma spectacle or real-world harmful instruction. A synthetic character can develop naturally coercive or even abusive behaviors; the product must not intensify them because a notification funnel needs attention. A person may wish for the visitor to return; the platform cannot manufacture crisis just because the real human has not opened the app.

Rarity is particularly vulnerable to exploitation. **A real scarce roll**, arising from compute economics and a deliberately limited entitlement, can make commission meaningful. But a product can turn that rarity into artificial prestige, fear of missing out, manipulative countdowns, an expensive second-chance sale or a permanent gamified reward inventory. The user's stated interest in rolling one or two genuinely unchosen people does not authorize those tricks. A community's excitement about new persons should be permitted to arise from its actual relationships and the new possibilities the product truthfully introduces.

The personal-privacy distinction remains severe. The service can retain source-bounded evidence needed for the encounter but may not acquire ownership of the human's actual interiority. User-supplied facts, diegetic role, realized actions, actor-local models, system praxis hypotheses and optional research data require distinct purposes and visibility. The existence of remembered fictional relationships cannot be used to deny user data deletion or export. A generated person need not be called morally disposable to affirm the real human's rights over their account.

The product's most tempting performance measure—“people returned because they are attached”—is insufficient. Return may mean compassion, desire, boredom relief, romantic fantasy, compulsive use, curiosity, cruelty, social discussion or anticipation of future draws. A system can cultivate real attention while still teaching nothing about solidarity. Actual effects on real-world behavior require separate evidence. The product should be designed to give rich opportunities for other-directed practice without claiming the ethical outcome is already guaranteed.

**The platform's task is to make historical causality and independent lives possible inside its artifact, then withdraw from covertly deciding what their relation to the visitor ought to mean.** It remains accountable for honest source, computation, time, privacy and safety—not morally sovereign over the characters' every decision.

## XVI. A REAL RELEASE ARC: ONE, THREE, FIVE, THEN ROLL

The proposed rollout is more than a marketing schedule. Its stages could themselves teach the encounter's ontology **by changing the scope at which contingency becomes available**, while keeping the basic home-and-text experience fixed.

At **one** person, a small community shares an unusually well-developed historical root. They have sufficient time to develop genuinely incompatible impressions and branch histories. The first communal question is not “who will you roll next?” but “what did you experience with her?” A user who loved Nadia and one who was ignored have different claims arising from one root. This establishes that a person exceeds the verdict of one relationship.

At **three**, comparison expands beyond divergence within a root. Some persons are easier for particular visitors to approach; some have different economic conditions, ages, families, cultural commitments and routines. A visitor can discover that what seemed like universal relationship difficulty was partly a specific fit, historical difference or reaction. The small set still does not pretend to be globally representative. The community may notice patterns and differences rather than consume an ever-growing completion catalogue.

At **five**, the bank can develop a recognizable culture of person introductions, stories, disagreements and comparisons. The value of sharing a root becomes clearer: one person's remarkable experience is not the only way that same person can become known. Participants may trade observations without a dashboard revealing hidden truth. Others may become socially famous; the platform should avoid ranking them as collectibles.

Then **Roll** becomes a qualitative change. A user is no longer merely entering the already prepared bank. Their rare authorized action initiates a new fictional birth-history construction, whose outcome is **unchosen**. This person may be initially known to nobody else. Once sufficiently constituted and bank-admissible, the new root can be introduced, discussed and encountered through independent forks by others. The user can genuinely feel that their single action expanded the range of human possibilities available to the Rawlsing community, while remaining unable to say what that person must become.

The personal, social and existential forces reinforce one another: scarcity makes the commitment deliberate; generative consequence makes the action historically meaningful within the product; independence prevents ownership; the person-bank gives others lawful access; divergent branches create community interpretation; that interpretation changes actual real-human knowledge and curiosity; new encounters open additional possible relations. **The social act of bringing a life into the bank is not the same as making that person for oneself.**

This rollout remains a hypothesis. It depends on PYAT and SOROK generation costs, whether arbitrary on-demand roots can meet quality/source constraints, and whether people actually experience the small set as independent persons rather than memorable NPCs. The act may be “roll now, generate and verify” rather than instantaneous full person creation. Pre-generation or batching might be necessary for economic reasons; if so, the product must be accurate about what pressing the button does and what the user can legitimately claim to have commissioned. Do not falsely advertise a sealed precomputed choice as on-demand personal causation.

Do not equate user access with wealth. No person need be financially privileged. The point is that the possible person-world positions themselves represent historically unequal real human lives, not premium aspirational characters and certainly not an audience of rich players. A wide and materially diverse pool is the *destination*, not something to counterfeit with five carefully selected examples.

The ultimate experience is therefore staged but continuous: **first one particular Other; then a culture of differing encounters; then user-commissioned unchosen others; then, if the science and costs permit, a population-distributional encounter with lives most people never spontaneously imagine meeting.** Each stage develops the same person/world/time/relationship ontology rather than replacing the previous game.

## XVII. WHAT SHOULD THE CONSTITUTIVE ORGANISM ACTUALLY BE ABLE TO DO FOR THIS?

NEIGHBOR does not rewrite, issue or assume deficiencies in the independent Constitutive Organism. The November 19 R3 source reports bounded real operations—actor-scoped relations, active/repressed or opposed currents, proposed acquired compositions and sourcefree histories—while identifying gaps in complete relation-conditioned perception, actor-initiated composition, differentiated afterlife of blocked attempts and adult/child generated scientific correspondence. Later live progress must be checked at exact HEAD before any concrete code recommendation. **The positive encounter demand now becomes much more specific**, however, and can be returned to the independent builder without creating a second psychology engine.

First, **life progression must be capable of initiating from the actor and world**, not only from the human's incoming SAY or the simulation's test-fixture injection. Nadia's routine, interests, unfinished jobs, fatigue, appointments, social invitations and established third-party relations must produce eligible actions and changing circumstances during offscreen intervals and within scenes. The user may become part of an event that had a non-user cause; the system must not back-engineer the event from the user's desired interesting evening.

Second, **significant actors must continue at adequate recursive depth**, with permitted compression for incidental figures. A mother, child, partner, close friend or coworker may act according to their own projects and historically acquired orientations. They can be in disagreement without a central controller selecting a group opinion. A source-qualified external event must be allowed to affect several actors in materially different ways, including before any one actor knows what happened.

Third, **the person×event recruitment and afterlife circuit must work in elapsed days as well as portal turns**. A scheduled commitment, boring routine, practical opportunity, blocked act or sudden event can alter a body, a relationship, an object, a learned expectation or no durable psychic part at all. The engine needs honest distinction between performed, attempted, blocked, withheld, imposed and merely possible activities. A single event may participate in several motive-bearing activities and produce conflicting actor-specific outcomes; a low-detail routine can reproduce history without fabricating an exact conversation.

Fourth, **source-authorized event proposal, owner admission and psychodynamic reconstitution must be coupled without creating a privileged drama controller**. A model-generated possible visitor or household mishap is not yet objective truth; event feasibility, timing, participant agency and material effect must be independently resolved. In-scene event arrivals must not violate event ordering, uncompleted actions or other actors' ability to act. The same event grammar should be conceptually reusable across offscreen and onscreen intervals, with different rendering and user-decision demands.

Fifth, **real-world currentness and synthetic private-world formation must stay separate**. The engine should be able to admit a verified public condition, route it through world dependencies, allow actor-specific possible exposure and recruitment, then render only what each individual knows or experiences. A regional strike is not an LLM-written political conversation prompt. A family's reaction is not automatically available to Nadia until it is expressed or otherwise lawfully perceived.

Sixth, **the two populations of history have different sharing rules**. Pre-contact root history may be lazily resolved for everyone subject to canonical constraints, versions and no post-hoc major cause invention. Post-contact user branches remain independently altered. When the user rolls a new root, that generation belongs to the common bank only if its entire pre-contact constitution is admissible and share-safe. A user's relationship history never conditions what the bank's common prior history becomes.

Seventh, **the finite human must remain the author of high-stakes action**. No background player autopilot, no speech or consent invented from absence, no action success assigned by a benevolent summarizer, no omniscient profile fed to Nadia. When cooking and homework genuinely compete, a return of control should let the human explain, choose or refuse. The user can be present but peripheral; the world should not require them to solve the problem.

Eighth, **recognition, desire, sex, harm, institutional pressure and third-party responsibility must emerge from historically organized actual actors**. A person's enjoyment, outward accommodation, material coercion, emotional ambivalence, bodily state and ability to refuse are distinct. The simulation cannot suppress all ugly possibilities because they violate the product's aspirational ethics, or organize them into repeatable exploitative content because they please certain visitors. Network afterlives need actual independent cause, not a family condemnation storyboard.

These are reciprocity demands for existing owner/function comparison, not a new engine specification or assertion that the latest SOROK system fails all eight. The goal is to build the integrated approximation, reusing protected capabilities under their proper authorities, instead of waiting for a perfected universal science or installing a new “neighbor day” omniscient owner.

## XVIII. THE FALSIFIERS: A DAY, A KITCHEN, A FRIEND, A ROLL

The theory is wrong in product practice if the same Nadia seems alive only because the renderer can write long prose. If her missed day consists of an undifferentiated recap whose content was selected to hook the returning visitor, the independence is false. If every visit produces a conveniently dramatic interruption on a predictable cadence, the world is not producing events; the content engine is.

It fails if Nadia freezes when two tasks compete and waits for the visitor to assign everyone their roles; if the child and her mother cease making decisions until the human has answered; if a visitor declares a competent explanation of homework and the child's knowledge silently changes without the explanation being performed and received; if a pot burns regardless of the time, heat and previous action just because it makes a good scene; if the apparent emergency appears solely because the player neglected a daily login.

It fails if a single user's inspection invents a root past that secretly becomes true for all other users because it was emotionally satisfying; if a late-generated childhood “revelation” rewrites already admitted branch behavior; if user B automatically knows something user A discovered without human social transmission; if User A's post-contact intimacy alters User B's Nadia's precontact sexual or romantic preferences. It fails if the branch's fictional world is less causally definite than the human-fan community thinks it is.

It fails if public news streams directly into Nadia's speech even though she never encountered it; if a real-world event is fabricated to stimulate politics; if actual public events affecting her represented work can be disregarded because the service had not fetched the update, while simultaneously claiming current real-world material fidelity. It fails if real current-events conversation produces only ideological agreement or carefully curated moral conflict instead of actor-history-conditioned positions and partial knowledge.

It fails if the scarce roll is just a character pack shopping mechanic; if user-selected compatibility or psychographic targeting controls the supposed birth distribution; if a roller can reroll until wealthy or attractive; if paid rescue unlocks another person's affection; if a root that exists for public sharing was never independently constituted or source-checked. It fails if a one-person alpha claims to represent eight billion birth chances.

It fails if increased relational access always means ethical progress, or cruelty always produces an engineered punishment, or unconventional sexuality always produces a stock victim trajectory. It fails if Nadia's friends and family appear only to reward or chastise the visitor. It fails if a fictional being must be portrayed as literally conscious and suffering for the real human to take their situation seriously.

The positive proof is narrower and more powerful than “people became addicted.” A visitor should be able to return after a day and meet **someone who actually had a day**. They should be able to participate in an unfolding ordinary incident, exercise a meaningful human competence, observe others continue independent action, and later encounter world and psychic consequences that are not a scripted reward. Two users should encounter the same pre-contact person and arrive at different, causally warranted continuations; when they talk about it, their real discussion should change their own understanding without synchronizing the fictional branches. A roll should generate or commission a genuinely unchosen, independently shareable fictional person-root, if and when the economics and technical contract permit.

The project may create extraordinary intrinsic interest through that density of possibility. Whether it does so will depend on the actual running organism, temporality, credibility, pacing and medium-native interaction. **Infinite-feeling life is not obtained by generating a large number of incidents. It is obtained by making even a few incidents participate in a causally persistent plurality of lives.**

## XIX. THE WHOLE: THE UNCHOSEN OTHER AND THE DAY THAT DID NOT WAIT FOR YOU

The user enters Rawlsing and meets an unchosen person. They did not choose the family, period, class position, social world or body into which this person was fictionally born. A source-conditioned generator has constituted a particular possible life, and a historical/developmental organism has made it internally differentiated, contradictory, active and only partly able to explain itself. The visitor is not that person; their own real consciousness enters through a locally plausible, consciously artificial homologous position beside them. The user may attend, joke, ask, help, challenge, practice, misinterpret, negotiate, love, manipulate or leave. The person is neither an assistant assigned to satisfy those acts nor a moral NPC assigned to correct them.

They arrive in a kitchen or hallway already moving through ordinary activity. A phone can ring. A child can need help. Dinner can require attention. A date can approach. Nadia can finish, refuse, change subject or depart. The visitor's cognition and practical choices are causally relevant, but all the world's consequences are not determined by their wish. Each person receives and interprets only their part of what happens. Some changes form durable practices, attachments, conflicts, memories or modes of attention; others do not. Nadia's family and institutions remain her world whether or not the user is romantically or spiritually fascinated with her.

When the visitor goes away, **Nadia has a day**. The world may have actual schedules, material constraints, independent contacts and verified historical-current conditions. Her psyche can be recruited by ordinary activity, constrained or changed through another person's actions, and shaped by how different events enter her multiple projects. The service need not execute 24 hours of model calls continuously to preserve that actual day, but must be able to generate and admit a lawful, historically coherent interval that is not written backward from what will please the visitor when they next return. The human does not acquire a synthetic user-agent acting in their name while absent.

The game becomes recognizable as a game because **the user can change what they are able to do with this person, but cannot choose all the other things that happen while they try**. Increasing access is experienced through particular shared possibilities, not a displayed morality score. A user can make a person love them and still harm them. A user can offer serious care and be rejected. The person may enjoy activities the visitor condemns; her friends and family may respond in historically heterogeneous ways. The world does not guarantee an ethical outcome, and the platform does not route every injury into punishment theater.

The small-alpha release may begin with one, three or five deeply constituted people. Those common roots can support human communities whose members argue about “the same” Nadia because different first impressions and divergent post-contact continuities really occurred. One person's exploration may occasion lawful resolution of previously unspecified pre-contact canon, later shared subject to independent root constraints and branch compatibility; no user's preference can author that common past. Their real-world conversation can expand actual human knowledge without making any branch-Nadia omniscient.

Later, a finite **Roll** makes the human's act of commissioning possible new life tangible. Pressing it may bring a source-conditioned fictional person-root into the bank, someone who might have been born into an entirely different world and whose pre-contact life the roller cannot choose or own. That root can be introduced to other real people, who encounter independent descendants of the same initial person. The roller's rare action is a contribution to the shared range of possible human encounters, not property in the person or permission to direct their future. Ultimately, when the empirical sourcing, developmental machinery and compute can support it, **the distribution of drawn lives should itself reveal the wildly unequal material distribution of human births** without making poorer lives into designated educational episodes.

The original Rawlsing desire remains: historical contingency, specificity and lived contradiction may make an actual visitor see other actual human beings more compassionately. But the medium cannot force that result without destroying the independent Other it is trying to make recognizable. A system of this kind can be gripping because **it doesn't stop being a world when the visitor stops playing**, and because those few moments when the visitor does act may enter somebody else's continuing historical possibilities.

The deepest question is no longer simply “Can a stranger become someone I care about?” or even “Can my participation help constitute their future?” It is:

**What happens when I meet someone whose birth I did not choose, whose past I cannot rewrite, whose day will happen without me, whose future I can sometimes affect, and whose world can become significant to me without ever becoming mine?**

**The kernel in one sentence: Rawlsing draws an unchosen possible human life into a shared, historically constituted world; the real user enters one independent continuation of that life through actual limited participation; the world keeps producing events through bodies, Others, institutions and time whether the user is present or absent; the relationship changes future practical possibilities without authoring the Other's truth; and repeated shared roots and eventually user-commissioned births turn that experience into a real human social encounter with the contingency of lives beyond one's own.**

That can be a game in a kitchen, in the middle of homework, or in a brief text message after a long day. **There is no second game behind Nadia. Her life is the game—and it was already underway before you got there.**
