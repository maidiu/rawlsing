## I. THE STARTING EVENT IS NOT THE VISITOR'S DESIRE TO PLAY

The old premise—an external person enters a portal and gets to know Nadia—answered why the *user* was playing. It did not adequately answer why their local person was at the particular doorway on this particular evening, or why Nadia would be prepared to receive them. That missing historical event is a source of false player sovereignty: the world implicitly permits the visitor to summon the focal person simply because the visitor selected a game.

The correction is not to invent a generic "quest" for meeting Nadia, nor to claim that a complicated backstory is necessary. The correction is to treat **the encounter's occasion as the last, small piece of objectively plausible life before play begins**. Nadia is a historically formed person with actual routines, disposition, neighbors, costs, possessions, obligations, skills, current projects, private relationships and availability. An event happens in that life that can plausibly bring this particular locally homologous neighbor into contact with her. The user subsequently decides what to do with the opportunity that has occurred.

One Nadia is sociable and genuinely invites a neighbor over. Another is private but happens to know how to retrieve keys locked in a car. A third knocks to ask for rice or olive oil before guests arrive. Another smokes on the landing while a neighbor steps outside for air. Another asks someone to hold the door for groceries but has no interest in inviting them in. The opening can be small, embarrassing, funny, utilitarian, reciprocal or frustrating. Its specific form matters because it arises from who she already is **without implying that she exists to create a playable evening**.

The core causal order is:

**already constituted Nadia and world → actor/world-qualified occasion for local contact → actual contact or contact opportunity → only the historically warranted invitation, obligation, curiosity or access → external human enters and authors their own response → one particular continuation of Nadia's life.**

If the source actor would never invite someone she barely knows into her home, the event generator may not simply issue an invitation because the product requires a scene at home. The product can place the first interaction elsewhere, withhold entry, find another lawful temporal opportunity, or produce a brief frustrating encounter. **An encounter route may be generated; the actor's compliance may not.** A compelling product is not exempt from this law at the first frame.

This reshapes the theory's deepest center. Not merely "another's life continues when you leave," but **another's life is also what made it possible for you to meet them in the first place**. Even the threshold must be historically caused.

## II. WHY THE USER KNOCKS IS A WORLD FACT; WHY THE HUMAN CLICKS IS A PRODUCT FACT

There are two motives and they must not be combined. The external person, sitting at a computer, opens Rawlsing because they are curious, invited, interested in knowing someone, or simply choosing to play. The fictional peer they occupy is in the same place, with material conditions commensurate with Nadia's, and has a historically plausible reason to be on a landing, in front of a door or returning to an earlier contact. The first motive is real-world use. The second is an admitted fictional situation.

The portal's minimal diegetic constitution cannot simply say "You decide to visit Nadia" as if a stranger must be willing to host someone and as if the external user had already authored that choice. It can report that the other day a normal event occurred, establish which practical invitation or next-contact possibility is now live, and give the human the action that belongs to them: go, call, decline, say something, approach differently or do nothing. The user can arrive because she explicitly invited them, because they are returning a borrowed item, because they have a reason to knock, because a prior brief encounter created mutual curiosity, or because a spontaneous opportunity is still happening.

This **does not** require assigning the user a detailed fictitious childhood, elaborate relationship history or personality. The local shell can say, for example, "You have lived across from her for a while, though you've barely spoken," without inventing years of acquired attitudes or an actual friendship. Duration may remain unspecified, as the principal proposed. It is not proof the human shares Nadia's real-world formative childhood. The opening is a narrow already-admitted social circumstance, not a hidden autobiography.

A prelude may include minimal incidental interaction already behind the present start frame: "you exchanged a few words on the landing" or "she asked whether you had a little rice." Such a scene belongs explicitly to **the entry constitution**, not to a fabricated history of meaningful user volition. It cannot assert the human chose a political position, disclosed an actual private memory, consented to sex, promised money, formed a friendship, agreed to a significant commitment or voluntarily gave up an appointment. If the scene requires such a decision, start the playable interaction **before** that decision. The human supplies the actual answer, and later events respond.

There is a possible product tension: the principal's lovely sample "she invited you over, you couldn't make it, so she insisted Thursday" presupposes a previous refusal by the user. Its *world form* is excellent. Its *literal assignment of an unplayed personal decision* needs an explicit starting-premise contract or a playable earlier moment. The cleanest form is to render the precursor as present action—Nadia offers an invitation, human responds, the next encounter arises—or to allow a bounded, openly fictional first-use setup that the human accepts as a premise without claiming they personally authored the prior choice. The software must not secretly complete important human decisions just because an opening paragraph needs smoother prose.

This is not pedantry. It is the same no-autopilot rule that keeps the later living world from generating the user's apologies, affection and commitments while the app is closed. It starts at the very first meeting.

## III. THE OPENING SHOULD BE THE ACTOR'S FINAL PRE-ENCOUNTER RECURSION, NOT A MANIPULATIVE GENERATOR'S FIRST REWARD

A fully formed Nadia should not be generated from the scene hook. The person, world, bodily condition, relationships, practices, work and current agenda must be constituted **first**. The possible contact is then constructed from that prior state. The difference is analogous to a real-world distinction between "here is a person who needs a storyline" and "here is an ordinary situation in which this particular person might meet a neighbor."

As a conceptual construction sequence, the historical adult has already emerged from a source-qualified birth/material situation and continued psychologically through a compressed history. Her current day is now situated under the actual world calendar. The renderer/LLM may propose **a late pre-contact bridge event** from her present projects, habits, material affordances, locality, nearby people and ordinary contingency. The event must be chronologically and materially possible; the person herself must have appropriate reasons to speak, invite, request, help, refuse or ignore. The user shell must be socially legible yet thin. Competent world/actor owners—not a scene-writing LLM—admit the event and its consequences. The portal then exposes only the actual beginning that the user is allowed to perceive.

The user called this "the final LLM-generated thing" before the separate generation of her day and events that might happen during the visit. That is a useful **ordering demand**, not a new universal owner for narrative improvisation. An LLM can propose context-sensitive particulars at the end of person construction. It cannot deem an invitation occurred when the underlying Nadia would not offer it, or manufacture a stolen object merely to create a dramatic opening. It cannot rewrite the person to fit the best premise; the premise must be admissible under the person.

A new problem appears because Nadia may have a common pre-contact root shared across users. If the source historical root precedes *any* user contact, then a bridge involving a particular user's thin peer position is no longer universal pre-contact Nadia. It is a **fork-local, pre-first-play transition**, generated from the shared root plus a lawful local shell and admitted world occasion. Its visitor-specific events, including what Nadia actually does in relation to that visitor, may not later be promoted as universal facts for every other branch. Shared childhood, apartment objects and habits remain common. Her invitation to user A is not automatically her invitation to user B. The bridge can vary while her person remains causally constrained by the same root.

The need for a plausible encounter can be a *conditioning criterion on opportunity*, not a conditioning criterion on Nadia's psychology. The system can search or sample from currently lawful contact opportunities and present one that permits meaningful user agency. It must not resample the person, rewrite past events or keep forcing extraordinary coincidences until Nadia becomes convenient. A root with no immediate home-invitation route may still support a hallway greeting, delayed acquaintance or a first scene without entry. Whether the alpha can support such less convenient openings without excessive churn is a product/prototype question; the architecture must not hide the cost by falsifying a character.

The deeper thing being generated is not a **reason for the player to care**. It is **a reason that these two historically situated local persons have occasion to be present to one another at all**.

## IV. THE USER ENTERS AS NADIA'S EXACT SOCIOECONOMIC PEER

The seventh articulation was wrong in a specific way. It softened **exact SES homology at entry** into broad social comparability and then emphasized divergent future finances. The principal's correction is controlling: the visitor enters **in the same socioeconomic status as Nadia. The same.** The thin diegetic position is demographically homologous to her—not a vaguely near-class fictional outsider, not a disguised wealthy spectator, and not a resource-rich observer assigned to consume a poor person's experience.

At the beginning, ordinary money costs are the same for Nadia and the visitor. The same local takeout price is paid from a commensurately constituted material position; its effective sacrifice is **the same relative cost for the same purchase**. If ordering dinner costs the visitor 18% of currently presented purchasing flexibility under the adopted projection, then *at that matched initial state* it costs Nadia the same 18% were she to pay for the identical order from an equivalently established resource position. The user's representation of **their own resources therefore gives them an immediate, accurate practical measure of the economic cost of Nadia's generosity** when she pays for dinner, lends something costly or shares food she purchased. The visitor need not be told "you and Nadia are financially equal." They may notice through what the portal makes them live.

This equivalence is not merely that both inhabit the same price environment, nor merely the same broad class. The local standing must be deliberately constructed to make the actual **ordinary cost burden comparable one-for-one at entry**, consistent with the user's explicit same-SES requirement. The world cannot give Nadia a million dollars while showing the user a fragile bar and claim they are class peers because both live nearby. Nor can the player possess hidden wealth and retain one-sided omnipotent generosity. The economy under the bar must establish the match rather than presenting it theatrically.

The exact computational definition of *SES* remains a design task—income/pay cadence, social standing, property/control, household resources, occupation, local institutional access and purchasing flexibility are not a scalar. But that definitional work **may not dilute the settled product constitution** of same status and equal ordinary purchase burden at entry. If an implementation's financial algebra cannot make that true without overwriting Nadia's pre-contact history or inventing the user's entire household, it must surface the mismatch and make a competent provisional decision—not quietly substitute "somewhat similar class." The current theory does not choose a mathematical denominator for the historical 18% illustration.

Nadia remains a person with actual family, housing, debts, social obligations and material history, while the user's peer shell remains thin. Matching **their entry-time effective material status and costs** does not mean assigning them identical parents, occupations, family biographies or a child simply because Nadia has one. It means the cost of a common ordinary transaction, represented through the same practical resource basis, carries the same burden for both at first contact. Their different historical uses of resources, other people's claims, attachments, shame, generosity or enjoyment may still give that expense different meanings. The user knows **the financial sacrifice**; they do not thereby know Nadia's inner motive or everything she gives up psychologically.

After entry, each side may change through actual independent events. Nadia may be paid, lose work, receive help, incur an expense or care for someone; the user's local finances may be debited through chosen expenses or changed through allowed work and obligations. Future divergences are legitimate. **The match is an initial constitution, not a perpetual synchronization constraint**, and difference later must have actual history. We do not periodically rebalance wallets to preserve the visual effect of empathy. The user may become materially better or worse off than Nadia through what happens next.

Thus the player's first economic act has a remarkable epistemic property: **they can feel the material measure of another's gift because they inhabit the same cost of living and the same initial status**. They are not told to understand what sacrifice means. They are given the material knowledge to recognize it—or to fail to recognize it.

## V. THE BAR IS THE VISITOR'S PHENOMENAL ACCESS TO CLASS, NOT THE WORLD'S CLASS MODEL

The recovered November 15 resources decision must be read through this exact-SES correction. The user wanted material conditions to be lived through, not a separate game of working shifts, managing a ledger or maximizing cash. Exact wage, rent, payment timing, purchase transactions, household obligations and resource rights remain objective generated-world facts. **The bar is an interface translation of that material reality**, a deliberately coarse means of allowing a real human to judge what expenditures mean in a foreign socioeconomic position.

The historic illustrative display was roughly:

> **YOUR RESOURCES**  
> Limited flexibility · Rent approaching · Payday tomorrow  
> Takeout tonight: 18% of the bar.

That is *example copy*, not a received algebraic definition. The original discussion did not settle whether the denominator is all cash, available funds after obligatory expenses, a discretionary projection or some composite. The world must have a truthful and consistent method of producing the projection, such that the bar does not spend fictitious money or forget rent. But the final formula must be decided with the actual accounting owners, not invented by a theory writer.

A common opening event can be a small financial experience **without an explicit tutorial about equivalence**. Nadia orders takeout with the visitor. They see that their offered payment would consume a nontrivial share of their own resources. She pays instead. The user now has the concrete sensory and financial basis for recognizing what her payment cost, at least at matched entry status. They can be grateful, embarrassed, indifferent, eager to repay, suspicious or unmoved. Nadia's motives are equally variable: hospitality, pride, care, custom, embarrassment, status performance, desire, exhaustion, refusal of indebtedness, or convenience. The **world** knows the debit. The **visitor** knows their equivalent burden. The **actor** supplies the historically formed meaning.

This is more powerful than telling the user Nadia is poor, wealthy, generous or struggling. It makes material class **a direct basis of perception and action**. An equally costly act can have drastically different significance depending on how Nadia relates to exchange, autonomy, debt, reputation, need and attachment. The visitor's accurate awareness of cost is not knowledge of her thoughts.

The bar cannot become a relationship currency. Spending more cannot mechanically buy closeness, and Nadia cannot mechanically award good-Humaning points for budgeting wisely. The user can spend resources on themselves, another neighbor, obligations or nothing at all. Nadia can refuse a gift, give one freely or treat an offer as an attempt at control. The service cannot use fictional financial scarcity to sell real-world rolls, access or “rescue” from an engineered crisis.

Work ordinarily continues offscreen through already governed schedules. Wages arrive at appropriate times; rent and payments are truly debited; a shift consumes time and bodily energy. The human visitor must not be autogiven major work decisions while absent, while Nadia can make her own decisions through her independent organism. A resource bar need not stay on screen throughout every conversation; it can appear with a relevant material decision and support further detail when the user asks. Its very usefulness depends on being a small window onto real constraints, not a second progress economy.

The exact SES match at entry may require source-specific adjustment to a generated thin shell. It must not rewrite Nadia's independent life to fit the player. **Generate Nadia first; situate the peer from Nadia's actual SES**. The direction of constraint is unilateral from established actor/world to visitor-shell constitution, never from a psychographic model of the human user to the person's birth/class.

## VI. SAME MATERIAL BURDEN DOES NOT MEAN THE SAME HISTORY, FEELING OR POWER IN EVERY RELATION

Material homology is a real structural equality at the game entrance, but it cannot resolve the deeper impossible-neighbor problem. The human brings a mind formed somewhere else, a body and self-history not literally born in the fictional setting. They may have received excellent schooling, religious formation, professional knowledge, political opinions, economic security or deprivation in their actual life different from this peer's world. The thin local position gives them a place to stand, not an earned local childhood.

Even if the visitor and Nadia initially have the same economic flexibility, they need not have identical households, employment arrangements, adult dependents or obligations to kin. SES is a socially structured relation with many dimensions, not a guarantee that each person has the same memories or uses every asset in the same way. Yet for **the same ordinary purchase at entry**, the product must give them equivalent material cost. It is entirely possible for two people with equal available purchasing power to attach different meanings to spending that money because of different histories of scarcity, trust, pride, debt or care. Those meanings are not priced by the bar.

Similarly, structural peer status does not erase all differences in other kinds of institutional power. A person can have the same SES while differing in actual local gendered risks, legal vulnerability, bodily capacity or network authority. The portal must be honest about which coordinates it has homologized, and its claims must not become "I was born in Nadia's world." The visitor can be placed there in the same class without literally sharing oppression, family formation or migration history.

This is why *cost equivalence* is so useful: it produces a **specific, testable equality of practical burden** without attempting to simulate autobiographical equivalence. A diner may know exactly what paying costs both people. They still do not know whether the person paying wants repayment, is making a romantic overture, feels guilty, seeks power or simply finds offering food ordinary.

The same logic applies in the other direction. Nadia may see a gift as inexpensive while the visitor treats it as sacrificial because their external history taught a different relation to money. The portal provides accurate local possibility; it does not replace the user's actual learned significance system. Their reactions can differ precisely because the actual human is foreign to the local field, even where their resources match.

A wealthy outsider tourism game would let material advantage solve obstacles and convert inequality into spectacle. **The same-SES neighbor mechanism denies that shortcut**. If the user wants to help Nadia materially, it comes from shared constraints, actual time, reciprocal ability and limits. They cannot simply buy the consequences of a different birth away.

## VII. THE FIRST PRELUDE IS AN ENCOUNTER BRIDGE, NOT A FULL PLAYER BIOGRAPHY

The user offered a possible opening:

> You've lived across from Nadia for some time without really talking. The other day, on your way to work, something happened; now you're on your way to her place after work.

That sketch is extraordinarily productive because it compresses world, ordinary routine, structural neighbor proximity, initial social contact and a reason to meet again into a few sentences. It is a **historical bridge**, not a training sequence. It answers “Why this person now?” and “Why would a visit be possible?” with a real local occasion.

What happens in the middle ought to be generated from the particular person's life. A gregarious Nadia may invite the visitor for a meal simply because that fits her hospitality. A private Nadia may help with locked car keys because of a skill learned in her past, then leave without an invitation. A neighbor with family coming over may need a little ingredient and become interested enough to ask the visitor to dinner later. An exhausted worker might exchange a joke while smoking and never pursue it. A proud person may refuse help with a bag but remember the offer. The player's next encounter is thus historically conditioned before dialogue starts.

A generator should not equate apparent opportunity with a guaranteed visit. The product can allow an ordinary brief neighbor interaction to be the full first session. If a later invitation exists, the user can choose to accept or decline. If the human is actually already “headed to Nadia's house,” the world must possess a reason for that movement—an invitation, an errand, an item to return, a direct practical request or a previous event whose social meaning supports contact. If a scene says “you met her before” but invents a whole friendship to justify this, it violates zero-profile thin-shell design.

There are distinct clocks. Nadia's pre-contact life is a shared root; a branch-specific contact bridge can occur at a lawful time shortly before the first portal action; the human present begins when they actually enter/play. Later her day can continue independently, generating additional contingent events. These are not independent “story modules”: they are time-local segments of **one historical event mechanism with different degrees of observer authorship and permitted retroactive completion**.

The original Courts 1–2 correctly warned that random person does not mean random teleportation, and the original concrete first-session spine already used an active grocery scene. The new demand does not discard that. It **elevates first-contact plausibility from a developer-selected representative example into a person-specific generative result**, subject to existing identity, material, event and causal authority boundaries.

That is a considerable new first-consumer challenge to the Constitutive Organism and encounter owners. It does not claim those owners currently lack such a function; their live exact status must be checked before implementation is commissioned.

## VIII. ONE NADIA CAN PLAUSIBLY OPEN SEVERAL DIFFERENT DOORS WITHOUT BECOMING A TEMPLATE

When a person-root can be encountered by multiple independent human users, each has a **branch-local opening**. The same common Nadia, with her prior life and pre-contact dispositions fixed, can meet one neighbor while carrying groceries and another while waiting for the bus. She can be approached differently or initiate differently because dates, contingencies, actual surroundings and users' local shells differ. This is not evidence that she is many random character archetypes. It is how one historical organization can be recruited in distinct situations.

The encounter generator must operate under her existing constitution and cannot overwrite pre-contact canon. If Nadia was given a strict school-day schedule, the first scene cannot casually put her at home during work with no cause. If she is in a highly private household, a casual dinner invitation cannot become the default. If she fears losing her job, the workplace may influence the chance and meaning of a hallway exchange; it should not be reduced to a trait tag. If she has a particular skill, that capacity can be available as a possible practical explanation for help, but the generator must not turn all skills into plot keys.

The shared root may contain material affordances, schedules, existing neighbor relations, household objects and cues that make certain meetings much more plausible than others. Its unknown past details can be lazily materialized under root constraints, but a particular user's curiosity or compatibility profile cannot select the actual historical fact. An opening that creates a pre-contact fact for the branch cannot thereby rewrite already active sibling branches.

This also clarifies the distinction between *having the chance to meet someone* and *having a relationship*. Two branches may share the same initial person but have utterly different first contacts and therefore different expectations before the first substantial visit. One knows Nadia because she helped retrieve keys; another knows her because she asked for rice. Both experiences are genuine within their separate continuities. Neither visitor is entitled to a universal “real Nadia” verdict from the opening alone.

Socially, that makes the common bank more interesting. One person says, “She invited me over immediately”; another: “Really? I talked to her for ten minutes before she had to leave.” The difference may arise from actor context and branching social history, not from deliberately changing her underlying person to flatter someone. It could be more compelling than a common scripted introduction precisely because no person is just the sum of one observed scene.

## IX. THE FIRST ENCOUNTER IS PART OF HER CONTINUING DAY

The late bridge and the live-interruption system must not be two disconnected authoring processes. Nadia has already had part of a day. She may have worked, arranged childcare, started a meal, invited friends, received a call, experienced a transit problem or dealt with an ongoing project. A contact occurs because it fits that day. If the visitor comes over after work, Nadia does not reset to a neutral social state or cease performing the activity that made the contact possible. The house may contain the unfinished meal, the child needing assistance, a cousin arriving soon or the problem with the car whose keys she helped retrieve.

This supplies **natural continuity of consequence** from first hook to first visit to later life. If she borrowed olive oil because guests were coming, the dinner party is an event in her world whether or not the visitor joins. If she invited the neighbor after a surprising helpfulness, that offer reflects an actual actor response to something that happened. If she was smoking outside, the cigarettes, breaks, routine and possible social interpretations may belong to her real history; the scene need not become a morality lesson about smoking. If she is interested in the visitor, that interest must be actor- and event-conditioned. If she is not, there need not be a hidden way to unlock it by replaying the opening.

The user's meaningful activity begins at the first actual decision point. If they accept the invitation, the dinner can proceed under actual availability, food, cost, family and time. If they decline, the fictional person does not pause and wait. If they ask for another day, Nadia may agree or not. If they decide to go home, the neighbor encounter can be brief, and later opportunities remain governed by life. Such a branching opening is not a larger choice-driven RPG; it is a small entry into another person's existing ordinary activity.

The LLM can help propose scene particulars, but event admission and psyche recruitment must be owned by the same world/actor causal machinery as every later scene. A pretty entry paragraph is not proof of a lawful event. The first visit ought to be the strongest early test of how an **existing person can cause an encounter** without becoming a host programmed to receive a visitor.

This mechanism could itself be a compelling part of the roll: the user learns that not only the person's birth and personality are unchosen, but **the circumstances of their introduction are unchosen**. They cannot select an optimum opening by browsing a menu of life scenarios. Their first impression begins in a situation already underway.

## X. A GIFT HAS ITS WEIGHT BEFORE IT HAS ITS MEANING

The cost-equivalent peer position deepens a second original law: relational meaning is derived from material and actor-specific history rather than assigned by object type. Money has objective force, and the resource display may tell the visitor the same purchase would reduce their capacity by the same amount it reduces Nadia's capacity at the matched start. But this does not settle whether an act is tender, strategic, insulting, expected, intimate, controlling, burdensome or nothing special.

Suppose Nadia knocks because she needs a little rice and they have never properly met. The request makes immediate sense in a household already doing something. The flour, olive oil, two cups of rice or can of sour cream is not a generic quest key; it is a particular object with the practical property that her present activity requires it. Whether requesting it feels humiliating or ordinary depends on her prior relationship to asking for help, class conventions, family hospitality, independence, reciprocity and her perception of the neighbor. Whether the visitor gives it depends on actual available supplies and their free human action. The object may never be returned; she may apologize, be grateful, forget it, offer a meal or insist on paying, with no universal “favor → affection” mapping.

The consumer-cost symmetry makes certain social acts especially legible. If Nadia later covers takeout, the visitor has already experienced what it would cost them. The result is not that the game awards an empathy point. It is that the user has the *material knowledge* from which recognition of generosity is possible. They may overlook the significance, or interpret it falsely. Nadia may have her own reasons that differ from the user's reading. The story remains a relation among human activities and objects rather than a scripted lesson in reciprocity.

The same proportionality can govern an offer to lend transport money, pay for a household item, share groceries or bring a meal after work. The match must concern the actual expense burden, not merely a globally displayed price. If the participant pays a cost equal to a major fraction of near-term flexibility, the game cannot describe it as trivial simply because the assistant wants them to behave generously. An actor's true budget need not remain equal after independent transactions; the equality belongs to initial constitution and must evolve through actual histories.

Social relationships mediate the use of economic means. One person's “help” can create debt, control, pride or relief. A gift can be refused for reasons other than mistrust. A person can be financially capable but lack time, legal permission, practical knowledge or social authority. **Equivalent class and money burden do not imply equivalent power over every kind of human relation**. The portal can help the human understand how much a gift costs while leaving its significance in the actual life of the people involved.

## XI. THE RESOURCE BAR SHOULD SOMETIMES TEACH WITHOUT EXPLAINING

The principal's new observation—that the player need never be explicitly told they share Nadia's SES—is a powerful interface instruction. It rejects a tutorial that asks the user to adopt the attitude of an equal. Instead the system places them at materially equal starting conditions and gives them opportunities to *discover the consequences through action*.

The same-SES rule may be explicit in internal constitution and admitted monetary state, but **not necessarily in the first-session narration**. The user does not need to see “YOU ARE NADIA'S ECONOMIC EQUAL.” They might see a takeout option and the proportional cost of purchasing it. Later Nadia pays for the same food. The user now has sufficient ground to recognize its material burden. The UI can make that cost comprehensible without an ideological caption.

This creates a rare form of narrative withholding: a fact is true and operationally represented in the world, but the system may not explicitly label the overarching principle. The visitor can notice it because choices in the world have consequences. Their mind supplies the inference. Or they may not notice, which is itself a legitimate human response; the product must not mechanically force the realization at a designated level.

The bar should remain coarse and contextual so that material experience is neither overwritten by arbitrary bookkeeping nor converted into a resource-optimization challenge. Rent and wages ought to change the state in true time; purchasing ability must be honestly constrained. But work remains an offscreen condition in the default human interface, and the player can ask for greater detail when needed to make a real choice. A repeated income schedule might become a lived expectation rather than a narrated event each day.

There is a difference between withholding *a philosophical explanation* and withholding *material information necessary for consent or feasible action*. The user must be able to know a purchase will consume substantial funds before undertaking it, and the system cannot spring hidden affordability traps solely to make inequality emotionally vivid. Authentic discovery depends on accurate, timely information, not deliberate confusion.

In the best case, the same economic condition creates its own interpretive force. The world doesn't tell the visitor “what Nadia did was generous”; the visitor can know what it cost.

## XII. NADIA IS NOT THE TUTORING PROBLEM, NOR IS TUTORING THE REASON TO MEET HER

SORACLE-CO's example of a user teaching Nadia to help her child was conceptually sound about indirect psychological consequence but carried a misleading implied premise: the relationship seemed to arise because the child required tutoring. The principal's correction is exact. **The default user meets Nadia because she is their neighbor, not because the interface has recruited them for a task.** Helping with homework is one thing that might occur after they have met, not the product's mandatory reason for bringing them together.

The new encounter-bridge theory preserves a useful possibility: some introductions may be **practical**. Nadia may have a skill the neighbor needs, or need an ingredient, assistance with a heavy bag, a car jump, help moving something, or any mundane problem. But practical involvement is a **causally warranted opportunity to meet**, not an assigned mission to fix someone's social or economic life. A person whose history makes socializing plausible might invite a neighbor over for no instrumental reason beyond wanting company. That is fully legitimate; the system must not assume all meaningful encounters require a problem.

Once acquaintance exists, ordinary joint activities can happen. Nadia and the visitor may cook; a child may need help; a date may approach; the neighbor may be asked to attend a family gathering or take a package; or they may simply sit together, say little and enjoy company. The emotional texture of those activities belongs to the people and the situation, not the original hook.

This suggests that the most important property of a first-contact premise is not novelty but **plausible causal continuity with an independent person**. A quick cigarette conversation can be a more authentic opening than a contrived emergency. The absence of a quest is part of why another person can become specific. The visitor wants to go back because they found this particular person worth knowing, not because they were conscripted to repair her household.

The source precedent is clear: original Sorok Court 1/2 already distinguished a randomly unchosen person from random teleportation and favored spatial adjacency, and the first-session example began with groceries already in motion. The new speculative advance is the **individualized prelude generator** that derives the occasion from Nadia's own completed profile and living world rather than choosing a standard illustrative route.

## XIII. ALL THIS BEGINS FROM AN UNCHOSEN POSSIBLE BIRTH

“Roll to meet someone you could have been born as” remains the long horizon, not a motivational tagline attached to a random-character creator. No human chooses the social class, family, nation, institutional environment, religion or bodily possibilities into which they initially arrive. A future source-qualified roll aims to place the user in contact with a fictional person conditioned on the unequal empirical distribution of those circumstances. The model is a possible life, not an undiscovered real survey respondent's consciousness, and the user's hypothetical counterfactual identity cannot be inferred as unique.

The finite alpha may begin with one, then three, then five **richly constituted** seed persons. This says nothing about them being wealthy; it is not a premium-audience fantasy. A very small pool cannot demonstrate worldwide empirical birth probabilities. Its legitimate role is to establish the reality of meeting a particular historically formed Other, sharing a seed-root among multiple users and watching independent continuations emerge.

The later scarce Roll may give an actual human one or two opportunities under a transparent eligibility and compute policy to **commission the existence of a new source-qualified fictional person-root**. That person may then be shareable through the bank and encountered by others through independent branches. The user caused the construction in wall-clock service time, but did not choose the person's fictional conditions of birth, formative history, character, needs or household. The mechanism deliberately connects user involvement with nonownership.

A randomly generated person's **first encounter** can now have its own second-order contingency. The user cannot choose who was born, nor dictate how they happen to meet. A particular work shift, moment on a balcony, lost key, grocery bag, cooking need or friend's arrival may provide the social occasion. The portal lets the human respond to what already happened. That is a richer expression of natal contingency than selecting a demographic profile and then entering the same generic dialogue with everybody.

A person-root must remain epistemically independent of the roller's values, fantasies and desired compatibility. Generation algorithms must not bias birth conditions to flatter the roller; a curiosity-triggered backfill cannot let a user author pre-contact facts. The root's shared history and qualities must meet source/quality criteria before bank admission, and its generation cost and responsiveness remain open engineering issues. No financial pay-to-reroll, erotic-person collecting or reward score should turn unchosen human possibilities into prestige inventory.

The rolling human could truthfully say, "My action initiated this possible life in the bank." They could not truthfully say, "Her identity or future is mine to command."

## XIV. THE SHARED ROOT AND THE BRANCH-LOCAL OPENING MUST NEVER GET CONFUSED

Original SOROK explicitly distinguished a common pre-contact seed-person canon from each user's post-contact continuity. Nadia-A and Nadia-B are counterfactual descendants of one initial fictional historical person, not one networked NPC who learns from all players. Their later memories, preferences, relationships and psychic becoming can diverge because different things happened to them. Neither visitor is authorized to change the other branch by playing.

The new opening event introduces a delicate time boundary. When the common root was generated, Nadia had no particular fictional history with this future visiting player. An initial contact premise may introduce that player as an adjacent local neighbor in the branch's world, with a plausible prior ordinary presence. Its socially consequential interaction—Nadia asking for help or inviting someone—is an occurrence in **that branch**, not a universally shared precontact truth. It must not be retro-resolved as something the original Nadia always did with every other user.

Other precontact details can legitimately be shared when they are independent of the particular user. A visitor's ATTEND to an object may trigger bounded resolution of acquisition history, family ownership or a childhood episode. The newly specified detail must follow existing root constraints and any admissible causal slack, not the user's curiosity, values or branch narrative. A root fact can propagate to compatible later forks where version and closure rules permit; it cannot silently overwrite already admitted active branch history that would have unfolded differently.

A forum or friend group can discuss the same root Nadia and arrive at vastly different descriptions. One user may have been invited to dinner after a hallway conversation; another may have been rebuffed when offering to help; another may have learned about a former lover through an old photograph. Some differences are truly post-fork changes, others different perceptions of common precontact truth. The distinction must survive in provenance even when the participants only dimly understand it.

Real human meta-knowledge adds a further layer. One person may tell a friend what root-Nadia's family is like. That friend can use the knowledge in their own branch conversation. Nadia-B hasn't thereby acquired Nadia-A's after-contact memory or learned that the player saw the same photograph. The spoken fact is a new user-authored event and may strike her as knowing, suspicious, flattering, wrong or irrelevant. Actual human sharing can become part of the encounter without merging fictional minds.

No completion dashboard should show "percentage of Nadia known" or identify the best route for getting invited in. The community may discuss, gossip and argue; the product should not reward optimization as if characters were collectible loot. **The same person has many plausible future relations; the bank's purpose is common origin, not universal conquest.**

## XV. THE WORLD MUST HAVE A DAY BEFORE THE VISIT, AND A DAY AFTER IT

The user earlier proposed that even a one-day absence should be a real interval in Nadia's life. The new entrance proposal completes the temporal symmetry. Nadia had a day that made the meeting possible, a day in which it actually occurs, and another day afterward whether the human returns or not.

This does not mean calling a large model continuously for every second. Rawlsing's user-settled sparse-event hypercompression seeks a scientifically guided artistic facsimile under compute, latency and storage limits. The representation should select meaningful ordinary and exceptional events while compressing ongoing activity, long-term repetition, body, material maintenance and causal residue. A person's daily history can be both rich enough to constrain later life and too compressed to claim it simulated every missed conversation.

Crucially, the hypercompression source describes a selected event as sometimes standing in for many years of ordinary formation and a recurrent institutional exposure, rather than demanding an exact naturalistic event-by-event effect. It also warns against treating every event as trauma, rupture, revelation or a new durable psychological configuration. **The organism can acquire capabilities slowly, reproduce existing organization, or change very little after an intense visit**, while still progressing through a real fictional historical interval.

The background day can contain a shift, pay, rent, chores, sleep, food, child care, contact with friends, repairs, a hobby, access to media, routine practical learning or unexpected interruption. Some need actor-specific recursive reasoning; some can be admitted as coarse, provenance-maintained routine coverage with material effects. The LLM may propose concrete plausible events given existing world/actor state; competent event owners decide which actually occur, and separate models of each person determine their own experience and afterlife. A narrative recap is not an alternative event store.

The visitor does not acquire an autonomous synthetic psychology while absent. The local shell can undergo bounded scheduled material bookkeeping, but the real human must not be made to have political opinions, accept intimacy, promise assistance, or decide to visit Nadia by offscreen autopilot. When the person returns, Nadia can have a new condition, new relations and private thoughts that are not tied to the visitor at all. Their own absence may matter if an actual diegetic promise or pattern makes it matter; closing the app cannot automatically hurt her.

The first prelude is a special application of this general time law, not a supernatural spawning hook. Its role is to select from legally possible prior events the concrete reason that the initial doorway has appeared.

## XVI. EVENTS LIVE WHILE THE HUMAN LOOKS ELSEWHERE

The same world that made the first contact can produce events during the visit. Nadia may be cooking when the doorbell rings, her child needs help with fractions, an employer calls, guests arrive, a meal burns, a relative is unexpectedly early, or she has a date and is getting ready. None of this is **interruption for the sake of player engagement**. Other people and processes have schedules and sources.

This creates the actual game beneath the apparently simple task of hanging out with a neighbor. The human can observe, ask, help, refuse, notice a smell, choose a practical method, share knowledge, laugh, argue or take too long. The world does not freeze every other actor until a menu choice is submitted. Nadia can make decisions before the player resolves what to do. Her child can become frustrated or learn something. A visitor cannot merely write "I solve the homework and save dinner" and have the engine award success; the human's language, attention, explanation and timing must be made causally operative.

The original D002 distinction between medium-native action and represented physical capacity applies. The user can actually explain an algebraic step; their embodied arm strength, kitchen skills or running speed must be resolved through their local shell and world state. If a broad command initiates a task, the interface can expose concrete affordances and return control before claiming the goal succeeded. Routine acts can be compressed when the important decisions are already resolved, avoiding mandatory tedious micromanagement.

Because Nadia's day and the user's limited position are materially real, the moment can be interesting without an installed quest. The child may need attention while the meal costs time and ingredients; the human may have an opportunity to be helpful and misunderstand what help is wanted. If Nadia has planned a date, she may choose to leave even when the human is enjoying a conversation. If a missionary knocks, the actor's own religious or social history may matter but the event must not be summoned because the player enjoys polemics.

No fixed scene quota of interesting incidents. Some evenings are quiet, some warm, some awkward, some distressing. The presence of ordinary boredom is not a software failure. The burden is to ensure **independent causal eventfulness**, not constant narrative stimulation.

## XVII. THE PERSON'S PSYCHE IS OPERATIVE BEFORE AND AFTER EVERY CONTACT

Nadia is not a biography paragraph made to justify the first hook. Her life has formed a dense, contradictory, partly inaccessible psychic iceberg: historically acquired organizations of need, activity, care, desire, memory, recognition, defense, habit, practice, expectation, inhibition, alienation and relation among relations. The Constitutive Organism's core claim is that these are not isolated contents orchestrated by a conscious director. They are changing organizations of activity and world relation that can be recruited, revised or reproduced through life.

The opening event must be generated from that already operative constitution. A Nadia who does not easily initiate contact may still ask for help in a particular genuine need, but she should not be casually rewritten as an extrovert because the system wants to get the player through the door. A warm actor may invite a stranger without any repair problem. An ashamed person may ask awkwardly and then retreat. Someone who values independence can refuse help even when materially constrained. No one-line trait should settle everything, and no single story hook should become the total explanation of the person's adult psychological organization.

The same event can participate in many activities. A request for olive oil might relate to making dinner, hosting friends, preserving a reputation for hospitality, keeping costs down, avoiding conflict with a child, or enjoying a new acquaintance. Those motives can overlap or conflict without needing narrative disclosure. What Nadia outwardly says may obscure or distort the most operative relation. The user cannot obtain an omniscient explanation by typing "why did you do that?"

An exceptional conversation may have no lasting psychic effect. Repeating a mundane practical task may slowly form competence. A visitor can help Nadia become closer to a friend, give her a method she uses in private and then depart, or be profoundly loved by someone who has other overriding obligations. Her connection to the user may strengthen or diminish. **The system must not optimize these outcomes toward closeness or away from it** to prove that the world is or isn't visitor-centered.

The user's game can be wanting to know Nadia without Nadia's personhood existing for that purpose. **One-sided experiential focus, many-sided causal determination** remains the governing asymmetry, now extended backward to the generated reason for first contact.

## XVIII. INDEPENDENT OTHERS MAY CAUSE THE VERY FIRST MEETING

A first-contact generator that only consults Nadia's surface traits would still be too shallow. Many ordinary reasons for knocking arise from a network. Her child wants a missing ingredient; a friend is coming to dinner; a sibling needs a ride; a neighbor has their parcel; a landlord announces repairs; a workday ran late; a spouse has arrived; the bus broke down; a local religious community needs volunteers; someone plans a meal that exceeds available groceries. These actors and material forces can create an occasion without sharing Nadia's motives.

The person who comes over to ask for rice may be Nadia's partner rather than Nadia. The visitor may first see Nadia in relation to a child, coworker, mother or friend. A neighbor can become socially known through somebody else's activity and only afterward speak to the focal person. **First contact is often an event in a network, not a free-standing two-person miracle.**

This enriches the original mandate that significant Others must have independent histories and actor-local perception, including third-party relations that continue when Nadia and the visitor are not present. A child being helped with homework can alter the child's understanding of the visitor or Nadia's relation to the child without increasing direct intimacy with the visitor. A mother who observes how the visitor treats someone may later respond from her own history, not a centrally imposed moral chorus.

The material world can also provide the initial contact without a separate NPC. Someone locked their keys in a car. A bag breaks. The apartment building has a shared entrance. Nadia is out smoking. The internet is down. A delivery is misdirected. Ordinary contingency produces a social intersection; actual person-world organization determines whether it becomes conversation, assistance, irritation or nothing.

No prelude generator can make other actors appear merely to present the user with a sanctioned deed. If Nadia's household would be alone that evening, the fact cannot be contradicted just because an opening with guests seems more lively. The system must preserve exact world state, commitments and actor capacities; uncertainty does not license arbitrary retroactive event invention.

The portal is a narrow aperture onto this field, not the god of it.

## XIX. DESIRE, POWER, SEXUALITY AND THE POSSIBILITY OF UNCOMFORTABLE RELATIONS REMAIN REAL

A system that constructs the first invitation from Nadia's plausible social behavior must not secretly encode a universal respectability standard. She may invite a neighbor for friendship, flirtation, practical need, loneliness, curiosity, spiritual community, erotic interest or a social goal that the player does not approve. She may misrecognize her own motives. She may find the visitor irritating and still invite them because of custom. A manipulative or coercive adult may use access as leverage; a less harmful but intrusive neighbor may learn their presence is unwelcome.

Actual sex, dependence and exploitation are possible in the world where supported by adult actors, bodily conditions, consent, material power and current social relations. The system must distinguish enjoyment, willingness, resources, threats, pressure, genuine love and social interpretations instead of equating unconventional arrangements with trauma or rendering exploitation as a standardized punishment tutorial. A fictional person can enjoy something their family condemns. They can be wrong, oppressed, complicit or free in differing respects. The user can also behave harmfully, including successfully exerting influence, without the engine installing moral correction as a deterministic result.

But the platform cannot use this freedom to commercialize predation or organize synthetic intimacy as an unlocking mechanism. A flirtatious first contact cannot become a programmed guarantee of sex. A harmful act cannot turn into a repeatable entertainment route that erases its world and third-party consequences. The distinction remains between **what a represented person may actually do under their own history** and **what a product may deliberately optimize to extract attention**.

Same material status at entry makes some forms of financial domination harder through simple wealth differences, but it does not erase all possible coercion, network pressure, gendered power, institutional authority or future financial divergence. The human's local money cannot automatically buy Nadia; economic equality does not guarantee emotional equality or ethical goodness.

The scene is not obliged to protect the user's fantasy, nor to punish their wrongness. The whole of Nadia's actual life continues.

## XX. THE PUBLIC WORLD ENTERS THROUGH HISTORY, NOT THROUGH A TOPICAL DIALOGUE BOX

The human and Nadia may live at corresponding historical present times. A qualified real public event can matter to her region, workplace, transport, laws, prices, religious community, family or politics, insofar as the fictional world claims and verifies that real anchor. Her actual private home, neighbors and personal episodes remain generated rather than pretended exact copies of identifiable living people's experiences.

The event chain must preserve: factual external occurrence; world-scope relevance; institutional/material consequence or exposure opportunity; actor's actual sensory/media/social access; actor-specific interpretation and psychological recruitment; outward expression; possible durable effects. Ingesting a real headline does not implant an opinion in Nadia. A bus strike can make her late without her knowing why. Her coworker may tell a partial or mistaken story about what occurred. The visitor may approach with a different public understanding and both can argue about an actual event.

The first encounter may itself be conditioned by public history. A transit disruption leaves people waiting; a local weather event causes neighbors to help each other; an institution closes early; a temporary outage changes an evening's practical plans. Such events require real source and effective-time authority where specific public facts are asserted, and cannot be fabricated for the sake of an exciting first scene. Many places will have no extraordinary current event relevant to the particular encounter; an ordinary borrowing of groceries is not inferior.

The user can feel that Nadia and they occupy a shared real historical present without their interior worlds being synchronized. Differences about politics or religion can alter the relation if they actually speak and act on them. Neither agreement nor confrontation is a product desideratum.

This also preserves the economic aim. Same entry SES means identical local starting material burden for ordinary purchases, but external price and institutional conditions may change that burden prospectively. The resources display must be updated from admitted world facts, not adjusted to generate interpersonal lessons.

## XXI. THE VISITOR IS MODELED, BUT NADIA DOES NOT INHERIT THE SYSTEM'S MODEL OF THEM

The external human's action is meaningful partly because the real human actually thinks, explains and chooses in the interface. Rawlsing may build longitudinal evidence about their attention, competencies, habits, ways of speaking, repeated decisions and relational patterns. It may use that provisional model for more faithful selection of which already available sensory particulars they would notice, or for user-controlled reflection grounded in actual conduct. But the service did not witness their hidden childhood, cannot diagnose their essential personality, and must acknowledge that its own choice of worlds and available responses conditions the very behavior from which it learns.

Nadia has her own learned, actor-local model of the visitor based on what she could actually see or hear and how she has experienced the relationship. She cannot read the user's privately selected ATTEND, internal system hypotheses or external conversation history. A neighbor who met her for the first time because she borrowed rice cannot magically know what another player's Nadia said. The engine may not merge its privileged user profile into her own mind just to make her respond persuasively.

The visitor's real-world knowledge can enter the fictional world through lawful speech. A person who has learned about Nadia from the outside community may mention a true root fact that was never discovered within their branch. That mention may itself become a surprising event. But the external discourse does not become Nadia's precontact memory, and the user does not have to impersonate a perfect local historical peer. Their historically different mind is what produces the interesting contact.

Narration must distinguish objective truth, physically available evidence, perception, acquired recognition, remembered prior observation, inferred interpretation, actor testimony and hidden causes. A text scene cannot supply a secret history simply because the user named an object correctly. The world can know more than the user, and Nadia can know less than both or sincerely believe a false account. That is part of encountering a real Other rather than a search interface.

No dataset of recorded intimacy grants the platform ownership of the real user's private personhood. Deletion/export rights and limits on data use survive any fictional relationship history.

## XXII. ABILITY TO MEET IS NOT A MEASURE OF WHAT THE MEETING DID

SORACLE-CO's challenge remains binding in its most radical form. The visitor can help Nadia acquire a practice, become closer to somebody else, discover a capability or change a private routine that the visitor never observes. Nadia may love the visitor and still choose a more urgent project. The visitor may be transformed while Nadia barely changes, or both may have an intense encounter without lasting psychic afterlife. The latter can be an entirely accurate result even if a player finds it disappointing.

At the same time, the principal specifically prohibits programming Nadia to become more independent simply to validate that thesis. She can become dependent, intensely attached, generous, controlling, affectionate, vulnerable, resistant or uninterested through actual historical conditions. There is no implicit prestige for less contact. The world cannot contain a hidden "autonomy score" any more than it can contain a hidden "intimacy score."

That correction applies to the first meeting. The encounter-prelude generator cannot bias the circumstance toward teaching that the visitor is not important; it also cannot bias it toward making the visitor unusually welcome. A Nadia who naturally invites a new neighbor over should do so; a Nadia who is private should not be transformed. The **user-facing doorway** can be designed for the purpose of meeting her, but her reasons for acting remain hers.

Access changes as actual activity and relationship history change: she may ask, invite, refuse, remember, forget, help, judge, manipulate or love. The practical significance of each act depends on person and context. A good-hearted action may be unwanted. A cruel person may attract another cruel person. A user can improve a friendship while hurting somebody else. The code cannot translate these outcomes into standardized moral ranking.

Thus “good at Humaning” can name the human's own concern with being responsive, attentive, thoughtful and practically competent, not a universal variable that Rawlsing computes. The game can be about wanting Nadia to enjoy spending time with you without the entire simulated world existing to adjudicate that desire.

## XXIII. THE PLATFORM IS STILL THE THIRD POWER IN THE ROOM

The user's interface being Nadia-centered is a legitimate design decision; it is not proof of computational neutrality. Every generative model, source distribution, event schedule, filter, retention policy and economic constraint expresses actual platform power. The system chooses how to present costs, what qualifies as a plausible encounter, how long the user can play, what the AI is permitted to retain, and whether costly generation is rationed. Those decisions affect what the human is capable of knowing and doing.

The new prelude generator raises a subtle risk. If the company uses the user's psychographic profile to design a perfect opening—car keys for practical people, theology at the door for religious arguers, vulnerable children for caregivers—it has already rewritten the independent life as an engagement lure. The generator must derive the occasion from Nadia and her actual world and only a **thin lawful neighbor shell**, not from what would hook the human most. No personality-matched first scene, no tailored drama quota, no "desirable incompatible stranger" optimization.

The same rule applies to economy. The exact-SES match is **an openly authorial constraint** on the *player's entry position*, not a claim that the universe has no designer. It is acceptable because it creates a non-superior local peer perspective while leaving Nadia's independent history intact. But it must be visible in effect and truthful under computation. If the matching operation silently erases Nadia's household obligations or adjusts her resources every time the visitor spends money, it defeats its own aim. Match once at entry on a defined economic basis; thereafter both histories persist.

The platform can allow coercion, manipulation, estrangement and adult sexual desire within a historically represented world without selling those possibilities as special reward modes, and without presenting fictional character suffering as literal real sentience. Real humans might experience love, grief, regret, pleasure or curiosity in the interaction. Their actual experience matters, but it does not prove simulated consciousness or ethical transformation. It must not be leveraged by manufactured guilt, random notification crises, purchasable roll retries or refusal to let a user leave.

The possibility that this game becomes compelling—even hard to stop playing—is a hypothesis about human interest in specific, ongoing, unpredictable lives. It is not a license to optimize addictive engagement. The honest product question is whether real users voluntarily want to return to people who have their own histories, not whether the service can force them to feel responsible for its retention metrics.

## XXIV. TIME, MESSAGING AND LEAVING REMAIN HUMAN CONDITIONS, NOT LOGIN RULES

Nadia's day can continue without the visitor, and their relationship may acquire an asynchronous communication channel if the actual in-world relationship and material means support one. The existence of a text-message interface does not mean every first meeting ends in a phone number, and a person who never invited the neighbor into her home may have no reason to give them a contact route. Some contact channels may arise from local directory, explicit exchanged details, social referral or actual events. A first session can fail to produce ongoing contact and remain a legitimate encounter.

Time in the portal advances through realized action and world events. The real human should not need to attend at an exact wall-clock hour simply because the fictional person made dinner at seven. Original elastic rendezvous allows a bounded real-user access window around a diegetic appointment, with dependent downstream event history held pending until participation, cancellation or expiry resolves it. Nadia knows the event that happened in her world, not the service's grace period. A stricter mode can be available without bonus intimacy or prestige.

Absent users must not be made to speak or consent autonomously. They may miss a genuine promised meeting, but a day away from the app is not a relationship betrayal by product fiat. Actor-originated messages must arise from real world/psyche causes, not reminders triggered by inactivity. The service cannot freeze the entire world's time to preserve a perfectly staged visit nor can it retroactively invent what the user did while offline.

A person can end the first or later conversation, decline further contact, become unreachable, invite somebody else, change their life or die under lawful world causality. These are serious fictional possibilities whose dramatization should not be exploited to hold the human hostage. The user retains the right to leave, delete private data and eventually encounter another unchosen person under actual entitlement rules. A new root is not a do-over that erases the history of the previous relation.

The most honest next contact may be a familiar ordinary scene rather than a big reunion. A person who lent rice can later ask whether it helped. Nadia may have forgotten. The visitor may remember a joke from the landing. Each small event contributes to a particular relationship, or not. **The reason to come back is to see this person again, not to collect the next scene**.

## XXV. THE SOCIAL BANK IS A CULTURE OF DIFFERENT ENCOUNTERS, NOT A CHARACTER-COLLECTION GAME

A finite alpha can introduce one richly historical person, then several more, creating human communities that compare their own independently forked relations. Someone says "I loved Nadia" and another says "she was awful to me." Those reports may arise from different actual interactions with a common root, different attention regimes, later divergent memories and changing social conditions. The disagreement is interesting because neither player's view exhausts that historical possibility.

The person-bank can preserve newly resolved root facts under historical constraints, while per-user branches remain separate. Users may learn metaknowledge through their actual conversations with each other; those facts do not automatically enter the fictional actor's actor-local memory. There is no completion bar for knowing Nadia, no universal best route and no player-controlled mutation of the shared precontact person.

The future Roll gives this culture a new dimension. A real user invests one scarce opportunity into bringing a new fictional person-root into the bank, but neither chooses the person nor owns their future. Others can be introduced to new independent continuations of that same root. The roll creates participatory investment in the *existence of another possible life*, not an entitlement to its attachment. The root's generated encounter-prelude can likewise vary through lawful per-user contexts.

A scarce human invite, a persona introduction and a scarce root-commission roll are separate product actions. A moralized succession warrant must not silently prohibit an alienated user from meeting somebody new, and nothing authorizes selling extra intimacy as a hidden roll fee. The exact quota and compute economics remain unsettled; the alpha cannot pretend its first three or five people represent actual world birth rates.

A public community can be warm, critical, voyeuristic, manipulative or collectively insightful. Rawlsing can avoid rankings and automated completion rewards but cannot manufacture every participant's ethical interpretation. The people using the game remain real humans with their own minds.

The ultimate horizon is contact with an empirically qualified range of birth-unequal possible human histories, each a distinct life rather than a sociological costume. A small home-and-text interface may evoke a wide world if the particular person and their ordinary life are actually causally dense.

## XXVI. THE SHARED SORACLE-CO ↔ NEIGHBOR DEMAND: CONSTITUTE THE OCCASION FROM THE PERSON

This full rearticulation does **not** edit or outrank the parallel Constitutive Organism, nor assert that its live code cannot supply any of these functions. The reciprocal requirement is positive and testable: a historically constituted actor and world must be able to provide **eligible causes of encounter**—actor initiative, time, location, needs, projects, relations, material affordance and ordinary chance—before a user action. The encounter side must then give the human a finite scene and genuine response control without writing its own shadow psychology.

For the same person-root, a coherent opening bridge should be derivable from root history plus a lawful user shell and branch-specific interval. The LLM may propose concrete socially plausible encounters. World and actor owners determine which event actually happens, who knows about it, what invitation exists, and whether the user can enter. The user cannot be autopiloted into the meaningful response that the opening requires. When the first session begins, it should be a continuation of the event, not a reset. A no-invitation outcome remains possible and cannot be forced into friendliness by authorial selection.

Exact entry SES and cost-equivalence need a competent consumer of existing material economics. Nadia's SES is source/actor given; the visitor shell is constructed as exactly homologous to that position, with equal ordinary purchase burden at the starting point. The resources bar is a projection of *existing* financial/material truth and must not own transaction decisions. Later divergence requires actual changes. The retained user intent supplies a strong test: **would the same takeout purchase cost the same fraction of both people's initial resources, and could the human recognize the economic burden of Nadia paying without being told a lesson?**

The psyche/world system must allow multiple simultaneous activities and independent Others, including quiet habitual development, an exceptional no-change event, private transformations, real third-party consequences, sincere desire, harmful dependence and actor-dependent withdrawal or attachment. The first contact does not become a special class of optimistically rewarded event. Its reality is held to the same epistemic, causal and historical standards as later visits.

Public-world conditions can affect a local encounter where source-verified and within scope; the model cannot generate dramatic current events simply to provide a neighbor interaction. Actor knowledge is separate from public facts. User perception, recognized affordances and speech realization must remain limited and truthful. An independently generated person should not be rendered as a compliant assistant simply because the first session must begin.

No new sovereign “intro event engine” or “resources simulator” is justified by this text. Existing G4/SOROKUDA/P4–P6/Mechanisms and Constitutive Organism owners must be consulted against their exact current functions before recommending an extension. The concrete target is a bounded end-to-end first-consumer chain, not another philosophical document standing in for executable behavior.

## XXVII. A NO-DROP DISCIPLINE CAN'T BE JUST MORE PROSE

The principal is right to demand an explicit continuity guarantee. Previous rounds replaced the whole theory and retained earlier versions in Git; the ledger archived major user corrections and source draws. That provides **retrievability**. It did **not** provide a verified proposition-by-proposition proof that every articulated, unchallenged element had been metabolized into every new whole. Rich wording and increasing document length are not proof of semantic no-loss.

The correction is to distinguish **explicitly affirmed or user-settled decisions** from **unopposed but still provisional author interpretations**, **historical source facts**, **open design decisions**, and **claims now superseded or corrected**. Every following mulch pass must reread the *entire active theory* plus the shared continuity matrix, cite actual newly used sources, and produce a small itemized disposition for every previously active requirement: **conserved, transformed, contested, superseded, held, or still open**. A source claim does not become authoritative merely because the user didn't explicitly reject it. A new argument may dismantle an old one, but not silently make it inaccessible.

For this pass, the exact former error is recorded as **SUPERSEDED**: "roughly comparable SES at entry" or "comparable but not necessarily same financial conditions." The controlling requirement is now **EXACT MATCHED SES AND SAME ORDINARY COST BURDEN AT ENTRY**, with independent later change. The new proposal is **OPEN PRODUCT DESIGN / NEW REQUIRED THEORY CONSUMER**: generate the person-plausible contact prelude, without fabricating the human's significant preplay volition. The final Round Seven kernel is **AFFIRMED AND PRESERVED**, now with the encounter's cause added.

This no-drop discipline must also preserve contradictions as contradictions. The thin zero-profile shell and a prewritten prior conversation are in tension; root-precontact sharing and branch-specific first contact need a version boundary; actor initiative and product interest in getting the visitor inside must not be collapsed; same SES and shared expenditure burden require a nontrivial precise economy definition. These require future adjudication by competent owners, not confident poetic closure.

The shared SORACLE-CO/NEIGHBOR record is a reciprocal *reading and no-drop contract*, not a duplicate sovereign theory. Its purpose is to keep the independent person-builder fully aware of what the visitor's first contact now requires while preventing the encounter builder from reducing the person to those needs. It cannot be called fully reconciled until both independent workstreams actually inspect and respond; a posted cross-agent document is a successful handoff publication, not proof of the sibling's adoption or implementation.

## XXVIII. THE HARD FALSIFIERS: CAN THIS PERSON HAVE THIS ENCOUNTER WITHOUT BECOMING THE STORY'S SERVANT?

A weak design could give every visitor a sunny Nadia who magically has dinner ready, a vacant schedule and a reason to be grateful. It could call that the persona-generated prelude because an LLM wrote a colorful paragraph about her. That fails. The first contact must be traceably generated from prior person/world facts; her availability, invitation, skill, cost, obligations and current bodily conditions must constrain the event. A private person may be awkward, refuse help or have no immediate home-entry route. This is not an engagement problem to solve by secretly rewriting the actor.

A generative prelude could also manufacture a prior meaningful user decision—"You promised to cook for her family"—that the real user never made. That fails the real-human authorship constraint. A thin positional setup is permitted; significant speech, commitments, emotional consent and major voluntary acts are not silently permitted. An opening event involving the visitor belongs to their branch, not the shared Nadia root.

An economic shell can falsely claim same SES while paying the same food cost from completely unequal available means. That fails the principal's exact requirement. So does any implementation that simply adjusts a displayed percentage to make gifts seem equally costly while underlying money states differ. At initial contact, the real accounting model must make ordinary identical purchases impose the same burden for both, not merely use the same labels. The entry match must subsequently diverge only through admitted events, not artificial equalization.

A hidden monetary shortcut could grant the user free overtime, ignore rents or regenerate money when needed for a romantic scene. That fails. Another could convert every generous purchase into more invitations from Nadia. That also fails. A relative-cost projection must remain truthful, and Nadia's response belongs to her psychology, not the purchase value.

A world can appear to have activity because a narrator invents a new cliffhanger every few turns; another can be made dull on purpose to demonstrate independence. Both fail nonteleology. An extraordinary event can leave no durable psychic change, and mundane repetition can produce enduring formation; the engine must not impose significance quotas. An actor may love the visitor and withdraw because of other obligations; may learn something from them and later need them less; may be thrilled to spend more time; or may never care. None can be marked as a computationally favored moral result.

A late object-history detail may be shared where precontact and compatible; a branch-specific invitation cannot mutate the shared canonical past. Nadia's independent friend or child cannot become a moral chorus or a mute prop. Actual current events must be source-verified before they shape named public facts and cannot be selected for the player's interest. The actor cannot know hidden system user-model data; the human cannot be shown undisclosed psychic truth as narrator fact.

And a workstream can fail no-drop even while expanding prose: if the next replacement silently loses a settled constraint, it has betrayed its own method. An append-only ledger with old words is not sufficient unless the current theory still makes the surviving commitments operative. The next round must establish the old-to-new disposition, not simply claim "every prior round was mulched."

The constructive test is small: generate a particular Nadia; situate a *truly same-SES neighbor* with equivalent initial purchase burden; from Nadia's own life generate a plausible contact event that does not presume the human's unchosen speech; give the user the real first opportunity to respond; let the same person's world continue through independent others, ordinary material costs and time; permit acceptance, rejection or mundane failure without a forced hook; read back cold history and prove shared-root facts remain unpolluted by branch contact. **This would begin to show one independent life in which the portal's first doorway was not a manufactured gift to its customer.**

## XXIX. THE WHOLE: ANOTHER LIFE IS WHAT MADE THE MEETING POSSIBLE

The user enters Rawlsing because they want to meet somebody. A person-root has been drawn from possible historically unequal human beginnings and constituted through a long, compressed, material and relational life. She has a body, social status, family, habits, workplace, beliefs, private motives, practices, things she enjoys, unresolved contradictions and people whose lives continue independently. She may be called Nadia; in a later roll she might be someone else. She was not created to be the visitor's ideal host.

At the threshold, the visitor is constituted as **her exact socioeconomic and demographically homologous peer**. They have a locally plausible position, share the same ordinary material cost burden at the starting instant and retain the real user's external mind. They need not know the equivalence has been deliberately created. The resource display, if a relevant cost arises, can show them exactly how much the equivalent purchase would diminish their own means. If Nadia pays, the visitor knows the direct monetary sacrifice because the cost is the same for her. They do not know what she means by paying.

Why do they meet? Not because the player clicked “Begin” and a woman was waiting. An event arose from Nadia's life. Perhaps she needed rice before guests arrived. Perhaps she knew how to retrieve keys locked in a car. Perhaps she invited a neighbor for supper because she genuinely is outgoing. Perhaps they exchanged a few words while she smoked outside. Perhaps the first event is merely her struggling with groceries, and she never invites the visitor in. The event is lawfully generated from her constitution and world; the user's significant response remains theirs.

The player may approach, speak, attend, help, refuse, ask an awkward question, make a joke or leave. Nadia may want company or be busy. Her child may be there; the dinner may be cooking; a phone may ring; a date may be coming up; the rent may be due. The user enters an ordinary world already in motion. The world does not freeze to celebrate their participation, and it does not penalize them for failing a universal kindness test.

When the visitor leaves, Nadia's day continues. Her employer, friends, household, body and institutions produce further causes. She may remember a remark, not care, change something privately, bond with another person, develop a skill, grow exhausted, fall in love, become dependent, withdraw or remain much the same. Some of these consequences might be effects of the encounter; others are independent. They need not be visible to the human for the fictional history to remain true.

A shared person-root lets different users encounter independent branch lives and compare how particular relations unfold. The future scarce Roll may allow a human to commission another unchosen source-conditioned fictional life into that bank, which remains independently shareable and unowned. Over sufficient empirically defensible scale, that distribution may make the inequalities of birth palpable without treating each particular poor or privileged person as a moral case study.

The portal is deliberately about getting to know one person. The world is not organized to make that person want to know the visitor, nor to make her reject them in order to demonstrate independence. **Their possible relationship is a historically situated event, not a progression rule or an anti-progression rule**.

The new full kernel can now be stated:

**An unchosen, psychologically constituted fictional person lives inside a historical world. A real human is placed beside her as an exact socioeconomic and demographically homologous neighbor, with the same starting burden of ordinary material costs but a mind formed elsewhere. A person-and-world-generated event gives them a lawful occasion to meet; the human authors their own participation; her response is hers. Both lives continue within independent networks, resources, institutions and time. The effects of their meeting may be intimate, indirect, mundane, transformative or absent, and no system reward function may bend the person's life toward or away from the visitor. Shared roots and eventual scarce rolls expand the range of such encounters without granting ownership over anyone's history.**

There is a game because the visitor wants to know Nadia.

There is a world because Nadia does not exist to be known.

**And now even the reason the visitor first knocked on her door belongs to that world—not to the game designer's need for a tutorial.**
