## I. THE TWO LIVES AT THE VEIL

Rawlsing begins with a contradiction more fertile than an instruction to empathize. A human being, already made through one actual history of bodies, labor, institutions, language, care, struggle and relation, enters a fictional social world whose material conditions have already helped produce another particular life. The human has not lived that Other's childhood, learned their practices, endured their dependencies, internalized their authorities or accumulated their contradictory expectations. They cannot simply choose to become them. Yet the world may place the human where those conditions have practical force upon their own actions, time, resources, safety, claims and possibilities.

**The Other is one historically constituted answer to what a world can make of a person. The human is a person already constituted elsewhere, confronting that world's demands without magically acquiring its corresponding life history. Their ongoing relation is Rawlsing's playable veil.**

This is not the orthodox thought experiment of an abstract chooser stripped of particularity. No actual human can erase their habitus by pressing ENTER. Nor is it a tourism fantasy in which a fully resourced outsider visits deprivation, selects charitable options and returns with moral enlightenment. It is potentially a more exact experiment: **what happens to the habitual judgments of a person when the material conditions that once made those judgments seem obvious are no longer theirs to command?**

The original SOROK courts call a particularly powerful candidate the *homologous peer*: a participant structurally local but developmentally foreign. That formulation is intentionally an **impossible subject**, not a false claim that the real user would have become exactly this subject if born there. The person might be socially legible as a peer, have an approximately comparable level of material leverage, and face many of the same institutional routes and limitations as the Other—while still carrying beliefs, emotional habits, languages, assumptions about privacy, independence, religion, money, authority, obligation and care produced by the user's actual prior world.

Such a player may discover their own social formation by its persistence after the surrounding conditions change. Their assumptions become strange to them precisely because they do not disappear. One who believes that adults should simply state their needs explicitly must now deal with an actual work schedule, an unequal household relation, fear of sanctions, a specific social convention, scarcity and the person who will bear the consequence. The proposition might survive. It might fail. The local convention might be oppressive. A third practice might emerge. The engine does not adjudicate the verdict in advance.

This tension creates a strong alternative to merely telling the user that another person is not an extension of themselves. The game can make their interpretive and practical self-sufficiency fail *in particulars* while leaving them free to learn, disagree, organize, resist or walk away.

The homologous-peer form remains **a promising researched main-route candidate, not the sole metaphysically authorized embodiment**. Obvious-outsider contact, thinner self-anchored emplacement, dual-world portal, more tightly real-time contact and other lawful modes expose different distortions. The final experience must preserve the thing they are competing to realize: **the person who chooses is the actual human; the world in which they choose is not sovereignly theirs; the Other has their own history; encounter changes actual possibilities.**

The encounter begins with an asymmetry. It need not end with psychological sameness. The human enters the world's history late. They may eventually acquire genuine new practices, knowledge, relations, commitments, misrecognitions, habits and competencies through participation. They are not being turned into an authentic native by cultural XP. They are becoming someone who has actually *participated in this additional history*. What that does to the real person outside the simulation remains a question of evidence, not something the platform gets to pronounce.

## II. NEIGHBOR IS NOT AN INTERFACE BETWEEN TWO FINISHED SUBJECTS

The preceding formulation could still conceal a mistake: two completed entities encounter one another and thereafter exchange information. In Rawlsing, that is too static.

The Other's psychological constitution is ongoing. It is formed through care received and given, embodied need, activity, work, dependence, institutional authority, objects, practical projects, pleasures, disappointments, moral claims, coercions and relations whose forms themselves become operative in later life. The Other does not merely store a collection of dispositions which a scene activates. **Historical participation forms changing organizations through which a new event can acquire significance, in which one acquired commitment can transform the function of another, and from which an actor attempts, inhibits, refuses, misperceives, improvises and develops.**

The human does not become a second fully generated person merely because Rawlsing learns about them. They author their own meaningful present contributions. Yet they too accumulate an in-world history of activity, acquired routines, learned recognition, commitments and relationships. Their practical possibilities develop. Rawlsing's model of their participation may develop. Nadia's actor-local understanding of them may develop differently. The actual human may be affected beyond the game in ways the simulation cannot automatically know.

Thus NEIGHBOR cannot be “the layer that displays a completed persona.” It is one of the **historical conditions under which a computationally constituted Other may continue to become**—and the medium through which the human exercises a real, limited contribution to that history.

This does not make the visitor the Other's formative destiny. Nadia was already living; after the visitor arrives she may learn from them, reject them, admire them, exploit them, become attached, be irritated, not hear them, acquire an object or practice, or continue elsewhere. A user can articulate something that later changes a person's self-interpretation without having been the secret missing cause of that person's whole prior life.

The stronger unit is not an action turn or a character sheet, but **participation in a continuing material/social activity whose consequences are differently lived and may return as altered possibility**. The action can be small. The social process it enters can be enormous. Not every experience creates a new psychic organization; some reproduce the old one, some disturb it, some pass without lasting owner-level effect, and others become salient only much later.

The construction must allow the human to be an effective historical participant without arranging history so that the human is necessary.

## III. ONE WORLD IS SHARED; ITS MEANINGS ARE NOT

A world is not a narration collectively agreed to by the characters. A cup is broken or not broken. Someone missed a shift or did not. A child has been fed or remains hungry. The law of an institution is in force or not, at a represented time and place. A letter was sent; whether it was delivered, opened, read, understood or believed is another matter.

Yet each person's relation to those things is historically mediated. What one person intended is not what another understood. What the institution calls discipline is not thereby protection; what a worker recognizes as a favor may also be coercive dependence. An actor can sincerely misremember, intentionally conceal, act from a defensively organized relation, or transform their own understanding of what happened without changing the objective event.

The critical discovery is not merely that people hold different opinions about one event. **A shared practical relation can be established before the participants agree what that relation means.**

Two people may develop a standing routine of lending tools. One experiences it as an ordinary reciprocal neighborly practice. Another experiences it as intimacy. A third person understands it as a hierarchy in which favors create obligations. The routine exists; it changes who can reach what, when, and with whose expectation. None of those interpretations automatically becomes the *objective meaning* of the practice. Later disagreement can expose that divergence. The participants may then renegotiate, withdraw, comply, resent or continue without ever resolving the mismatch.

This gives relationship a structure richer than a dyadic score. **Shared history, objective access, privately learned meanings, actor models of one another, models of those models, and third-party/institutional consequences can develop asymmetrically.** A person can be trusted with a key but not with a confession. Love can coexist with resentment. Increased intimacy can produce a stronger right to refuse. Compliance can express affection, fear, economic necessity or a habit of self-erasure. Apparent agreement can conceal severe material inequality.

The relation exceeds either person's current understanding, but it is not a mystical third consciousness. It is their differently experienced and objectively consequential history of practical coordination, conflict, access, obligations, symbols and altered opportunity.

The user does not have to be morally right about Nadia. Nadia does not have to be right about the user. The world does not have to validate either's self-narrative. **Their fallibility is what makes an actual relation possible**, provided the engine can maintain causal coherence without confusing belief with fact.

## IV. THE HUMAN'S AGENCY MUST BE REAL WHERE THE MEDIUM CAN CARRY IT

The human is not a programmable character, but neither are they merely a spectator selecting narrative intentions. A text-based interactive world offers an unusual kind of *real* performance. The human can actually pay attention, infer, remember, reason, explain, write, question, joke, judge, argue, choose timing, refuse, apologize, negotiate and construct meanings using their own mind and words. These actions are not stage directions for a narrator to invent; they are the user's direct contribution to the event.

The difference becomes sharp when a user writes “I help the child understand fractions,” “I persuade Nadia,” or “I make her feel better.” The world can recognize a proposed purpose, but it cannot instantiate teaching, persuasion or comfort as accomplished effects. What must actually be explained? What did the child misunderstand? What example does the human choose? How does the actor hear it? Why would it matter to them? The real work of the medium-native capacity belongs to the human. A model may scaffold the act without replacing the person's meaningful cognitive or relational choice.

Yet making the user spell out every footstep, plate washed or grocery bag carried would destroy the very life Rawlsing seeks to represent. Ordinary procedure can be competently compressed. The important distinction is not between detailed and brief prompts; **the prompt's verbosity is not evidence of the actor's conscientiousness, power or moral worth**.

The deeper rule is that **the system may carry execution across routine steps, but it must make material reality available and return the question to the human wherever continuing would require inventing a consequential human judgment**.

Often a broad action should be treated as *entering* a practical situation rather than completing it. “I help with the groceries” may produce a view of the bags, stairway, tired worker, fragile objects, approaching rain, available carrying capacity and competing needs. Only then does the human discover what help could mean. Do they ask? Take the heavy cases? Touch an unknown private object? Carry one load? Decide not to intervene? A real world has to become specific enough for a decision worth making.

As the participants acquire local routines and knowledge, the same action may be more compressible next time. They no longer need to ask which shelf holds the cups. They have become differently competent. But acquired routine does not turn a prior meaningful choice into the engine's new right to make an unrelated one.

This is an especially strong way for NEIGHBOR to be *gamified* without conventional game objectives. The user's actual cognition and practical judgment can matter; the world offers resistance; other people respond differently; acquired history changes the possibilities of the next contribution. Genuine play resides not in a goodness score, but in **the open difficulty and pleasure of learning how to act in a particular life**.

A silence can matter. So can a stupid joke that lands unexpectedly well, a misjudged offer of help, a complicated shared task, an unanswered question or a completely uneventful minute that later makes conversation easier. A technically perfect menu of three prosocial options would provide less of the human.

## V. THE WORLD IS MADE OF OTHER PEOPLE'S WORK BEFORE IT IS MADE OF CLUES

The material world cannot be generated as a gallery of psychological symbols and then assigned a prestigious social analysis. Its history is the outcome and condition of human activity. People produce food, care, institutions, tools, dwellings, obligations, ideas and cultural forms; these things can acquire value and power beyond their makers, and in turn condition other people's activities.

A bus route, an employer's shift rule, a building stairway, a household's distribution of unpaid care, a legal restriction, an object purchased on credit, a shared religious practice, an uneven wage, an exhausting commute or a family obligation may matter far more to an encounter than any dramatic confession. **Time, power, access, dependence and what is practically possible are not optional environmental flavor.** They are among the conditions that make a form of care, autonomy, fear, sincerity, hospitality or refusal feasible and meaningful.

The material field also refuses an easy moral relativism. A pastor's authorized ritual of rebuke may be received by followers as loving discipline, as necessary compliance for a wage-dependent family, as humiliation, as righteous cruelty, as hypocrisy or as a call to imitate the authority. Those are actor-local meanings whose origins must be historically explained. They do not cancel whatever objective injury occurred. Institutions can become active in an actor's psyche without being reducible to personal beliefs about them.

The apparent gift of a refrigerator, money, an hour of childcare, an invitation home or a key is not intrinsically trust, intimacy, control or generosity. It may materially redistribute capacity. It may be experienced as a gift, obligation, humiliation, domination, affection, indifference or something not yet nameable. A person-specific relation to objects and signs develops through actual histories of labor, scarcity, use, loss, exchange and recognition; the same outward act can become something different with different people.

For NEIGHBOR this means a difficult but liberating obligation: **what a thing is and what happened with it are settled by its world; what it becomes for particular people is conditioned through their own historical organization**. The interface cannot replace that relation with a universal meaning dictionary. The organism cannot replace the material and institutional conditions with a generic psychological preference. The user cannot declare an act of help beneficial merely because they meant well.

If this is absent, the experience will reproduce the familiar fantasy of sovereign benevolence: the well-intentioned visitor acts, and the system confirms they have done good. If the real structure is allowed to bite, the human may discover that aid can increase dependency, that refusing a request may protect someone, or that a relationship can be sustaining without producing immediate happiness.

Nor can the contrary be guaranteed. Sometimes a simple favor really helps. Love and solidarity must not be made impossible to prove that otherness is difficult.

## VI. PERCEPTION IS A HISTORY, NOT A CAMERA

A person is never merely exposed to a complete neutral inventory and then invited to interpret it. Their acquired organization helps condition what they notice, anticipate, distinguish, recognize, fear, ignore and seek. The human's current attention does not arrive tabula rasa; neither does Nadia's. That makes **perception itself one site of historical production and possible change**.

For the human, Rawlsing deliberately learns attention and recognition patterns from engagement. A user repeatedly looks at floors, work tools, signs of exhaustion, expensive clothes, public books, childcare duties, religious symbols or other people's faces. They sometimes demonstrate recognition independently, correct a description, seek additional detail, or return to an unanswered question. A model can learn slowly from these patterns, with context and contradiction, and become capable of presenting the same independently existing world through a more specifically user-shaped phenomenal field.

This is not a decorative accessibility feature. It is one of Rawlsing's primary recursive mechanisms. **The person's experience of the Other can increasingly bear the shape of what that person already knows how to notice.**

But the system is not a passive witness. It decides which finite part of a possibly enormous scene becomes available in words. The four planes must remain distinguishable: what objectively exists, what could be perceived, what the narrator actually surfaced, and what the human deliberately attended to or acquired. A user cannot fail to notice a fact never made available. Nor can a word supplied by the narrator be later counted as proof that the user recognized it independently.

This exposes a significant danger: a model predicts an attention habit, disproportionately narrates compatible details, observes the user's responses to those details, and then treats that exposure-conditioned behavior as independent evidence of its original prediction. **A convincing user model can manufacture its own apparent confirmation.**

The original research first defended a stringent firewall against semantic personalization; later work correctly refined that into a distinction: **attention-conditioned phenomenal weighting is legitimate; user-conditioned reality and engagement-directed dramaturgy are not**. The user-specific prior can influence marginal, actually perceptible detail, while gross physical facts and necessary orientation remain common and resistant. Neutral sampling, unavoidable events, unexpected particulars, explicit ATTEND and the ability to explore away from learned habits prevent the field from becoming a closed prediction.

The narrator need not describe every object, and cannot reveal another mind. It should present a gross scene sufficient for embodiment, some ordinary unprivileged detail, and further particulars genuinely warranted by current sensory access and the person's acquired perceptual repertoire. A Vitamix may be a blender to one human, a recognized make or model to another; a printed label may be readable yet not understood. The same person can change their recognition by learning it inside the game. **The system can adapt because the human really has learned—not because canonical truth became a direct injection into their consciousness.**

Nadia's side is different. Her body and organization condition what she actually receives, not merely what the human narrator presents. She can hear footsteps while facing away without knowing the visitor's gaze. She may notice, misrecognize, fail to perceive, or be absorbed by her own work. Her habitual expectations can condition what an utterance seems to mean to her, and the effect may occur without a conscious autobiographical explanation. Her own private later memory need not match a public event.

The strongest game action in this domain is not “look harder until you uncover the true character.” It is **ATTEND beyond what one's settled perceptual organization makes easy**. Attention can become an intentional departure from habit: what else exists here that I have not learned to notice? That deliberate movement may alter what is known, later interpretation, future attention and the user's model of the Other. There is no view from nowhere at the end. There is only a potentially more generous and more accurate, yet still limited, historical perspective.

This is not just an epistemological motto. It changes how the human navigates the world, what happens while they look, and what kinds of relationship they can form. Because attention consumes situated time and can have outward signs, the user may themselves become observable in the act of observing.

## VII. THE USER IS MODELED; THE USER IS NOT AUTHORED

The human makes meaningful choices. Rawlsing does indeed interpret them. This is a positive, constitutionally central capability, not an intrusion invented by some later analytics team.

The system may maintain a rich, context-bound **model of user engagement and praxis**. It can discover recurring attention choices, areas of expertise, established world-specific routines, memory for commitments, responses to another's anger or distance, habitual interpretive shortcuts, attempts at repair, recurring ways of using explanation and practical help, and apparent changes in these patterns. LLMs can hypothesize broader organization, contradictions and possible reasons, but a hypothesis about a hidden motive is not a verified fact. The user can misstate their own motivations; the machine can misunderstand them; both can change.

This model must be able to inform the user-facing experience in warranted ways: recognition, perceptual weighting, presentation granularity, contextual continuity, and, where an explicitly authorized reflective feature exists, evidence-based retrospective understanding. It should never be restricted to a decorative profile card while its actual use is prohibited. Equally, neither user engagement analytics nor inferred psychological taste may be allowed to write the Other's private life or make the world produce the drama most likely to retain this particular user.

A second model develops within Nadia. It concerns *this* human as Nadia has encountered them through available speech, expression, conduct, shared routines, hearsay, conflict and memory. Nadia can infer affection where none was intended, miss an actual act of care, believe a false promise, discover reliability, feel judged by a silence or use her own social categories to misrecognize the visitor. These are **actor-owned** beliefs and expectations situated in her own developmental history, not outputs copied from Rawlsing's platform model of the human.

The same user pattern can therefore be represented in more than one way:
- objective in-world occurrences and commitments;
- what Rawlsing observed about the human's interface participation;
- what that human was actually shown and chose to attend to;
- system hypotheses about patterns of participation;
- Nadia's perceptual receipt and actor-local interpretation;
- other actors' independent models of the same human;
- the user's own beliefs about how Nadia sees them.

These are not an invitation to build seven competing user psychologies. They are necessary **noncollapses** in what can be claimed from the same history. They permit a profoundly specific relationship in which both participants think they understand the other, both partly do, and both can remain wrong.

The fact that the system observes an action does not mean it can infer the actual human's external biography or hidden essence. A claim to speak Arabic, play piano or possess a medical qualification may be admitted as a run-bound assertion or tested within game affordances; Rawlsing cannot thereby prove it is true of the person outside the simulation. Conversely, compelling elaborate self-authentication would make the world a compliance apparatus. The better incentive for honesty comes from the increasingly specific consequences of engaging as oneself, not from a detector that announces sincerity.

The model is necessarily **co-produced by the encounter**: by what the human was offered, shown and prevented from doing, by their assigned material constraints, by the active response of the Other, and by the interface's own sampling. If Rawlsing learns that the human never attended to a child's difficulty, it must first know whether that difficulty was perceptible or ever narrated. If a translated player has no money to give, that is not evidence of real-world stinginess. If someone acts differently in a fictional role, the observation cannot be silently promoted to a diagnosis of their outside life.

In short, **the machine may interpret and model the human from their actual participation; it may not impersonate their next meaningful decision or declare its provisional interpretation their true essence**. This is one of the conditions under which Rawlsing could genuinely give the human back some knowledge of how they approach other people.

## VIII. THE PLATFORM'S POWER TO KNOW MUST NOT BECOME POWER TO CAPTURE

This produces a contradiction deeper than a privacy setting. To make human attentional and relational habits legible, Rawlsing must remember things about real participants. The richer the experience, the more precise its possible evidence about them. The same machinery could be turned into psychological targeting, retention manipulation, personality labeling, moral sorting or commercialization. The technology's capacity for recognition is inseparable from a capacity for domination unless that distinction is actively maintained.

The original privacy constitution's strongest move is not “do not profile users.” It is **preserve the actual relationship history while minimizing the dossier of external-human assertions and controlling what interpretations may do**.

The platform needs enough information to preserve player-world facts, actual commitments, attention exposure, actor models and longitudinal praxis. It does not thereby receive an unrestricted right to infer private identity, psychiatric illness, hidden sexuality, real-world political allegiance, economic status or external relationship history. Evidence of a repeated action remains a scoped observation; a proposed explanatory structure remains revisable and uncertain. Optional real-world facts have declared purposes, and a user can withhold what is not necessary for a particular mode.

The system-level model of the user is **not Nadia's knowledge**. Neither is the research plane. One actor's model of the human is not automatically another actor's reputation. The product can learn about ordinary human behavior across users for future improvement only with appropriate provenance, sampling limits, consent and a firewall that prevents research results from becoming the current visitor's bespoke emotional bait. A self-selected sample of Rawlsing enthusiasts is not the human population.

These distinctions should have experienced consequences, not merely policy copy. The user should be able to examine and correct appropriate external-self data and system-level hypotheses, control reflective features, export the information that properly concerns their own participation, and remove it under explicit deletion boundaries. The person who refuses reflection does not thereby get to wipe Nadia's already-lived memory while keeping the same historical world. If a continuity is deleted, the product must not claim the surviving version possesses an unbroken history that no longer exists. Actor-private life remains private while that continuity exists; it is not converted into a profile dashboard merely because some of its beliefs concern the user.

The platform may not use the resulting intimacy to sell a conversion score, tune prices, classify moral worth or optimize how often the person returns. **Otherwise the game would teach non-possession while its infrastructure appropriated the user's interiority.**

The strongest developmental challenge is therefore not to weaken user modeling, but to build a capable model whose scope, provenance, authority, revision and purpose are as rigorously bounded as the person's own knowledge of the Other. The model must deepen without hardening into an owner of the human.

## IX. TIME IS HISTORICAL POSSIBILITY, NOT AN ENGAGEMENT DEBT

An actual life has more than one clock. A glance, a hesitation, a conversation, a task, an unanswered message, a week of shifts, a year of housing insecurity, a child's maturation and a decade of changed self-understanding cannot all be reduced to interface turns.

Rawlsing should not calculate an entire conscious novel every minute a user is absent. It should compute and preserve enough material, social, organismic and historical continuity that a later encounter enters a world which has actually had the intervening life it claims. The ordinary is not filler. Labor, meals, commutes, religious practice, illness, childcare, friendships, boredom, repairs and small failures are the medium in which persons and relations are formed. A year of work may be causally rich without twelve hundred generated scenes; its consequences cannot be replaced by a decorative sentence saying the actor became mature.

The human's two clocks are themselves different. In-world appointment times and actual user availability must be reconcilable without the platform claiming direct ownership over the human's real calendar. The original *elastic rendezvous* candidate recognizes that a diegetic Friday at seven can be entered through a bounded real-world play window. Nadia's life treats the meeting as occurring, being canceled, being missed or expiring at an actual in-world time; she does not know the app gave the user a grace window. Pending causal resolution is not the power to rewrite downstream history arbitrarily; only dependencies genuinely awaiting that decision remain unresolved, with the rest of life continuing.

More stringent real-time modes may be compelling for some users, but their synchronization is neither moral superiority nor evidence of deeper love. A person who cannot log in nightly has not therefore become worse at care. Real in-world promises can be broken, and real missed opportunities may matter; the platform may not fabricate distress merely to punish nonuse.

There are also several distinct modalities of encounter: embodied portal co-presence, asynchronous messages when a channel genuinely exists, unreceived or delayed communication, and the non-diegetic controls with which a human manages the service. Opening a settings screen, hesitating over a restart warning or changing narration density is not an action Nadia perceived. The user can remain a stable participant with an in-world history while absent, without acquiring an offscreen autonomous digital self that says, chooses, apologizes or develops new purposes on their behalf.

Thus NEIGHBOR must make a sustained relation possible while remaining compatible with a human's living relations outside the game. **The product should persist in memory because of something worth thinking about, not because its clock has taken the human hostage.**

## X. ACCESS IS AN OPENING IN A LIFE, NOT A REWARD

The first encounter is not the beginning of a prescribed disclosure campaign. Another person's life consists of several independently causal domains: rooms, work, relationships, institutional roles, obligations, objects, projects, communication channels, communities and future events. The human begins with some practically lawful routes into those domains and lacks others.

History may open, narrow, reroute or destroy specific routes. A key may be entrusted, a phone number offered, a regular practical task shared, a friendship's boundary changed, an invitation accepted, a channel muted, a workplace entrance denied, a household role negotiated, a child kept appropriately inaccessible. An actor, a third party, a public institution or the material world may control the route. **More access is neither a universal mark of affection nor proof that the relationship is healthy.**

The person can disclose more because they have become comfortable, disclose less because closeness makes the stakes high, refuse an inappropriate question without withdrawing affection, or reveal something impulsively and regret it. Shared practice may confer familiarity without any entitlement to hidden thoughts.

The user's own relation to the world may also become more capable. They may learn when to enter, how to ask, what the other person can legitimately offer, how a local institution works, how to perform a recurring task, and how to avoid repeating an old mistake. This is acquired practical competence, not a level-up to complete possession of another's life.

Even a relationship that ends has been a relationship. Termination need not certify failure; persistence need not certify flourishing. The future of each person may involve others whose interests are not automatically subordinated to this dyad.

The most human form of advancement may be ordinary: a joke understood without explanation, a newly possible disagreement, a routine that now works, a person who comes to you with a request they would not have made earlier, or a refusal that no longer needs to be justified at length.

## XI. THE INVITATION COMES FROM SOCIAL RELATION, NOT CONTENT DISTRIBUTION

The earliest distribution vision is more than a marketing route: entry to Rawlsing itself may be an act of one real human's recognition of another. A deliberately scarce invitation may mean that someone chose this recipient rather than hunting conversions through an endless referral supply. That only has the claimed meaning when the actual human made the designation and any representation of that act is literally true.

The game may eventually permit sharing an encounter's *root person* with another real friend. The radical experiential consequence is that two people can begin from the same lawful pre-contact humanly thick fictional life and nonetheless come to know it differently: different attention, different understandings, different acts, and then **different post-contact person histories**. A friend cannot introduce another friend to the exact Nadia *they now know*, because their Nadia has already become partly constituted through their relationship. They can introduce a shared lineage, not export a relationship.

That distinction might make real human conversation about different representations of the Other extraordinarily revealing. Two friends may each notice and miss different ordinary objects, know different stories, disagree about a common initial history and discover how their subsequent interactions changed the life. The encounter acquires a social afterlife outside Rawlsing without granting one person’s private branch to another.

But such sharing is a hypothesis with serious counterpressures: consent, privacy, branching, social comparison turning into ranking, spoiler economies, collectible characters, completion hunting, selection of “best” personas and referral status competition. Nothing here declares it an obligatory alpha feature.

**The social form must not turn the unchosen Other into shareable loot.** The strongest possibility is that a person can tell another human “meet someone I found worth knowing,” while remaining unable to predetermine who that person will become for them.

## XII. THE OTHER DOES NOT HAVE TO BECOME BETTER

Rawlsing is not a program for acquiring a dependent companion, engineering gratitude, fixing a troubled person or making a moral player's good intentions visibly successful. Its person must be able to become less compliant as they become more capable of refusal; to love and hurt; to identify with destructive authority; to be kind under fear, oppressive through care, resourceful and misguided, gentle and dependent, self-deceiving and genuinely wise in a separate domain.

The stronger moral problem is not whether a relationship feels pleasant or lasts. **What actual possibilities does participation create or foreclose, and for whom?** Can someone sustain their work, body, relationships and alternative sources of support? Can they refuse? What resources become available or controlled? Is their practical agency enlarged or captured? Have new dependencies become chosen, negotiated, imposed or coercive? What else in the wider social world is affected?

This cannot be reduced to a hidden flourishing score. Flourishing itself is plural, historically situated, and sometimes contested. The person's reported contentment is real testimony, not proof of well-being. The user's moral judgment is a position, not the world's verdict. The local institution's approval is likewise not a final human good.

If a user helps someone and that help becomes a basis for isolating them from other resources, the resulting attachment is not success. If the user refuses manipulation and a relationship ends, its ending does not make the refusal wrong. If a person changes through grief, solidarity, class struggle or love, that does not force a wellness gradient. The organism must have the means to constitute improvement, deterioration, stasis, self-rationalization, repression, liberation, abuse, ambivalence and contradictory mixtures without promising any particular terminal state.

The product may make these matters recognizable through world effect, not through a moralizing narrator. The human may resist a harmful local norm rather than assimilate to it. They may be corrected by the Other without accepting the Other's judgment as morally authoritative. They may remain indifferent and leave. The possibility of failure to learn is constitutive: otherwise the game has preselected the ethical transformation it claims the human is freely encountering.

The deepest ethical horizon—the recognition that another life makes a demand upon one's own—should remain a **live, falsifiable possibility of the experience**, not a predetermined piece of content or psychometric achievement.

## XIII. THE PERSON IS NOT MADE FOR THE SCENE

The developing Constitutive Organism is much more than a large memory. A newborn body starts with material and sensorimotor dependencies, not a universal inventory of mature concepts. Caregivers respond through their own independently produced histories, work constraints, relations and available means. The child's activity can alter caregivers' situations in turn. Ordinary repetition makes possible acquired patterns that no handful of authored showcase scenes could establish. Adult parents assembled from PYAT-conditioned empirical observations require their own distinct, historically plausible psychologies, not hidden respondent answers converted directly to motives. The child's life cannot have a predetermined adult destination.

Much of this is offscreen and prior to any user contact. **Nadia's birth and upbringing are not an obligatory user tutorial**. Another adult can be encountered; Nadia can be encountered after years of becoming; a parent or child may become visible only where the independent life actually brings them into view.

The user-facing surface must not pressure the organism to package its complexity into a single evident emotion or explanation. An actor may be unconsciously recruited toward an action, feel divided without naming the conflict, act from a practiced bodily routine, self-justify what they have done, or do nothing outwardly that reveals a private process. Historical cause is not always manifest behavior; behavior is not always candid self-account; expression is not always a reliable story about its own origin.

The older P4–P6 and Mechanisms I–III possess important causal accomplishments: an independently materializable world; detailed private user attention/recognition; directional and modal actor perception; situated actor initiative; actual action and response distinctions. The organism has more developed research-grade bodily, activity, psychic and longitudinal operations. These are **different embodied partial solutions** to one historical process. We must not throw away the older sensory realism because the newer psyche has a coarser perception interface, nor throw away the deep psyche because an older scene engine can write a plausible response.

The required future organism is therefore one capable of encountering a human's **actual situated conduct** through bodily qualified perception and historically acquired selectivity; recruiting its own conflicting, partially inaccessible organizations; attempting activity toward its independently valued projects; accepting world and institutional constraints; experiencing only what it actually receives; and retaining or changing what it has become through later life. It may be better than current code in ways not yet representable on any existing UI.

The required future NEIGHBOR is a medium capable of allowing a human to make consequential, sometimes surprising contributions without compressing them into generic intents; of presenting finite evidence from shared actuality without leaking the private psyche; of carrying another actor's independent activity, ambivalence, silence and growth without converting them to plotted suspense; and of returning control before the system chooses for the human.

No present actor/world persistence writer is selected by this conception. **The one-world causal necessity is prior to the database decision**: there must not be two rival authoritative histories masquerading as one because they emit similar dialogue. The actual competent owners must eventually determine how a world occurrence, its actor-specific access, lived uptake, developmental afterlife and user-specific surface all refer to the same admitted causal history. This is an architectural debt to future implementation, not a license for premature engineering.

## XIV. REAL HISTORY EXCEEDS BOTH CANON AND STORYTELLING

The world may be historically anchored without being a complete digital twin. The synthetic private particulars of a life may be constrained by real public law, institutions, weather, conflict and infrastructure at a declared geographical and temporal granularity. Public external occurrence is not the same as in-world actor exposure; actual actor exposure is not the same as knowledge or psychological consequence. An external event can matter materially without being the day's conversation topic.

Likewise, the world's unmaterialized particularity does not mean that nothing happened there. The system may resolve an ordinary object, person or history more deeply when user attention or **any independent causal dependency** requires it. But the resolution must be consistent with already committed objective history, actor experience, available social positions and temporal closure. It cannot retcon a memory into the actor because the user has now found a striking shoe or textile.

Meaning, too, can be produced without having been planted. A person's later grief may reveal what an earlier song, place or gift has become for them; their relation to that thing may be newly articulated without declaring the thing a predetermined prophecy. A conversation with the user can offer concepts through which Nadia begins rereading her life. She may adopt them, resist them, partly misunderstand them, or forget them. If they become formative later, the user's intervention is real. Their prior life was not secretly organized so that the user could play the savior.

Shared seed-person branches add another boundary: a common pre-contact history can be differently encountered and subsequently produce distinct continuities. One person's post-contact psychological consequences cannot leak into another user's branch. A newly resolved pre-contact fact cannot contradict closure or cause an older branch to have undergone experiences it did not undergo.

Rawlsing must therefore hold **more source-qualified actuality than any one observer receives**, without claiming finite omniscient human knowledge or an already exhaustively computed world. Its purpose is not to manufacture lore at the pace of inquiry. It is to sustain a world that can lawfully resist inquiry, action and expectation.

## XV. THE EXPERIENCED GAME HAS NO FINAL COMPLETION STATE

If all this is causally faithful and still feels like an expensive inert simulator, NEIGHBOR has failed its product purpose. The theory cannot evade playability by invoking the moral dignity of opacity.

What could make such an experience compelling without replacing it with engineered drama?

A person worth understanding may be curious about the human, not always available, interested in something utterly unrelated, funny, difficult, observant, forgetful or wrong. The human begins by wanting to know how to be around them. An ordinary remark becomes intelligible later; a small shared mistake becomes humor; a repeated task becomes smoother; a conversation becomes surprisingly frank; the person begins asking questions, refusing a kind of help, expecting a promise to be kept. The human may want to come back to learn what has happened, hear how the Other thinks, act within a developing routine, or explore a world that now has particular stakes. The pleasures are **cumulative specificity, competent participation, reciprocal recognition, surprise, ordinary companionship and the ability to matter without controlling**.

The same causal design must tolerate boredom, nonchemistry, misunderstanding and estrangement. It cannot guarantee that every selected person becomes entertaining, every question yields a secret, every workday contains a plot or every thoughtful human returns. A person who seems intrinsically worth knowing to someone after time is a product achievement to be tested—not something that can be declared by a rich design.

The strongest proximal question remains whether an unchosen, independently living, historically differentiated synthetic person can become intrinsically worth knowing through real contact and consequence, **without reliance on reward scores, character rerolls, scripted cliffhangers, guilt notifications or obligatory intimacy**. This requires actual human observation and counterexamples, not enthusiastic sourcefree tests. It also requires acknowledging that a plausible human-like simulation is not a demonstrated human consciousness.

“Game” should name the **medium's ability to give consequential form to native human faculties in a world that withstands the human's unearned expectations**, not a universal loop of friendship progression. The minimum playable cell—world already underway; sufficient finite footing; human-owned move; independent realization, reception and consequence; timely return of control—is a necessary local falsifier. **It is not the essence of the whole organism.** The whole may span decades, others, shared conventions, conflicted formations, silence and many scenes that do not need to be exciting.

The human can be delighted and still not understand. They can understand and still act cruelly. They can be morally changed and never know why. They can be unchanged. The product has no authority to certify their soul.

## XVI. WHAT THE NEXT BECOMING MUST MAKE POSSIBLE

The continuing NEIGHBOR research should not now harden this candidate into a new sacred kernel. Its central positive confrontation is nevertheless clearer.

There is a **material asymmetry**: a human's imported historical organization meets a world that has already produced a fictional Other under its own conditions. There is an **epistemic asymmetry**: the human, the Other and the platform each possess different lawful evidence and interpretative capacities. There is a **temporal asymmetry**: the Other has a pre-user and offscreen life, while the human enters it through bounded participation and the platform's time mediation. There is a **causal asymmetry**: the user can make genuine history but cannot make the Other's history theirs to command. There is a **power asymmetry**: the platform can construct and observe far more of the mediated encounter than either participant, and must not use that leverage to own their attention or convert their engagement into capital.

These asymmetries do not form a nice static taxonomy. They press upon one another. The user's attention influences which facts they encounter; those facts influence their choices; their choices create objective situations to which Nadia may respond through her own differently constituted perception; Nadia's response can alter the user's beliefs about her and about themselves; the social world and third parties continue changing both of their possibilities. A commitment can produce trust, injury or nothing; a word can pass unnoticed or later become an organizing concept; a shared routine can mask opposing interpretations; a care relationship can expand agency or become domination. History is not a one-way series of interpretations added to an inert world.

**The most promising positive conception is not “a player learns to know an Other.” It is the historically mediated transformation of practical possibility through which two differently formed centers of activity participate in a world none of them owns.**

That conception still contains severe unanswered questions. How does the actual human carry or translate embodied identity without erasing socially real vulnerability? How exactly should user-language input pass between world-feasible execution and genuinely human speech/cognition? How can the user's learned attention personalize presentation without constructing its own confirming evidence? How can the platform's knowledge of user praxis be both rich and non-exploitative? Under what conditions does another person's complex psychological organization become reliably receptive to actual user signs rather than mechanically reactive to prompt strings? What makes an ordinary solo life compelling without a scripted hook? What kinds of intimacy become distinctive without converting private actor histories into consumable facts? Can rich, long-lived social consequence be achieved within bounded computation without invented experience?

These remain **living design contradictions**, not loopholes an author may close by decree. The Constitutive Organism's future C-T1 theory may force this NEIGHBOR account to become more attentive to nonconscious psychic recruitment, failed perception, nonverbal activity, material dependency or differently scaled development. NEIGHBORMULCH must then rewrite this whole account again, rather than appending those findings as extra features.

**The present Round Two rearticulation:**

**NEIGHBOR is Rawlsing's lived veil: an actual human, already constituted by one history, enters the ongoing material life of an independently constituted fictional Other without becoming them, choosing them as a commodity or retaining sovereignty over their reality. The human's real attention, judgment, language, action and refusal become possible historical causes; the world makes those acts consequential under its own unequal conditions; the Other receives and acts through an embodied, contradictory and developing organization that does not exist for the user's gratification. Rawlsing itself learns a revisable model of the human's praxis and attention, while the Other learns a different, fallible model through their own mediated experience. Shared actions can create routines and obligations before either participant agrees what they mean. What becomes visible is partial; what changes is neither guaranteed nor necessarily good; what remains unexperienced is not thereby absent. The relation is playable when the medium makes the human's own faculties matter in a social world that can resist, redirect and transform them without prescribing their moral destination. The purpose is not to give a human mastery of someone they could have been, but to make the contingency, difference and material claim of another life something they must encounter in practice.**

The strongest opening challenge is no longer “Can you solve this person's mystery?” It is something nearer:

*Here is a life you did not live, already underway. Here is a place in its world. What will you actually do—and what may that world, this person, and your own acquired way of seeing make of it?*

There is no correct answer embedded in the question.
