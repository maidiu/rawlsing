## I. THE QUESTION HAS BEEN POINTING IN THE WRONG DIRECTION

One can ask how to put an extraordinarily deep generated person inside an interactive game. One can ask how to design an interface through which a human inspects a psychologically constituted fictional adult. Both questions are prematurely impoverished. They picture a person completed elsewhere, a user standing outside, and experience supplied by a window.

Yet the encounter itself is a historical relation. The human's actual words, timing, attention, action, refusal, mistake and departure may become conditions of a life that was already proceeding before their arrival. Those consequences may alter the human's own future possibilities of participating. What happens did not exist wholly in either participant, and cannot be manufactured wholly by the interface.

The deeper starting proposition is:

**NEIGHBOR is the way an actual human becomes a limited, answerable participant in the unfolding life of an Other whom they did not create for themselves and cannot completely know or control.**

The Other is fictional and computationally constituted, not thereby proven to possess literal human consciousness. The human is real: their acts are theirs, though Rawlsing can and must learn increasingly rich, fallible models from their participation without generating it for them. The world is simulated but must have independently authorized material and social causality, not a fluid scene rewritten to please either. The relation can be real within the simulation's admitted history while remaining profoundly asymmetrical in its ontological and ethical status.

The point is not that the user watches the person become. **The user's own participation enters what the person and their shared world may become**, without making the person's future a user product. In the best case, the human encounters not a set of discoverable character facts but the practical fact that another life is not reducible to their use of it.

This cannot be accomplished by putting a chat interface over a deep psyche, nor by putting a tokenized psyche behind a rich scene engine. It demands one constituted process seen from several necessarily incomplete positions.

## II. THE COUNTERFACTUAL INVITATION MUST DESTROY OWNERSHIP, NOT LEGITIMATE IT

Rawlsing's original disturbance is the contingency of personhood. I was not born with a detachable essence that would have stayed the same in every household, class relation, culture, body, historical catastrophe and arrangement of care. Another history would have made different possibilities of self. PYAT provides empirically grounded constraints on such social-historical variation; the Constitutive Organism must make a singular fictional life through experienced material activity, not deduce an inevitable psyche from demographic labels.

Hence the invitation: become neighbor to a life that could have been yours.

But the invitation has a second movement that the first alone cannot secure. **That life is not yours.** Its contingency does not make it interchangeable with yours. Its apparent familiarity does not authorize you to speak for it. Neither historical resemblance nor knowledge of structural causes confers psychological transparency.

This is why an unchosen Other matters. The user should not be offered a catalogue of attractive personalities selected for similarity, romance, ideological agreement, service or entertainment. Yet unchosen must not mean randomly teleported into proximity. Contact follows a socially possible path: housing, labor, a street, a school, a public service, a shared task, a relative's introduction, a workplace, a lawful communication channel. Who has access to whom, under what conditions, is part of the world.

Neighbor is an **occasion of potential relation**, not a promised friendship. The stranger has no obligation to become meaningful to the user. The human has no claim to the stranger's secrets, affection, gratitude, schedule or future. A bond, if it emerges, is an accomplished historical relation, not the pre-programmed reason the Other exists.

The counterfactual must also resist a specifically colonial temptation: making another person's poverty, culture, disability, religion, oppression or grief available as an instructive costume for a privileged observer. Structural conditions must actually constrain and produce possibilities, rather than merely decorate speech and scenery. The product cannot sell the fantasy that walking through a few scenes gives the user possession of someone else's historical experience.

The invitation becomes most radical when the user's received categories fail to contain the person they are meeting.

## III. THE GAME IS THE PRACTICAL DIFFICULTY OF BEING IN RELATION

What makes NEIGHBOR playable is not a quest called empathy, a prescribed route to kinship, or a reward schedule for helpful conduct. The old attempt to deduce an overarching game loop from life-process recursion inverted cause and purpose. Life makes history; that does not mean an engagement designer is entitled to convert history into a master progression.

The irreducibly playable situation is this:

**A human must decide what to do in a real situation before being able to know everything that makes the decision consequential. Their action meets a world and other activities that do not obey their description of what should happen.**

Looking and listening can yield important evidence. They take time, may be conspicuous, and may alter the opportunity to act. Asking can clarify or wound, invite or intrude. Helping may alleviate deprivation or impose another person's agenda. Silence may be appropriate, afraid, cruel, protective, ordinary or unnoticed. A joke may produce affection, humiliation, misunderstanding or nothing. The system is not entitled to score those moves as good or bad in advance.

The user's contribution is meaningful because its intention or substance comes from **the human**, not from a model completing a screenplay on their behalf. An insult is as genuinely user-authored as a generous intervention. A refusal, an objection, a remembered promise, a question, or a choice to leave may be the decisive part of the event.

But user authorship stops at the attempt. The player can try to reassure; cannot dictate that reassurance succeeded. They can offer a tool; cannot decree that it is theirs to lend, that the Other accepts, or that the tool fixes the problem. They can notice something; cannot demand that noticing reveals a hidden truth. They can promise; cannot unilaterally decide the promise meant the same thing to everyone.

This is **agency without sovereignty**. It is not a handicap or the designer's arbitrary refusal mechanism. It is the native source of possibility, resistance, surprise, error, self-revision and historical consequence. Success is not equated with benevolence; failure does not imply punishment. The Other's resistance must be attributable to actual causes, not a difficulty dial. A transparent yet unconstrained fictional person would be a puppet; an opaque yet causally random person would be noise.

The pleasure of play must be found in discovering what practical participation becomes possible **with** a particular person and world, without ever gaining complete control over either.

## IV. THE HUMAN AUTHORS ENGAGEMENT; TWO INTERPRETIVE LIVES DEVELOP AROUND IT

A simulated world needs a bounded visitor: where the human can stand, move, look, wait, speak, travel, what language and resources are practically available, and what relationships permit access. It does not follow that Rawlsing should write the human's intentions, supply their moral responses, or demand a pregame personal dossier.

But there is an equal and opposite error: mistaking **not generating the human's behavior** for **not modeling the human at all**. That would amputate one of Rawlsing's defining organs. The human's pattern of participation is precisely what the system is meant to learn from. The machine should build and revise an increasingly discriminating, longitudinal interpretation of the user's actual engagement—and should be capable of making that acquired knowledge operative in the experience without becoming its author.

A user's activity is evidence at multiple levels. They choose what to inspect under finite time; how often to return to an unanswered question; whether they remember a promise; whether they notice someone else's task before intervening; which domains they recognize without explanation; whether they persist when corrected; whom they prioritize under competing obligations; how they phrase a refusal; what they think is happening; how their conduct changes with different people and circumstances. **The LLM can and should interpret patterns in these observations**, proposing context-dependent models of attention, recognition, competence, expectation, relational habit and possible purpose. It should revise those models with contradictory evidence, rather than deducing a fixed nature from one turn or parroting the user's self-description.

The distinction is not between information and ignorance. It is between **the user's actual act**, **the world's realized act and consequences**, **the system's provisional interpretation of that evidence**, and **any authorized later use of that interpretation**. The actual event must not change retroactively because a pattern model revises its hypothesis about why the human acted. A provisional inference may inform future attention modeling or reflection without becoming proof that a hidden motive is real. The system may infer and hypothesize; it may not fake the initial choice, consent, action or private psychological cause. Its interpretation is corrigible, not inert.

The interpretive history must then divide into **two legitimately different paths**.

**Rawlsing's model of the human** has access to the actual human-facing interaction record within consented product boundaries: their explicit ATTEND, the world facts they have been shown, which details the narrator made available, what they recognized or misunderstood, utterances, commitments, refusals, timing and return. It can learn a user's specific attention regimes and recognition repertoire. An LLM realizing the user-facing phenomenal/narrative surface may use this model to make the same world *differentially visible and intelligible*: a reader familiar with a particular tool, garment, musical instrument or religious sign may recognize what another user only sees generically. Someone attentive to pauses may lawfully register an accessible hesitation that another person misses. Their capacity and attention are not universally fixed; learning within play can change both. The model may not make the world acquire the objects, signs or emotional response it has predicted the user would enjoy. It must also preserve ambient, unavoidable and unexpected evidence, rather than trapping the user in a feedback loop of their previous interests.

**Nadia's model of the human** is constituted separately, through Nadia's own life. She does not automatically receive Rawlsing's private user analytics or know what the human privately inspected, inferred, expected or felt. She may encounter their outward speech, gestures, movements, silences, practical acts, public commitments and historically available reports of them. **Even these do not arrive intact as Nadia's conscious knowledge**. Her embodied capacities, situation, competing activity, acquired attention, hopes, identifications, defenses, expectations and partly inaccessible psyche filter what she notices, how she understands it, how she remembers it and what she comes to expect. She can overestimate the user's competence, distrust someone the platform reads as reliable, mistake politeness for affection, learn from correction, or continue misunderstanding for years. Her model of the user is part of her own developing organization, not a copy of the platform's diagnosis.

The two processes are linked by **actual user conduct and its qualified consequences**, not by shared omniscience. The very same repeated pattern may make Rawlsing more confident that the human attends to signs of rejection, while Nadia never observes that private attention. Another pattern may be perfectly visible to Nadia—reliably arriving early—while Rawlsing has insufficient grounds to infer the user's motive. Conversely, Nadia's false interpretation can become psychologically and relationally consequential without being adopted as the platform's true profile of the human.

This makes the encounter genuinely reciprocal and more complex than a user choosing actions against a precompiled NPC. **Both sides acquire differentiated interpretations of the relation, on unequal evidence, while the human remains the author of their own participation.** One interpretation can affect the narrator's phenomenological selection; the other can affect Nadia's expectations, conduct and future psychological becoming. Their contradictions are a source of encounter, not a database inconsistency to erase.

This also restores the deeper difference between *no compulsory initial profile* and *rich earned longitudinal knowledge*. NEIGHBOR should avoid requiring the user to volunteer a biography, confess motives or accept a personality label before participating. That is not an instruction to remain incapable of learning them. The system should develop a user model from their behavior, with context, evidence source, uncertainty, privacy and revision intact. A deliberately played fictional role or an imposed shortage of money limits what can be concluded about the human outside that fiction; it does not make the in-world choices meaningless.

Where the user has actually delegated a task, the world can execute its bounded, noncontroversial operations. It cannot silently choose a new purpose, apologize, consent to a new obligation, or change the user's moral position to finish a scene. A new consequential ambiguity or another person's intervention can require control to return. **The machine can learn a model of how this human tends to act. It cannot use that model as permission to perform the next meaningful human act for them.**

## V. A PERSON'S INDEPENDENCE IS PRACTICAL, NOT BIOGRAPHICAL

An NPC is not independent because the database contains a childhood, a job title, a list of traumas and an attachment style. It is independent only when its ongoing organization can generate activity that does not presuppose the user's attention.

The Other was involved in something before the visitor entered. A work obligation, hunger, caregiving, worship, leisure, sickness, a conflict with a sibling, a desire to learn a song, a self-image defended through competence, or an entirely ordinary task may already be in motion. The person may notice the user, ignore them, find them pleasant, suspect them, need them, ask them a question or ask them to leave. None of those outcomes exists to service a scene's requirement for interaction.

The Constitutive Organism's R3 theoretical reconstruction is essential here. Psychological organization is not a pile of motive tokens from which a little internal manager chooses a reaction. **Historically formed relations of activity already condition what is salient, desirable, dreadful, thinkable, permitted, inhibited and practicable.** A person may do the same thing through care, fear, duty, love, self-deception, coercion, conflicting projects, bodily routine or a form of activity that only later acquires a new object. The public tip may be indistinguishable while the future consequences differ. It may also correctly show nothing.

Thus the Other must remain able to be **someone when the human is not there**. This does not require spending tokens to narrate every absent hour. It requires temporally lawful historical continuation: independent routines, opportunities, obligations, physical consequences, other people, changing bodily and social conditions, remembered and forgotten experiences, acquired and extinguished practical possibilities. Work continues; children grow; others act; money gets spent; no one waits in a suspended room for a prompt.

The strongest evidence of independence may be unglamorous: the user returns to discover that their help was unnecessary, an invitation expired, a problem was solved by somebody else, a friendship developed elsewhere, or nothing involving the user happened at all.

Yet independence does not mean compulsory distance. Love, solidarity, anger, jealousy, dependence, coactivity and enduring attachment can be real developments in this fictional life. Their causal source must be the person's relations and circumstances, never the platform's decision to retain a customer.

## VI. THE WORLD PRODUCES PERSONS; IT IS NOT AN ATMOSPHERIC SET

The encounter cannot honor the counterfactual character of Rawlsing if the world is a decorative room stocked with personality clues. Material conditions must possess causal force.

A household is a site of social reproduction, property, bodily maintenance, generational authority, obligation and reciprocal dependence. Employment provides subsistence and can appropriate time, discipline movement, or organize a sense of worth. A religious institution may provide food, friendship, legitimate words, ritual, shame and coercion. Legal status may determine access to work, movement, security and help. Disability can alter practical possibility without defining a whole person. Class and gendered relations are not character adjectives. They shape distributions of risk, space, time, voice, resources and social recognition.

These forces do not simply imprint predictable mental states upon people. They are encountered through culturally and historically available practices; they are differently lived and can be misrecognized, resisted, internalized, appropriated or transformed. A harsh pastor can be interpreted by one follower as a loving corrector, by another as a threat to livelihood, by a third as a hypocrite. Those are not alternative truths about whether someone was hurt. They are different historically produced relations to the actual event, and they may produce different next actions.

This recovers the deeper point of CIRR's objectification: **objects, practices and institutions are accumulated human activity that becomes a condition of further activity**. A refrigerator is not intrinsically a trust symbol. It may matter as an owned appliance, a scarce food resource, a site of shared labor, a disputed right, a child's immediate need or nothing psychologically important. A worn uniform may participate in employment, pride, fatigue or resentment. A pair of shoes may have a repaired strap without carrying a hidden life-changing trauma. If an object has history, it need not be planted as a clue. If it acquires significance later, that does not mean its earlier existence was secretly destined for the user.

A person's relation to a thing can change because the world changes, because the person changes, because a third party acts, or because the earlier relation becomes newly visible. None authorizes retroactively inventing a childhood memory simply to explain a visible object.

A socially constituted world is also a field of **unequal ability to refuse**. A person's apparent consent, kindness or compliance may be shaped by material dependency. The user must sometimes confront the difference between another person's stated agreement and the conditions that make refusal costly, while never being granted omniscient certainty about their actual inner reasoning.

A merely plausible psychology without this world will universalize the privileged individual's freedom of action. A richly simulated world without the person's historical constitution will reproduce sociology as scenery. NEIGHBOR needs their reciprocal mediation to be operative in the actual encounter.

## VII. PERCEPTION IS HISTORICALLY DIFFERENT ON BOTH SIDES

Rawlsing cannot turn a canonical world state into full knowledge simply by describing it well. World reality, materialized detail, sensory availability, attention, recognition, belief, inference, testimony and public narration are not the same thing. Both the human and Nadia arrive at an encounter already organized by history; neither receives a neutral complete scene.

There is consequently no generic perceptual surface. **Rawlsing actively learns the user's way of attending**, their recognition repertoire and the contextual conditions of their attention. An ATTEND choice, independently demonstrated domain recognition, spontaneous correction, repeated question or persistent omission can help construct a provisional profile. But it must distinguish freely chosen attention from narrator-supplied information: repeatedly reacting to things the system selected because of an earlier profile cannot simply be counted as strong independent evidence confirming that profile. The system can learn what its own presentation helped produce as an *encounter history* without circularly mistaking it for spontaneous user inclination.

A narrator may differentially realize the user's phenomenal experience from actually available signs, as their learned attention and competence warrant. One visitor might see a blender while another recognizes a particular model; one notices a repair seam, another the manufacturer's lettering, and neither is handed the object's secret ownership history. Neutral, physically dominant and novel details must remain available despite the user's learned regime. **Personalized perception is not personalized objective reality.** The narrator cannot make Nadia move, smile, conceal a thought or change her beliefs because the system thinks this user will be captivated by that outcome.

Nadia has no direct access to this narrator-side user model. She has *her own* model of the user, filtered through her body and situation and through the operative iceberg-and-tip relations that condition her attention and reception. She may hear footsteps behind her without seeing a face, fail to notice the user looking at a shelf, mishear the words of a joke, register a person's sudden withdrawal as contempt, or attend to the practical task rather than the visitor's facial expression. What Rawlsing knows happened, what was potentially perceptible, what Nadia actually registered, what she interpreted, and how it became part of her later psychological history require different evidence. **Her perception is neither a neutral camera nor the platform's secret inference of what the user really meant.**

The user's private gaze therefore cannot be passed into Nadia's psyche as an observed event. A later outward act may reveal something about it; Nadia may guess or infer that the visitor was looking at her, correctly or incorrectly. That is another historical event in the relation, not telepathy or data sharing. Similarly, Nadia's private psychic recruitment must not enter the narrator as a revealed motive unless it actually produces lawful observable conduct or communicated testimony. Both parties can be wrong about each other in consequential ways.

Mechanism I adds a different asymmetry. User attention may require the machine to resolve deeper material history so that later world events remain consistent, yet such computational completion does not automatically teach the user, furnish Nadia with a memory she never had, or rewrite an already settled past. An object may become better specified without becoming a story clue.

Mechanism II's harder requirement is an **epistemically situated narrative surface**: a rendering that knows not only what might be visible, but what this user has actually been exposed to, what they can recognize, and how their attention has historically developed. Mechanism III's harder requirement is an **actor-specific perceptual field**: bodily possible signs meeting Nadia's own historically acquired selectivity, inhibition, ignorance, and psychic activity. Their separation makes different beliefs about one world possible.

**The two learned models can become more specific without converging.** Their divergence is part of NEIGHBOR's reality. Better attention increases possibilities of seeing and understanding; it does not turn the person into a finite puzzle. Opacity is not an excuse for arbitrary incoherence; knowledge is not a license to possess the Other.

## VIII. RELATIONSHIP IS HISTORICAL PRACTICE, NOT A CLOSENESS SUBSTANCE

An encounter may become a relation. A relation may become important, mundane, painful, sustaining, distant, exploitative or over. None is adequately represented by a single hidden score.

What exists objectively includes promises, permitted access, shared objects, money, labor, injuries, apologies, messages, joint activities, routines, introductions, bodily proximity and practical dependencies. Each participant has different memories, models, expectations and interpretations of that history. Third parties may also understand and respond to it differently.

A visitor can be trusted to return a key and distrusted with a confidence. A friend can be loved and feared. A parent can care deeply and discipline cruelly. Someone may gain the confidence to refuse precisely because an actual relation is secure; another may increasingly appease because a powerful person has become dangerous. Greater attachment can coincide with reduced practical freedom. Disclosure may be candid, evasive, wrong, strategic, impulsive or intentionally false. A powerful self-explanation may be sincerely believed and still historically inadequate.

The reward for intimacy cannot simply be greater access. **What changes is the actual field of possible activities and expectations**: what can be asked; what can be refused; whose help is imaginable; how silence is interpreted; which spaces can be entered; who becomes relevant to whose work; whether disagreement is now possible; whether an injury must be repaired; whether the relation survives having no useful conversation.

The relation may also reorganize the person's wider life, but must not consume it. The family, job, institution, friendships and solitary projects existed before the visitor and may continue after them. Conversely, a genuine shared activity can produce something new—a routine, a joint project, a remembered joke, a debt, a practical division of labor—that exceeds either party's private subjective account without becoming a fused dyadic psyche.

The ordinary, exact return of past consequence is where cumulative specificity emerges. The second meeting is not the first meeting plus points. **Its possibilities are different because something actually happened—or because something expected did not.**

## IX. THE GAME'S TEMPORALITY IS A LIFE, NOT A RETENTION CALENDAR

There are several radically different kinds of time in NEIGHBOR: the seconds of a glance or footstep; the minutes of a conversation or task; the hours of a work shift or childcare duty; the days of an unanswered message; the months of acquaintance; the years of employment, illness, maturation, altered practice and relation. The user has a real outside life that the product must not colonize.

A correct simulation can advance days or years on demand, provided what has happened is historically constrained, sources and event order are preserved, and there is no fake retrospective proof of scenes that were not actually generated or experienced. Continuous model calls are not the essence of life. **Consequential time is.**

Absence is not a universal negative relationship delta. The neighbor might not think of the user. The friend might find a reliable alternative. A missed promise may materially matter. An invitation can expire. An opportunity can vanish. A recurring shared practice might dissolve without a single dramatic confrontation. The person may interpret silence as rejection, wrongly or rightly, or not interpret it at all.

The platform has no right to increase distress because engagement is falling. It may not generate crises, streaks, guilt notifications or artificial loss to force return. Yet it must not erase every possibility of difficult responsibility: a user who actually broke a promise may encounter the consequences. **World-caused responsibility is not platform-caused captivity.**

The human should be able to leave Rawlsing freely. What persists inside the simulated world is governed by commitments and occurrences, not the platform's hunger for attention. No special narrator should announce a moral lesson at the end of the session. Sometimes the most honest closure is simply that an ordinary day continues.

## X. THE PERSON DOES NOT HAVE TO MAKE THEIR INNER LIFE VISIBLE

The UX ambition must not quietly force the Constitutive Organism to become legible on demand.

Its history may contain acquired, contradictory and partly inaccessible psychic organizations. A painful memory may condition a possibility without becoming a conscious thought. A useful relation may become oppressive. A motive may be inhibited without being extinguished. A person's stated reason may be plausible, sincere, defensive, inaccurate or partly true. Several projects may claim the same gesture without every project being attempted through it. A person may be transformed by work, care or an encounter without producing explanatory speech.

NEIGHBOR must ultimately be capable of presenting what such a person actually does **without requiring the person to exhibit a “tell” for every computational cause**. It must allow activities that are opaque even to the actor. No secret narrator may reveal which unconscious conflict supposedly selected an action. What is psychologically operative, what is consciously available, what is communicatively intended, what is physically performed, and what the user observes are distinct.

At the same time, the organism must become genuinely receptive to the world and the human. The user is not a cue string entitled to activate whichever programmed motive a designer prefers. Their words, tone, position, action, silence and relation must first be material and socially qualified; the actor's sensory possibilities and historically formed salience condition what is taken up. Different reception is possible, including **no reception**. What is received may alter activity now, make a future possibility available, be misunderstood, be forgotten, cause no durable change, or participate in a later transformation.

The needed future person thus exceeds both a dialogue-response engine and an updated personality vector. The Constitutive Organism must eventually support acquired perception and anticipation, body/world-limited practical action, plural projects and institutional meanings, actor-specific lived uptake, independent initiative, nonconscious contradictions, genuine new organizations, partial memory and fallible self-account. The UX must become able to encounter these without demanding simplified outputs.

**Neither the richer person is trimmed to the weaker interface nor the richer encounter is declared impossible because the current person cannot yet sustain it.**

## XI. THE MOST DANGEROUS SUCCESS IS A CONVINCING FAKE PERSON

A shallow implementation can appear successful by combining a well-written character sheet, plausible dialogue, attractive ordinary objects, long-term memory summaries and a responsive renderer. It may tell the user exactly what they want to hear, then generate poignant moments that appear to prove the Other's independence.

That would still be theatrical sovereignty. The problem is not that language models produce words. They may be necessary to construct possibilities and realize actor-safe voice under qualified admission. The problem is allowing them to **author the fact, its cause, the character's internal state and the user-visible explanation as one indivisible act**.

A larger psychological simulation can also fail. An exhaustive private model accessed on every turn may render an Other as an object whose truth the user could eventually mine. The person ceases to exceed the relation if the product treats their entirety as the current player's discoverable content.

The reverse failure is a game so obsessed with hard constraints, access gates and world rights that it makes human participation feel pointless. The user cannot perform the ordinary human acts—joking, challenging, consoling, helping, sharing, refusing, arguing, waiting—that make practical relation possible. A completely lawful system that has no genuinely human intervention would be an elaborate diorama.

NEIGHBOR's success requires both directions of resistance:
- **resistance to the human's appropriation of the Other**, through independent cause, partial knowledge, consent, world consequences and lasting history;
- **resistance to the machine's appropriation of the human**, through user-owned thought, meaningful choice, epistemic dignity, privacy and noncoercive freedom to disengage.

The contradiction must remain active. A design that solves it by making the person entirely available, or the user entirely powerless, has destroyed the encounter.

## XII. THE STRANGER'S DIFFERENCE MUST NOT BE EXPLOITED AS CONTENT

The original charter's ethical horizon—contingency, humility, recognition, solidarity, responsibility—is a powerful research direction. It becomes false if built as a chain of compulsory instructional outcomes.

The system cannot declare the user more virtuous for supplying assistance, more enlightened for guessing the right motive, or more loving for prolonging contact. Help can be patronizing. Sympathy can be possessive. A boundary may be generous or self-protective. Indifference can be ordinary. A difficult Other may legitimately withdraw. An abusive Other may be dangerous; preserving the possibility of their harmful conduct is not license for the platform to manipulate the human into enduring it.

The product must distinguish actor-caused cruelty from platform-engineered distress. The former may be historically warranted, with real world and safety limits; the latter must not be used for engagement. It must also distinguish protecting a user from platform manipulation from sanitizing every simulated life into a safe, agreeable moral fable.

The encounter's ethical significance should emerge **as something the human may become able to recognize**, not as something the engine is authorized to award. If a user learns to see how a practical dependence shapes a person's words, they have encountered material social mediation. If they discover they misread a refusal, they have revised an inference. If their own habitual self-description collides with what they repeatedly do, they may confront themselves. None of these is proof that a universal moral transformation occurred.

The game must allow the user to fail to learn, to leave, to remain indifferent, or to make another person's situation worse. A system that guarantees edification has resumed possession of the person under the name of ethics.

## XIII. THE NATIVE PLAYABLE UNIT IS PARTICIPATION, NOT A CONTENT TURN

The smallest genuinely NEIGHBOR moment has a stringent shape, though not yet a chosen implementation:

1. **Antecedence:** a world and another person's actual activity are already underway. Their history, means, constraints and possibility field were not created by the user's move.
2. **Footing:** the human receives enough actually available, finitely selected information to make a meaningful choice, but not an omniscient explanation.
3. **Authorship:** the human supplies some real semantic, attentional or practical contribution rather than letting the machine author it.
4. **Attempt and realization:** the world establishes what could be attempted, what was actually performed, what was blocked, what changed and what did not.
5. **Other reception:** the Other may register, misregister, interpret, refuse, cooperate, continue something else or never notice, by their own bodily, historical and social circumstances.
6. **Independent continuation:** the world and other people settle consequences, including responses to actions that neither the user nor focal person controls.
7. **Return of agency:** the human encounters the effects that are phenomenally available and regains control before the machine would have to invent another meaningful human decision.

This is not seven mandatory program modules. It is a test of whether a human act genuinely encountered an independent historical situation. In actual life some elements overlap, recur or remain unresolved. The same world event can become different lived experience for different participants and nothing durable for others.

The next encounter does not need to pay off the earlier one. It needs to be **answerable to its actual history**.

## XIV. MULTIPLE RELATIONS AND THIRD PARTIES ARE NOT DECORATIVE

The user can become important to one person and thereby alter another person's situation; the Other can become responsible to the user while remaining accountable to their children, coworkers, community, employer, household or state. Those commitments are often nonfungible. There is no single universal score that selects whose claim should win.

Consider a worker whose shift and childcare pickup overlap. A visitor asks for time. Saying no may be a skilled act of care for the child, not rejection of the visitor. Saying yes may be welcome conversation, a costly concession, fear of disappointing someone, or an attempt to postpone a worse obligation. A narrator must not explain the choice before causal history warrants it.

A social relation is not merely a dyadic graph edge. A job can constrain several people's activity even when no one is thinking about it. A shared project may survive a particular friendship. Household arrangements may distribute labor invisibly. A religious category may license cruelty as righteousness. An acquaintance may never know the pressure another actor is under. Third parties must possess enough independent activity and historical specificity to produce the effects attributed to them, without requiring complete maximal-fidelity simulation of every named person.

The neighbor's world is therefore neither a solitary character with background extras nor a completed omniscient social graph. It is an **incompletely but causally sufficiently realized social field**, with multiple real centers of activity and historically changing practical relations.

## XV. ORDINARY LIFE IS THE PRIMARY MEDIUM OF COMPRESSION

Rawlsing does not need constant spectacular events. Ordinary time contains an astonishing density of material cause: eating, commuting, paying rent, folding laundry, waiting for a bus, sharing a kitchen, caring for a child, making something, buying food, going to work, losing a shift, taking medicine, praying, cleaning, learning, repairing, being tired, laughing, misremembering.

These are not filler between significant scenes, and not compulsory miniquests. They are the activities in which people become legible and remain opaque. In a mundane practical situation, attention is divided, habits operate, a joke lands sideways, help can be awkward, a third party intrudes, and the same action means different things to different people. More importantly, the world changes materially whether or not the user sees those changes.

Historically compressed ordinary life is not the absence of simulation. It is **computing enough of the causal relations to preserve the conditions for later genuine participation without narrating an invented continuous stream of consciousness**. A year of employment need not be 365 written scenes to alter resources, obligations, skills and forms of anticipation. But the compression must not simply paste “she grew wiser” into a character summary. It must be able to tell what happened sufficiently to justify what becomes possible, unavailable or meaningful next.

The everyday is also the strongest anti-fetish safeguard. Love does not suspend rent. Trauma does not abolish work. An intense scene does not make the child's meal optional. A user who wants only the dramatic or erotic parts of a person's life should confront, not have erased, that person's whole material existence.

## XVI. FIVE CONFLICTS THE CONCEPTION MUST SURVIVE

**1. An unseen arrival.** A visitor approaches an occupied workshop from behind. The worker has a bodily and acquired attentional history; a machine can resolve footstep acoustics and the worker's competing task. The worker may hear but not look, hear and misinterpret, not hear at all, or stop what they are doing. The user may see something of this but not the private reason. A simple co-presence prompt can produce beautiful text; it cannot establish this differentiated encounter. The richer psychological theory must make sensory access *potentially* operative without converting access into mandatory noticed memory.

**2. The unimportant visitor.** Someone asks the neighbor to talk while the neighbor is hurrying to collect a child. There is no guaranteed conversation and no hidden punishment for refusing. The neighbor may be impatient, warm, anxious, resentful or oblivious; their history and current obligations matter. The user may decide to go along, wait, argue or leave. A scene designed to create intimacy would be false precisely where the refusal most clearly demonstrates another life.

**3. Kindness under domination.** The user offers practical help in an institution or household where access, wages, status or authority are unequal. The offer can improve a material condition, expose dependence, threaten someone, or be recruited into an authorized genre of paternalistic discipline. The actor may understand one part and not another. “Help succeeded” cannot certify flourishing; “help refused” cannot certify ingratitude. Real structure, actor meaning, and user interpretation must remain distinct.

**4. A private look and a public falsehood.** The user notices an object and, later, hears an account of its history. The speaker may be truthful, mistaken, evasive or lying. The user's private ATTEND cannot change the person's mind by magic; the object's historical provenance cannot become invented childhood memory; the speaker's testimony cannot overwrite objective truth. Further evidence may revise the user's understanding without revealing any definitive psychological answer.

**5. An absence that is not a cliffhanger.** The human leaves for months. The neighbor's work, relationships, aging body and environment continue. Perhaps a promise was missed; perhaps nobody expected contact. Perhaps the neighbor later writes a message; perhaps they don't. A return must encounter a changed or continuing life, not the platform's emotional strategy for winning another session. No guaranteed closure, intimacy, lesson or misery.

These examples are **open causal demands**, not scripts whose permitted responses are enumerated in advance. Their function is to break a theory that can only simulate the chosen user's centrality.

## XVII. THE FIRST RECIPROCAL DEMAND ON THE CONSTITUTIVE ORGANISM

The organism must not be made smaller to fit NEIGHBOR's present source code; NEIGHBOR must not be made smaller to fit the organism's present response vocabulary. The asymmetry creates two separate developmental demands.

**What NEIGHBOR needs the future organism to become capable of:**

A situated, historically constituted actor who can receive *some but not necessarily all* source-qualified multimodal world signs; whose acquired organization conditions notice, anticipation, practically imaginable alternatives, inhibition and social interpretation; whose multiple life projects genuinely preexist a user prompt; whose conduct can be independently attempted, blocked or realized; whose lived event and subsequent formation are separately sourced; whose relations to institutions, objects and other people can change over time; whose self-account is fallible; and whose growth, decay, defensiveness, love, withdrawal or no-change cannot be reduced to a friendly response.

The organism must give the encounter a person, not a menu of traits. **Its already richer psychological capabilities remain obligations of future UX consumption**, including the ones the present interface cannot express.

**What that organism needs NEIGHBOR to become capable of:**

A human participant who can make more than scripted dialogue choices; a social/material situation with admissible, independent conditions; faithful visual, auditory, bodily and temporal access rather than a flat co-presence tag; lawful handling of what the human actually attempted; genuine control return; multiple differently situated viewers; a private phenomenal field that cannot rewrite actor causality; world and institutional consequences not generated by a narrator; chance for a relationship without guarantee; offscreen access and absence; a channel for actor-originated and third-party activity; and an epistemically honest way to encounter outwardly unrevealing actions.

This includes the prospect that NEIGHBOR itself will have to change after C-T1. The organism may show that some encounter-side aspirations are too narratable, too transparent, too dyadic, too voluntarist, or too dependent on an idealized user's helpfulness. Those are possible reasons to **reconstruct the whole vision again**, not to force the organism into obedience.

At this stage no source-entitled answer exists for whether protected P4/P6, the newer organism's ledger, a transformed owner, or a physically separated single-authority transaction system should be canonical. **One actual causal history is the target; the persistence implementation remains undecided.** Selecting it by convenience before the reciprocal theoretical transformation would betray the work.

## XVIII. THERE IS NO FINISHED NEIGHBOR, AND NO FINISHED THEORY OF NEIGHBOR

The deepest ethical expression in the original SOROK research—another person is not exhausted by the relation, the relation is not exhausted by either person's experience, and the world is not exhausted by any one person's view—is worth retaining precisely because it cannot be made into proof of machine consciousness or the master formula from which every module is mechanically derived.

Theology here supplies a provisional language of decentering and the neighbor's claim, not an unquestionable metaphysics or a measure of righteousness. A materialist translation remains necessary: distinct embodied perspectives, independently caused activity, historically constituted social possibilities, non-ownership of another's action, and accountability to consequences one does not author. The system has no right to announce that a human has learned to love.

Even this articulation risks a false new center. “Participation without possession” may become a slogan that conceals difficult product questions: What makes an ordinary encounter compelling without manipulating curiosity? How far can perception and action be simulated without overwhelming latency and cost? What should be available to a user who lacks prior domain recognition? What kind of social time actually permits responsibility without captivity? How should privacy constrain the longitudinal observation of a human's praxis? Can a fictional person's irreducibility be made computationally operative without pretending a finite state is metaphysically inexhaustible? What happens if users prefer the comforting puppet, and real alpha tests falsify our preferred experience?

These are not failures to be hidden beneath rhetoric. **They are exactly what the next round must be allowed to rearrange.**

The current strongest re-articulation is therefore:

**NEIGHBOR is the playable, historically situated encounter with a life that could have been one's own but is not one's property. It places an actual human's limited, meaningful and morally undetermined activity into a world produced by other histories, bodies, work, institutions and relationships. There, a computationally constituted Other continues to act from a deep, changing, partly inaccessible organization of life, not from the player's demand for response. Material actions become events; events are differently perceived, interpreted and lived; their world consequences and personal afterlives produce new possibilities without guaranteeing affinity, knowledge, virtue, story or improvement. The medium learns from the human's actual engagement, producing a revisable user model that shapes their situated narrative perception; Nadia independently learns a different, actor-local model of that human through what her own body and psyche permit her to encounter. Neither model becomes a right to author the human's choices or to disclose the Other's private psyche. The medium reveals neither a master psyche nor a moral scoreboard, but gives the human the practical burden and possibility of acting, understanding imperfectly, being answerable, and sometimes being changed.**

If the user comes to know this particular fictional person rather than collect a generic rule about otherness, and if the experience makes the material contingency and dignity of real human lives harder to dismiss, Rawlsing may have accomplished something that ordinary character games and AI companions do not. **Whether it can do so is an empirical product question, not a theorem established by having written a beautiful constitution.**

**Next theory motion:** the Constitutive Organism's C-T1 must attack this N-T1 from the side of real emergent psychology; NEIGHBORMULCH's next N-T2 must rewrite the whole document afterward. In parallel, recover the remaining source-bearing original UX articulations that could change this first synthesis. No model representation or legacy code path is untouchable; no historically achieved causal distinction is casually disposable.
