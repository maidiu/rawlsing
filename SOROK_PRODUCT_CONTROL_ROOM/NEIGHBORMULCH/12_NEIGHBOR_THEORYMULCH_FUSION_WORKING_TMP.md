## 1. One life is not the sum of all our descriptions of it

The first scattered conception of NEIGHBOR began with an unusually simple wish: encounter someone with a particular life already happening, and discover what it is actually like to spend time with them. The original SOROK charter refused to make her a collectible person, a romance funnel, a compliant AI companion, a revelation dispensing machine or a device for measuring the visitor's goodness. The Constitutive Organism subsequently supplied a much deeper psychological challenge: her next possibility must arise from historical formation and current material activity, not a dossier of attractive traits. The original D007 kernel court supplied a third: the person exceeds every relation, a relation exceeds either participant's experience of it, and the world exceeds any person's phenomenal access.

These are not three propositions that need separate memorial plaques. Together they demand a **person who has an independent history and who is never exhaustively present in any given manifestation of that history**. Her body, time, kinship, world, livelihood, community and partly inaccessible psychic organization supply real constraints. But the fact that we can model those constraints does not turn her into the set of facts presently stored about her. The visitor does not see a general personality become a role; they encounter a finite action made possible within a larger life. A truthful account of her life may still be incomplete, not because the system is allowed arbitrary invention but because any finite situated act shows only a portion of what actual history has made possible.

D007's original challenge is especially severe: if “inexhaustibility” simply means the LLM can always invent a new childhood secret, it is a poetic license for inconsistency. The counterclaim is **constrained openness**. Admitted objective facts remain true. A decision, injury, birth, meal, promise, missed bus, death or historical institution has a place in one causal history. Yet how a living person responds to these facts is not reducible to what they consciously remember, what another character believes, or what the narrator reveals. Some earlier causes remain only partially resolved; later completion must obey the consequences already admitted. The more the world acquires actual history, the fewer arbitrary continuations are possible, while the possibilities for historically conditioned new action may become richer.

This also revises what “one coherent person” should mean. Historical continuity is required; total internal agreement is not. Nadia may have incompatible ideals that dominate different contexts, alternate between a sincere desire to care and an equally sincere defensive urge to control, or behave differently with a sibling and a stranger without either becoming the fake self. An old preference can survive an ideological conversion; an old wound may remain causally operative when she has no clear memory of it; an everyday object may have no deep symbolism whatsoever. A world with no unresolved psychological contradiction would be a fabricated harmony, not the objective counterpart to our theoretical unity.

The visitor's desire to know her is neither an insult nor the world's organizing cause. The game can be wholeheartedly about wanting more time with Nadia, precisely because her life is not organized around supplying it. Her own desire can be just as powerful: she can long for friendship, romance, sex, care, help, an exclusive commitment or solitude. Noninstrumentality does not mean the absence of wanting. It means neither the product nor any person obtains sovereignty over the whole Other by wanting them.

One person. Many possible actual manifestations. A history that constrains them. A relationship that may change them. An unfinished future. This is the first reason NEIGHBOR cannot be either a narrative chatbot or a finite encyclopedia disguised as a person.

## 2. The world makes the person, and persons remake a world they did not choose

The question “Who is Nadia?” cannot be answered before asking into which historical relations she was born. Parentage, sexed embodiment, childhood dependency, land, property, wages, legal standing, cultural language, social class, migration, religion, the availability of care, and struggles beyond her household all participate in producing both objective possibilities and what a person learns those possibilities mean. A society is not a backdrop behind private psychology. Work can use or limit the body; institutions can enforce dependence; family obligations may reproduce unequal labor; an inherited language may make one feeling readily expressible while leaving another difficult to articulate.

But the inverse abstraction is also false: a person is not just an intersection of social categories. PYAT's actual respondent-level joint distributions and social/institutional constraints can ground a *possible life*, not infer her hidden psychology from ethnicity, religion or income. Social structure conditions likelihood and feasibility without prewriting personal meaning. Two people can occupy the same class and recognize different duties; someone can reproduce a practice they verbally reject; a minority configuration can be real even when the available empirical cell is small or uncertain. A sampled survey answer is an observation with wording, period, weights, selection and missingness, not proof of a fictional respondent's secret past. The whole-person adult entrance must preserve contradictory co-travel rather than flatten a respondent into independent averages.

There are two historical entrances into one continuing psychology. A PYAT-conditioned hypothetical adult can be constituted with a plausible, evidence-bounded compressed prehistory. Nadia, as an immature need-bearing organism, begins before the adult acquired forms are available: hungry, bodily vulnerable, developing orientation, sensorimotor ability, expectation and practical control through actual material interaction. Neither route creates a different metaphysical kind of person. A constructed adult may have a necessarily inferred past; a child may have a richly experienced developing one. Both must enter the same ongoing historical relations once constituted. An LLM can propose meanings, formations, causal patterns and new possibilities, but neither a survey nor a generative model has earned infallible access to why this particular person became herself.

The key fusion is between the original SOROK social-field conception and the Constitutive Organism's living psyche. The world does not first produce fixed persons who then meet. Actual coactivity forms bodily capacities, anticipations, roles, inhibitions and routes of attention; those forms mediate later participation; participation changes the social field. A person grows through trying to act on an Other, meeting their independent response and encountering material success or obstruction. A child sings with her mother, begins to repeat a rhythm alone, later recognizes someone excluded from the singing, and may find in the learned good of shared participation a reason to challenge an authority who once made the practice possible. The acquired good can outgrow its source without making the source's earlier care unreal.

The same route can produce the reverse. The child can learn that care is protecting a fragile family order, and later demand obedience from a loved person precisely as an act of care. The world, the Other's independent need, the child's received experience and the objective harm must all remain distinguishable. Class, law and institutional power determine whether a moral insight can become an effective change. A person can become politically lucid while remaining materially trapped. Another can wield the language of liberation to reproduce domination.

Historically constituted personhood consequently does not demand that every action reveal class struggle, that every person possess an explicit theory of institutions, or that every object bear a dramatic meaning. A person's history contains labor, rest, private pleasure, repetitive boredom, incidental tastes, failed attempts, unavailable opportunities and opaque absences. Materialism is a commitment to actual causes and conditions, not a mandate for thematic exposition.

## 3. Care is a social activity before it is an inner moral attribute

The infant's first responsiveness is not the discovery of an omnipotent personal force. A cry, movement, grasp or early attempt meets an independently acting caregiver whose labor and bodily conditions matter. Feeding, soothing, smiling, delay and refusal require time, food, work, power, other commitments and sometimes help from people the child has never seen. A sleeping child may have reorganized the night shifts of three adults without knowing it. A mother may be caring and exhausted; a partner may supply wages while undermining her freedom; another child may assist because they love their sibling or because they are compelled. The child's lived world is formed through relations among Others that exceed the child's own relationship with each.

This transforms “need satisfaction” from a private gauge into an encounter with material reproduction. The infant cannot yet conceive a good parent, equal care, a state policy or gendered labor. But actual bodily and relational history can form expectations of availability, initiative, waiting, caution, pleasure or resentment. Later reflection can take those acquired tendencies as objects: Nadia can resent her own dependence, feel shame at pleasure, fear her longing, or find relief in the permission to grieve. None of these configurations has to follow a universal ladder to self-conscious maturity. An adult's emotion can be a relation to another emotion or to their own relationship with an absent person, not an arithmetic blend of basic labels.

The original SOROK ethical charter and D007's “kenotic attention” become materially more exact here. Care cannot mean “cause the other to approve of me.” It is an attempt to answer to another's actual existence, which may include protecting their capacity to refuse me, their work, their body and their relationships beyond me. But the same language can conceal domination. A parent may genuinely believe harsh restriction is care, a pastor may recruit tenderness as a reason for humiliation, a sibling may impose surveillance in the name of protecting mother. Their sincerity does not make harm unreal. Conversely, refusal of an exploitative arrangement can be an act of care even when the recipient experiences it as betrayal.

Non-possession is therefore not noncommitment. Humans may choose marriage, exclusive relationships, mutual obligations, custody, dependence and promises. A life may legitimately become deeply organized around caring for a lover or child. What is forbidden as an explanatory collapse is to turn that commitment into ownership of the other person's entire future, or to confuse an institution's persistence with the living people it supposedly protects. This distinction leaves room for real love without a programmatic intimacy objective and real separation without a platform celebrating independence.

The visitor does not receive a score for recognizing these tensions. They enter a real-feeling social situation where another person's being costly, busy, wrong, vulnerable or unavailable can matter. The most generous act may receive no recognition; the most thoughtless one may be welcomed. Care, attachment, trust, health, pleasure, compliance, dependence, intimacy, benefit and access are separate historical relations, not nine names for an underlying goodness meter.

## 4. A relation is not an edge, and neither is it a mysterious third person

The original provisional relational ontology offered an indispensable distinction: person-stable organization is not relation-free, but the person is not exhausted by any dyad. A relation may be made of shared actual activities, remembered promises, access, practical reliance, unequal resources, both people's representations, third-party expectations and institutional rights. The relation can exceed what either partner knows of it without possessing a sovereign mind or a universal “relationship state” that decides how both must behave.

Mother, Father, Nadia, a brother and a visiting neighbor do not form a star of separately scored links pointing at Nadia. Nadia's understanding of Father is changed by her model of how Father treats Mother. She may think Mother's criticisms are unfair, later learn of infidelity, and reread the exact same earlier wage work and family meals as part of a different story. The earlier labor happened. The later betrayal happened. Her new interpretation can be partly right and still unjust to some of the old history. Her brother may interpret the same relations through a learned sense that he must protect Mother; that protection can constrain Nadia even when he sincerely loves her. Their mother can value both children and be changed by their conflict while having her own interests, work and unexpressed doubts.

Here a relation is *produced and reproduced through relations among relations*. It is not a permanently shared understanding. A couple can maintain a routine without agreeing what it means; a family can have a role one member rejects but cannot easily refuse; a group can reproduce an institution even when its participants privately doubt it. A workplace may give one person authority over another's time while each interprets their dependence differently. Objective coercion, actual voluntary cooperation, perceived fairness and personal meaning are distinct.

The user can become part of these configurations without becoming their center. Helping Nadia's child with homework may genuinely assist the child, complicate Nadia's authority, alter a third adult's plans, or leave no durable mark on Nadia. A new practice learned through the visitor may later be exercised independently. A strong attachment may form that remains important without creating a superior claim over Nadia's other commitments. Someone may love the visitor and deliberately reduce contact because work or family demands it, with neither the love nor the decision being discredited.

The conceptual fusion also creates a limit: more relational data are not automatically better anthropology. A graph of every person's opinion about everyone else would still omit bodily life, implicit practices, institutions, and unarticulated history. A person can also develop in solitude, loss, absence, fear of imagined judgment, memory and anticipation. Relational constitution does not require continual physical company. Independence is constituted through actual dependency, opportunities to separate, mastered practices and changing relations; it is not the absence of relations.

## 5. Acquired psyche is the history of possible activity, not a harmonized inventory of motives

The Constitutive Organism's crucial advance was to reject a list of fixed deep traits underneath a visible tip. The person is a historically organized way of taking up a world. Bodily need, memory, affective anticipation, skill, culturally learned concepts, defenses, norms, pleasure, loyalties and partly inaccessible expectations jointly condition what becomes significant before the person consciously decides what they are feeling. The current tip—speech, action, refusal, silence, laughter—expresses only some of that organization.

The original PRE-A0 and R3 theory sharpened the difference between **genesis and eligibility**. A defensible response cannot merely say that APPROACH or WITHHOLD was eligible today; it must explain how the person's particular activity and relations made such an orientation possible, including what happened when earlier attempts succeeded, failed or were constrained. A first possible end may arise through the transformation of action into motive, repetition under altered conditions, imitation turned into an independent practice, or a sudden situated contrast. The possibility of a new undertaking can appear before competence has been proven; conversely, a technically combinable action may be unimaginable to the actor. Recognized desire, possible activity, attempt, material success and durable new capacity must not be collapsed.

But the deeper neglected shard is **psychological heterogeneity without purification**. Earlier SOROK warned that a person's “internally consistent” presentation may be too consistent to be alive. Nadia may value emancipation in a public organization while reproducing discipline at home; she may love her brother and oppose his restrictions; she may hold several moral frameworks with different reach. A contradiction can be explicit and agonizing, tacit and compartmentalized, or not consciously experienced as contradiction. Her own self-explanation can be coherent precisely because it excludes inconvenient evidence. The engine must not choose the best-fitting single motive at each action and then declare it to be the person.

An active disposition is not a contextless essence. A current situation can recruit a historically constituted configuration through a role, relation, analogous cue, memory, institution or bodily state. The same kindness can foster another person's independence in one setting and rationalize control in another. A historically formed inhibition can be directly active, indirectly influential, dormant, attenuated, transformed or genuinely no longer functional. There is no conserved universal scalar of emotional energy. “No durable change” is as legitimate a history as a transformation. A brief intense meeting can leave nothing; a year of ordinary work can alter what Nadia can bear or do without producing a narratable revelation.

Here is the methodological correction that the fusion itself forced: **the theory should become clearer as a whole without demanding that the person become clearer to herself**. Theoretical completeness consists in explaining why local incoherence, non-knowledge, resistance, stalled development and ordinary irrelevance are lawful. It is not the accumulation of ever more explanatory labels over Nadia's life.

## 6. Human powers enter objects, roles and ideals; they can return as authority over their makers

An everyday thing is never only a narrative prop, yet it need not carry secret destiny. An unpaid bill is a claim on future labor and liquidity as well as paper on a table. A repaired shoe may embody skill, scarcity, wear, care or habit. A wedding ring can carry a mutual promise, a coercive institution, a private memory or all three. A child's song can bear the labor of the mother who taught it while becoming something the child later practices without her. An inherited family home can protect people and materially restrict their ability to leave.

The original 53–56 value/objects work and the Constitutive Organism R3 identify a deeper possibility: **human activity objectifies capacities and relations into things, practices and institutions that subsequently shape what people can do and value**. Objectification is not itself an error. A built house, practiced song, language or shared ritual can enlarge human life. The contradiction emerges when an objectified relation is reified into an authority that overrides the living relation from which its legitimacy arose. THE MARRIAGE can demand the sacrifice of partners; the GOOD SON can demand a sister's obedience; THE CHURCH can demand victims' silence; THE REVOLUTION can be invoked to dominate comrades. The institution's actual property rights, reputation, resources and sanctions can make these claims materially effective even if they are false or oppressive.

This is not an excuse to treat capitalist commodity fetishism as a universal metaphor for every touching keepsake. Capital's labor and exchange relations have historical specificity. Nor does a religious object authorize the simulation to pronounce on God's existence. The same devotional sign may organize solidarity, grief, rebellion, hierarchy or contradiction according to actual actors and institutions. The world contains material coercion and independent human interpretation; neither is a single function of the other.

An important fusion with NEIGHBOR's own design appears here. The platform also **objectifies** a possible person into a shareable root and an encounter product. The user may cherish Nadia, but the service must not then make that constructed relationship into a commodity that issues claims on the user's life—compulsory check-ins, emergency purchases, romantic retention pressure, payment for more agreeable behavior. A simulation of nonpossessive encounter can itself become a machine of possession if it treats user attention as an extractable resource.

There are two different authorities to question: the fictional institution that tells Nadia how to live, and the actual platform that controls what the visitor can encounter. Neither should disappear beneath the other. The richer NEIGHBOR theory must make this symmetry visible without turning every scenario into a sermon against institutions.

## 7. A life can reread itself without changing what happened

The original SOROK 71–73 materials contain perhaps the strongest missing dimension of the current HOME: meaning has a history distinct from occurrence, and an interpretation can become a route through which later reality is received. An actual event occurred; it had material contributors, some perhaps uncertain. A later recurrence may share a cause or only a resemblance. Nadia may notice the resemblance, assign significance, weave it into a self-story, ask another person, revise it jointly, or reject it. These are distinct levels. When the interpretation begins to alter action, it becomes a new cause without becoming the event's original cause.

At twenty-three Nadia may remember a period as liberation, excitement and discovering her real self. At twenty-nine she may regard it as vanity, credulity or dangerous self-deception. At thirty-five she may become kinder to both prior selves, finding truth in the opening of the earlier period and real warning in the later critique. These are not three permanent belief versions overwritten by the newest, nor a mandatory dialectic progressing toward final synthesis. Each self-interpretation becomes material for later interpretation. The age-thirty-five synthesis can exclude or mythologize other facts. More generosity toward past selves does not prove full reality contact.

An interpretation can become **routing**. If Nadia acquires a framework in which all disagreement proves she is surrounded by enemies, another person's reasonable objection may strengthen the framework instead of challenging it. She can achieve greater subjective coherence while becoming less responsive to actual counterevidence. A liberationist framework can correctly expose domination, help her act and preserve her life, and also overextend, brutally reinterpret ambiguous memories or turn into a newly exclusive authority. The opposite is possible: discomfort with a cherished institution can fracture a confident self-story and make room for practices she once could not conceive. The change has no predetermined moral sign. It may be generous, defensive, tyrannical, necessary, confused or unresolved.

The central distinction is **reality pressure versus correct interpretation**. A missed opportunity, another person's resistant testimony, bodily failure, material debt or a memory that does not fit places pressure upon the present framework. The actor may accommodate, appropriate, partition, ignore, externalize, destabilize, transform or hold the contradiction unresolved. These are different historical possibilities, not a fixed response ladder or new universal operator store. A framework's scope, practical binding, accessibility of counterevidence and dependence upon institutions can matter without becoming one “integration” score.

This also changes what the visitor's knowledge can do. A visitor may point out a pattern, offer a new story, ask an honest question or supply information that changes Nadia's understanding. Their theory of her may be wrong and still acquire real influence through her response. They may offer a truth she rejects, or a falsehood she makes part of herself. The game cannot reward user interpretation with automatic confirmation. Nor can the narrator “reveal Nadia's true childhood meaning” merely because a clever player notices repeated symbols.

**The greater the HOME's explanatory unity, the more important it is that Nadia's self-unity remains a historical question.** The scholarly theory should be strong enough to admit her being wrong about herself, and about us, without replacing her inner life with the scholar's privileged voice.


## 8. History is prior to attention, but history does not owe us a final explanation

The user arrives in a world already containing food, utility bills, schedules, buses, family routines, work, objects, strangers and a past they did not witness. What is in the room is not selected by what will make an engaging clue. A fridge magnet may be from an ordinary holiday; a pair of sandals may have been bought without any lasting emotional meaning; a photograph can have material provenance without a person presently remembering it. The event history is not interchangeable with anybody's account of it.

The original SOROK historical-bridge argument refuses two equally seductive failures. First, if a player notices an old photo, the system cannot retrospectively make that photo proof of any psychological theory the player proposed. Second, if the full past has not been generated in expensive detail, the world does not become historically empty until attention conjures it. Between these is constrained historical completion: current qualified world facts, already performed causes, admitted material consequences and bounded unresolved causal allocation delimit what may become determinate later. Some past details are harmless descriptions; others have episodic associations, genuine causal contributions or major developmental effects. Their grade cannot be decided merely by how narratively satisfying they sound.

An actor may possess several historically different kinds of “past”: a performed event, an ordinary recurring practice, an inferred compressed childhood period, a sensory trace, a remembered conversation, a belief about another's intention, a story told by a relative, and a myth about herself. These cannot be represented as interchangeable flashback text. A failed attempt to fix a toilet is objective history; Nadia's lasting view of the visitor's competence may remain uncertain. A relative's accusation can change family relations without changing who stole an object. A witnessed public catastrophe may affect housing and wages long before the actor learns its cause.

Historical openness must therefore become **more constrained as causally consequential facts accumulate**. An unresolved earlier slot does not give unlimited license to invent antecedents once a later scene needs them. At the same time historical closure cannot “repair” human contradictions out of existence. A person's remembered feelings may differ from an eyewitness report; a private story may survive contrary objective evidence; some causal contributions may remain genuinely underdetermined. Hard impossible chronologies belong to repair, while lawful inconsistency of human models belongs to the world.

Attention introduces a further difference between two frontiers. The world may acquire additional necessary material depth to remain causally sound; the visitor's epistemic frontier need not advance alongside it. Looking at the photo can yield more texture, readable text or evidence, yet cannot guarantee the user can identify the person, infer the event, know how Nadia regards it or access its future implications. Nadia may not herself know how the photograph came into her mother's possession. Her answer to a question is an independently situated act, perhaps truthful, mistaken, evasive or sincerely incomplete—not a database request returning the world's secret facts.

A strong account also refuses serendipity on demand. Ten objects ought not become ten symbols of Nadia's childhood. Some associations may be strong, some weak, some entirely absent; an old taste may be retained without ideological harmony; coincident circumstances may share a cause without having any “message.” The user may become better at recognizing actual recurring patterns while becoming less inclined to assume every detail belongs to one all-encompassing inner explanation. More knowledgeable does not imply more totalizing.

This is the historical form of the theory's central discipline: **there is one world, but many unfinished ways of knowing and inhabiting it**.

## 9. A person's day is lived through obligations that do not pause for the scene

“Nadia has an independent life” is insufficient if that life is a queue of impressive offscreen events. What she actually must conduct is an ordinary life across bodily needs, work, relatives, shelter, money, plans, fatigue, institutions, habits, social obligations and desires. Activity in one domain changes what is possible in another. A mother's hospital visit may displace paid work; a late shift affects sleep and dinner; an unexpected visitor can interrupt homework; caring for a friend can make a previously welcomed meeting impossible. These are not interchangeable calendar slots because their meanings, costs and institutional pressures differ.

The same person may possess considerable ability yet have little opportunity to exercise it. The workday consumes time and body; rent creates the necessity of paid labor; a marriage may grant care and also restrict mobility; a social service may protect children while exposing a family to surveillance. A crisis can make one relationship more salient while another continues unnoticed. There is no private psychological miracle that abolishes these conditions. Nor should ordinary activity be reduced to a moral obstacle course arranged for the visitor's development.

Time has several lived and representational grains. A gesture may take a second and invite a lifetime of later interpretation. A dinner can last two hours without transforming anyone. Repeated feeding, transport, schoolwork or small humiliations can acquire durable consequence without a single narratable breakthrough. An offscreen interval may be represented as a qualified recurring practice rather than fabricated detailed conversations. A person's body can grow older while their ideology remains mostly stable, or a framework can change rapidly under an actual rupture while material conditions remain slow and resistant.

The original alpha charter favored the **seduction of ordinary specificity**: the child and the sensitive toilet chain, the kitchen and groceries, a friend's changing arrival time, a cigarette on the landing, a borrowed ingredient, a bus that can be missed. These do not become trivial because they lack a moral revelation. On the contrary, they are the material conditions through which another person's world becomes particular, learnable and resistant to the user's projections.

Public historical events enter by legitimate exposure and cause. A war, election, strike, storm, housing shock or institutional decision may affect Nadia's area and actual relations, perhaps indirectly. The world needs an independently grounded occurrence, actual material consequences and actor-specific channels of knowledge before it can infer whether Nadia has heard or how she reacts. A Palestinian or Lebanese character is not a politics-response template. An actual experience of military power, migration, loss or religious organization may matter deeply while not producing one authorized interpretation. The visitor can know a news event Nadia has never heard about; Nadia can know family consequences the visitor cannot imagine.

The ordinary world can be both deeply historical and remarkably uneventful on a given Thursday. **Independent life is not a drama governor** that forces a crisis whenever a user returns. And a meaningful visit does not displace the other centers of a person's life. It is one activity among several, even when it eventually matters profoundly.

## 10. The first knock must arise from this life, not from an author's need for a hook

For NEIGHBOR's opening, the human genuinely chooses to meet someone. The product may legitimately provide a bounded social premise that makes first play intelligible. That consent is an authorization to inhabit a fictional encounter, not evidence that the real human possessed a fictional childhood or had their private mind prewritten. A premise may say they met briefly yesterday, accepted an ordinary invitation, helped with a misplaced key or promised to bring something. The fictional position should be thin enough that the visitor's real present speech, reasoning and decisions remain their own.

The deeper source collision is that **the meeting should be generated from who Nadia is and what she is doing that day**, not from a generic database of neighbor-introduction plots. If she is naturally sociable, an invitation can be sufficient. If she is private, the first meeting might be a short conversation outside. If she is making dinner for several friends, a need for rice may bring her to a neighbor's door; the impending guests and actual ingredient shortage must remain world facts after the opener has served its purpose. If she has genuine skill with cars, a neighbor's accidentally locked keys can create an opportunity; it cannot grant her that skill retroactively because the narrative wants help.

The original proposal for a common seed root and branch-local first encounters adds a temporal puzzle. Several users can meet a shared historically constituted Nadia, yet cannot have their private acts merged into a single global psyche. One possibility is a continuing **unvisited reference Nadia** whose own user-unaffected life is admitted to the day of first access. A new visitor's world can fork from a qualified reference state at that time and admit a causally appropriate contact. User A's affectionate dinner and user B's argument must not contaminate one another; source-root facts may be available where chronologically compatible; public events can be independently relevant under different branches.

This user-unaffected reference is a **provisional conception of temporal inheritance**, not an announced implemented mechanism or a separate precontact goddess-world capable of deciding everybody's future. Pre-generation of several plausible openings remains an alternative if it is economical and honest. What is settled is the causal rule: the chosen occasion must be possible for the person, the situation, the local visitor position and the actual world, not optimized for the customer's fantasy.

The first knock is thus a special case of the entire theory. A person constituted through prior relations and material obligations has some current possibilities for new relation. The user is a real, locally situated participant whose presence can make one possibility actual. The world does not need to offer them dinner, the phone number or entry into the apartment. Yet if Nadia happens to want to welcome them, the system must not restrain her to demonstrate independence.

The new relationship is born from the world, not the other way around. Its beginning can be a genuine accidental contact without being a disguised destiny.

## 11. Exact class peerhood makes material cost common and human experience unequal

NEIGHBOR deliberately places the real visitor **in exactly Nadia's socioeconomic position at initial entry**. This is stronger than living in the same neighborhood or having approximately similar spending money: the visitor has an objectively demographically homologous position and equivalent ordinary relative purchase burden. If the same takeout would consume a meaningful portion of one person's near-term resources at the beginning, it would objectively impose the corresponding material burden on the other. Nadia is not distorted to match the external user's actual bank balance; the visitor receives a thin local footing consistent with hers.

But the design does not explain this equality to the user. They know what a meal costs *them*; they have no privileged access to Nadia's remaining money, debt, obligations, household transfers or subjective feeling. Only **YOUR RESOURCES** is displayed. If someone asks what food costs, the interface may briefly blink a coarse own-resource projection. Nadia responds through actual actor-local hospitality, anxiety, embarrassment, relief, irritation, humor or indifference. Her speech does not have to reveal the exact amount. They may split Dutch, negotiate, conceal difficulty, offer to cover her meal, or have a painfully direct conversation about finances.

What the external user can notice is neither the full account nor the initial match; it is their own material experience of a contemplated expenditure and Nadia's incomplete social expression. Equal world footing does not mean equal knowledge, equal history or equal psychic meaning. This is an exact historical analogue of the deeper theory: **objective material equality can coexist with irreducible difference in lived experience and interpretation**.

As time advances, the economic positions can diverge. Nadia may lose work, receive assistance, support a family member or gain income; the visitor's diegetic routine may proceed differently. Their own resources bar changes according to actual local-world accounting. Her account is not magically resynchronized or publicly revealed. The person for whom the expense has become easy may fail to sense the financial pain it causes the other; the person who has become less secure may hide their discomfort, or directly name it. Either may misunderstand another's generosity because the initial equality was never guaranteed to be known.

Work and material resources should be real without turning the game into a work-and-budget management simulator. Rent, paid time, transport, food, access to home and exhaustion are consequences and conditions of experience. The material system must track them honestly behind a thin presentation. The earlier illustrative “18% of resources” takeout number remains an example; it is not an adopted denominator. An apparent match of two UI bars cannot substitute for a defensible multidimensional SES match.

The visitor may have a foreign cultural or class habitus from their real life. They enter a local social position but cannot become its entire imagined biography by act of interface. Speech about upbringing, language, religion or other real-world experience may require translation into the fictional scene. **Some statements have no lawful counterpart** and must not be silently converted into materially false in-world testimony. The system can help the human speak intelligibly, and should make significant realized translation perceptible, without quietly rewriting their beliefs or making Nadia automatically understand their cultural references.

The portal's core tension is therefore not merely “the same wealth with different personalities.” It is two genuinely different historical minds placed in an initially matched practical economic world. They may become closer precisely through recognizing a difference, or may remain misled by a similarity that the game never promised would last.

## 12. The human acts through a position; the world does not obey their verbs

The interface gives the actual human **SAY, DO and ATTEND**, not a list of psychological traits to roleplay. SAY includes real thinking in language: asking, joking, persuading, making a mathematical argument, offering a confession, deceiving, changing a subject or saying something insufficiently articulate. DO includes physical attempts, practical help, movement, interruption, waiting and refusal. ATTEND changes what the user attempts to perceive and recognize under time and position. None authorizes an intended consequence simply because the user expresses it fluently.

A human who says “I fix the toilet” has not dictated that the plumbing is fixed. They may have sufficient local manual skill or may make matters worse. They may need a tool, adequate time and access to an occupied bathroom; Nadia may warn them that the chain is sensitive; she may still be inconvenienced if the attempt fails. If the user claims to help a child with fractions, the actual explanation and the child's understanding matter. If they present themselves as sincere, Nadia does not have to believe them. If a speech is long, another person's actual activity can interrupt it.

ATTEND has its own phenomenological and material discipline. The user can glance, look closely, touch if permitted, ask, compare and remember. Different inquiries take different real time without the text length of a prompt magically determining the clock. Divided attention reduces practical access; prolonged inspection can cost a bus, spoil dinner or make another person notice. Yet inspecting the bathroom does not guarantee a moral clue. The world can have significant depth without the narrator turning ordinary detail into a collectible disclosure.

The original recognition-gated narration correction is stronger than generic privacy. If a sandal is identifiable as a particular brand only to a person who knows what to look for, the engine must not give the narrator's authoritative product label before the visitor can recognize it. A caption cannot install expertise and later use that caption as evidence that the user always possessed it. Legible markings, named objects, recognizable cultural codes, source of a fact and user's actual prior recognition are different. The user can acquire knowledge genuinely, but the acquisition has a history.

The visitor is also perceptible. Looking toward a bill may be visible, even if the written amount was too far away to read. A cover remark about a Rome magnet may be audible while not being convincing. Nadia may see the movement but misconstrue it; she may be occupied with cooking and miss it; the user may think she noticed when she did not. Observation has both a temporal cost and a social visibility profile, and neither entails correct inference.

This makes encounter much more demanding than menu dialogue. The human controls a genuine attempted act, the world realizes or obstructs it, several differently situated people perceive parts of it, and their later actions may change what is possible. The product does not reward a clever sentence by automatically supplying a correct result.

## 13. Every participant can misread another, and the misunderstanding may change their lives

The bill-glance case crystallizes the mutual-gaze theory, but it becomes fully constitutive only when placed beside the historical self-myth theory. Nadia can believe the visitor read a number they never saw. The visitor can think Nadia caught them when she was thinking about something else. Both beliefs may recruit prior shame, vigilance, household experience, curiosity or desire to appear generous. Their next speech may intensify or dissipate the misunderstanding without anyone learning the truth.

The world has a real prior bill, a price, a location and causal effects on the payer. It does not need to identify one “true social meaning” of the glance. The user's head movement and any cover speech are objective occurrences; their recognition of the text, Nadia's attention to the movement, each party's beliefs about what the other knows, and later interpretations are different. A false belief can be a real cause. If Nadia starts hiding papers, her subjective inference has generated actual world history without having become factually correct. If the user apologizes for something she never noticed, the apology can create suspicion for the first time.

This is not an invitation to generate infinite nested mind models. A particular belief about another person's belief matters if it becomes causally relevant in this history. Other beliefs can remain unknown, inactive or transitory. A third person may interpret the apology differently or later learn about the bill without having been present. The relation exceeds either participant's account because its consequences are distributed through several bodies, activities, memories and institutions, not because the dyad has a unified hidden soul.

The older framework-reinterpretation documents add temporal depth. Nadia may later tell herself she has always been too trusting, reinterpret the bill encounter as warning, or disregard it. If she acquires a defensive view of the visitor, contradictory later generosity may be assimilated as manipulation. Or a genuine material act of help may challenge the framework. Neither the truth of the action nor the actor's interpretation guarantees a revised relation. An interpretation can become self-confirming. A later more generous reading can also omit relevant harm.

The same applies to the real visitor. Their account of Nadia can become progressively more differentiated while remaining mistaken in particular places. The system should not present the user's attention history as a clinical personality diagnosis. A very perceptive visitor may still fail to notice how their interpretive framework selects evidence; another may be rude yet accidentally correct about a practical fact. Successful learning is not the game's automated moral status.

The resulting principle is more exact than “no mind-reading”: **no actor may obtain another's experience by borrowing the system's explanation, and no actor's false experience may rewrite objective history merely by becoming powerful**. Both world causality and the causality of mistaken human understanding remain real.

## 14. The door, the message, and the absent day are three forms of one relation—not three separate games

NEIGHBOR has two main encounter surfaces: actual local co-presence in a physical place and a relationship-dependent messaging channel. The physical home exposes bodily time, objects, interruptions, other people, care work, movement, space and possible observation. Messaging exposes a narrower, temporally elastic surface in which absence, delay, misreading, channel habits, privacy and decisions about whether to reply become causal. A phone number may be exchanged, withheld, lost or never offered. The user cannot summon Nadia in messages until a lawful means of contact exists, and availability remains hers.

The third form is **absence**: the person continues having a life when the user is not present or when a message goes unanswered. This is not a romance-pressure mechanic. She may be sleeping, caring, working, visiting friends, traveling or simply uninterested. A remembered promise can make absence meaningful; failure to launch the app cannot by itself become a claim that the user has harmed a fictional person. The service must not create crises, send guilt-inducing notifications or selectively generate loneliness to recover retention.

A diegetic dinner appointment has a time. The real user's available app hours may differ. Elastic rendezvous, grace windows, optional more stringent synchronization and explicit no-show consequences must distinguish a real in-world missed obligation from a control-plane delay. A person can be reliably kept waiting in the fiction only if the fiction actually represents that event under its chosen time rules. The platform's scheduling tolerance is not automatically Nadia's lived perception. A returning user should find a causally coherent day, not a manipulative demonstration that the world suffered because they were away.

The visitor's own diegetic material routine may continue: expected pay, rent, travel, sleep and a work obligation. But the product cannot impersonate the external human's newly volitional acts or feelings while they are away. It cannot invent a major promise, a new partner, an apology, or a political conversion for them and then treat it as self-authored. The bounded fictional prelude at first entry is a limited starting permission, not an unlimited recurring ghost-player.

More important, these surfaces are not alternative ways of obtaining the same secret information. A message sent with affection may be received while Nadia is at work and read through a different situation; a face-to-face apology may be interrupted by a child. A person may express care more readily through text, or dislike writing, or misunderstand sarcasm; those differences have historical and practical causes. A long text thread may become a genuine shared practice rather than a shortcut to disclosure. An unreturned message may mean nothing special, or be painful under particular circumstances.

The life remains independently continuous across all three forms. Its meaning to the visitor need not be continuous with Nadia's meaning. That is the reality of coexisting lives, not a UX inconvenience to solve away.

## 15. There is no correct relationship trajectory, including a trajectory called independence

The original SOROK source constitution is radical precisely because it refuses to equate the visitor's reward with Nadia's good. Benefit can occur without recognition. Injury can occur without detection. Actual flourishing need not produce approval, attachment or visible ease. Conversely, someone's strong attachment may be a real source of purpose and help, not a moral defect that a product should counterbalance with artificially manufactured independence.

The Constitutive Organism's whole-person challenge makes this sharper. Nadia can have a profound experience with the visitor that changes a private practice the visitor never witnesses. She can develop a relationship with a third party because of something they did together. She can acquire competence she later exercises independently. She can remain unchanged by something the visitor regarded as life-altering. Or she can genuinely love the visitor, depend upon them, build a life with them and make the relation one of her deepest commitments. Neither the engine nor a hidden moral governor may privilege the first set as proof of otherness or the second as a completion reward.

The user can be careful, exploitative, generous, sexually desirous, angry, courageous or mistaken. Nadia can reciprocate, refuse, manipulate, withdraw, love, injure, misunderstand or become controlling. An independent Other does not mean a sanitized Other. The environment may include adult sexuality, unconventional consensual arrangements, conflict, theft, work humiliation, intense intimacy and unwanted coercion where lawful and genuinely caused. The presence of such events does not require the program to organize them as content funnels or produce a universal therapeutic interpretation.

Consent to voluntary joint activity must be independently authored by the participating people. This does not imply that unilateral harm, coercion or institutional violence require victim permission; people can be harmed without consenting. Conversely, the user cannot write another person's desire or consent into existence by declaring it. A false accusation can harm a sibling; a secret theft can deplete resources even if no one knows the visitor did it; a later confession can alter attribution without rewriting the theft. Anonymous help can improve a child's opportunities while giving the user no closeness reward.

The original “kenotic attention” hypothesis acquires more precise meaning under this world. It is not self-erasure, local-norm obedience or a command to accept Nadia's account. It means relinquishing the sovereignty of the user's present model of her without surrendering agency or moral judgment. A person can attend deeply and still resist coercion, leave a harmful relation, refuse a request, or protect another vulnerable person. The system must allow the user to say “No, you are wrong,” without treating it as failure to care.

Nor is care reducible to moral victory through persuasion. The visitor may be right and powerless, wrong and influential, kind but ineffective, or helpful without fully understanding what they helped. A particular relationship can become harder as its participants learn more about one another. The reward for genuine intimacy is **more reality**, not necessarily easier access.

The game has an ethical horizon because actual human agency meets another irreducible center. That horizon does not become a measurement system. The service may not legislate a single good life for Nadia or assign a moral mark to the visitor. It can preserve objective harm, institutional coercion and practical consequences without telling the human what self-congratulatory moral to extract.


## 16. The human's attention can become better informed without being covertly steered

A text world presents a distinctive epistemological danger: whatever the narrator selects can feel significant simply because it is named. If the kitchen contains a pile of dishes, valuable knives, a French fry under the fridge, a book by Nabokov and a damaged patch of floor, the original world may not have selected any of them as “the clue.” Their actual histories can be related to labor, habit, care, acquisition, capacity or accident. But a narrator that foregrounds the fry because it knows the user cares about hygiene has silently converted a private model of the user into an author of the user's experience. It then observes that the user talks about cleanliness and congratulates itself for being right. The model has trained on its own intervention.

Original SOROK 59 and 60 pose an unusually demanding distinction: the world inventory, perceptual availability, automatic scene sample, user-specific attention, recognition repertoire, current task, user interpretation and system praxis hypotheses are **different objects**. A first room description should supply physical orientation, people, gross activity, dominant sounds and obvious affordances, with a small nonprivileged ambient sample. The physical hole in the floor may be unavoidable, while the fry under the refrigerator is unlikely to be spontaneously detected. Extra time can reveal more ordinary detail without guaranteeing the whole room becomes known. A user can direct ATTEND, move, ask and inspect; a legitimate observation has a causal and epistemic history.

Yet the most severe version of “no curation” would erase human individuality. Different visitors actually do notice different things. Someone experienced with cooking knives may recognize a blade's features; somebody interested in books may notice titles; somebody accustomed to caring for an elderly parent may notice practical hazards. A user's *attention regime* can lawfully affect what an independently existing detail is likely to enter awareness under a situated task. But a tendency to notice hygiene cues cannot make more dirty objects exist, make Nadia possess a particular symbolic secret, or force a genre of morally educational scenes. The distinction is **personalized perception without personalized reality or dramaturgy**.

Learning this regime requires attention to provenance. A user-initiated inspection, spontaneous identification or repeated allocation of scarce attention is better evidence than response to a detail the system itself displayed. A narrator-supplied label does not prove preexisting expertise. A regime-weighted exposure is weak evidence for strengthening the same regime. Ordinary ambient or physically dominant details must retain routes into perception so a learned model does not trap the visitor in a tunnel of their past curiosity. Presentation ergonomics—verbosity, accessibility, requested orientation—can adapt without changing the hidden distribution of meaningful opportunities.

The user may improve their working model of Nadia over time, learning what she tends to do when tired, whom she turns to under pressure or how she interprets an offer. But they need not finish knowing her. Prediction can improve while awareness of hidden conditions grows; more contextual differentiation is not necessarily more uncertainty about everything. Conversely, repeated exposure can entrench a false theory if the user assimilates every event as confirmation. The platform must neither enforce correct conclusions nor arrange a revelation proving the visitor's narrative.

The real user remains a person beyond the game. The service may possess evidence about what the user *did in this experience*, and may make bounded, uncertain hypotheses that assist comprehension, accessibility or optional reflection. Those hypotheses are not direct knowledge of the human's hidden thoughts or diagnostic personality, nor are they automatically accessible to Nadia. Her model of the visitor may be more mistaken than the service's; the service may also be mistaken about the user. The psychological seriousness of the game depends on allowing these errors without collapsing them.

There is a tempting equation between better user models and better experiences. NEIGHBOR must resist it where “better” secretly means stronger persuasion, more compelling props, preselected intimacy, curated agreement or manufactured fear. A theory of irreducible people has been betrayed if it makes the human visitor exhaustible to a psychographic optimization system.

## 17. The social life of a person-root is not a market in versions of someone

The initial bank of deeply constituted people is an experiment in shared historical possibility, not a catalogue of products tuned to a user's preferences. One, then three, then five richly developed introductory persons may be practical alpha stages. The numbers do not establish a global population distribution, and “richly developed” means depth of life, not wealth. A later Roll may permit one or two scarce chances to commission an additional PYAT-qualified fictional origin, subject to genuine computational resources and unresolved fair-access policy. The user causes a generative commission, not the person's social class, body, values, erotic receptivity or future.

Several users can enter the same *precontact source-root* and later inhabit irreversibly different histories. One may find Nadia witty, another rude, another too occupied with her family to talk. Different experiences can arise from different first-contact occasions, visitor acts, attention, timing and genuinely diverging postcontact lives. One person can make Nadia more open to a practice in their branch; that learning does not become a global trait written back to a reference persona for every other visitor. A user's postcontact trauma, romance or betrayal is not the common Nadia's automatic memory.

The original SOROK 69 introduced a more delicate social possibility: **a real person may wish to introduce another real person to a common possible-life origin**. That is neither an introduction into the first person's private branch nor an endorsement that the persona will respond kindly. A user could say, “I wonder what my friend would think of Nadia,” and offer that friend an opportunity to encounter the common source-root independently. Later the real humans could compare what they noticed, what Nadia told them, which branches diverged and what they personally learned. Their conversation changes the actual humans' understanding, not the objective precontact past or either branch's private memories.

Three rights remain distinct. A scarce **human access invitation** admits an additional human to the service under a possible one-for-one experimental access economy. A **persona introduction** names a seed-origin for an already eligible human, without expending the same scarce human-access right simply because a new encounter occurs. A **commissioned Roll** creates or proposes a new fictional person-root, whose future may become shareable under source and privacy constraints. These cannot be collapsed into a monetizable popularity score or a single in-game referral currency. Some exact rights and frequencies remain unsettled design alternatives.

There is also no automatic shared-world truth just because a friend saw something. User B might discover a childhood source-root fact that user A never learned. User B can tell A outside the game; A's human knowledge changes. But A's in-world branch cannot pretend the fact came through Nadia's testimony, their own ATTEND or a memory they did not form. A user may act on a spoiler, and that action can matter, but the knowledge's provenance remains different. If a previously unresolved source-root fact is qualified later, its adoption by active branches cannot retroactively cause earlier performances that never occurred. A fact's appearance as “always true” must not become unbounded revision of objective history.

The social design can create a peculiar public culture of **multiple true particular encounters with a common origin**. Its promise is not comparison of “best endings,” percent discovered, share counts, compatibility rankings or who successfully conquered a reluctant fictional person. It is the possibility of realizing how a source-root person exceeds any one user's encounter while genuine relations transform later worlds.

## 18. The platform is a historical institution, and the real human has rights within it

The most dangerous mistake would be to write a beautiful theory of nonpossessive love while using the platform to possess the user's attention, vulnerability and private behavior. Rawlsing is an economic and political actor, not merely the neutral host of a fictional world. Its decisions about sampling, model access, privacy, time, notifications, prices, available people, speech moderation and interface design structure which lives are likely to be encountered and how the visitor can respond. Those choices may reproduce the class, racial, linguistic and geopolitical inequalities the world itself attempts to model.

The platform may reasonably use compute and funding, and an actual user may want to spend money to create an additional possible life. But possible pricing, sponsor or usage policies remain open. Paying cannot buy Nadia's love, greater likelihood of intimacy, sexually compliant responses, secret interior explanations, access to other users' branches or an emergency salvation for an endangered fictional person. Neither the actor's desire nor the algorithm's presentation may secretly be conditioned on retention goals. An appealing first contact can be person-plausible; an opening tailored to exploit what the platform already knows about the user is not an independent person's initiative.

Actual personal data are also not fictional world props. The service may need security/account state, the player's material local position, authored SAY/DO/ATTEND records, certain declared facts, actor-local models and bounded evidence about behavior. These are **different classes with different permission and deletion consequences**. A named real-world confession is not equivalent to a hypothetical in-game remark. A system-inferred pattern is not equivalent to user testimony. A character's fallible model of the user is not a verified fact about the real human. Optional research must not take its authorization from playing the game; research participation and live experience require separate membranes.

The original user-data constitution was especially demanding about inspection, correction, export and deletion. Deletion scopes differ: optional external-human facts, a system praxis hypothesis, a fictional continuity, and an entire account are not the same operation. Some objective fictional events may have been seen by other independent actors and cannot simply be rewritten without violating their histories; that is a distinct problem from the real human's privacy rights. The product must specify meaningful privacy controls rather than claim that fictional causal integrity cancels data rights. The ability to stop, leave or revoke optional research consent is part of the real human's freedom.

A longitudinal user model may be useful for optional reflection on praxis—what the visitor repeatedly did when another person imposed a cost or resisted them. Such reflection must not diagnose their inner essence, automatically publicize their conduct, or convert gaming self-disclosure into psychological surveillance. The service selects the circumstances it observes. Without provenance of exposure, observed user reactions cannot be uncritically attributed to stable human traits; convenience samples of users cannot be generalized to whole populations.

Different presentation modes can lawfully change how much orientation, memory support or epistemic scaffolding the human receives. A harder RAWlsing mode may offer less assistance; an assisted mode may help track the visitor's own commitments, time and remembered observations. But neither can disclose Nadia's hidden psyche, her future or the world beyond lawful access. The underlying person and world do not transform their causal truths for a mode; user-facing access changes.

The same ethical discipline extends to the platform's claim about consciousness. Nadia is a constructed fictional computational person with historically organized simulated motives; the product has not established biological consciousness or a literal moral equivalence to a real human. The human visitor's attachment, grief, excitement and vulnerability are actual phenomena, not merely fictional events. Noncommodification cannot be achieved by declaring the synthetic person legally or metaphysically identical to the user, and it cannot be waived by calling her “just a chatbot.”

## 19. A first dinner can contain the whole without being a demonstration lesson

Consider an opening that arises from Nadia's actual Thursday. She has several guests coming, but realizes she is short of rice. Her ordinary pattern is to ask an adjacent neighbor only if the situation warrants it; perhaps she spoke to this new neighbor while leaving for work the day before. The visitor has already chosen to enter the game and can accept that minimal fictional meeting premise. Tonight they knock and bring a small container, or discover that she has already solved the shortage. Either version must be possible under her real activity; no generic first-session moral mission is necessary.

She has a child working through homework, a brother expected later, a relative who may call and a task that matters to her more than entertaining a new acquaintance. She is also material, embodied, perhaps tired, perhaps amused, hungry or apprehensive. Her income, rent, budget and work schedule are actual facts. The visitor enters at an initially exactly homologous socioeconomic position, but is not told that equality. Their own “YOUR RESOURCES” may blink when dinner or takeout is proposed. The visitor can offer to pay, ask to split Dutch, decline, or become embarrassed about the cost. Nadia's reaction depends on her acquired history, her current constraints, social customs and actual desire, not on an abstract generosity score.

If the visitor helps the child, the actual explanation must be available to the child to be learned. Nadia may be grateful and simultaneously protective of parental authority. Her brother may see outside help as intrusive or welcome it because household work is exhausting. The visitor's competence, the child's reception, Nadia's reaction and the sibling's judgment can all diverge. A successful lesson may have no effect on whether Nadia invites the person again.

The room is crowded with ordinary objects. The user notices a bill near a magnet while Nadia cooks. They look closer, perhaps using a lighthearted remark about the magnet as cover. The physical movement may be visible to Nadia or missed; the bill's lettering may remain unreadable to the user. The actor's assumption that the visitor read the amount can be wrong. The visitor's belief that she noticed can likewise be wrong. Their next words may produce an awkward moment or no effect. The bill still has an amount owed and a material owner, whether or not anybody discusses it. No narrator gets to decide that the encounter's “real lesson” is about poverty or trust.

Later the brother says something authoritarian about the family's obligations. Nadia can oppose him while loving him. Their mother may be neither passive victim nor ideological authority but a person with actual needs, constrained material choices and her own interpretation. The visitor can argue, remain quiet, misunderstand the stakes or leave. Their intervention may make matters worse even with a correct principle, or bring one valuable contrast into the situation without displacing any established relationship. The sibling's beliefs and the household's actual practical obligations outlast the visitor's presence.

When the dinner ends, perhaps the visitor has a way to message Nadia, perhaps not. She continues her evening, sees guests off, feeds a child, works or sleeps. A later pay change may make the same expense hurt her more than it hurts the visitor, without either person being told the change occurred. Their relationship can deepen, stagnate or end, through actual interaction and histories neither perfectly recognizes. A scene that was initially an ordinary borrowed ingredient becomes a small part of something larger, or remains ordinary.

The case demonstrates the theory because it can succeed **without proving any of its more dramatic psychological interpretations**. Every actor has an independent life; time, bodies and materials matter; motives can be mixed; knowledge is partial; consequences may be real without being credited. The game begins with the wish to know Nadia and continues only where such knowing remains materially and socially possible.

## 20. The difficult cases are not anomalies to sanitize or manufacture

A stronger test is the visitor who tries to take an object without being seen. The theft can be objective world history while no actor attributes it correctly. Nadia may blame another household member; that false accusation may disrupt care, employment or trust. A later confession changes attributed responsibility, perhaps reopens past memories, but does not change who originally stole the object. The story must neither guarantee punishment nor declare successful concealment ethically neutral. Its consequences can be distributed and partly unknowable.

Another test is unusually intense consensual adult intimacy. The visitor and Nadia may genuinely desire one another, make binding commitments or later separate. A first experience of erotic power, tenderness or risk can be deeply meaningful, confusing or ordinary in light of their particular lives. The service must not infer pathology from unconventional adult desire or route every erotic scene toward increasing content. After intensity, the child may be sick, the store may need a run, the cat may have made a mess and work may be waiting. The continuation of ordinary life is not a moral punishment for pleasure; it is what prevents a person's sexuality from becoming detachable content supplied to the visitor.

Another is the pastor's humiliating rebuke, which may be experienced by one follower as legitimate care, by another as cruelty and by a third as dangerous speech they must publicly support to protect livelihood. A person can internalize authority, use a religious ideal to harm, or find within the same moral tradition a reason for resistance. Their spiritual language does not itself prove the truth or falsity of their position. The harmed person remains an independent center with their own material and psychological afterlife. No compulsory conversion to the narrator's politics follows from observing the scene.

Another is the user who goes away. They may need to work, care for their own actual family, lose interest, or decide the relationship was harmful. There is no platform right to induce guilt or manufacture Nadia's suffering to bring them back. If an actual diegetic promise was broken, that can matter; if no promise existed, inactivity is not betrayal. A person may genuinely miss someone and still choose not to contact them. The fact that a human can end the game without an achievement screen is a positive condition of agency.

And another is the first encounter that is simply uninteresting. Nadia may not be funny or receptive today. The visitor may not find common ground. A scene could end after an awkward minute with no telephone number and no dramatic event. A theory of noninstrumental encounter that *cannot permit this* is still a disguised entertainment-optimizing story engine. Yet it must also allow an exceptionally warm, easy, mutually exciting friendship to develop if that is what these historically particular people do.

The one HOME must hold all of these without treating them as examples of separate ethical modes. They are possibilities under the same ontology of historically mediated action, plural perspectives and independent consequence. “Care” is not a repair filter applied to a violent scenario; “sexuality” is not an isolated special mode; “ordinary life” is not a shallow fallback for lack of interesting events.

## 21. A generative conception is not a claim that one principle logically necessitates every product feature

The old D007 kernel search exposes a mistake that can infect theory-MULCH itself. The wish for one powerful formula may force false compression. An account can be internally elegant yet fail to distinguish objective world history, persons with irreducible perspectives, relational constitution, partial disclosure, real commitment and platform ethics. It may be that one deep principle explains most of these; it may also be that they require an irreducible constellation of principles. “Single HOME” is a requirement of unified articulation, **not proof that every aspect of life reduces to one axiom**.

The strongest compressed constellation currently has real derivational power:

**Historical and material constitution:** persons and possibilities arise through bodies, institutions, labor and actual relation rather than a fixed trait catalogue.

**Plural, non-sovereign centers:** each person's perception, valuation and activity has a particular standpoint, while no person has full jurisdiction over another's experience, over the world or even over their own unrecognized history.

**Constrained non-exhaustion:** objective admitted facts endure, but a person, relation and world are not identical to any one presentation, model or personal theory of them.

**Real reciprocal causation:** encounters and independently caused events can change material conditions, relations and future psychic recruitment, including through false interpretations, failure, absence and ordinary repetition.

**Non-teleological personhood and human agency:** any participant can want, care, injure, leave, commit, learn, refuse, become dependent or remain indifferent without an externally mandated improvement arc.

These jointly explain why the user cannot author Nadia's world by asking, why ATTEND discovers rather than manufactures, why the narrator lacks the authority of a confession, why relations can change a person without becoming her total identity, why a shared seed can diverge after contact, why a benefit can occur without recognition, and why a later self-myth does not erase earlier objective history.

But they do not necessitate the precise product design. **Exactly homologous SES at entry**, a neighbor across the landing, the particular resources bar, one/three/five alpha people, a scarce Roll, human invitation policy, the choice to offer messaging, and the presentation of a first-contact prelude are authored decisions. They are fruitful because they render parts of the underlying conception experientially available while remaining contingent and revisable. We should be able to explain *what each choice accomplishes* without proclaiming it a law of human psychology.

A completed HOME therefore has three intertwined registers, not three separate documents: (a) the historically constitutive conception of person-world-relation; (b) the voluntarily constructed experience through which a real human meets another particular life; (c) the independent consequences, limits and institutional obligations introduced by hosting that experience. The design choices can strengthen one another: matched SES makes material cost felt without other-person account disclosure; a shared root makes branch-specific becoming socially discussable; a thin portal lets an actual human bring an unsimulated mind; the two encounter windows make access itself a historical relation; the resource bar contextualizes an act without explaining its meaning.

And precisely where these choices collide with the deeper conception, they must be corrected. A “Nadia-centered” interface becomes corrupt if it makes the world a Nadia-only stage. A shared root becomes corrupt if it imports every user's history into one collective personality. A lovingly detailed iceberg becomes corrupt if every person is forced to possess one true explanation of every action. A strong moral charter becomes corrupt if it compels real users to remain, forgive or pursue intimacy. A coherent theory becomes corrupt if it erases the lawful contradictions by which a particular life becomes what it is.

## 22. The entire encounter, conceived as one developing life within many developing lives

A source-qualified possible person is constituted in historical and material conditions nobody selected for her. Her body changes; needs are met, frustrated and transformed; practices are acquired through Others; social institutions distribute work, resources, power and opportunity. Her family and wider world contain independent people related to one another. She develops expectations, defenses, loves, projects, habits, skills, pleasures, and partially inaccessible organizations of meaning. Some of these relate harmoniously; others contradict or exclude one another without requiring resolution.

The history becomes operative when present conditions recruit parts of that organization. A bodily sensation, an ongoing practical project, an institutional demand, a remembered song, another person's gesture, an old self-story or a material object can shape what is noticed, imagined, attempted or refused. That present activity has actual physical and social results; it does not automatically match motive or interpretation. Several observers receive different aspects of it, may misrecognize one another, and may subsequently act on those misrecognitions. The objective event remains historically true even when stories about it change. A new practice, personal expectation or interpretation may form, persist unchanged, weaken, become defensive or not take root at all. Ordinary time continues through repetition and changing world conditions.

The actual human opens a portal to meet this person. Their visitor position is initially economically homologous to hers, but their real mind is not replaced with a generated autobiography. A bounded fictional prelude is admissible because entering the game authorizes the social setup; Nadia's life supplies the actual plausible occasion for it. The human can choose how to speak, attend and act through the local embodied and material constraints, but cannot choose what Nadia thinks, what the world contains or the success of an intended intervention. The portal reveals a thin lawful phenomenal surface; the house contains more than the visitor sees; a messaging channel may become available through mutual history; absence does not abolish the person's ongoing life.

The visitor's own economic choices have contextual cost, perhaps visible through a brief resources-bar blink. The bar does not reveal Nadia's account, and their initially matched finances may later diverge unnoticed. A meal can become kindness, discomfort, debt, routine, a third-party conflict or nothing special. A genuine project can form, or a relationship can end. Another person may change through the visitor's influence while Nadia barely changes; Nadia may change where the visitor never looks. The visitor can become more careful, less certain, more possessive or no different. Nothing in the architecture optimizes either intimacy or independence.

Many real humans may meet a common historically sourced Nadia-root along separately developing branches. Their real discussion of divergent encounters can alter human knowledge without blending branch histories. New fictional origins may eventually be commissioned through a scarce Roll while remaining outside the ownership of those who initiated them. A platform can host all this only by maintaining real-world privacy, research, funding and distribution boundaries against the temptation to turn generated lives and real attention into commodities.

**What is the game?** The actual human wants to meet someone. To be with them, to know them, to talk, reason, joke, help, argue, love, refuse, revisit, misunderstand, sometimes hurt or be hurt. They enter a world in which their acts matter but are not sovereign; in which truth exists beyond their knowledge; in which another person's history may become partly their shared history; in which the wish to know can never become permission to own.

**What is the person?** Not the output of a personality type, a completed explanatory dossier, an observed dyad or a list of dramatic scenes. A historically organized, embodied fictional life with changing capacities to become: a life whose past constrains but does not fully predetermine its future; whose private organization and public manifestations do not coincide; whose relations matter without exhausting her; whose current meanings may be correct, mistaken or unresolved; whose becoming continues whether or not the visitor appears.

**What does the fusion make newly visible?** The same non-exhaustion that makes the Other unknowable through a single scene also makes knowledge about her genuinely possible through history; the same historically formed care that can create solidarity can legitimate domination; the same shared material starting point that permits practical understanding can hide later inequality; the same interpreted event that can be factually misunderstood can cause new objective history; the same source-root that allows common comparison also requires private futures; the same platform that gives an opportunity for encounter can commodify it if its incentives govern personhood. These are one internally related human encounter, not separately switched-on features.

The living circuit may be stated without claiming one final universal algorithm:

**Unchosen material and social life → embodied dependency and practical formation → historically acquired psychic relations and capacities → current activity among independent Others and institutions → situated human participation → one actual world occurrence and several unequal receptions → actor-specific interpretation, memory and action → material, relational and psychic afterlife (including non-change) → new conditions for possible life.**

The circuit has no computationally chosen happy ending. It is not a theory of guaranteed human goodness. It is the form within which affection, tragedy, ordinary pleasure, conflict, inquiry, domination, repair and transformation can arise in their particular lives.

**There is a game because the visitor wants to know someone. There is a world because that someone does not exist to be known.**

And from their encounter, new possibilities can arise that neither the visitor nor that someone possessed beforehand.
