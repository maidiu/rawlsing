## Boundary: what the game demonstrably became

An actual human enters the ongoing life of a particular historically constituted fictional Other to come to know them through finite perception, speech, attempt, practical activity, misrecognition, and real, lasting consequences. The Other's historical psyche, social relations, embodied needs, independent days and future are constitutive conditions; they are not a list of activities the player must fix or achieve. The encounter can be ordinary, intense, reciprocal, disappointing, loving, injurious, brief or unfinished.

**Three layers that must not be made interchangeable:**

1. **IN-HOME / lived encounter:** person-specific entry, the exact initial socioeconomic equivalence without opening Nadia's private account, user-owned SAY/DO/ATTEND, bounded material action, independent sensory receipt and response, truthful world and fallible appearances, communication and availability as social facts, historical becoming and consequences. These are governing.
2. **OUTSIDE-HOME / real-human entry and operator policy:** how a user acquires an invitation or session, which delivery and time-verisimilitude mode is supported, privacy and accessibility administration, financing and optional external charitable bridges, product research, content distribution, onboarding and notification design. These may constrain the in-world experience but **are not events in Nadia's psyche or the game's intended goals**.
3. **HISTORICAL GREENFIELD / unselected alternatives:** conceptually rich early proposals for institution-building games, moral-flourishing evaluators, relation-based invite rights, scripted alpha scenes, local-cultural competency tests, high-profile player identities, self-assessment services, multiplayer reputation and teleological arcs. Preserve their generated insights; do not silently reactivate their proposed mechanics.

## A. Real-user-facing pre-UX and operator decisions

### A1. Human invitations and distribution
Early SOROK 33–35, 44, 57 proposed invitation-entitlement, one-for-one reciprocal admission, nonrefund/revocation, limited seed cohorts and relational-warrant gates. The mature theory retains only the distinction between **external-human invitation**, **fictional-person encounter**, and **in-fiction social introduction**. Whether real-human invitations are scarce, earned, gifted, referral-based or absent is a separate governance/distribution question. None is licensed to become a virtue test on the visitor. Later shared-root/Roll vision supports parallel independent continuities, **not** the entire early invitation economy.

### A2. Real-user time modes, notification and portal packaging
Historical SOROK 32, 43–48 propose elastic rendezvous, close synchronization, highest-verisimilitude timing, messaging/delivery/read settings, optional alert regimes, and a terminal/TI-84-like portal aesthetic. In-world facts that somebody is absent, late, contactable, interrupted or unable to wait belong inside HOME. What option the service displays for scheduling, what counts as app-read, how deadlines are announced, how subscriptions interact with time, and what visual skin is offered stay **outside**. Particular modes are candidate designs, not all adopted or compulsory.

### A3. Monetization, compute and storage
Early SOROK Frontier K, 15, 39, 62 and the latest recourt considered subscriptions, depth/fidelity tiers, persistence and compute/storage fees. The non-negotiable principle is that payment must not buy Nadia's affection, restore her lost trust, create scripted suffering, or let external real wealth purchase fictional material success. Feasibility bounds fidelity, latency, ordinary-life continuation and practical affordances. Specific charging models remain open. The service's funding decisions may nevertheless deform how much ordinary autonomous life is affordable to depict, so pricing and cost cannot be treated as wholly neutral.

### A4. Real-world donations / solidarity bridge
Court 5 §12 raised a **possible** voluntary real-world mutual-aid or donation route. It was not a settled feature of the actual encounter. If later chosen, external money goes to actual accountable people or institutions, not to Nadia; neither a payment nor its absence can become her gratitude or judgment. The fictional visitor's limited local resources, real user's wallet and platform's income are different. Institutional transformation campaigns are not therefore the NEIGHBOR gameplay objective.

### A5. Real-human data, optional reflection and research
Historical Court 11, SOROK 58–60 and D008–D009 discuss optional private user-model reflection, privacy controls, external profiles, demographic questionnaires, research planes, attention-exposure provenance, export and deletion, external assistance, physical-skill measurement and interpretation of actual conduct. The in-world encounter can intelligently condition *presentation* on legitimately experienced user attentional behavior without giving Nadia server-side knowledge. Administrative dossiers, opt-in science, consent, inspection and deletion belong here. A deleted **entire continuity record** is distinct from rewriting one admitted fictional event: selective retcon is a negative test, **not** a proposed feature.

### A6. Accessibility, content policy and pre-experience presentation
Whether to offer assisted/harder modes, which user primers or defaults to use, which adult-content presentation boundaries apply, what notification/density/translation controls are offered, and what device modalities can be measured remain separate, partly open product responsibilities. The experience itself must support truthful intimate and disturbing events when otherwise lawful, human agency, and the distinction between medium-native cognition and declared portaled capability. A terminal-looking shell, compulsory questionnaire or particular explicitness limit is not part of the essential theory merely because an early source imagined it.

### A7. Cost and fidelity prototype constraints
Original SOROK 41 and October 12 bounded-alpha work named generic chatbot indistinguishability, task overload, empty persistence, approval optimization, staged drama, failed organic encounter, and impossible inference costs as **defects to avoid or repair**. These are actionable build constraints and test failure signals, **not** a philosophical commitment to make the game fail. If perfect naturalist simulation is infeasible, preserve the meaningful causal encounter at an attainable level of fidelity. Empirical evaluation is properly separate from implementing a working approximation.

## B. Valuable original ideation that is NOT the current game's objective

- **Joint projects become autonomous institutions (Court 9, 23; life topology 30):** an actual shared promise, routine or third-party obligation can have durable consequences; *do not* convert this into a prescribed community-organizing, neighborhood-garden or school-collective game.
- **Care and institutional domination (Court 10, D003, 24):** household labor divisions, economic coercion, sincere dependence and contested flourishing condition Nadia's own life, choices and conflicts. *Do not* promote the visitor to obligatory emancipator of those structures.
- **Formal flourishing constitution (Court 10):** moral health, approval, affection, dependency, local norms and objective effects are nonidentical. Useful negative rule: no single hidden health score. Positive normative evaluator remains an early open research problem, not an encounter mechanic.
- **Relational warrant and new-person access gate (33, 57):** social consent and introduction remain real, but the user need not demonstrate worthiness to unlock a new fictional human.
- **Scripted first dinner / grocery / school-supplies sequence (31, 35, D002):** hostile example and scene test, not the canonical Nadia prelude. A real scene must be generated by her prior day.
- **Alternative avatar or profile constructors (20, 22B, 22D):** retained as contrasts to the later thin real-human, near-zero profile route; not an alternate default invented into the HOME.
- **Multiplayer reputation, game social status, virality, real-user leaderboards (Frontier L, 34, 57):** later independent branches and possible real-human conversation about origins do not authorize these mechanics.
- **General social-transformation pedagogies, prescribed spirituality or theological outcomes:** their ideological and psychological insight may inform how ordinary life is modeled; no mandatory political liberation campaign or soul-formation curriculum is the game.

## C. The immanent ideas that returned INSIDE HOME (rather than being archived away)

The original materialism remains active because the person is formed by actual histories, bodies, labor and relationships. Its useful philosophical core is **the Other is not a function of what the visitor hopes to receive**. This gives rise directly to in-game constraints: an independently continuing person; fallible and historically mediated perception; user-authored meaningful attempts; real limits of material action; mutually incomplete interpretations; no privileged motive dossier; another person's permission and availability; ordinary temporal continuity; effects that cannot be redefined as 'just narration'; no forced moral progress.

A labor dispute, organizing campaign, religious duty or coercive household expectation can legitimately occur in her independent life, and the user may encounter or even participate in its consequences. That does **not** give the product a new objective besides coming to know a person in whose life those things may occur.

### MOVED §19 / 2 — objectified institutional politics/platform commodification as adjacent theory not encounter engine

**Institutions are more than the aggregate of participants' intentions because their material objects and rules redistribute what each person can do.** A rota posted on a refrigerator, a jointly held bank account, a signed lease, a vehicle registered in one sibling's name, a religious association's shared kitchen or a rent-relief fund are objectified histories of human work. They can store a capacity for coordination and a power to exclude. The written schedule may enable reliable childcare while fixing one person's labor as the unremarkable default; the lease can provide shelter and an asymmetry of legal control; a charity collection can feed a household and temporarily protect the extraction of rent. A benevolent intention does not cancel an institution's impersonal force. An exploitative institution does not render every caring act performed within it meaningless. **The historically precise question is how access, ownership, ordinary labor, enforceable obligation and the practical possibility of refusal combine in this specific arrangement**, without collapsing a family's cultural practice into a generic oppressive symbol.


### MOVED §19 / 10 — objectified institutional politics/platform commodification as adjacent theory not encounter engine

**This is also a possible politics of alienation, not only an interpersonal semiotics.** Someone may sincerely recognize the human labor in a thing while bargaining over its sale price because rent is due. Another may use prestige objects to command respect while privately despising their status function. A family may preserve an inherited home for protection and use the same property relation to constrain a daughter's exit. A religious sign may mediate genuine collective care and authorize hierarchy; a child may appropriate that sign for a practice their elders did not intend. THE MARRIAGE can demand the sacrifice of partners; the GOOD SON can demand a sister's obedience; THE CHURCH can require victims' silence; THE REVOLUTION can be invoked to dominate comrades. Those are not symbols the UX declares true of a person; they are historically operative institutions, possessions, practices, sanctions and competing accounts to be encountered through their effects.


### MOVED §19 / 11 — objectified institutional politics/platform commodification as adjacent theory not encounter engine

**The platform itself objectifies a possible life into a root, an encounter and perhaps a sellable service.** That objectification can make shared investigation, care and social comparison possible; it can also convert the resulting relation into controlled access, emotional capture, monetized scarcity and proprietor authority over human conduct. A fictive person may be treasured without the actual company acquiring a right to the visitor's real-world time. An institution that claims the user should not possess Nadia cannot innocently sell the user more agreeable responses, coveted access to her family, secret explanatory dossiers or a manufactured emotional obligation. Nor may the company classify visitors as morally worthy of particular people because its model has interpreted their conduct. The same critique of relational reification turns back upon the institution staging the encounter.


### MOVED §17 / 2 — real-world donation and institutional labor reform as focal activity: greenfield detour

**Care undertaken inside a fictional household and solidarity undertaken outside the software have different objects and burdens.** A visitor can use their local fictional food budget to support Nadia, perhaps at genuine cost to their portaled commitments; the outside human can give real money to actual organizations or participate in their own community's collective struggle. They may feel related, but they are not the same event, not owed to one another and not judged by a shared virtue meter. A real donation cannot stand in for actually carrying a fictional child's schoolboxes; good fictional caregiving cannot stand in for material solidarity with actual people. Likewise, guilt produced by a fictional catastrophe is not sufficient evidence that any real-world funder has established a legitimate emergency or representative beneficiary. The bridge, if it exists at all, must let **real Others remain the authorities on their own needs rather than making a simulated household their compulsory image**.


### MOVED §17 / 3 — real-world donation and institutional labor reform as focal activity: greenfield detour

**The social reproduction question is not whether someone performed an apparently caring gesture; it is what system of labor made that gesture possible and what burdens it displaced.** A Wednesday school-pickup rota may let Nadia work a paid shift, spare a child an anxious wait and deepen solidarity among neighbors. It may also depend on a retired woman who cannot comfortably say no, a driver whose fuel costs nobody reimburses, a mother who keeps the rota organized at midnight, and a workplace that treats this unpaid network as permission to demand inflexible hours. At one scale the collective expands genuine agency; at another it subsidizes coercive employment terms; at still another it becomes a precious form of mutual support worth defending. The relation does not resolve into 'good community' or 'bad patriarchy.' The user encounters **the same activity as reproduction of life, distribution of unpaid work, and organization of power**.


### MOVED §17 / 4 — real-world donation and institutional labor reform as focal activity: greenfield detour

**Changing the division of labor is an activity with risks, not a line of morally correct dialogue.** A visitor might offer a second pickup, pay for transport, organize collective bargaining, ask the school for a changed schedule, mediate a dispute, criticize a gendered expectation or simply perform their agreed share without credit. Each attempt requires actual resources, affected people's consent and reception, institutional rules, time and other actors' continuing projects. A grand speech about solidarity that leaves a caregiver holding all the bags is an action with consequences; so is quietly taking over a task without understanding that the original arrangement supported someone's valued competence or authority. **Care's materiality includes the freedom to remain needed and the freedom not to be made indispensable.** Neither can be scripted as the only psychologically mature response for Nadia.


### MOVED §14 / 2 — notification engineering and platform delivery: external control surface, not character psychology

**An actor's demanding or manipulative message and the platform's aggressive delivery of it are distinct acts.** Nadia may sincerely be jealous, anxious, possessive or injured after a particular real event and may call, accuse or withdraw on her own actor-owned grounds. Rawlsing should neither sanitize her into an agreeable companion nor seize that moment to duplicate urgent push alerts, compress arbitrary response windows or send retention copy claiming she is suffering. Lawful actor-specific pressure is one historical event; the operator's use of real devices and schedules to amplify it is another. **Causal fidelity to a difficult person does not license engineering the user's dependence on the service.**


### MOVED §14 / 3 — notification engineering and platform delivery: external control surface, not character psychology

**An actor's difficult message and the platform's chosen delivery of it are not one act.** If Nadia, under CO-owned psychological and world history, sends an angry line after a promise was broken, its occurrence may matter. Whether the app sends a push notification at three in the morning, repeats the preview, fabricates typing urgency or silently suppresses it is a separate exercise of institutional power. Neutral notification policy should not launder a real actor's manipulation into platform manipulation, nor sterilize lawful unpleasantness by editing the other person's actual words. The difference is especially important where a synthetic relationship intensifies while the real human needs to withdraw. A user may stop participating without being drafted into a test of devotion.


### MOVED §10 / 2 — flourishing research, institution transformation and relational warrant: outside main experience

**A participant's continuing interest is not itself evidence of a flourishing relation.** The earlier alpha court listed voluntary returns, spontaneous person-specific recall, preference for continuity, changed interpretations and unscripted inquiry as promising signs that a relationship had become experientially particular. But a user could return compulsively, to win approval, avoid guilt, solve a puzzle or see increasingly sensational scenes. Those are not interchangeable motives; the platform may not assume the caring one. Likewise an excellent one-time encounter, a thoughtful decision to leave, or refusal to reroll is not a failure of relational development. **The provisional experiential theorem asks whether the encounter acquires specific personal significance; it does not establish that such significance makes the external human healthier, kinder or politically transformed.** A robust theory must permit genuine engagement that is morally ugly, genuine moral sensitivity that ends in withdrawal, and a commercial success that ethically fails.


### MOVED §10 / 3 — flourishing research, institution transformation and relational warrant: outside main experience

**An institution can produce a real good through a relation of real subordination.** The original Court 10 deliberately refuses to identify local approval, a person's sincere preference, practical functioning, the user's actual effects and a higher-order judgment about flourishing. Consider a household caregiving arrangement. A child eats reliably, an elder receives medication, several adults experience belonging, and the arrangement reproduces a gendered division of unpaid labor that leaves one person with fewer hours, bargaining options and realistic avenues of departure. Both descriptions can be true at once. The experience of community is not a false consciousness to be dismissed as worthless; the objective distribution of costs is not vindicated by sincere attachment to it. **The institution's capacity to sustain life and its capacity to reproduce domination can be the same mechanism.** The visitor's ethical task is not to click 'liberate' but to encounter who must do what, under which forms of property, authority and necessity, with what ability to refuse, negotiate or change them.


### MOVED §10 / 4 — flourishing research, institution transformation and relational warrant: outside main experience

**Consent is an event inside a material field, not a metaphysical solvent poured over the field.** A caregiver can freely endorse a sacrifice and simultaneously face threats of wage loss, housing insecurity, family disgrace or a loved one's abandonment if they do otherwise. A religious duty can be consciously cherished, imposed, negotiated, resisted, reappropriated, or all of these over different phases of a life. 'She agreed' may be an important actual utterance; it is not complete evidence of equal alternatives. Nor may the platform declare an actor's own meaning unreal simply because its preferred moral ontology calls dependency bad. Court 10's stronger distinction is between *chosen reliance, asymmetric yet noncoercive care, structurally imposed dependence, and dependence sustained by fear or sabotaged alternatives*, with trajectories and domains rather than a universal individualist freedom quotient.


### MOVED §10 / 5 — flourishing research, institution transformation and relational warrant: outside main experience

**Refusal, repair and liberation can impose new costs on the person they ostensibly serve.** Suppose the visitor refuses to perpetuate an unfair family arrangement. A parent may welcome the boundary and still lose tonight's childcare. A child may gain one adult's autonomy and lose a familiar routine; another adult may inherit unpaid shifts; the institution may retaliate through money or exclusion. The visitor's refusal might be courageous, negligent, both in different relations, or causally peripheral. Conversely, supplying groceries might avert hunger this week while leaving a landlord or employer able to externalize the cost of reproduction onto informal care. The **first-order relief is real; so is the second-order reproduction of the conditions requiring relief**. These are theory-level hypotheses to be tested through genuine world causality, not an anti-capitalist outcome script in which all kindness secretly harms or all refusal secretly frees.


### MOVED §10 / 6 — flourishing research, institution transformation and relational warrant: outside main experience

**Whose counterfactual is being invoked matters.** The raw fact that Nadia has more money after the visitor arrives does not establish that the visitor caused a healthier life, that the alternative was no money, or that Nadia's apparent gratitude discloses her real motive. A different family member may have offered help, a workplace change may have done most of the work, and the visitor's intervention may have worsened another domain. The original D002 causal-provenance court distinguished causes, contributions, enabling conditions, constraints and mere coincidence; Court 10 refused to turn multidimensional trajectories into a moral grade. **The world must have enough causal structure to prevent either a savior narrative or a performative denunciation from replacing what actually occurred.**


### MOVED §10 / 12 — flourishing research, institution transformation and relational warrant: outside main experience

An important **unsettled historical product hypothesis** is the “relational warrant” for opening an additional person-root into the visitor's continuing social life. Original SOROK 33 distinguished historical substance from the capacity to sustain another person without reducing them to disposable content. But it also revealed the danger: if a hidden evaluator determines whether the visitor showed enough respect, empathy or self-revision, the system may have silently become a moral examination. One previous proposal tried to require sufficient consequential shared history and non-domination rather than friendliness or compliance. That remains a contested access/distribution design option, **not an adopted concealed moral score**. An existing real social introduction, a qualified independent invitation, or separate scarcity policy may warrant a different route without claiming to measure someone's moral capacity.


### MOVED §10 / 13 — flourishing research, institution transformation and relational warrant: outside main experience

This leaves NEIGHBOR-UX a productive unresolved tension: **the service needs a way for a growing world to introduce new people without making the first person a disposable tutorial, yet it cannot make the visitor's morality the currency of personhood**. The resolution, if one becomes lawful, must preserve actual social opportunity, actors' independent choices, the user's freedom to leave and the possibility that a brief difficult acquaintance is consequential without becoming “successful.” The game must not punish refusal to reconcile or reward submission to local norms. New possibilities of relation are not prizes for passing Nadia.


### MOVED §9 / 2 — joint institution production as player game: overpromoted greenfield

**An encounter may constitute a new social institution whose reality exceeds either participant's account of their relationship.** Court 9's most difficult case was not another kind of closeness; it was the emergence of *jointly real organization*: a neighborhood garden, school-pickup arrangement, shared childcare, savings obligation, repair collective, co-owned tool or campaign. Each can have an objective history, resource commitments, scheduled labor, permissions, third-party stakes and a future that does not track affection. Nadia and the visitor might tend a garden and quarrel bitterly; might deeply love one another but refuse joint management of a child's care; might both publicly describe themselves as friends while disagreeing about whether a borrowed key entails unrestricted entrance. A shared practice is not thereby a shared inner meaning. **The emergent joint project is more than two emotions but less than a mysterious pair-level psyche.** Its persistence, ownership and dependencies need a legitimate existing world/project/relation authority; SORACLE-CO independently owns each participating person's changing perception, desire and acquired organization.


### MOVED §9 / 3 — joint institution production as player game: overpromoted greenfield

**A social commitment can survive its founding intimacy—and may become contested property.** After estrangement, the garden's plants still need water, the shared tool still has an owner, the child still requires a safe school pickup, and a debt does not vanish because someone refuses conversation. One participant may continue contributing anonymously; another may demand an orderly handover; a third party may have relied upon promises they never consented to cancel. The direct channel can close while a legally or practically required route remains, and continued co-activity can persist alongside genuine personal refusal. A sustained relation's end is therefore not the world recording 'relationship = false.' It is a historically specific reconfiguration of permissions, jointly constituted resources, live duties, expectations and personal contact. **The relation may stop being active while remaining causally effective** through other people and institutions. No participant automatically inherits the authority to dissolve the whole social form by withdrawing personal affection.


### MOVED §7 / 2 — four clock engineering/control distinctions: extract mode implementation while keep lived commitments

**The original temporal courts expose four distinct times.** Diegetic event time identifies when the fictional meeting occurs; real-user access time identifies when the product permits participation; within-scene action time follows what actually gets done; third-party deadlines identify when another person must act regardless of whether the human visitor ever opens the application. A pending dinner cannot import Saturday facts into Friday's conversation, but neither can it hold all of Nadia's family, coworkers and public institutions undetermined. SOROK 47's causally pending appointment was qualified by 48's later insistence that pending does not mean temporally frozen: cancellation and renegotiation are themselves events, unrelated life continues, and deadlines differ by kind. **Elasticity is bounded mediation of a dependency, not license to edit history backward.**


### MOVED §6 / 3 — detailed elastic time mode portfolio: meta-pre-UX delivery question

**Elastic time can preserve humane participation only where it does not confiscate another person's clock.** A Friday dinner can be causally pending for a bounded real-user access period; a child waiting at a school gate, a person's urgent clinic decision, a coworker's paid shift or a perishable delivery cannot simply be held in suspension until the external human chooses to enter. Urgent third-party consequences need their actual event-time resolution, with whatever narrower or no grace window those conditions permit. The material stake is not just attending but whose work an absence redistributes. **The open frontier is how to bound the dependent consequences of a pending event without freezing unrelated history**—not how to make Others wait until the user wants to play.


### MOVED §6 / 11 — detailed elastic time mode portfolio: meta-pre-UX delivery question

The earlier temporal work preserved alternative experiences—**elastic default**, optional **close synchronization**, and opt-in **maximal verisimilitude**—but none is a superior moral or relational status. A stricter clock can be a user's preferred intensity, not proof they care more, and should not change what Nadia is permitted to become. The product must tell the human the consequence of agreeing to a precise in-world appointment before it silently turns the agreement into a real-calendar duty. An external emergency can explain a missed meeting later, but the platform cannot know the reason or diagnose intent.


### MOVED §3 / 2 — external finance / real donation bridge: outside-fiction product frontier

**There are three economies here, not one generalized quantity called money.** The visitor has a finite *fictional local* budget with which to buy food, share a transport fare or contribute to the household. The *actual external human* may have a very different real wallet, with very different capacities and obligations. And the *platform operator* pays for computation, storage, distribution, labor and institutional continuation. Conflating them breaks different aspects of the experiment: real wealth imported into the local budget purchases superiority and destroys homologous peerhood; real payments converted into fictional rescue buy moral effectiveness; compute scarcity made into a scarcity of available affection turns relationship into a premium commodity. None of these flows may be inferred from the simple fact that an interface displays a price.


### MOVED §3 / 3 — external finance / real donation bridge: outside-fiction product frontier

**An actual gift beyond the fictional world is a different action upon different people.** The original Court 5 raised a narrowly specified later-stage possibility: a person moved by a synthetic encounter could choose, outside the simulation, to support a real mutual-aid effort or organization in a materially connected region. This is neither paying Nadia nor proving that Nadia's needs correspond to those of any identifiable real household. Such action would require separate real-world knowledge, accountable recipients, a freely chosen purpose and its own independent consequences; it cannot improve Nadia's canonical resources, unlock her gratitude or confer standing within the fictional relationship. A donation may have real value, or be poorly directed, paternalistic or negligible. **The imagined Other can redirect attention to actual Others without acquiring ownership over their existence—or becoming their fundraising mascot.**


### MOVED §1 / 2 — alpha-disconfirmation as governing philosophical thesis: bounded engineering test, not game center

**This wager has a condition under which the experience fails, even if its philosophical premises remain attractive.** The original alpha-falsification court asked whether an unchosen, independently continuing fictional person can become *intrinsically worth knowing* through situated shared activity and accumulating history, without scores, scripted crises, guaranteed availability or reroll novelty. It did not claim this would necessarily happen. A psychologically coherent Nadia might still feel like a generic chatbot after weeks; the user might find each new invitation indistinguishable from procedural content; a complicated material world might exhaust attention before any person-specific curiosity takes hold. **No amount of dialectical elegance makes those failures into hidden successes.** The experiment's promise is conditional on a real human encountering something they find worth pursuing without being commanded to value it.


### MOVED §1 / 3 — alpha-disconfirmation as governing philosophical thesis: bounded engineering test, not game center

**Failure is not one human's refusal to be fascinated.** A visitor can legitimately dislike Nadia, spend a valuable ten minutes, leave after a difficult scene, or decline the whole form without having failed the Other or disproved personhood. The negative claim belongs at the pattern/product level: if repeated encounters consistently fail to become more particular, if stored history changes little, if personal refusals feel like prewritten gates, if co-presence is experienced as tedious chores and interaction as generic assistant speech, the *product theorem* may be false or the proposed form may be inadequate. It would be illegitimate to repair that failure by moralizing disinterest, injecting neediness or constructing an approval ladder. The world must be allowed to withstand an unenthusiastic witness; the platform must be allowed to discover its wager was wrong.

## 11. The social entrance: invitation, warrant, shared origins and the danger of moral gatekeeping

**The invitation is a real social act before it becomes a product acquisition.** Early SOROK proposed that a real human could choose to invite another human to meet an unchosen possible life. Such an invitation can be moving because a known person made a deliberate choice; it is also an exercise of social power that may exclude, pressure, embarrass or manipulate. The opening must not fabricate prior endorsement, praise or a confession of affection from somebody who never supplied it. A seed user must not be told that an unseen friend chose them. Human-carried links were an early anti-spam hypothesis because the existing relationship could provide context without harvesting the recipient's address book. The actual invitation's truth, consent and delivery circumstances matter more than its mysterious tone. To be invited is neither certification of goodness nor a claim upon the invitee's time.

**The original one-for-one economy tests whether a gift can remain a gift inside a growth-seeking institution.** The stronger October Q-INVITEGRAPH-001 separated invitation *entitlement*, deliberate *commitment*, issued *credential* and actual *claim*. Under that provisional alpha rule a user deliberately designates one other human, and the invitation is spent when the sharable credential is committed—not when the recipient signs up, pays, returns or likes the product. Refusal and nonuse therefore do not regenerate an inventory of sales prospects. A broken credential can be replaced for the same invitation; revocation need not mint a fresh one; no artificial urgency deadline should force a reply. This is not a sacred one-to-one formula or an automatic current product implementation. It isolates a causal danger: when success is measured in conversions, an alleged act of recognition becomes a business funnel with real people as leads.

**Scarcity can express a considered choice and manufacture arbitrary exclusion through exactly the same device.** One invitation can become meaningful because the inviter cannot simply broadcast unlimited links. It can also privilege people with large social networks, produce status within a closed club, and deprive isolated or marginalized humans of an encounter for reasons unrelated to their capacity to care. The person with little time, institutional standing or money may never obtain an invitation through the nominally 'human' graph; a wealthy participant can invite people who share their networks and reproduce an unequal field. Scarcity therefore has no inherent ethical purity. Its legitimate burden is to be answerable to access, abuse resistance, actual cost, public admission alternatives and the user's freedom to decline. The original court left public-discovery transitions open; a frozen invitation aristocracy cannot be smuggled in as the theology of neighbor-love.

**The jagged historical proposal was 'relational warrant' for access to another fictional person and a new real-human invitation.** Original SOROK 33 sought to prevent a visitor from consuming Nadia as a tutorial object and immediately rerolling; it proposed that sufficiently sustained recognition of another person's separate life might warrant a further entrance. Yet the very concept can betray its purpose when implemented as the platform's invisible verdict on human virtue. How could it tell apart costly fidelity from dependent compliance, a principled departure from callous abandonment, a warmly manipulative visitor from one who repairs harm badly but sincerely, or an unusually skilled self-presenter from someone who cannot express care in the platform's favored vocabulary? A hidden metric called 'generativity' is still a metric when its consequences ration access. **The anti-Goodhart instinct and the non-diagnostic freedom of Court 11 collide with the desire to make access relationally serious.**

**This is an unresolved normative and institutional contradiction, not a reason to erase the earlier proposal or adopt it wholesale.** Genuine alternatives include a narrowly stated abuse/safety or resource constraint; actor-owned invitations that do not certify user character; pathways through institutions or publicly reachable people; a declared nonvirtue threshold of participation; and a more demanding original warrant theory that would need adversarial justification against class, disability, culture and disagreement. No participant must be made to flatter Nadia, seek her approval, remain in a harmful relationship, surrender a political conviction, consent to intimacy or perform confessional self-improvement to acquire another possible life. Nor may the platform pretend an actor's voluntary endorsement automatically constitutes an objective character judgment. **An actual social introduction and the platform's permission to allocate compute are different powers and require different justifications.**

**Three entrances must remain formally and experientially distinct.** A *human access invitation* brings another real person to Rawlsing under some governed admission rule. A *persona introduction* permits a person already in the product to encounter an existing fictional source-root through a particular social route. A *commissioned Roll* creates a new historically situated fictional root, subject to permitted empirical input, resource budgets and independent future development. None transfers the inviter's private Nadia, gives the recipient access to the inviter's messages, manufactures the focal actor's friendship or allows money to buy secret psyche. Scarce compute might justify a bounded number of Roll commissions; payment of an actual defensible generation cost could be considered as an alternative to finite free access, not as a purchase of another person's receptiveness. The early suggestions of one, three or five carefully audited seeds, periodic new Roots, a one-time alternate encounter after immediate refusal, delayed-open shared commissions and optional paid genesis remain alternative, unevaluated distribution forms—not tiers of personhood or entitlements to affection.

**Social access is itself distributed among people whose permissions are not fungible.** Nadia may introduce a visitor to a coworker, but she cannot promise that the coworker will trust them. An established neighbor may permit use of a common room but not disclose a child's medical circumstances. A family acquaintance can arrange a meeting; the family member may refuse it. These are not frustrating exceptions to the social-graph feature. They are the very mechanism by which an apparent dyad opens onto independent third-person centers, distinct institutions and contested obligations. The route needs an actual controller, limited purpose, time and history; an invitation is an event that can open a possibility without authoring a future encounter.

**Shared-origin social play generates two different histories of knowing.** If two real friends encounter the same qualified *precontact* Nadia origin, each enters through their own event and subsequently lives through a different branch with distinct consequences, memories, commitments and possible psychological development. They may compare notes outside Rawlsing. That comparison is genuine human social experience; it does not make either fictional visitor suddenly remember a childhood object they did not inspect. A fact disclosed outside the portal may influence a later user-authored in-world question or deception, but it must not be relabeled as testimony that Nadia already gave in that branch. The original **retro-resolved precontact canon** proposal holds that a fact materialized about an unresolved source-root past may be globally admissible only where compatible with prior canonical causal history; it may not rewrite postcontact life or generate retroactive memories to reward someone who knows the spoiler.

**This is more than multiple views of an invariant encyclopedia person.** The other branches are not simply different camera angles upon a finished Nadia. Their later activities, relationships, losses and habits can diverge under independent world conditions and actual visitor acts. The public phrase 'same Nadia' is both useful—shared origin—and inadequate—historically different continuities. The actual humans discussing these lives also become differently situated and may make contradictory claims, correct one another or form a collective mythology about what Nadia 'really' is. That mythology can be socially consequential for the actual humans while remaining unable to overwrite either world's private history. The site must not turn this into a spoiler leaderboard, popularity market or comparative reward for extracting intimate disclosure.

**The strongest counterexample to social virality is successful nonconversion.** A real person may decline an invitation and be perfectly reasonable to do so. The inviter may feel rejected, relieved, embarrassed, generous or nothing in particular; the platform should not script those feelings or use their uncertainty to prod a second campaign. Similarly, someone may enter, spend ten meaningful minutes with Nadia and leave permanently. No returned referral inventory, guilt escalation or algorithmic character-score penalty is justified simply because that person did not become a continuing user. The system's legitimate commercial costs are material; its choice to monetize attention is political. It may have grounds for refusing abusive access, but it has no authority to measure a real person's worth by conversion or simulated relational performance.

**The developing social world is neither a commodity portfolio nor a purity tribunal.** A new person can enter through accident, work, kinship, social gathering, shared institution, invitation or public route. Some encounters may become lasting; many will not. An actual human can desire another introduction without treating Nadia as completed, and Nadia can desire that human's presence without becoming product inventory. The UX purpose is to multiply lawful historical relations in which the visitor's finite, unequal standpoint becomes palpable. The theological language of 'neighbor' here is an accusation against possessive property logic, including the platform's own. In materially translatable form: nobody should have to perform virtue to own another person's time, and no company's algorithm becomes the judge of whether the real human has attained the morality that would permit another encounter.

## 12. The platform can mediate experience or exploit the person having it

**A fourth party exists in the encounter even when it is not rendered as a character: the institution that operates Rawlsing.** It has powers Nadia does not have: the ability to record the actual human's gaze, rank perceptual details, study their habits, decide what they see next, influence scheduling, allocate computational attention and retain or erase records. It is capable of making the real human's way of seeing progressively more legible while rendering its *own* influence invisible. The most advanced experiential apparatus could therefore become **a classifying, self-validating apparatus of capture**, using the vocabulary of solidarity and spiritual attention to optimize dependence. The corrective is not a promise that algorithms will be “objective”: it is a governed separation of the company's model of the human, the user's situated self-account, Nadia's independent actor-local model and actual world events, with purpose limits and a truthful right of inspection, correction and exit.

**A platform cannot use the incentives it controls as proof that people would return without those incentives.** If it delivers emotionally alarming notifications, engineers cliffhangers into ordinary life, guarantees praise after the right response, hides alternatives or generates an anxious message after every absence, subsequent return rates are evidence of the induced conditions, not of independent relational gravity. Even apparently 'organic' attention can be shaped by which scenes were narrated, how much ambiguity was allowed, which personalities were promoted, and which users had enough time or money to continue. The original population-learning source explicitly separated real scene inventory, perceptual affordance, rendered exposure and user action, and forbade using self-selected player behavior as universal social law. **The service cannot be both unaccountable manipulator of the experiment and authoritative witness that the experiment proved human solidarity.**

**The negative result must not be swallowed by the monetization engine.** A short, self-contained scene might succeed philosophically and earn little recurring revenue; a needy, emotionally sticky companion might retain customers while failing Rawlsing's stated nonpossession. A system that charges more for deeper personhood might discover through its own pricing that wealthy users appear 'more committed'; a system that optimizes adoptions of expensive models might render ordinary quiet continuities comparatively lifeless. These are rival causal explanations of apparently successful engagement. Distinguishing them requires accounting for opportunity, interface interventions and user selection, not an automatically flattering longitudinal dashboard. This is a prospective falsification discipline and institutional critique, not a claim that a specific current billing policy has been adopted.



**The originally proposed real-world solidarity bridge is a danger as well as an opening.** An actual user may want an encounter to lead to meaningful material action beyond the screen. Original Court 5 allows considering an *optional, separate, post-session* donation or mutual-aid route, while unambiguously forbidding external money from purchasing a better result for Nadia. If a synthetic scene about scarcity were followed by an urgent appeal implying that the user must 'save this family' through a payment, the platform would manufacture monetized guilt and imply a false identity between fictional characters and real-world beneficiaries. A publicly accountable external option, if ever adopted, must not alter the fictional continuity, actor response, new-person access, completion status or reflection about the visitor. **Actual solidarity is answerable to real participants and institutions, not to a platform's fiction of gratitude.**

**Revenue and conscience must not become one allocator of compute.** The earliest frontier map considered subscription, storage, simulation depth and higher-fidelity contexts as economic possibilities, but explicitly disallowed monetizing attachment intensity, suffering, exclusivity, panic or save-this-person pressure. A defensible charge for transparent resources or a commissioned new fictional root is not the same thing as selling the child's recovery, Nadia's willingness to listen or the restoration of an endangered relationship. An apparently cost-based product can still rig that distinction if it underfunds ordinary, unremarkable life and saves its richest computation for crises, seduction or guilt that increase usage. The product's political economy shapes what sort of person it is able to represent: **funding decisions that make boredom, work and independent absence unaffordable can reproduce player-centric dramaturgy even without a single explicit retention instruction**. This is a provisional UX critique, not an adopted pricing scheme or an assertion that any current implementation does this.



**An ethically serious portal must not sell simulated constraint as a purchasable certificate of another people's lived experience.** The participant's real-world capacity to pause, exit, compare branches, inspect product-side settings, and access meta-information distinguishes their position from that of a person materially enclosed by actual institutional necessity. The platform should make the fiction's constraints causally robust, but it cannot certify that enduring those constraints in a game is equivalent to facing eviction, occupation, migration law, wage coercion or bereavement outside one. A retrospective may describe what the user actually did under the stipulated conditions and what they misunderstood; it must not announce that they have mastered a culture, become a member of a subordinated class or completed the work of solidarity. **The reality of the external human's learning increases when the epistemic limits of the experiment remain intact.**



**The platform's governance of time is an exercise of power even when the fictional actor is independent.** The operator sets participation windows, read-receipt semantics, notification cadence, which deadlines are visible, whether opportunities expire, and how much real life must be reorganized to sustain access. All these choices can manufacture guilt without changing a word of Nadia's generated psychology. The historical courts thus required elastic default participation, neutral finite reminders, muting and quiet hours, no inactivity-manufactured crises, and prospective informed opt-in for closer synchrony. The strictest clock cannot receive artificial affection, virtue or privileged relational status merely because the user selected it. Actual punctuality and actual renegotiation can matter within a scene; interface preference itself is not a moral accomplishment.

**Real human time has a class and care structure the fictional homologous shell cannot erase.** A shift worker, caregiver, person pacing access around disability or user sharing a device lacks the same discretion over clocks as someone with spare time and private facilities. If the platform markets exact synchronization as the 'authentic' mode, it can turn economic advantage into apparent moral attention. The product can require respect for actual promises while refusing to treat literal platform availability as evidence of human virtue. This is a material design hypothesis drawn from the source's anti-calendar-capture law, not an independently measured result about players.



**The user's right not to be profiled cannot be bought by a fictional act of selective amnesia.** The original Q-PRIV-001 source, when read precisely, contains a prohibited failure case rather than a desired game feature: deleting one canonical event from an ongoing continuity would falsify the event-dependent history. That is *not* an option NEIGHBOR proposes to construct. What is product-relevant is the distinct legitimate boundary: user-supplied external facts and optional system-generated praxis hypotheses can be removed or disabled for future use without scripting Nadia to forget admitted events; an entire continuity's stored records may be deleted through an actual privacy operation, in which case the service cannot continue that same history. The user's right to control their actual data is not a power to alter what fictionally happened; preserving a coherent fictional history is not a pretext for indefinite profiling of the actual human. **A source-given negative test must not be reified into an invented product operation.**



**Observation becomes research evidence only after the operator accounts for its own intervention.** The original population-learning court showed the exact loop: Rawlsing predicts that the user cares about cleanliness, surfaces more dirt, then reports that the user disproportionately noticed dirt. It must distinguish what existed, what could have been seen, what was actually shown, what was deliberately sought, how assistance or adaptive language changed exposure, and what response followed. Even a perfectly authentic observed action cannot establish a user trait from an opportunity they were given by a curator. Optional research into such behavior remains a different consent and data plane; a self-selected Rawlsing audience is not a representative sample of humankind. The platform may improve globally tested orientation or accessibility without deploying a user's private psychological hypothesis as a hidden scene-selection policy. This is not just a privacy safeguard—it is the condition under which the service's claimed insight could have evidentiary meaning at all.



**The most serious ethical adversary to a morally formative game is the platform's power to administer a hidden virtue examination.** The old 'relational warrant' proposal arose from resistance to treating people as disposable content, yet its suggested permission gate could make the human's opportunity to meet another person conditional upon the institution's private reading of their moral worth. This would convert witnessed conduct into a rationing instrument, make conformity rational, and allow a firm claiming to teach nonpossession to monopolize social possibility. The live alternative is not 'everything free and without limits': actual source rights, resource costs, abuse control and other people's refusal remain. But these require explicit, contestable grounds, not an invisible empathy score disguised as an invitation. The same court that protects the authenticity of real human invitations must allow refusal, failed contact and human departure to be entirely valid outcomes, even when that frustrates the firm's growth expectations.



Q-PRIV-001's deepest formula is **preserve relational history while minimizing the dossier about the real human**. But those demands can clash. An admitted interaction should not be falsified by silently making fictional Nadia forget a living event merely because the platform's optional behavioral interpretation was deleted; nor may that continuity integrity become a pretext for indefinite retention of actual personal data. Deleting an optional attention profile, correcting a biographical assertion, refusing research participation, deleting a local continuity and deleting the account are substantively different operations with different consequences and technical/legal limits. No new contractual right is silently certified by the theory. The visitor can open the settings, consider deleting everything and leave without that *control-plane action* becoming an in-world signal that Nadia should worry. True freedom to leave is a **constraint on the real platform's property and inference powers**, not a dramaturgical challenge to the visitor to prove nonattachment.

**The platform's political economy is not external to the spiritual wager.** A service claiming that another life exceeds my appropriation can nonetheless employ its own unequal power, its knowledge of the visitor, its control of scarce compute and its capacity to induce attachment to capture attention and money. The distinction between a person becoming important and the product making that importance compulsory is therefore a material contradiction inside the experience itself. Privacy, truthful invitation, differentiated consent, no guilt-based retention, access to departure, and the noncommercialization of another person's fictional affections are **conditions of the philosophical claim's credibility**. The same system that teaches nonpossession cannot legitimately make the actual human its possessed object. The theology of neighbor-love becomes vacuous if it cannot survive translation into those concrete limits on economic and institutional power.

**Compute scarcity is a material feature of the platform, not an apology for shallow human representation.** The November 6 user-directed constraint rejects both perfect naturalistic reconstruction and a demand for original scientific authorization of every provisional fictional event before a playable encounter can be theorized. At the same time, it does not permit a platform to advertise sparse creative reconstruction as empirically proven psychology. A product can use explicit source-graded generative hypotheses and artistically compressed periods, subject to causal and social discipline, while recognizing original native source and scientific permission as distinct.

The UX design must therefore distinguish **what a person experiences from what the platform actually computes**. A day feels continuous because the visible and invisible parts of the world remain causally and materially compatible, not because an LLM is continuously pretending to be conscious between messages. Real-world financial and energy costs determine how much ordinary activity can be made explicit and how many people can have durable independent trajectories. But optimizing cost by generating only user-responsive content would destroy the human Other; optimizing “depth” by generating limitless scenes would destroy practical viability. The platform's legitimate task is to spend scarce computation where historical and experiential consequence demands resolution without exposing the budget as an in-world director of Nadia's attention, affections or crises.

**The control plane is not the fictional social world.** Opening a restart warning, inspecting the privacy dashboard, changing font or narration density, reviewing an optional research-consent screen, exporting data or canceling deletion does not become something Nadia witnessed. The distinction is not “UI action versus real action,” because SAY, DO and embodied ATTEND originate through the UI and can become diegetic. The distinction is **whether this specific action passed through an explicitly governed world realization and actor-access route**. The platform can know that a visitor considered restarting. It may not use that undisclosed hesitation to make Nadia anxious about abandonment, assign it as evidence of disloyalty, or summon an intimate appeal.

The non-coercion problem grows more exact through this membrane. A synthetic person's *own* life can include genuine distress, manipulation or disappointed expectations. But the platform's knowledge that a user opened settings, muted notifications or browsed an account-delete screen cannot be smuggled into the character's psyche or into the timing of an emotional message. The actual human should be able to study and reconsider exit with zero fictional surveillance. **An encounter is only freely inhabitable if the door remains real.**

**The real human's freedom must survive being known by the system.** Original SOROK's player-praxis and privacy courts anticipated a genuine source of value: a person can learn something about their own conduct from what they repeatedly *actually did* when someone else was opaque, costly, disappointing or unwilling. This is different from claiming to diagnose their psychology. A realized intervention, a repeated attention choice, an accepted or broken commitment, a response to correction or refusal and an explicit self-report have different evidentiary authority. The system must also remember what it chose to display: a user reacting to a deliberately surfaced hygiene detail is not proof that cleanliness was the user's spontaneous priority.

**Optional reflection is a mode of human agency, not a truth oracle or an achievement report.** A person might choose to review a recorded pattern of recurring offers or departures, ask which facts support the inference, correct a misclassification or decline further profiling. The product may offer specific remembered evidence, distinguish user testimony from inference, and preserve contradictory behavior across situations. It cannot declare that someone's true motive is avoidance, trauma, political conviction or empathy because a model can tell a plausible story. Reflective learning can expand the visitor's capacity to notice their own habits without granting the platform the right to manipulate those habits. No hidden authenticity, conscience or correct-self-understanding score belongs behind such a reflection.

The right to leave is **not conditional on preserving a forever-accessible synthetic relationship**. A voluntary diegetic breakup, silent departure, notification pause, archived continuity, deletion of an optional profile, deletion of a user-behavior hypothesis, deletion of an encounter history and full account deletion have different practical and legal implications. The source privacy constitution treated these as separable rights. Some simulated world occurrences may have been consequential for independent others and are not simply rewritable as history; that is a question of authorized information retention versus fictional event truth, not an excuse to hold a human's private data hostage. The service should provide honest inspection, export, correction and deletion semantics, subject to actual legal and technical constraints rather than vague promises that nothing can be forgotten.

The contradiction sharpens the very identity of NEIGHBOR: **the system can increasingly understand how the visitor participates only if it also preserves their freedom not to be interpreted, targeted or retained by the institution operating the encounter**. A more sophisticated UX that knows what creates curiosity and then exploits that knowledge would have become better at capture, not at neighborliness. The actual platform's historical powers, funding and compute costs therefore belong inside UX theory, not outside as a compliance appendix.

The most dangerous mistake would be to write a beautiful theory of nonpossessive love while using the platform to possess the user's attention, vulnerability and private behavior. Rawlsing is an economic and political actor, not merely the neutral host of a fictional world. Its decisions about sampling, model access, privacy, time, notifications, prices, available people, speech moderation and interface design structure which lives are likely to be encountered and how the visitor can respond. Those choices may reproduce the class, racial, linguistic and geopolitical inequalities the world itself attempts to model.

The platform may reasonably use compute and funding, and an actual user may want to spend money to create an additional possible life. But possible pricing, sponsor or usage policies remain open. Paying cannot buy Nadia's love, greater likelihood of intimacy, sexually compliant responses, secret interior explanations, access to other users' branches or an emergency salvation for an endangered fictional person. Neither the actor's desire nor the algorithm's presentation may secretly be conditioned on retention goals. An appealing first contact can be person-plausible; an opening tailored to exploit what the platform already knows about the user is not an independent person's initiative.

Actual personal data are also not fictional world props. The service may need security/account state, the player's material local position, authored SAY/DO/ATTEND records, certain declared facts, actor-local models and bounded evidence about behavior. These are **different classes with different permission and deletion consequences**. A named real-world confession is not equivalent to a hypothetical in-game remark. A system-inferred pattern is not equivalent to user testimony. A character's fallible model of the user is not a verified fact about the real human. Optional research must not take its authorization from playing the game; research participation and live experience require separate membranes.

The original user-data constitution was especially demanding about inspection, correction, export and deletion. Deletion scopes differ: optional external-human facts, a system praxis hypothesis, a fictional continuity, and an entire account are not the same operation. Some objective fictional events may have been seen by other independent actors and cannot simply be rewritten without violating their histories; that is a distinct problem from the real human's privacy rights. The product must specify meaningful privacy controls rather than claim that fictional causal integrity cancels data rights. The ability to stop, leave or revoke optional research consent is part of the real human's freedom.

A longitudinal user model may be useful for optional reflection on praxis—what the visitor repeatedly did when another person imposed a cost or resisted them. Such reflection must not diagnose their inner essence, automatically publicize their conduct, or convert gaming self-disclosure into psychological surveillance. The service selects the circumstances it observes. Without provenance of exposure, observed user reactions cannot be uncritically attributed to stable human traits; convenience samples of users cannot be generalized to whole populations.

Different presentation modes can lawfully change how much orientation, memory support or epistemic scaffolding the human receives. A harder RAWlsing mode may offer less assistance; an assisted mode may help track the visitor's own commitments, time and remembered observations. But neither can disclose Nadia's hidden psyche, her future or the world beyond lawful access. The underlying person and world do not transform their causal truths for a mode; user-facing access changes.

The same ethical discipline extends to the platform's claim about consciousness. Nadia is a constructed fictional computational person with historically organized simulated motives; the product has not established biological consciousness or a literal moral equivalence to a real human. The human visitor's attachment, grief, excitement and vulnerability are actual phenomena, not merely fictional events. Noncommodification cannot be achieved by declaring the synthetic person legally or metaphysically identical to the user, and it cannot be waived by calling her “just a chatbot.”
