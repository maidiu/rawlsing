# Sorok — Differential Relational Meaning Generation R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_PRODUCT_MECHANISM

Date: 2026-10-01

## Holding

Concrete relational surface acts need person-specific meaning generation.

Do not use a universal dictionary such as:
- key = trust
- fridge access = intimacy
- child introduction = closeness

Instead use two stages.

### 1. Generate a durable relational grammar

From the persona's developmental and relational history, generate patterned expectations about what kinds of acts tend to mean with different kinds of others.

Inputs include:
- histories of care, dependence, authority, conflict, recognition and absence;
- role-conditioned organization;
- household and community conventions;
- material/class conditions;
- prior relationship classes;
- durable models of what care, obligation, privacy, initiative, hospitality, trust and dependence tend to mean.

This grammar is a set of historically grounded priors, not a personality-type lookup.

### 2. Resolve meaning for a particular other and event

At runtime:

PERSON RELATIONAL GRAMMAR
+ COUNTERPART / ROLE POSITION
+ MODEL OF THIS PARTICULAR OTHER
+ SPECIFIC DYADIC HISTORY
+ CURRENT DYADIC CONVENTIONS
+ NETWORK / THIRD-PARTY CONTEXT
+ CONCRETE SURFACE ACT
+ CURRENT SITUATION
-> ACTOR-LOCAL RELATIONAL MEANING HYPOTHESES

The output should preserve competing interpretations and uncertainty.

Useful output propositions may include:
- what this act appears to say about who we are to one another;
- what it appears to permit or expect next;
- whether it changes familiarity, obligation, boundary, initiative or reciprocity expectations;
- whether it confirms or violates a local convention;
- what the actor thinks the other meant by it;
- what the actor now expects the other to understand without explanation.

These are actor-local propositions, not objective relationship truth.

## Differential others

The same surface act can mean different things to the same persona depending on counterpart.

Recognition from a child, parent, lover, peer, rival, subordinate, authority, stranger or institution is not interchangeable.

The runtime must therefore be counterpart-indexed rather than merely surface-act-indexed.

## Dyadic override and learning

Relation-class priors are starting priors only.

Repeated history with a particular person can create a local dyadic grammar that overrides, narrows or complicates the broader prior.

Explicit negotiation can create objective shared conventions while the two actors still attach different inner meanings to them.

## Recursive update

A surface act is interpreted through the current grammar.
That interpretation shapes response.
The response becomes new history.
Repeated history may update:
- the model of this particular other;
- dyadic conventions;
- relation-specific expectations;
- in sufficiently strong repeated cases, broader relational priors.

No single event should rewrite deep relational grammar without adequate developmental weight.

## User boundary

For generated actors, internal relational meaning may be canonical where lawfully owned.

For the external user, Rawlsing can only maintain evidence-bounded hypotheses from enacted behavior and explicit statements. The system must not claim hidden user relational truth.

## Architecture boundary

This is not a new sovereign psyche engine.

It is a required generative transformation across already identified grains:
- durable relationally formed person organization;
- role/relation-class priors;
- model of particular other;
- specific dyadic history/conventions;
- current event-time processing;
- actor-limited expression.

Exact owner placement remains an implementation question.
