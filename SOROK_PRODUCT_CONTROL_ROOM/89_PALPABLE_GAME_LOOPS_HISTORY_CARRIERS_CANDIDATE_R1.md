# Sorok — Palpable Game Loops: Candidate History-Carrying Mechanisms R1

Date: 2026-10-03

Depends on:

## 0. Design target

Make causal history **felt** without translating it into:
- score;
- reward currency;
- relationship level;
- unlock tree;
- quest completion;
- hidden approval meter;
- guaranteed disclosure/intimacy.

Candidate principle:

> **MAKE CAUSALITY LEGIBLE WITHOUT MAKING VALUATION LEGIBLE.**

The player should often perceive:
> what I did changed the situation

without receiving:
> that was the correct relationship move.

## 1. History-carrier triad

A consequence feels especially real when some combination of three properties exists:

### TRACE
The event leaves an observable residue.

Examples:
- repaired drawer;
- broken cup;
- unread message;
- changed plan;
- borrowed book absent from shelf;
- groceries now in the apartment;
- remembered disagreement;
- a new standing permission.

### RE-ENTRY
The residue later becomes relevant again through ordinary causality.

Examples:
- Nadia uses the repaired drawer;
- the missing book matters later;
- another actor remembers the disagreement;
- a prior promise constrains scheduling;
- a joke becomes local shorthand;
- an object appears again in a photograph.

### AFFORDANCE CHANGE
The history changes what is now possible, expected, awkward, easy or unavailable.

Examples:
- user can now enter without asking in one specific context;
- a route closes;
- a recurring invitation exists;
- a third party now knows the user;
- an apology becomes possible because an offense exists;
- a favor creates a practical dependency;
- an object is no longer available because it was damaged/lent/lost.

Candidate theorem:

> **TRACE + RE-ENTRY + CHANGED AFFORDANCE makes history tactile.**

Not every event needs all three.

Forcing every act to echo would itself become artificial significance.

## 2. History-bearing mundane objects

Use ordinary material state as persistent memory carriers.

Examples:
- user helps fix a drawer;
- user leaves a mug somewhere;
- Nadia lends a book;
- shoes finally break;
- a plant is moved;
- groceries bought together get consumed later;
- a note remains on the fridge;
- a borrowed tool returns damaged or not at all.

The object is not a quest token.

Its changed state simply persists.

Later scenes can encounter the persistence without a "callback" announcement.

## 3. Relationship conventions as felt state

Local conventions can make relational history perceptible without meters.

Examples:
- whether the user normally knocks;
- where they sit;
- whether they help themselves to tea;
- whether Nadia texts before calling;
- whether a certain joke has become safe/annoying;
- who normally pays;
- whether the user is expected to greet the child;
- whether a recurring task has become ordinary.

Once a convention exists, a later deviation can matter.

Do not encode:
`CONVENTION = RELATIONSHIP LEVEL`.

The convention is a specific history-conditioned practice.

## 4. Commitments as natural loop generators

Promises/plans/favors create future causal stakes without quests.

Examples:
- call tomorrow;
- return a book;
- pick something up;
- help move a shelf;
- meet after work;
- bring something for dinner;
- check in after an appointment;
- do not tell a third party something.

These create:
- expectation;
- opportunity;
- possible failure;
- scheduling constraints;
- later interpretation.

A commitment is not a mission objective.

It is history that can later constrain the relation.

## 5. Persona initiative as palpable independent life

The independent-life loop becomes much more visible if the persona sometimes initiates because of their own causal state.

Examples:
- asks for practical help;
- sends something because it reminded them of the user;
- cancels because work changed;
- invites the user into an existing plan;
- says they cannot meet;
- follows up on something previously discussed;
- asks a question;
- contacts someone else instead.

This must remain:
`PERSONA-CAUSED INITIATIVE != PLATFORM RETENTION NUDGE`.

No engagement escalation.

No "we miss you" platform logic.

## 6. Scene-openings that carry history

Later encounters should often begin from a world already changed by prior events rather than resetting to neutral chat context.

Examples:
- the repaired drawer is now in ordinary use;
- the user enters via a route previously established;
- a third party recognizes the user;
- Nadia is already irritated about a missed commitment;
- an earlier borrowed object is sitting ready to return;
- a routine greeting occurs automatically;
- a previously unresolved practical problem has worsened or disappeared.

Do not force a conspicuous callback every session.

The principle is:
> **history changes initial conditions.**

## 7. Routines and meaningful deviation

Repeated ordinary practice can create low-level expectations.

Then deviation becomes perceptible without an authored "event."

Examples:
- Nadia usually texts before arriving;
- user normally takes shoes off;
- they usually cook when they meet;
- the child usually interrupts;
- Nadia normally avoids a certain subject.

A deviation may mean:
- nothing;
- circumstance;
- mood;
- changed relationship;
- hidden world cause.

Do not automatically interpret the deviation.

The user notices a changed pattern and may investigate.

## 8. Simultaneous demands and finite attention

Palpability increases when the user cannot inspect every available thing at once.

Example:
- Nadia is telling a story;
- child is doing something in the room;
- kettle is boiling;
- unfamiliar object is visible;
- phone rings.

The user may:
- stay with the story;
- shift attention;
- act materially;
- miss something.

This should not become punitive branching/FOMO design.

The goal is situatedness:

> **ATTENTION HAS OPPORTUNITY COST BECAUSE LIFE IS SIMULTANEOUS.**

## 9. Cross-context recurrence without scripted callback

A fact becomes palpable when it returns through another lawful route.

Examples:
- shoes seen in room later appear in a photo;
- a person mentioned in conversation later enters the scene;
- a workplace complaint later affects scheduling;
- a book on shelf later becomes part of a discussion;
- a child repeats something the user once said.

Do not select the recurrence because it would be narratively satisfying.

Use actual causal/history relevance.

This preserves:
`RETURN OF FACT != AUTHORIAL FORESHADOWING`.

## 10. Third-party memory and network consequence

The relation should sometimes become palpable through people other than Nadia.

Examples:
- sibling remembers meeting the user;
- child has their own impression;
- coworker knows the user's name because Nadia mentioned them;
- friend has a different interpretation of a shared event;
- third party independently contacts the user where access law permits.

This demonstrates:
- the world is networked;
- relationship effects can leave the dyad;
- Nadia does not own third-party relations;
- each actor has their own model and memory.

## 11. Knowledge should change future perception, not world truth

When the user lawfully learns something, later narration can become more specific because the **user** now recognizes it.

Example:
first:
> a blender.

Later, after learning:
> the Vitamix.

This gives player learning a palpable effect without changing the object.

Likewise:
- names;
- places;
- people;
- rituals;
- books;
- tools;
- habits.

Compact:
`USER KNOWLEDGE CHANGES FUTURE LEGIBLE DESCRIPTION != USER KNOWLEDGE CHANGES REALITY`.

## 12. Branch-local continuity surfaces

A user may need help remembering a long continuity.

A lawful support surface could summarize only:
- user-observed events;
- user-acquired facts;
- explicit commitments;
- established access/conventions;
- user-facing messages/calendar facts.

It must not reveal:
- hidden person truth;
- actor-private state;
- completion percentage;
- root dossier;
- system causal explanation.

This is not a quest log.

Candidate framing:
> **continuity aid, not objective tracker**.

Exact necessity remains open.

## 13. Foreclosure and lost possibility

Irreversibility becomes palpable when opportunities can pass.

Examples:
- a first impression cannot be replayed;
- an event happens without the user;
- a temporary invitation expires;
- someone else solves a practical problem;
- a conversation moves on;
- an object is discarded before investigation.

Do not use product-side FOMO.

The lost possibility arises from world time and causal reality.

## 14. Native-skill tasks

Mundane situations can become playable when success depends on capacities the actual user exercises through the medium:

- remember something;
- explain;
- persuade;
- notice;
- infer;
- choose timing;
- ask a good question;
- interpret ambiguity;
- decide whether to intervene;
- communicate a difficult point.

Do not auto-resolve these through high-level prose when the human can actually do the meaningful part.

## 15. Palpability without forced significance

A dangerous implementation would ensure every session contains:
- a callback;
- a new access route;
- a conflict;
- a reveal;
- a relationship delta.

That would restore authored dramaturgy.

Instead use a **distribution**:
- some actions leave obvious traces;
- some consequences are delayed;
- some are inert;
- some never return;
- some routines simply continue;
- some sessions are mainly ordinary life.

The loop should be palpable statistically and longitudinally, not mechanically announced turn by turn.

## 16. Candidate design formula

A strong Rawlsing interaction often has:

`SITUATED CHOICE`
→ `OBSERVABLE OR STORED CONSEQUENCE`
→ `PERSISTENCE`
→ occasional `LATER RE-ENTRY`
→ possible `AFFORDANCE / EXPECTATION CHANGE`.

The feedback is:
> history exists and constrains the next situation.

Not:
> you earned progress.

## 17. Prototype questions

1. How frequently must prior history visibly re-enter before the continuity feels causally persistent?
2. How much ordinary/inert play is tolerable before the loop feels mushy?
3. Which consequence carriers are most legible without feeling authored?
4. How much user-facing continuity support is needed after long absences?
5. Can routines/conventions become perceptible without an explicit relationship model?
6. How much persona initiative feels like independent life rather than companion-product pursuit?
7. Can finite attention create situatedness without FOMO or punishment?
8. Do material traces produce stronger felt continuity than conversational callbacks?
9. Can changed affordances be discovered naturally rather than announced?
10. What alpha scenarios best falsify the claim that history itself is rewarding?
