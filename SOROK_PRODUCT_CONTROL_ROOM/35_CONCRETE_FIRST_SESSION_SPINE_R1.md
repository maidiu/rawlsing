# Sorok — Concrete First Session / First Contact Spine R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_ALPHA_EXPERIENCE_PROTOTYPE__NOT_SCRIPT_CANON

Date: 2026-09-30

## 0. Purpose

Instantiate the current product laws into one concrete first-use sequence.

This is not a required Nadia script.
It is a hostile prototype for discovering missing mechanics.

Sequence:

`REAL HUMAN INVITATION`
→ `SHORT PRIMER`
→ `PORTAL ENTRY`
→ `THROWN INTO LIVE SITUATION`
→ `BEGINNING-TO-KNOW`
→ `RECIPROCAL INQUIRY`
→ `NATURAL SCENE TERMINATION`
→ `OUTSIDE-PORTAL HUB`
→ `MESSAGE CHANNEL IF LAWFULLY ESTABLISHED`.

---

## 1. First screen — invitation

Referral-route example:

> **You have been invited to Rawlsing.**
>
> Someone who knows you chose to send this to you.

If the inviter supplied an authorized note, show it separately and exactly/faithfully.

Do not imply more praise than was actually given.

The user can:
- accept;
- decline;
- inspect what Rawlsing is before accepting at a bounded level;
- optionally see who invited them if inviter disclosure permits.

---

## 2. The invitation should not explain the whole product

The invitation's job is to create enough curiosity and legitimacy to enter.

Do not front-load:
- architecture;
- scoring explanation;
- relationship theory;
- simulation disclaimers beyond what is materially needed;
- a long feature tour.

---

## 3. Primer — challenge, not tutorial

Candidate conceptual copy:

> **A life has been drawn.**
>
> You did not get this one.
> Someone else did.
>
> You are about to live beside them.
> You will not be told who they are.
>
> Pay attention. Ask. Remember. Guess wrong. Learn.
>
> They will be learning you, too.
>
> The world will keep moving while you get to know each other.

Then a minimal portal-law line:

> You may say or attempt anything. What you type is an attempt; the world determines what actually happens.

Then:

> **ENTER**

---

## 4. Interface transition

Accepting ENTER visibly changes surface.

Outside surface:
- familiar messaging / app shell;
- clean contemporary UI.

Portal surface:
- terminal/TI-calculator/old-computer aesthetic;
- text-forward;
- spacious enough for long natural language;
- no stats;
- no relationship meter;
- no avatar paper doll;
- no quest log.

The aesthetic change should make mode change felt before explained.

---

## 5. First portal load — already in motion

Do not spawn the persona standing neutrally awaiting the player.

Example scene:

> 5:34 PM.
>
> The stairwell smells faintly of fried onions and damp concrete. Your apartment is 4B.
>
> Across the landing, the door to 4A is propped open with a grocery bag.
>
> A woman is trying to get two more bags through the doorway while a child inside calls something you can't make out. A phone is buzzing on the kitchen counter.

No:
- `NADIA — LEVEL 1`;
- explicit psychology;
- tutorial marker saying HELP HER;
- glowing interactable object.

The user can do nothing, speak, attend, help, go inside their own apartment, etc.

---

## 6. The first contact route can be mundane

If the user engages, the woman/persona responds from their actual current situation.

Example possibilities:
- accepts help;
- says she's got it;
- asks user to hold the door;
- barely acknowledges them because she's busy;
- child becomes the first real conversational bridge;
- user says something and gets a short answer;
- user waits and conversation begins after the immediate task.

The encounter does not need a dramatic hook.

---

## 7. Task-entry mechanics teach the world without tutorial text

If user says:
> I help with the groceries.

Portal reveals concrete affordance state and returns control.

The user learns by doing that:
- broad intent opens a real situation;
- details matter;
- time passes;
- the persona has an agenda;
- the world does not fill in the user's meaningful choices silently.

No explicit mechanic explanation is required unless confusion occurs.

---

## 8. First conversation should be reciprocal if causally natural

The persona should not merely answer questions.

Possible early reciprocal moments:
- `You just move in?`
- `What do you do?`
- `How do you know that?`
- `You always carry that much at once?`
- `Where'd you learn to cook like that?`

These arise only if current history/context gives the persona reason to ask.

The point is that the user feels:

> **I am also becoming an object of curiosity and interpretation.**

---

## 9. The first scene must contain ordinary hooks

Potential hooks visible in the flow of life:
- child/homework;
- work uniform/badge;
- religious object/practice;
- half-heard phone conversation;
- music;
- a joke/opinion;
- a practical problem;
- a place/person mentioned;
- a magnet/photo/object;
- a disagreement with the child;
- an invitation she is considering;
- something she dislikes.

None is guaranteed to produce depth.

The user decides what interests them.

---

## 10. Do not force friendship chemistry

The first scene may be:
- warm;
- awkward;
- funny;
- neutral;
- brisk;
- mildly irritating;
- oddly compelling;
- mostly practical.

The success criterion is not:
> user and persona bonded.

It is:
> the persona became particular enough that the next encounter would not be interchangeable with another stranger encounter.

---

## 11. Natural termination

The scene must have a real temporal horizon from the outset.

Example:
- Nadia has to leave by 6:05 for a shift;
- child must be taken somewhere;
- user has a portaled obligation;
- meal is about to begin;
- bus arrives.

As time advances, that constraint becomes pressing.

At termination:
- persona leaves;
- user leaves;
- both separate;
- conversation may end mid-topic;
- not everything gets resolved.

This teaches:

`YOU CANNOT EXHAUST A PERSON IN ONE SESSION`.

---

## 12. Contact-channel formation

Before separation, a message channel may or may not be established.

Possible routes:
- she asks for user's number for practical reason;
- user asks and she agrees;
- building/neighborhood directory already creates lawful contact;
- user is added to a local group;
- no channel exists yet.

The first run should not fake a WhatsApp thread if no relational/world event created it.

---

## 13. Portal exit

When co-presence ends, UI transitions back to outside-portal shell.

If contact was established:

> **Nadia**
> no message yet / last active unknown / whatever metadata is lawfully visible.

If no contact was established:
- no Nadia thread appears;
- the user may need another physical/local encounter route.

This makes contact itself meaningful.

---

## 14. First message should not be guaranteed

Nadia may:
- send something practical later;
- follow up on something;
- send nothing;
- reply only if user messages;
- message days later after a causal reason arises.

Do not manufacture:
> It was nice meeting you 😊

unless this actor in this history actually sends it.

---

## 15. Outside-portal user options

Potential actions:
- open established thread;
- send a message;
- wait/leave;
- re-enter portal only through an actual meeting/physical opportunity;
- inspect remembered history with provenance if assisted-memory mode exists;
- manage product settings.

Do not provide:
- `Visit Nadia` button;
- teleport-at-will;
- relationship progress screen.

---

## 16. Second encounter

A second embodied scene may arise from:
- chance/local adjacency;
- user proposing a meet and persona agreeing;
- persona invitation;
- practical reason;
- shared event.

When it begins, the first encounter already matters.

Possible evidence:
- remembered name/detail;
- a joke callback;
- changed greeting;
- changed expectation;
- unfinished topic;
- slight guardedness;
- practical assumption based on what happened before.

This is the first proof of persistent personhood.

---

## 17. First-session reward target

The user should ideally leave with some combination of:
- curiosity about the person;
- awareness of one mistaken assumption;
- one remembered detail;
- sense that the persona learned something about them;
- a tiny shared history;
- uncertainty about what the persona thought;
- desire to see what happens next.

Not all must occur.

---

## 18. Failure modes this prototype exposes

- persona waits like chatbot;
- grocery/task mechanics dominate the encounter;
- every question receives exposition;
- user can interrogate indefinitely because time has no teeth;
- persona never asks user anything;
- scene ends by UI button rather than life;
- message channel appears magically;
- first message is engagement bait;
- next meeting is menu-driven teleport;
- ordinary hooks are too weak to sustain curiosity;
- first scene is so constrained that user cannot meaningfully choose.

---

## 19. Alpha spine

Current strongest simple alpha product:

> **One invitation. One unknown person. One finite first encounter. One life that continues. One messaging channel if you actually establish it. One next meeting that must arise from the relationship/world.**

Everything else can grow from proving that experience.
