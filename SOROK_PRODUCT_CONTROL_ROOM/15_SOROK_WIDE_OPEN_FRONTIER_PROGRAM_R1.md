# 3. Immediate exploration sequence

Do not begin by writing schemas for all of this.

## Court 1 — PLAYER / ENCOUNTER PREMISE

Questions:
- Who is the user in-world?
- Why did they meet this person?
- Why can they plausibly become important to one another?
- How much freedom does the user have to define material capacities?
- What forms of encounter preserve randomness without contrivance?

Why first: the action system cannot be designed in a vacuum if we do not know what kind of social creature the player is.

## Court 2 — WHAT IS A RELATIONSHIP STATE?

Questions:
- What changes when two people become closer?
- What is person-owned, dyad-owned and merely believed?
- What does trust permit that did not exist before?
- Can a relationship have durable properties irreducible to either person?
- What does repair actually change?

Why: this is the game object even though it should never look like a score object.

## Court 3 — LIFE OUTSIDE THE USER

Questions:
- What happens while the player is gone?
- What events happen for reasons unrelated to the player?
- What makes Nadia contact or not contact them?
- How much life must actually be simulated?
- What is sufficient for the user to experience another person's independence?

Why: this is the difference between a chatbot and a person-with-a-life.

## Court 4 — SUCCESS WITHOUT A SCORE

Questions:
- If friendship is not flourishing, what is gameplay progress?
- What makes the experience rewarding?
- How should increased intimacy increase stakes/difficulty?
- What makes caring competently discoverable without a morality meter?
- What would a 20-year relationship retrospective show?

Why: without this, Rawlsing may be technically extraordinary but still not a game/product.

# 4. Program law

The purpose of the next phase is not to maximize the number of resolved questions.

It is to produce a **provisional coherent picture** in which ontology, causal mechanics, relationship dynamics, world-time, moral physics, UX and alpha scope mutually constrain one another.

A locally elegant answer should be rejected if it makes another core frontier impossible.

---

# 5. Current next move

Begin with Court 1 and Court 2 together.

The player/encounter premise and the relationship-state premise are mutually constraining.

Use concrete lives/scenes, not abstract schemas, and continually crosscheck inherited Rawlsing ownership, relational ontology, alpha playability, privacy, anti-domination and implementation cost.

The result should be a provisional product-world premise, not a final architecture.
