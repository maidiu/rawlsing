# Sorok Courts 1–2 — Player / Encounter Premise + Relationship-State Provisional Synthesis R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_IDEATION__NOT_ADOPTED__CROSS_CUTTING_D001_D002

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Why these two courts are coupled

The player cannot have an action grammar until Rawlsing knows what kind of participant the player is.

The relationship cannot have a coherent state model until Rawlsing knows how the player lawfully enters another person's life.

So Court 1 (player/encounter) and Court 2 (relationship state) should be developed together.

---

## 1. Player premise — bounded diegetic self

Strong current hypothesis:

The player should be **behaviorally themself but materially/socially represented through a bounded diegetic shell**.

This separates at least five things:

1. real human user;
2. explicit user-supplied stable facts/constraints, if any;
3. diegetic player-presence facts needed by the simulated world;
4. actual in-world user actions/utterances;
5. actor-local models/inferences about the user.

Do not collapse them.

### Why not simply 'the literal real user'?

The generated world still needs lawful answers to:
- where the user lives/stands;
- why they know Nadia;
- what languages they can use;
- whether they have a car/tool/money/key;
- what obligations bring them back into contact;
- what Nadia already knows about them.

Rawlsing cannot safely infer all of this from the user's real life.

### Why not a fully fictional player-character?

That weakens the central longitudinal claim that the product is observing the user's own relational praxis.

If the user is primarily roleplaying someone else's personality, later reflection becomes evidence about role performance rather than necessarily about the user's ordinary ways of relating.

### Candidate compromise

The player's **choices, words, attentional allocation, promises, avoidance, repair, curiosity and treatment of others** are authored by the real user.

The world provides or requests only the minimum diegetic material facts necessary to make those choices causally possible.

This remains a hypothesis and requires privacy / roleplay-mode adjudication.

## 2. Possible player-presence modes

Do not freeze one yet.

### Mode A — self-anchored

The user explicitly provides a small set of real-world facts relevant to play, for example:
- age band;
- languages;
- transport capability;
- broad work/time constraints;
- perhaps name/pronouns;
- voluntarily declared resources/skills.

Advantage: strongest claim that this is 'you in relation'.

Risk: privacy burden and mismatch between real geography/material life and generated persona world.

### Mode B — veil-embedded self

The user brings their choices and consciousness, but Rawlsing assigns a bounded local embedding:
- you recently moved here;
- you live nearby;
- you are part of this institution/project;
- you possess only explicitly declared/generated ordinary capacities.

Advantage: strong gameplay plausibility with little personal-data collection.

Risk: reflection on behavior is still real, but some choices are conditioned by assigned circumstances.

### Mode C — explicit role-authored player-character

User intentionally defines a fictional role/self.

Advantage: creative/replay value.

Risk: should probably weaken or disable claims about longitudinal real-user praxis.

Candidate direction:
modes may coexist, but the core alpha should choose one rather than blur them.

## 3. Encounter law — random person does not mean random teleportation

The persona should be unchosen by the player.

But contact must arise from an independently plausible social route.

Candidate encounter-route families:
- spatial adjacency: neighbor / same building / same block;
- institutional adjacency: coworker / class / community organization;
- network adjacency: friend-of-friend / relative-of-acquaintance;
- practical relation: volunteer / shared task / mutual aid;
- digital adjacency: language exchange / group chat / hobby community.

The route must explain:
- why contact exists;
- why repeat contact is plausible;
- what each person currently expects of the other;
- what spaces/information are initially accessible;
- why disengagement is also possible.

Encounter route != relationship destiny.

---

## 4. Alpha encounter hypothesis — neighbor / local adjacency is unusually strong

For an alpha, a neighbor-like relation may be the cheapest rich substrate.

It naturally supports:
- repeated but nonmandatory contact;
- hallway/street/home scenes;
- practical help;
- packages/noise/transport/childcare/food/tools;
- gradual access to household/family life;
- asynchronous sightings and absences;
- overlapping local events;
- plausible reasons not to talk;
- relationship escalation without formal quest assignment.

It also avoids needing to simulate an entire shared workplace or school.

Potential opening premise:

The player's bounded diegetic shell has newly entered the same local environment as the randomly generated person.

This is not adopted. It should be compared against other encounter routes for contrivance, cultural breadth, class assumptions and replayability.

---

## 5. Repeat contact must have non-romantic causal support

The product must not rely on:
- fate;
- instant chemistry;
- both people mysteriously wanting to keep talking;
- notification manipulation;
- the persona needing the user.

Early repeated contact should emerge from mundane adjacency, obligations, practical situations, routines, shared institutions or network overlap.

Relationship becomes voluntary only gradually.

This matters spiritually: the user did not choose the person because the person was already gratifying.

---

## 6. Relationship outcome is not predetermined friendship

The ostensible game may invite the user to build a relationship.

But lawful histories should include:
- friendship;
- warm acquaintance;
- practical mutual aid without intimacy;
- ambivalent attachment;
- conflict;
- estrangement;
- one-sided interest;
- dislike with respect;
- failed repair;
- long absence and later return;
- relationship that matters deeply for a finite period and ends.

Therefore:

RELATIONSHIP DEVELOPMENT != FRIENDSHIP LEVEL INCREASE.

A failed or bounded relationship can still be a real Rawlsing history.

---

## 7. Candidate relationship ontology — keep the objective dyad sparse

Strong current hypothesis:

Most things we casually call 'relationship state' should **not** be stored as one objective pair vector.

Separate at least:

### A. Objective shared relational history

Facts such as:
- encounters/communications that actually occurred;
- promises and commitments;
- invitations;
- practical obligations;
- access permissions;
- shared projects/resources;
- injuries and repair attempts;
- mutually enacted routines;
- explicit agreements;
- network introductions;
- current practical dependencies.

### B. Actor A's relation-specific state/model

For Nadia, potentially:
- expectations of the user;
- model of user's reliability;
- domain-specific trust;
- attachment/importance;
- grievance;
- anticipated reactions;
- what she thinks the user understands;
- what she thinks the relationship is;
- what she permits herself to need/ask/show with this person.

### C. Actor B's relation-specific state/model

Corresponding but non-identical user-side/object-model material where system authority permits it.

### D. Mutually recognized relational facts

Examples:
- both explicitly call one another friends;
- agreed childcare arrangement;
- shared key;
- established weekly dinner;
- mutually acknowledged rupture;
- declared romantic/exclusive agreement if such modes ever exist.

These are objective because the mutual acts occurred, not because the system inferred a hidden essence of the relationship.

---

## 8. Avoid the hidden friendship meter in architecture too

A UI can avoid displaying a score while architecture secretly behaves as:

closeness = 0.73

That would still flatten the ontology.

Candidate principle:

many apparently scalar relational concepts should instead be represented as **predictions, permissions, expectations, histories and domain-specific dispositions**.

Example:

TRUST should probably not mean one number.

It may mean different predictions such as:
- will keep a confidence;
- will show up when promised;
- will not retaliate against disagreement;
- can safely be vulnerable with;
- can be trusted with child/money/home;
- will listen to practical instructions;
- can tolerate saying no.

These need not move together.

---

## 9. Intimacy as affordance / expectation transformation

Candidate definition:

Intimacy is not primarily a quantity of hidden information revealed.

It is a relational history that changes what actions, needs, expectations, risks and permissions become causally available.

With intimacy, a person may become newly able to:
- ask for help;
- expect remembrance;
- protest neglect;
- be angry without assuming abandonment;
- act silly/playful;
- reveal incompetence;
- depend in one domain;
- say no;
- involve family;
- grant physical/home access;
- expect practical reciprocity.

Therefore increased intimacy may make the relationship harder.

The user matters more, so more can be expected of them.

---

## 10. Relationship progress should be visible through world change

Rather than a meter, the user experiences:
- different invitations;
- different rooms/access;
- different kinds of requests;
- being introduced to people;
- being omitted from things;
- practical dependence;
- routines forming;
- silence becoming meaningful where it previously was not;
- anger becoming possible where politeness previously dominated;
- private jokes/history;
- expectations around remembering/returning;
- network consequences.

These are gameplay-readable without exposing hidden internal truth.

---

## 11. Repair is not score restoration

Candidate repair sequence:

injury / failure
→ perception and attribution
→ recognition or denial
→ explanation / apology / restitution / changed conduct
→ actor-specific interpretation
→ evidence accumulated about future reliability/conflict safety
→ possible changed permissions/expectations.

Repair does not erase the original event.

Successful repair may increase one kind of trust while leaving another reduced.

Failed repair may itself become more important than the original mistake.

---

## 12. Abandonment / absence is a real action

If the user stops showing up:
- Nadia's life continues;
- the relationship changes only through lawful interpretation/expectation;
- she may care, not care, resent it, adapt, forget, contact, or not contact;
- later return occurs into a changed world.

The product should not guilt-message merely to preserve retention.

Absence should be a causal fact, not a monetization trigger.

---

## 13. Player-reflection consequence

If Rawlsing later reflects user praxis, it should primarily reflect **realized user behavior across relation histories**:
- promises kept/missed;
- responses to withdrawal;
- attention patterns;
- boundary reactions;
- repair behavior;
- whom the user helps and how;
- how they react when care is not rewarded;
- how they behave once another person expects something.

It should not pretend to know the user's private motive merely because the diegetic shell is called 'you'.

---

## 14. Hard tests for Court 1

1. Can a random persona enter the player's life without obvious matchmaking/game contrivance?
2. Can the user plausibly say no and still have a coherent world?
3. Can the encounter produce repeat contact without the persona being needy or fascinated by the user?
4. Can the alpha avoid collecting sensitive real-user biography?
5. Can the system distinguish real-user behavior from assigned material circumstances?
6. Can multiple encounter routes preserve the same core product theorem?

---

## 15. Hard tests for Court 2

1. Can Nadia trust the user with her child but not with emotional vulnerability?
2. Can she be attached while expecting unreliability?
3. Can the user and Nadia disagree about whether they are close?
4. Can the relationship have a real commitment neither person feels warmly about?
5. Can repair increase conflict safety while decreasing practical trust?
6. Can intimacy increase demands and annoyance without being interpreted as relationship deterioration?
7. Can a long relationship matter without ever becoming friendship?
8. Can the architecture represent all of this without a hidden scalar closeness variable?

---

## 16. Current provisional lean

Most promising combined premise:

**The user enters as a bounded diegetic version of themself into a causally plausible, unchosen relation of repeated local/institutional adjacency. The relationship is represented primarily as objective shared history/permissions/commitments plus asymmetric person-owned models and expectations, not a friendship score. Intimacy is the historical transformation of what the relation makes possible and costly.**

This is a product hypothesis for further court, not an adopted architecture requirement.
