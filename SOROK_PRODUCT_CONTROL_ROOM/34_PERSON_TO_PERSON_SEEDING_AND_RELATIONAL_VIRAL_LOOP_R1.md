# Sorok — Person-to-Person Seeding and Relational Viral Loop R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_DISTRIBUTION_THEOREM

Date: 2026-09-30

## 0. Core proposal

Rawlsing's initial distribution may be deliberately person-to-person rather than open discovery.

Before public press/discovery creates organic inbound demand:

> **Every new user arrives because another real human invited them.**

This is not merely growth mechanics.

It aligns product distribution with Rawlsing's relational thesis.

---

## 1. Entry is itself relational

The user's first fact about Rawlsing is not:

> I found an app.

It is:

> **Someone who knows me chose me for this.**

That creates:
- curiosity;
- seriousness;
- social proof grounded in an actual relationship;
- a sense that entry carries relational meaning;
- an immediate connection between real-world relation and the synthetic relation about to begin.

---

## 2. Referral claim must be literally true

Rawlsing must never fabricate:

> someone in your life said you impacted them positively.

If the opening uses that premise, an actual person must have supplied the referral.

Possible inviter action:
- nominate one person;
- optionally write a short note;
- optionally choose whether their identity is revealed immediately;
- consent to the exact wording shown to invitee.

Rawlsing may summarize only within explicit consent or use fixed wording that accurately reflects the act of referral.

---

## 3. Strong candidate invitation framing

Conceptually:

> Someone who knows you asked Rawlsing to extend you an invitation.
>
> They believe knowing you has mattered in their life.
>
> A life has been drawn.
>
> If you accept, you will meet them through the portal.

Exact copy remains open.

Important:
- no fake testimonial;
- no exaggerated claim;
- no anonymous manipulation;
- inviter controls whether identity/note is disclosed.

---

## 4. Scarcity creates meaning

Initial invitations should likely be scarce.

Possible model:
- seed cohort receives a small number of invitations;
- invitations are not purchasable;
- users do not receive unlimited invite codes immediately;
- later invite availability may depend on product-level relational warrant or elapsed meaningful use.

Scarcity can communicate:
> this is something you pass to a person, not a link you spray.

Do not turn scarcity into status theater or resale.

---

## 5. Viral loop aligned with core product

Potential loop:

`REAL HUMAN RELATION`
→ `INVITATION`
→ `PORTAL ENCOUNTER WITH GENERATED PERSON`
→ `RELATIONAL PRAXIS / WARRANT`
→ `ABILITY TO INVITE ANOTHER REAL HUMAN`
→ repeat.

This creates a growth mechanism that is structurally homologous to the game itself:

> relation opens relation.

---

## 6. Invitation should not certify goodness

Being invited does not mean:
- morally good;
- empathic;
- healthy;
- trustworthy in every domain;
- endorsed by Rawlsing.

It means only that a real person chose to extend access.

Similarly, earning an invitation to give should not become a visible moral badge.

No:
- invite level;
- virtue score;
- social rank;
- public invite count leaderboard.

---

## 7. Relational warrant may govern outbound invites

Strong candidate:

A user begins with no outbound invitation rights.

After Rawlsing has sufficient evidence of relational warrant in the user's synthetic relationship history, it may offer:

> **There is someone in your life you may invite, if you want.**

This would make the viral hook itself one of Rawlsing's deepest rewards.

Not:
> You beat Nadia.

But:
> **The system is now willing to let your relational world propagate outward.**

Exact warrant remains Court 10 / succession work.

---

## 8. Inviter choice is itself meaningful

The user must decide:
- who in their actual life they want to invite;
- whether to explain why;
- whether to attach a note;
- whether to reveal themselves immediately;
- whether this person would actually want the experience.

This can create a real-world reflective moment without forcing disclosure.

Do not require the inviter to submit a psychological assessment of the invitee.

---

## 9. Cold-start / seed cohort

Someone must originate the graph.

Possible seed mechanisms:
- founders personally invite initial cohort;
- researchers/testers receive direct seed invitations;
- a tightly bounded alpha cohort receives origin invites labeled as such.

Do not fake prior referral for seed users.

After enough graph growth, person-to-person invitation can become the dominant entry route.

---

## 10. Press/public-discovery transition

Later publicity may make open discovery unavoidable/desirable.

Possible later modes:
- public waitlist requiring sponsorship/referral;
- public application plus relational invitation;
- open access while keeping referral as privileged narrative route;
- completely open access after network effects mature.

Do not prematurely assume the initial invitation-only structure must remain forever.

---

## 11. Growth advantage

The loop has several organic growth properties:
- every invite carries interpersonal meaning;
- invitees are primed to take the experience seriously;
- users have a natural story to tell about how they entered;
- the product is socially transmitted rather than ad-explained;
- scarcity can increase curiosity;
- the invitation itself can become a memorable ritual.

Potentially:

> the first viral story about Rawlsing is not 'download this app' but 'someone sent me through the portal.'

---

## 12. Risks

### Exclusivity/status capture
Invitation scarcity becomes elite cachet.

### Referral pressure
Users feel obligated to invite or accept.

### Manipulative praise
Invitation copy overstates what inviter actually said.

### Social embarrassment
Invitee rejects/ignores invite; inviter should not receive shame-inducing feedback.

### Abuse / targeting
Invites used to harass or repeatedly contact someone.

### Popularity bias
People with broader social networks propagate faster than isolated users.

These need product constraints.

---

## 13. Anti-growth-hacking law

The referral mechanic must never incentivize:
- mass invites;
- contact scraping;
- invite streaks;
- referral rewards;
- public invite counts;
- pressure notifications;
- escalating scarcity FOMO.

The loop should grow because the invitation means something, not because growth mechanics exploit people.

---

## 14. Deep theorem

> **Rawlsing can begin as something passed from person to person because a relationship is the reason anyone enters at all.**

And:

> **The distribution graph can mirror the game's moral architecture: relation opens the possibility of another relation.**
