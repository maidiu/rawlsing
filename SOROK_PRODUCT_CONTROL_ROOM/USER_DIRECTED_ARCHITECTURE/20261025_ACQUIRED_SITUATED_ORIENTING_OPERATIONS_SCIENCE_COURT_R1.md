The unit is NOT merely vocabulary acquisition. ## 3. DIRECT PRIMARY-THEORY COMPARISON

Vygotsky: definitions and word repetition are not adequate evidence of acquired concepts. Signs/words are tools for attending to and isolating relations in practical activity. Concept formation is a history of operating with signs, not memorizing names. Public meaning and actual personal use can diverge.

Galperin/Talyzina: the orienting BASIS of an action and its executive component are distinguishable. A person can learn what to inspect in a problem and how to organize an action. This offers a finite programmable conditional mechanism; the pedagogical progression material action -> overt language -> internal action should NOT become a universal developmental pipeline or age schedule.

Leontiev, Sève and SDTF T1: purposeful activity, goals/actions, condition-regulated operations, personal sense, use of time and acts-capacities reciprocity. A concept is developmentally consequential where it changes relation to practical objects and future capacities. It may be deployed without conscious articulation.

Holzkamp / SDTF T2: objective social possibilities become actor-specific premises, grounds and practical alternatives through situated social life. New means of orientation can enlarge the space of subject-grounded possibilities without determining the action selected. A person may know what is possible but choose to preserve local dependency for entirely intelligible reasons.

Lahire: plural historically acquired dispositions can be active, dormant, inhibited or contradicted across contexts. Local acquisition is not global transfer.

Dreier: the person's participation across places/relationships/obligations matters; an operation may exist within collaboration while independent enactment is blocked by resources, status or local power.

Rogoff: learning is transformation of participation in a mutually changing social practice, not simple transfer of knowledge packets from exterior to an isolated inner mind. Support can be constitutive of current ability, not necessarily an inferior stage.

Karmiloff-Smith: implicit competence may precede access to its own internal relations and later reflexive redescription. Siegler's overlapping waves/microgenetic approach: strategic variability, old strategies returning, and new forms coexisting undermine a tidy staircase. Mischel/Shoda: context-conditioned organization predicts patterned heterogeneity rather than uniform trait expression.

Luria's supplied Cognitive Development documents historical changes in categorization alongside schooling and social reconstruction, but cannot justify universal literate/industrial superiority or direct culture label -> concept/intellect.

## 4. SEPARATE DISTINCT EVIDENCE; THERE IS NO SIX-STEP PERSON SCORE

(a) SIGN ENCOUNTER: an actor witnessed a gesture, word, instruction or model; no uptake implied.
(b) DISCRIMINATION: the actor distinguished a relation in a specific context, possibly without knowing a term; not automatically general competence.
(c) CONCEPTUAL STANCE: actor can formulate or use a contrast for reasoning under ADP017 and language constraints; not necessarily practical application.
(d) GUIDED PARTICIPATION: an independently acting other/tool/scaffold enables supported performance; competence belongs to the encounter and shared activity.
(e) INDEPENDENT SITUATED USE: actual actor performs a distinguished operation under recorded material/relational conditions without the named scaffold; not global transfer.
(f) HISTORICALLY REPRODUCIBLE MEDIATION/PRACTICE: an authorized CAN011 rule warrants persistence of a relevant activity-organizing relation under particular context/history.

These are orthogonal evidence dimensions with possible multiple sequences, uncertainty, regression and context-dependent activation—not a rank from child to ideal adult. Sometimes action-first, then word. Sometimes word-first, then practical uptake. Sometimes supported performance exists indefinitely without independent enactment because the surrounding practice is constitutively collective. A person who can make the distinction may nevertheless elect NOT to use it.
