## 5. The BIG developmental finding: new CONCEPTUAL AND PRACTICAL OPERATIONS emerge

(1) organismic and affect-regulatory capacities, developmentally changing and individually variable;
(2) experiential discrimination and pattern sensitivity, which may function without articulate language;
(3) socially accessible symbols/words/gestures/rules and their practical use;
(4) actor-acquired ability to categorize, compare, decouple, reinterpret, plan or self-regulate using those means;

## 6. Biology/culture: scientifically distinguish constraints, not a false dichotomy

**Biological commonalities**: organismic maintenance/need, development of neural/attention/memory/affect regulatory capacities, maturational constraints, individual differences. They constrain possible functioning at specific times. Their existence does not determine that each child already uses mature emotion categories, theological distinctions or political concepts.

**Experience/culture**: alters accessible roles, objects, categories, scripts, meanings, opportunities, practices, tools, expectations, networks and even embodied/regulatory development. It does not follow that every expression or affect category is completely arbitrary.

**Gene/environment interplay**: 2007 Gottlieb probabilistic epigenesis and neuroconstructivist sources explicitly argue multilevel development; 2025 Kandler & Instinske review finds polygenic and poly-environmental intertwined contribution, not one gene/one trait. 2026 Haehner et al meta-analysis of 229 longitudinal studies (N=230,492) finds differences in trait change persist across adulthood. 2024 Bleidorn review says environment AND genetics influence change, but precise causal mechanisms remain insufficiently understood.

**Emotion concepts**: Widen/Russell tasks suggest broad-to-differentiated child categories; TASK EVIDENCE on labels/facial expressions is NOT proof that young children only have two biological emotions. Recent experience sampling and emotion granularity work examines context-dependent distinctions; language-only granularity can be culturally biased. 2025 basic-versus-constructed emotion debate is actively contested; Barrett et al explicitly dispute a neat biological-emotion/constructed-feeling split proposed by opponents. Hence DO NOT constitutionalize an “Inside Out five emotions added one by one” stage schedule, a settled two-factor valence/arousal theory, or a universal social-construction-only answer.

**Recommended falsifiable model posture**: multi-timescale developmental constraints plus actor-specific acquired conceptual/functional repertoire; representations are not all present at birth. Treat representational availability and physiological capacity separately, with interactions through legitimate events/practice. Use experience and concepts to alter what the person can discriminate, contemplate, narrate or do, not to create a fixed per-culture list of emotional states. Do not infer “more emotion vocabulary” automatically entails finer subjective feeling, healthier regulation or identical cross-cultural dimensional meaning.
