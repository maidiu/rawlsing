The access topology is only the extensional surface: who can go where, contact whom, borrow what, participate in what, under what conditions. Underneath it has to be an intensional relational-semantic substrate: what these acts mean to each person, given the relational grammar history has formed in them.
And crucially, the surface is not just a readout of that substrate. It also constitutes and changes it.
Your example is basically perfect:
Nadia: “Come over whenever. Help yourself to the fridge. Take any books you want.”
Objectively, several access facts now exist.
But Nadia may mean:
You’re not a guest anymore. I’ve stopped maintaining that formal boundary with you. Why are you still asking me like we barely know each other?
While the user may mean:
She is very generous with these specific things. Precisely because I care about her, I’m not going to presume that “fridge” generalizes into “whole house / family / privacy / unlimited familiarity.”
Neither is misreading the objective permission. They are operating with different grammars for what that permission signifies relationally.
So we need at least this distinction:
objective relation-history fact
→ “Nadia gave standing fridge permission.”
Nadia’s meaning of it
→ “This expresses that he belongs here informally now.”
user’s stance toward it
→ “This is a specifically bounded generosity.”
Nadia’s model of the user’s stance
→ “Why does he still think he has to ask?”
user’s model of Nadia’s meaning
→ “She probably means this literally, but I don’t know how far the implied familiarity travels.”
And then the really human loop begins.
User asks again.
Nadia experiences the asking itself as new relational evidence.
Maybe:

Or:
He doesn’t trust that I mean what I say.

Yes. I think that is the generative bridge we were missing.
Within the theological-Marxian reading you’re using, the key thought is basically: human value does not originate inside the object. Human activity and relation become materially borne by the object. Fetishization is what happens when that social/human provenance disappears from view and the thing confronts people as though the value belonged autonomously to the thing itself.
For Rawlsing, that gives us something much stronger than “objects have symbolism.”
It gives us:
\[ \text{PERSON'S VALUE-FORMATION HISTORY} \times \text{OBJECT'S MATERIAL/HUMAN HISTORY} \rightarrow \text{ACTOR–OBJECT VALUE RELATION} \]
and then:
\[ \text{ACTOR–OBJECT VALUE RELATION} \times \text{THIS OTHER} \times \text{THIS DYAD'S HISTORY} \times \text{MODE OF SHARING} \rightarrow \text{RELATIONAL MEANING} \]
That is, before Nadia decides what lending this book to this person means, the system should already know something about what books—and this book—are to Nadia as repositories of human value.
A useful actor-object value representation might contain things like:
	•	its use in her life;
	•	labor or care invested in it;
	•	who made/bought/gave/repaired it;
	•	what period of her life it belongs to;
	•	which people it materially connects her to;
	•	whether it represents deprivation, abundance, survival, aspiration, sacrifice;
	•	replaceability versus scarcity;
	•	exchange price and commodity/status significance;
	•	debt, obligation, inheritance, ritual, taboo;
	•	whether possessing it gives recognition from others;
	•	whether she experiences its value primarily through those human relations or partly as autonomous property/status/exchange-value.
Then you ask the second question:
What does placing this value-bearing thing into relation with this particular other do?
That is where the differential other becomes decisive.
The same annotated book might mean:
	•	to her child: “I am giving you part of what formed me”;
	•	to a peer she respects: “I think you can inhabit this intellectual space with me”;
	•	to a lover: unusually intimate self-exposure;
	•	to a coworker: completely ordinary lending;
	•	to someone careless with possessions: a surprisingly risky act;
	•	to an authority figure: maybe presentation of cultivated self;
	•	to somebody she distrusts: perhaps “you may read this, but I am not letting it leave my house.”
The book hasn't changed. Nadia hasn't even necessarily changed.
The human relation into which the book is projected has changed.
And then the act itself changes the object.
If the user annotates that book too, now it can become a shared artifact. If they lose it, the loss isn't equivalent to its $12 replacement cost. If they return it six months later with a note inside, the book now literally contains dyadic history. If Nadia later gives it away, that action has a new meaning because the object's value-history has changed.
So the recursion is:
human relations produce object-value → object mediates a new human relation → that relation changes the object's value → changed object-value mediates later relations.
That is extremely Rawlsing.

≠ player state
≠ supplied facts
≠ event history

The research idea fits beautifully if we make it a separate causal plane.
I would distinguish roughly:
	•	play data: what happened in this continuity;
	•	presentation settings: explicit accessibility/verbosity/interface choices;
	•	praxis analysis: bounded hypotheses derived from play;
	•	optional research data: demographics, attitudes, values, etc. supplied under separate consent;
	•	population findings: aggregate results used to improve later Rawlsing models.
And the crucial arrow is:
OPTIONAL RESEARCH
→ POPULATION-LEVEL ANALYSIS
→ FUTURE GENERATOR / MODEL / PRODUCT REFINEMENT
not:
OPTIONAL RESEARCH ABOUT MATTHEW
→ CHANGE WHAT MATTHEW'S NADIA SHOWS HIM.
This could be tremendously useful for your sociology.
For instance, instead of relying entirely on inherited datasets for correlations between structural position, cultural background, values, personality dimensions, relational attitudes, religiosity, political-economic attitudes, etc., Rawlsing could eventually accumulate a new empirical corpus.

our current model assumes A and B covary under these conditions; actual respondents show much more heterogeneity;
or:
this supposedly universal personality/value relationship disappears when stratified by material security;
or:

or:

That could improve the joint distribution from which people are generated, without ever personalizing the synthetic person to the research donor.

That last piece is huge. If we study whether users investigate Nadia's material environment, the research record has to know:
what was actually in the room;
what was perceptually available;
what the narrator surfaced;
what the user explicitly ATTENDed to;
what the user subsequently inferred/did.

One correction to your proposed privacy copy
I would not say:
“Rawlsing will never collect or sell your personal information”
if immediately afterward we're inviting them to tell us demographics, values, geography, attitudes, etc.
That is information about the person, even if we don't want their name.
The stronger truthful promise is more like:
Rawlsing does not sell your personal information. Ordinary play does not require a demographic or psychological profile. Separately, you may choose to contribute information to Rawlsing’s research into human social and psychological variation. Research participation is optional and is used to improve population-level human realism, not to tailor your synthetic relationships to you.
Then we can be even stronger architecturally: research records can be separated from ordinary account/profile state, demographic fields can be coarse, rare combinations protected, and the live game doesn't even have access to the research record.
One other scientific caveat: Rawlsing users will be a wildly self-selected sample. That doesn't make the research useless at all, but it means we shouldn't take “37% of Rawlsing users with X believe Y” and call that a population prevalence estimate. It would be excellent for discovering relationships, falsifying assumptions, improving our model, and generating hypotheses. Representative societal claims would require better sampling.

The product gets better because we can empirically learn how much text, what scene structure, what perceptual sampling rules, etc. let humans actually inhabit these spaces.
The generator gets better because optional aggregate human research can sharpen our sociology.
And the sociology gets better because the privacy/ontology separation prevents the engine from contaminating its own observations by tailoring what it exposes to the person it is studying.
The compact law I'd use is:
Learn from people at the population level; encounter each player through what they actually do.

Yes. I think you just found the distinction that makes the whole thing work:
Nadia and her world stay independent of the user. The user’s perceptual route through that world does not have to be generic.
That is different from tailoring Nadia to the user. And I think it is actually necessary if we want text to stand in for embodied perception rather than for a novelist telling everybody what matters.
The thing I would now call a regime of attention is not “what topics this user likes.” It’s closer to a probabilistic model of what features this particular human tends to register, recognize, inspect, and discriminate.
So when Matthew and another user enter exactly the same canonical kitchen, physical salience still matters for both: the Everest of dishes is hard to miss; a hole in the floor is hard to miss; Nadia standing at the sink is hard to miss. But beneath that common floor, their phenomenal surfaces can diverge.
One person’s eye catches the worn Birkenstocks, grime line around the baseboard, laundry accumulation and french fry. Another person's eye catches the Vitamix, Japanese knives and stand mixer. Another immediately reads the Nabokov spines and notices Me Talk Pretty One Day. Another spots the festival photographs and recognizes where one was taken. Another sees prayer flags but doesn't distinguish them from generic decorative bunting. Another identifies their actual form and perhaps has cultural knowledge about them.
Those are not different Nadias.
They are different epistemic trajectories through Nadia.
And that is fantastic for Rawlsing.
The system needs to distinguish noticing from knowing what you noticed
There are several layers here that text tends to collapse:
visible form → readable marking → identification → domain knowledge → interpretation.
Take the blender.
A completely generic perceptual rendering might be:
A heavy blender sits beside the mixer, its clear pitcher cloudy from use.
If the logo is plainly facing the user, maybe:
A heavy blender marked VITAMIX sits beside the mixer.
Someone who knows blenders now thinks, oh damn, Vitamix. Someone who doesn't simply registers a blender.
If the user's history has already demonstrated considerable culinary-equipment knowledge, Rawlsing might lawfully render:
A Vitamix 5200 sits beside the mixer.
But it still should not say:
an expensive high-end blender suggesting Nadia is serious about cooking.
That's interpretation. Let the user do that.
Same with the knives. There's a large difference between:
several knives
and:
three narrow dark-handled knives on a magnetic rail, Japanese markings near their heels
and:
three carefully maintained Japanese knives
and:
$700 worth of Japanese cutlery.
Different user knowledge entitles different levels of automatic recognition.
That gives us another very useful separation:
ATTENTION REGIME != RECOGNITION REPERTOIRE.
I can be highly attentive to knives and know fuck-all about knives. Or be a knife nerd but currently engrossed in Nadia telling me her mother is sick and fail to notice them altogether.
So yes, there can be a feedback loop — but it needs an escape valve
I think I was too absolutist before about forbidding user-model influence on semantic perception.
A better law is:
The attention model may affect the probability that an already-existing perceptible fact enters awareness. It may never affect whether that fact exists.
And then the critical anti-loop rule is evidence provenance.
Suppose Rawlsing automatically shows me the dirty dishes because its model already thinks I'm cleanliness-sensitive. I comment on them. That is weak evidence that I'm cleanliness-sensitive, because Rawlsing itself caused the exposure.
But if, several times, I independently do things like:
I glance around the baseboards.
What's under the fridge?
How clean is the countertop?
Is that dish towel damp or dirty?
then that's strong evidence about my attention.
Similarly, if Rawlsing says:
There are books under the coffee table.
and I immediately ask:
Titles?
then later spontaneously recognize Nabokov editions, ask about marginalia, notice the translation, etc., that's increasingly good evidence about my literary attention/recognition repertoire.
So every learned-attention datapoint should know how the relevant information reached me:
user-targeted, neutral ambient, attention-model-weighted, physically unavoidable, task-required, pointed out by Nadia, etc.
And the model should learn much more strongly from the first category than from its own output.
That prevents:
system predicts dirt attention
→ system shows dirt
→ user reacts to dirt
→ system becomes certain user attends to dirt
→ EVER MORE DIRT.
Which would be hilarious but epistemologically catastrophic.
There should also always be some exploration mass—details outside the user's established regime can still enter awareness.
So the user's attention model biases perception; it does not become a prison.

OBJECT / HISTORY FACT

→ present recruitment in this event
→ behavior/expression.

Given the particular person and world that already exist, what additional concrete reality would most lawfully have been true all along?

At the past-side boundary, it knows roughly what Nadia was like eleven years ago: her stage of life, money, relationships, geography, ideological/social organization, etc.
At the present-side boundary, it knows:
owns old Birkenstocks
they survived ~11 years
still wears them
doesn't want to replace them
plus present Nadia's already-computed V3 organization.
And between those endpoints it already has major historical checkpoints:
suburban/normie-ish Nadia
→ exploratory/travel/elephant-pants Nadia
→ grad-school/decolonial-theory Nadia
→ present Nadia.
The historical generator's job is therefore not:
invent something interesting eleven years ago.
It's:
find a historically plausible event at T−11 whose forward consequences can traverse the already-established Nadia history and arrive coherently at present Nadia.
That's much closer to smoothing in a state-space model than normal forward generation: reconstruct a latent past while conditioning on both earlier context and later observed state.
Then you run it forward.

Where late generation should be allowed to alter Nadia
I think you need roughly four causal weights, conceptually:
DESCRIPTIVE
→ can fill historical/material detail; no durable psyche rewrite.
EPISODIC
→ adds memories, object histories, local relationships, habits.
SYMBOLIC / RELATIONAL
→ can add durable valence, recurring memory recruitment, preferences, emotional significance.
DEVELOPMENTALLY FORMATIVE
→ would materially alter V3 organization or major life trajectory.
The first three are good candidates for lazy historical resolution.
The fourth is dangerous.
If ATTEND discovers something that would require:

then that probably should not be invented lazily at age 34 unless that event was already latent in the compiled developmental spine.
That kind of thing should usually be generated during deep precomputation.
So I would impose a strong rule:
Late historical materialization may explain, specify, and locally enrich established developmental state; it may not casually manufacture a new developmental cause whose absence would have made current V3 materially different.
That's the engineering boundary that makes the whole idea tractable.

How delusional is the overall idea?
The bounded version is not delusional. It is ambitious but very plausible engineering.
The delusional version would be:

No. That explodes combinatorially.
The plausible version is:
deeply precompute the high-causal-weight developmental spine; represent life as sparse event history + state checkpoints; lazily resolve lower-weight particulars under both backward and forward constraints; propagate only typed causal residues through the affected cone; and reject/version anything whose required downstream rewrite exceeds the reconciliation budget.
That is not science fiction.
It's difficult software. But it's recognizably difficult software: event sourcing, dependency graphs, state snapshots, deterministic seeded generation, constraint checking, incremental recomputation, caching, and LLMs used as bounded proposal engines.
The LLM should not be the database and should not be the causal simulator.
It should be very good at:

Then code adjudicates.

That is exactly the thing a generative engine is for.
