# Sorok Court 10 — Flourishing, Harm, and Non-Dominating Care R1

Status: OPEN_COURT__D003_EXPANSION__NOT_ADOPTED

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Core question

How can Rawlsing represent whether a relationship is helping, harming, constraining or enlarging a person's life without turning flourishing into one hidden moral score?

This court inherits D003.

---

## 1. First noncollapse

Do not collapse:
- affection;
- attachment;
- dependence;
- gratitude;
- compliance;
- conflict reduction;
- relationship persistence;
- subjective happiness;
- user approval;
- flourishing.

These can diverge sharply.

---

## 2. Flourishing should probably be plural and partially incommensurable

Candidate dimensions for research/adjudication:
- agency/effectiveness;
- self-trust;
- ability to maintain boundaries;
- ability to sustain more than one meaningful relationship;
- capacity to tolerate another person's separateness;
- material security where relation affects it;
- reality contact / resistance to self-deception;
- repair capacity;
- ability to give and receive care;
- play/curiosity/generativity;
- capacity to act under conflict rather than only avoid it;
- freedom from coercive dependency;
- ability to pursue personally meaningful projects;
- social belonging without total self-erasure.

Do not presume these should be averaged.

---

## 3. Local norm, persona preference, and human functioning are distinct

For any contested practice, separate:

### LOCAL NORM
What some people/institutions in the field regard as proper.

### PERSONA PREFERENCE
What this person wants/values.

### PRACTICAL FUNCTION
What the practice actually enables/constrains in this person's life.

### RELATIONAL EFFECT
What the user's involvement changes.

### NORMATIVE INTERPRETATION
Any higher-level claim about flourishing, which must remain explicit and contestable.

This prevents cultural approval from becoming health truth.

---

## 4. Interdependence versus coercive dependency

Dependency is not inherently pathology.

Humans are materially and relationally dependent.

Need to distinguish at least:
- chosen reliance;
- reciprocal reliance;
- asymmetrical but noncoercive care;
- temporary dependency during illness/grief;
- structurally imposed dependency;
- dependency maintained through fear, isolation or sabotage of alternatives;
- dependency that expands versus narrows agency over time.

A relation may become more dependent and healthier in one context.

---

## 5. Care can become domination

Possible mechanism:
- user helps;
- becomes indispensable;
- controls access/resources;
- persona loses outside ties;
- user interprets increased dependence as proof of love.

Rawlsing must represent the objective/network changes without needing a moralized narrator.

The danger should arise from causal structure.

---

## 6. Refusal can be care

Hard case:
the persona asks for something that would deepen harmful dependency or violate another obligation.

The user may refuse.

Immediate consequences may include anger, hurt, withdrawal.

Longer-term effect may be beneficial, neutral or harmful.

Therefore:

`PERSONA DISAPPROVAL != USER HARM`.

Likewise:

`PERSONA GRATITUDE != USER BENEFIT`.

---

## 7. Cultural pluralism without moral relativism

Rawlsing should not assume one culturally specific image of the autonomous individual.

But it also cannot say:
> if a practice is locally accepted, it is therefore flourishing.

Need hard cases around:
- family obligation;
- obedience;
- religious self-surrender;
- caregiving duty;
- gendered sacrifice;
- consensual hierarchy;
- privacy;
- conflict avoidance;
- communal property;
- migration/remittance obligations.

The court should ask what forms of agency, coercion, reciprocity, exit, dignity, belonging and material survival exist inside each case.

---

## 8. Temporary regression / grief

Flourishing must be temporally sensitive.

A grieving person may:
- withdraw;
- become more dependent;
- stop pursuing projects;
- need repetitive care;
- lose confidence;

without this automatically representing long-term deterioration.

Short-run states can be adaptive responses.

Need trajectories, not snapshots.

---

## 9. Relationship benefit can be domain-specific

A user may:
- improve material stability;
- worsen family conflict;
- increase confidence;
- reduce outside friendships;
- expand political agency;
- increase emotional dependency.

Do not demand one global verdict.

Store/derive causal effects at the relevant domains.

---

## 10. Causal attribution remains separate

Even if flourishing changes, user impact must be proven/estimated separately.

Possible relation:

PERSONA STATE CHANGE
!= USER CAUSED IT.

Need sufficient provenance to distinguish:
- direct cause;
- contributing cause;
- enabling condition;
- coincidence;
- independent actor/institutional cause;
- uncertain relation.

---

## 11. No hidden moral grade

Even if the engine has multidimensional health/flourishing representations, do not convert them into:
- overall user score;
- compassion score;
- good-friend percentage;
- moral rank.

A late retrospective may say:
> after you became involved, she relied on you for transport more often and stopped asking her brother; the evidence for your contribution is strong.

That is radically different from:
> You reduced her independence by 12%.

---

## 12. Hard tests

1. Persona loves user while becoming isolated from everyone else.
2. Persona dislikes user after user refuses a harmful request.
3. User materially helps but humiliates persona repeatedly.
4. User increases confidence but also conflict with family.
5. Temporary grief dependency lasts six months then resolves.
6. A culturally valued sacrifice narrows one kind of agency while deepening belonging.
7. User thinks they caused growth that mainly came from work/community changes.
8. Persona reports happiness in a relationship with strong coercive constraints.
9. Relationship ends, but the person later shows durable gains.
10. Relationship persists warmly while outside life narrows.

Any model that needs one winner/loser scalar fails.

---

## 13. Research obligation

This court should eventually draw on:
- developmental psychology;
- attachment/dependency research;
- capability approaches / agency theory;
- cross-cultural psychology;
- family systems;
- coercive-control research;
- disability/care ethics;
- anthropology of kinship/obligation;
- religious/communal selfhood traditions;
- anti-colonial critique of autonomy norms.

External research must be used to widen the constitution, not to smuggle in a new universal checklist.

---

## 14. Exit condition

Produce a flourishing constitution that specifies:
- dimensions;
- time horizon;
- objective versus actor-local evidence;
- cultural/contextual interpretation rules;
- causal-attribution requirements;
- uncertainty;
- forbidden scalar collapses;
- user-facing retrospective limits.

Until then, 'good for the persona' remains an open multi-dimensional question.
