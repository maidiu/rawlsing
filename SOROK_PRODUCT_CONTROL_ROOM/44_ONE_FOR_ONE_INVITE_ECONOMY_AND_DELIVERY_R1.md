# Sorok — One-for-One Relational Invite Economy and Trustworthy Delivery R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_DISTRIBUTION_CONSTITUTION__DELIVERY VARIANTS OPEN

Date: 2026-09-30

## 0. Core economy

Current preferred rule:

> **A user receives no outbound real-human invitation until relational warrant is established. Thereafter, each new synthetic persona allocated to that user's Rawlsing continuity yields exactly one outbound invitation to another real human.**

Thus:

`RELATIONAL WARRANT`
→ `NEW SYNTHETIC PERSON MAY ENTER USER WORLD`
+ `ONE REAL-HUMAN INVITE BECOMES AVAILABLE`.

This is one-for-one relational propagation, not referral farming.

---

## 1. Important noncollapse

`NEW SYNTHETIC PERSON ALLOCATION`
!=
`OUTBOUND REAL-HUMAN INVITATION`.

They are separate events that may be granted by the same warrant milestone.

The user does not 'trade' one for the other.

---

## 2. Invitation scarcity

One invite per newly allocated persona gives the invitation genuine stakes.

No:
- unlimited codes;
- invite bundles;
- referral rewards;
- invite farming;
- pay-to-invite;
- public invite-count prestige.

A user may have at most the invitations actually earned under this law.

Whether a declined/expired invite returns to the user remains open.

---

## 3. Early deliverability problem

An anonymous platform-generated message such as:

> A friend of yours from Fargo, ND, who feels you mattered in their life, has invited you...

has serious cold-start problems:
- looks like spam/phishing;
- may be filtered;
- recipient has no brand trust;
- coarse location can feel creepy;
- anonymous praise can feel manipulative or fabricated even when true.

Therefore early Rawlsing should not rely on cold anonymous platform mail as its primary viral channel.

---

## 4. Preferred early delivery: human-carried token

The inviter receives a one-time signed Rawlsing invitation link/token.

They transmit it through an existing human channel they already use with the invitee:
- SMS;
- WhatsApp;
- Signal;
- email;
- DM;
- QR/in-person;
- other ordinary sharing.

This solves:
- spam shield;
- trust;
- address acquisition;
- creepy unsolicited platform contact.

The landing page can then verify:
> this invitation is real and single-use.

---

## 5. Mystery can survive without anonymous sender

The sender can be known while the invitation remains charged with mystery.

Possible framing:

> **I had one invitation. I chose you.**

Then Rawlsing:

> A life has been drawn.
> If you accept, you will meet someone through the portal.

Unknowns remain:
- why exactly the inviter chose the recipient;
- what Rawlsing is going to ask of them;
- who the generated person will be;
- what life was drawn;
- what will happen in the encounter.

Early product should prefer **trusted mystery** over anonymous mystery.

---

## 6. Avoid 'alternate self' collapse in invitation copy

Current Sorok theorem is:

> the generated persona carries an alternate-life burden but remains irreducibly another person.

Therefore avoid literal copy:
> meet your alternate self.

Prefer something like:
> meet someone whose life could have been yours under another roll;

or the more oblique:
> a life has been drawn. You did not get this one.

Exact copy remains open.

---

## 7. Optional inviter note

Inviter may optionally attach a short note.

Potentially:
> I wanted you to have this.

or their own words.

Rawlsing should display the inviter's words exactly or under an explicit character limit.

Do not paraphrase them into stronger praise.

---

## 8. Anonymous invitation may become viable later

Later, after Rawlsing has brand recognition or a voluntary waitlist, platform-mediated anonymous/semi-anonymous invites may become practical.

Example:
- recipient has already opted into a Rawlsing waitlist;
- known/verified contact information exists;
- an inviter spends an invitation on that registered person;
- recipient receives expected Rawlsing mail/push rather than unsolicited stranger mail;
- inviter may choose reveal-now / reveal-later / remain private under defined rules.

This should not be the initial viral dependency.

---

## 9. Seed cohort

Initial graph still needs origin nodes.

Candidate:
- founder/research alpha seed users are explicitly marked as seed invitees;
- they begin with a generated persona;
- they receive no outbound human invitation until relational warrant;
- first earned invite starts person-to-person propagation.

Do not fabricate an upstream human inviter for seed users.

---

## 10. Growth shape

One invitation per newly allocated persona tends toward a slow branching relational graph rather than explosive virality.

That may be desirable:
- quality control;
- social meaning;
- manageable infrastructure;
- scarcity;
- reduced spam;
- user cohort grows with product evidence.

Actual acceptance/retention determines effective reproduction rate.

---

## 11. Outstanding exact questions

Still unresolved:
- is invitation consumed on SEND or ACCEPT?
- can a technical delivery failure restore it?
- does an explicit decline restore it?
- does the inviter learn acceptance/decline?
- can invite be revoked before acceptance?
- expiry duration, if any;
- can inviter attach identity/location metadata;
- exact abuse/report/block controls;
- exact relation between synthetic-person warrant and outbound-human-invite warrant if later evidence argues for separation.

---

## 12. Deep theorem

> **Rawlsing's viral unit should be a person choosing one other person, not a user distributing referral inventory.**

And:

> **Early mystery should come from the veil and the encounter, not from making a trustworthy human invitation look like phishing.**
