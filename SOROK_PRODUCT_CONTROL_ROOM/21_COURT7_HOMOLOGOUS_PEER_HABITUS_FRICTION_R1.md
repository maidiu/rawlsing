# Sorok Court 7 — Homologous Peer and Habitus Friction R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_PRODUCT_ONTOLOGY__NOT_ADOPTED

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Core insight

Homologous-peer transposition is not merely resource balancing.

It creates an experiential structure in which the user is socially legible as a local peer while carrying a consciousness actually formed elsewhere.

This generates **habitus friction**.

The persona cannot simply explain every difference as obvious outsider behavior. The user's assumptions may instead read as locally odd, elite-coded, unusually individualistic, unusually cosmopolitan, unusually conservative, unusually direct, unusually indirect, or simply unfamiliar.

These are actor-local interpretations, not objective cultural verdicts.

---

## 1. Operationalizing the alternate-self impulse

Original Rawlsing aspiration: 'here is your alternate self.'

Sorok cannot literally turn the actual user into the person they would have become under another developmental history without replacing the user with a generated alternate person.

Homologous-peer mode offers a different operationalization:
- retain the actual user's present consciousness, judgments and habits of attention;
- place them in a social/material position structurally comparable to the encountered person's;
- make them socially legible through the local field rather than as an obviously privileged visitor;
- let the encountered person embody the thick developmental reality of that world;
- make competent relationship action require learning that reality from within the interaction.

The user begins outside the veil mentally while being placed inside comparable constraints materially/socially.

---

## 2. Neighbor-love operationalization

Candidate structure:

1. the user is materially positioned as a peer/neighbor;
2. the other person is not chosen for compatibility;
3. the user retains their own consciousness rather than roleplaying the other;
4. the user must learn how this particular person/world actually works;
5. attempts to care succeed or fail through causal reality, not through the user's self-conception;
6. the relationship recursively changes what both people can expect, ask, resist or become.

This preserves the moral subject as the user while forcing them into the practical horizon of another person's life.

---

## 3. Social unmarkedness as a mechanic

A strong candidate property of homologous-peer mode is that the player is not automatically marked as an obvious foreign outsider.

That means the persona may interpret the user's foreign-formed assumptions from inside local categories rather than excusing them as visitor behavior.

This creates self-recognition pressure.

But the system must never convert one persona's judgment into objective cultural truth.

---

## 4. Assimilatory tension

If the user wants to be understood, trusted or regarded as competent, they may feel pressure to learn:
- local etiquette;
- local practical realities;
- moral language;
- class-coded behavior;
- religious/cultural references;
- privacy norms;
- reciprocity expectations;
- family/household conventions;
- what different people regard as generous, rude, weak, arrogant, loving or humiliating.

The user may respond by:
- adapting;
- code-switching;
- asking;
- observing;
- selectively accepting local norms;
- resisting;
- persuading;
- trying to change others;
- building a hybrid relational grammar;
- withdrawing.

All are possible actions with consequences.

Adaptation must not be silently treated as winning.

---

## 5. The user may challenge the local field

The user is not a passive anthropology student.

They may judge a local norm harmful, unjust, manipulative or simply mistaken.

They may argue, persuade, organize, refuse or model alternatives.

That may have beneficial, harmful, mixed, relational and network consequences.

The game must preserve the possibility that the user is sometimes right against local convention.

Otherwise homologous-peer mode becomes conformity training.

---

## 6. Anti-essentialism law

LOCAL FIELD != ONE CULTURE.

Do not generate a single cultural consensus.

Generate particular people situated by:
- household;
- generation;
- class position;
- religion or nonreligion;
- gender/role history;
- neighborhood/institution;
- peer networks;
- material pressures;
- developmental history.

Another equally local person may reject the same norm.

A user's apparently nonlocal idea may already have local analogues, subcultures or political/religious traditions.

Homologous-peer mode should expose intra-field contradiction, not flatten the local population into a single ethnographic voice.

---

## 7. Persona != culture spokesperson

A focal persona's judgment is actor-local.

If she says the user sounds overly foreign-influenced, that establishes her interpretation and perhaps something about her own reference groups.

It does not establish:
- objective foreignness;
- unanimous local agreement;
- one authentic local position;
- the persona as correct cultural representative.

This follows inherited perspective asymmetry.

---

## 8. Habitus learning becomes gameplay

The product should not rely on tutorial text like 'in this culture, family obligation works like this.'

The player learns through:
- consequences;
- observation;
- ordinary objects;
- overheard interactions;
- invitations/exclusions;
- arguments;
- mistakes;
- third-party disagreement;
- practical dependence;
- relationship histories;
- explanations when actors choose to explain.

Learning the world is therefore necessary for competent relationship action rather than optional lore collection.

---

## 9. Why homologous-peer may outperform obvious-outsider mode

Obvious-outsider mode creates an explanatory shield: the user can attribute every conflict to being foreign.

Homologous-peer mode removes some of that shield.

Friction confronts the user's actual assumptions and relational habits more directly.

This may increase self-recognition, though it also makes the metaphysical fiction more demanding.

---

## 10. Why homologous-peer may outperform native-alternate mode

Native-alternate mode solves social coherence by generating the user as locally formed.

But then the game loses the actual user's inherited interpretive habits.

Homologous-peer mode preserves the productive contradiction:

LOCAL POSITION + NONLOCAL FORMATION.

That contradiction is what forces learning.

---

## 11. What is carried, translated or bracketed?

Still open.

Candidate decomposition:

Carry strongly:
- present normative judgments;
- memories/intellectual knowledge;
- habits of attention;
- emotional/interpretive tendencies actually belonging to the user;
- values/ideology/religion or nonreligion;
- humor/taste;
- real relational praxis expressed through choices.

Translate structurally:
- wealth/purchasing power;
- institutional leverage;
- housing security;
- labor position;
- transport;
- network reach;
- ordinary access to services;
- social power.

Design axis / possibly translate:
- ethnic/racial legibility;
- nationality;
- accent;
- body;
- gender presentation;
- education credentials;
- occupational status;
- legal/citizenship status;
- family status.

Keep off-stage or selectively admit:
- real employer;
- complete real social graph;
- total family simulation;
- irrelevant real-world logistics.

No category is frozen.

---

## 12. Embodiment remains a hard problem

To be socially unmarked, the player may need some locally legible embodiment.

But changing embodiment changes the user's social experience and can alter the meaning of actions.

Questions include:
- retain gender?
- age?
- disability?
- perceived nationality?
- accent?
- body presentation?
- educational markers?

Homologous-peer mode therefore may need an explicit embodiment-translation constitution rather than arbitrary cosmetic mapping.

---

## 13. Good and bad learning patterns

Good:
user makes a remark; persona reacts unexpectedly; another local actor agrees with the user; a third actor disagrees with both; the player learns a more precise field of positions.

Bad:
user violates a hidden rule; game punishes them; persona lectures them; user learns a stereotype.

---

## 14. Homologous peer as veil training

The user begins with:
'I know what makes sense.'

The world answers:
'you know what makes sense from where history placed you.'

Because the user's material affordances are locally peer-bounded, they cannot remain safely above the consequences.

Playable movement:
ASSUMPTION → FRICTION → ATTENTION → INFERENCE → ACTION → CONSEQUENCE → MODEL REVISION.

---

## 15. Deepest relation to the veil of ignorance

The game cannot erase the user's knowledge of who they are.

It can partially operationalize the veil by separating:
WHO YOU ARE PSYCHICALLY NOW
from
WHICH MATERIAL/SOCIAL POSITION YOU GET TO ACT FROM.

The user loses the guarantee that their inherited assumptions will remain practical or socially intelligible under the assigned position.

---

## 16. Candidate theorem

Homologous-peer mode places the actual user's historically formed consciousness inside a locally peer-equivalent social/material position, so that competent love requires learning another person's thick reality without either roleplaying that person or retaining the external power to remain above their world.

This is currently the strongest conceptual successor to the original alternate-self Rawlsing premise.

---

## 17. Hard tests

1. Can a persona interpret the user's assumptions as unusually foreign-influenced without the game declaring that interpretation objectively correct?
2. Can another local actor disagree and reveal intra-field contradiction?
3. Can the user challenge a harmful norm without adaptation being treated as mandatory?
4. Can peer normalization remove outsider purchasing-power privilege without erasing meaningful embodiment?
5. Can the user remain enough themselves that later praxis reflection bears upon them?
6. Can the game avoid requiring a full fictional native biography?
7. Does local learning become instrumentally necessary rather than tutorialized?
8. Does the persona remain one person among billions rather than a national/cultural representative?
