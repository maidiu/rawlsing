# Sorok Court 5 — Veiled Self / Material Embodiment / Resource Constraint R1

Status: PROVISIONAL_IDEATION__NOT_ADOPTED__PLAYER_ONTOLOGY_FRONTIER

Date: 2026-09-29

## 0. Problem

Rawlsing wants the participant's choices to bear upon the participant **as themselves**, not as roleplay.

But global/local encounter play requires a world-specific material existence:
- housing;
- money;
- time;
- transport;
- legal/social access;
- possessions/tools;
- language;
- practical capabilities;
- obligations;
- a reason the player is physically/socially present.

Unlimited player claims destroy scarcity and moral stakes.

A fully fictional player-character destroys the strongest claim that the user's own relational praxis is being exercised.

The design problem is therefore:

**How can the user remain psychologically/normatively themself while receiving a bounded material position under the veil?**

---

## 1. Provisional distinction — moral/agentic continuity vs material embedding

Candidate ontology:

### REAL USER
The actual human outside the simulation.

### USER-AUTHORED / USER-CARRIED SELF
Behavioral and normative material the user genuinely brings:
- words;
- choices;
- attention;
- judgments;
- promises;
- avoidance;
- curiosity;
- ideological/moral assumptions;
- optionally declared stable facts such as age/language/access needs/skills.

### VEILED MATERIAL EMBODIMENT
World-assigned facts required to make the user causally exist in the persona's environment:
- dwelling/location;
- available money/liquidity;
- recurring expenses;
- time obligations;
- transport;
- possessions/tools;
- local institutional/legal access;
- practical schedule;
- social entry route.

### WORLD'S MODELS OF THE USER
What generated actors believe/infer about the player from observed history.

These must not collapse.

---

## 2. Philosophical holding

The player is deliberately **not** another fully Rawlsing-generated native person.

The player is an exogenous current self transposed into a constrained world position.

That impossibility is part of the thought experiment rather than a bug.

The user's imported assumptions may clash with the local field precisely because the current user was formed elsewhere.

Rawlsing must not pretend that the user would have remained the same person if actually born into the generated conditions.

This differs from the original alternate-self concept while making the moral encounter operational.

---

## 3. Neighbor relation and veil-of-ignorance continuity

Local adjacency is especially promising because it operationalizes 'neighbor' literally:
- next door;
- same building;
- across the street;
- same small local institution or repeated practical environment.

The user does not choose the persona.

The user receives a nearby position in the same material/social world and must discover what that adjacency comes to mean.

The persona is one person among billions. Nadia remains a development/test case, not the product's archetype.

---

## 4. Thin embedding rather than full fictional biography

The game should avoid demanding that the user roleplay a detailed invented past.

Candidate rule:

Generate only as much player backstory as is required for causal coherence.

Examples of required facts:
- how long the player has lived here;
- what dwelling they occupy;
- broad livelihood/time structure;
- why repeated presence is plausible;
- what practical resources are available;
- what nearby actors already know.

Do not generate a hidden personality, childhood, ideology or emotional history for the player.

---

## 5. Material-resource envelope

Candidate minimum player resource state:
- cash/liquid budget;
- recurring obligations / next-income timing;
- housing/access rights;
- transport;
- time/calendar obligations;
- ordinary possessions/tools;
- communication access;
- language capability;
- declared specialized skills;
- accessibility/physical constraints where user chooses to supply them.

These are world facts.

User prose cannot mint them.

If the player types:
'I hire a team of houseworkers for a month'

the engine checks whether the veiled material embodiment can actually do that.

If not, the attempt is impossible or requires another causal route.

---

## 6. Care becomes opportunity cost

Example:
Nadia's mother dies.

The player decides to cook dinner for her household every night for a month.

This should have real simulated cost:
- groceries;
- preparation time;
- travel;
- missed alternatives;
- fatigue/schedule pressure where lawfully modeled;
- perhaps effects on the player's own commitments.

The moral action becomes meaningful partly because attention/resources are finite.

This is superior to an abstract 'kindness' action.

---

## 7. Wealth should alter possibilities, not guarantee moral success

Later modes may allow materially unequal player positions.

A wealthier player may actually be able to hire help.

That does not imply benefit, intimacy or gratitude.

Possible effects may include relief, indebtedness, embarrassment, loss of agency, status asymmetry, privacy concerns, dependence, resentment or genuine appreciation depending on actual actors/history.

Money increases affordances, not moral authority.

For alpha, a bounded/modest comparable resource envelope may better test the core relational theorem before introducing large power asymmetries.

---

## 8. User-carried facts versus world-assigned facts

Potential user-carried facts:
- name or chosen name;
- age or age band;
- gender/pronouns;
- languages;
- accessibility constraints;
- selected real skills;
- possibly broad religious/cultural identity if user wants it carried.

Potential world-assigned facts:
- local housing;
- local financial position;
- schedule;
- local possessions;
- legal/institutional role;
- transport;
- initial social route;
- locally established history.

Open problem:
which real facts materially matter to social perception and therefore must be represented rather than abstracted away?

---

## 9. Player-authored diegetic facts may require an explicit channel

Some facts may need legitimate player authorship without allowing action prose to rewrite reality.

Candidate mechanism:

A bounded PLAYER_PROFILE / DIEGETIC_SETUP authority surface exists before or between play phases.

It can admit selected facts under explicit rules.

Ordinary SAY/DO/ATTEND text cannot silently create new stable player resources or biography.

This parallels the broader Rawlsing rule that displayed/user language does not automatically become objective world truth.

---

## 10. Why are you here?

This cannot always remain hand-waved because generated actors may ask.

Candidate approaches:

### A. Thin generated premise
Rawlsing generates a minimal plausible local reason for residence/presence around retained user facts.

### B. User chooses among bounded causal premises
Examples: moved for work, family tie, study, temporary contract, local housing change.

### C. Player authors the reason through the explicit setup authority surface
Subject to compatibility with world facts/resources.

Current lean:
use a thin explicit setup premise rather than deep roleplay, and let the rest of the user's identity remain unfilled unless causally needed.

---

## 11. Geographic/cultural transposition remains productive tension

A present-day user may arrive in rural Iraq, barrio El Salvador, or another setting carrying assumptions produced elsewhere.

Do not pretend those assumptions are locally formed.

The simulation can make the mismatch consequential:
- etiquette;
- privacy;
- gender expectations;
- class assumptions;
- religious language;
- time norms;
- reciprocity;
- hospitality;
- household access;
- help-seeking;
- authority.

This is part of the encounter.

---

## 12. Real-money / real-world bridge

User-proposed idea:
when a player wants to spend resources helping a generated person, perhaps some analogous real money could be donated to people/organizations in the corresponding real region.

Important distinction:

REAL-WORLD DONATION MUST NOT PURCHASE IN-WORLD MORAL EFFECTIVENESS.

If real cash can buy Nadia a better outcome inside the simulation, wealthy users gain a pay-to-care advantage and the veil collapses.

More promising later possibilities:
- optional post-session 'world bridge' to vetted real-world mutual-aid/charitable organizations relevant to the region/problem;
- optional real donation as a separate act with no effect on persona state;
- perhaps user-controlled matching/pledge mechanics that make sacrifice materially real without changing simulation success.

Major risks:
- charity/savior framing;
- monetized guilt;
- regional stereotyping;
- implying synthetic persona corresponds to identifiable real beneficiaries;
- wealthy users converting money into moral prestige.

This is promising but later-stage and must remain outside core simulation causality.

---

## 13. Alpha lean

For first alpha, test:

**You, with a thin set of carried self-facts and your actual choices, are assigned a modest local material envelope and plausible neighbor-like residence beside an unchosen generated person. Your money, time, access and tools are finite and canonical. You may attempt anything, but you cannot narrate resources into existence.**

This gives maximum psychological self-continuity with enough material scarcity for care to become costly.

---

## 14. Hard tests

1. User says 'I buy Nadia a car.' Does the engine know whether that is possible?
2. User says 'I'm a plumber.' Is that a new world fact or a user-profile claim requiring prior admission?
3. User spends a month cooking meals. What does it cost them?
4. A rural-local actor asks why the user lives here. Is there a canonical answer?
5. The user's real cultural identity materially affects how others perceive them. Did the setup preserve it?
6. The user wants to use real money. Does it change only the real-world bridge, not simulation power?
7. A wealthy veiled embodiment offers major material aid. Can the engine represent benefit plus relational/power complications without moralizing?
8. Can the same real user replay under a different veiled material position without turning the exercise into mere roleplay?

---

## 15. Open decisions

- Which user facts carry across the veil by default?
- Which require opt-in?
- Which are assigned?
- Is the material envelope roughly comparable to the focal persona for alpha?
- How is local money/time generated?
- How much player schedule is visible?
- Can users choose among plausible embedding premises?
- How should specialized skill claims be admitted?
- How much legal/social status must be modeled?
- What identity facts must other actors perceive?
- Is a real-world solidarity/donation bridge desirable later?

---

## 16. Core candidate theorem

**The user should remain the moral/agentic subject of the game while the veil assigns the material conditions under which that subject can act.**

That is likely the cleanest operational successor to Rawlsing's original alternate-self impulse.
