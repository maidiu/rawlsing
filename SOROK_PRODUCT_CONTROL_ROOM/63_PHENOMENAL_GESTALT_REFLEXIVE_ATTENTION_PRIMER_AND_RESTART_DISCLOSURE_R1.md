# Sorok — Phenomenal Gestalt, Reflexive Attention Primer, and Restart Disclosure R1

Status: `PROVISIONAL_PRODUCT_UX_EPISTEMIC_CONSTITUTION`

Date: 2026-10-01

## 0. Problem

Text cannot reproduce a simultaneously scannable visual field.

Rawlsing therefore needs:
- a shared coarse phenomenal frame sufficient for orientation;
- user-specific variation in peripheral/particular noticing;
- deliberate ATTEND for deeper or counter-habitual inspection;
- enough transparency that perceptual personalization does not become an invisible epistemic trap.

## 1. Global scene properties versus local particulars

Not all perceptual facts are equally attention-regime dependent.

Some properties are gross enough that most normally sighted/available observers would register them immediately:
- spectacular cleanliness;
- overwhelming clutter;
- a strong smell;
- a room made nearly impassable by objects;
- obvious darkness;
- extreme sparseness;
- obvious damage;
- unusually expansive or cramped spatial feel.

These belong primarily to **shared coarse gestalt**.

Other facts are much more dependent on:
- sightline;
- learned attention;
- object expertise;
- current task;
- recognition repertoire;
- deliberate inspection.

Examples:
- identifying a Vitamix model;
- recognizing expensive Japanese knives;
- reading Nabokov titles;
- identifying mid-century furniture;
- distinguishing an incense boat;
- noticing a worn shoe brand;
- seeing a small fry under a refrigerator.

Therefore:

`GLOBAL PHENOMENAL GESTALT`
!= `REGIME-SENSITIVE PARTICULARS`.

## 2. First-pass scene law

An initial scene should normally provide:

1. spatial orientation;
2. gross scene gestalt;
3. present actors and obvious activity;
4. a low-resolution field of ordinary visible categories;
5. a bounded number of regime-sensitive peripheral particulars.

This avoids both:
- exhaustive text dump;
- adventure-game noun inventory.

Example pattern:

> The kitchen is lived-in rather than clean: dishes in the sink, things left on the counters, photographs and keepsakes continuing into the living room. From here you can see books, instruments, a large rug and laundry over a chair.

A particular user's regime may then make one or two further particulars readily register.

## 3. Samia example

A highly controlled, affluent-minimalist visitor room may create a nearly universal first impression such as:

> unusually immaculate, sparse, deliberate, open and expensive-feeling.

That gestalt does not require the user to identify:
- Rothko;
- an incense boat;
- furniture period/style;
- designer brands;
- object provenance.

Specific cultural recognition belongs to later/individual layers.

## 4. Attention personalization should concentrate at the margins

The stronger and more physically dominant a feature is, the less the user-attention prior should matter.

The weaker/more particular the feature, the more lawful variation there is between users.

Conceptually:

`GROSS REALITY` → high commonality
`ORDINARY VISIBLE FIELD` → moderate commonality
`PERIPHERAL PARTICULARS` → strong attention-regime variation
`EXPERT RECOGNITION` → recognition-repertoire variation
`ATTEND` → deliberate resolution / departure from default.

## 5. Reflexive disclosure theorem

Rawlsing should disclose that:
- the world/person is independent of the user;
- representation is necessarily selective;
- the system may learn what the user tends to notice;
- this can make the user's phenomenal surface increasingly shaped by their own attentional history;
- ATTEND can be used to seek beyond default patterns.

The disclosure must not imply a final "view from nowhere."

The developmental aim is not:
> escape perspective.

It is:
> become aware of perspective's limits and deliberately exceed its habits.

## 6. First-use disclosure should be cryptic

Before first persona contact, do not over-explain the attention model.

A short warning should alter posture without encouraging attention-profile gaming.

Candidate register:

> **What you notice is not all that is there.**
>
> **ATTEND.**

Or:

> **You will not see everything. What catches your attention will shape what you come to know. ATTEND when you want to look further.**

Exact copy remains a prototype question.

## 7. Restart is the right moment for fuller disclosure

When a user moves toward abandoning/restarting a continuity, the perceptual theorem now has lived referents.

The product can disclose more explicitly:
- the person/world exceeded what the user saw;
- the user's phenomenal surface was partly shaped by their own attention;
- another observer could have experienced a strikingly different version of the same pre-contact person;
- canceling restart does not restore first contact;
- restarting does not unlock unseen content from the abandoned person.

The message must not be manipulative guilt copy.

It should explain the irreversible epistemic/relational fact.

## 8. Restart disclosure theorem

> **You have never seen all of this person.**

And:

> **Going back is not going back to the beginning.**

If the user cancels restart:
- the current continuity resumes;
- prior impressions remain;
- prior acts remain;
- missed possibilities remain missed;
- future repair/revision remains possible.

## 9. Anti-completion warning

Do not imply:
- unseen detail can be collected by replaying;
- alternate first meetings are unlockable;
- the person has a finite completion percentage.

Restart creates another history.

It does not reveal the remaining percentage of the abandoned person.

## 10. ATTEND as developmental action

ATTEND is not only "look closer."

It can function as:

> **deliberately direct perception outside what your current attentional habits would have given you for free.**

This creates a dialectical loop:

`PRIOR ATTENTION HABITS`
→ `PHENOMENAL SURFACE`
→ `DELIBERATE ATTEND BEYOND HABIT`
→ `NEW DISCOVERY / FRICTION`
→ possible revision of future attention.

The system must not score this as moral progress.

## 11. Compact laws

> **The world is independent of you.**

> **Your experience of it is not.**

> **Attention personalization belongs strongest at the perceptual margins, not in gross reality.**

> **ATTEND beyond what comes naturally.**

> **A continuity ends with unknowability intact.**

## Attention transparency without attention identity

The product may explain that represented perception is selective and may adapt to demonstrated attention.

It should not turn the learned attention regime into an ordinary-play identity label.

Preserve:

`ATTENTION MODEL TRANSPARENCY != USER ESSENCE CLAIM`.

If a user explicitly requests reflection, prefer contextual evidence-linked description of repeated behavior, with uncertainty, rather than categorical identity language.

Privacy inspection rights remain separate from gameplay presentation.

Compact:

> **ATTENTION HISTORY MAY BE DESCRIBED; ATTENTION IDENTITY MUST NOT BE DECLARED.**

See SPD-0241 / RISK-197 / artifact 81.
