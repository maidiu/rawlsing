## 5. Why this grain is minimal

D13 distinguishes **physically necessary information** from **convenient materialization**.

### Too small: scalar respondent/question rows

They are not generally injective over:

- multiselect sets;
- repeated events;
- rank/order;
- routing states;
- source-native topology.

D13C proves the current shared substrate lacks first-class event/multiselect/rank identity and uses a scalar duplicate key.

### Too small: selected E1 aggregates

Selective E1 conditionals cannot reconstruct:

- a future conditioner;
- a new conjunction;
- a new branch;
- branch-conditioned cotravel;
- new risk sets;
- k-way/XOR geometry;
- autonomous search outside the materialized predicate.

This is aggregate noninvertibility.

### Still too small globally: selected E2 aggregates

An exact E2/k-way contingency is sufficient for **its fixed bounded query** when target/condition/risk-set/topology/measure/provenance are exact.

It cannot be reconditioned on a dimension not present in that contingency. Therefore C7 receives:

`SUFFICIENT_ONLY_FOR_BOUNDED_FIXED_QUERY_FRONTIER`

not full-frontier sufficiency.

### Broader than necessary: source bytes + normalized incidence + caches

A hybrid retaining exact source snapshots, complete normalized event incidence, every authority sidecar and selected derived facts can satisfy the D12 frontier in principle.

But if normalized event/coordinate incidence has already preserved every source-native empirical distinction and exact interpretation authority, permanent duplication of all source bytes and derived aggregates is not information-theoretically necessary.

C10 therefore is sufficient in principle but not minimal.
