## Governing rule

- governed positive RR_NORM on the norm side (already supplied);

## Stage 1 — norm contract
For each exact `(source_family, wave, semantic_unit_id)` once, read the exact norm text plus governed semantic summary and classify:
- `EXPLICIT_ACTOR_TARGET_ACTION`: source meaning supplies enough actor/action/target structure to compare to endpoints.
- `NO_EXPECTED_CONDUCT_CONTRACT`: the RR_NORM-positive item is approval/evaluation of an arrangement, value, fairness, suitability, etc., but does not itself encode an expected-conduct actor/action contract.
- `AMBIGUOUS_NEEDS_VOZHD`: source meaning in the pack is insufficient to delimit the contract.

`RR_NORM` positivity alone is never relation truth.

## Stage 2 — exact opportunity
Compare the Stage-1 norm contract to the frozen endpoint actor/target/action fields and exact endpoint question text.
- Positive requires `MATCH` on actor, target, and action domain.
- Negative requires at least one governed `MISMATCH`.
- If no dimension is governed mismatched but one or more needed dimensions remain `UNKNOWN`, BLOCK.

A `NO_EXPECTED_CONDUCT_CONTRACT` norm makes its exact C4 opportunities negative on action-domain prerequisite failure; do not invent an actor/action merely to preserve a candidate.

## Safe batching
Code may group and propagate only after an exact governed selector has been proved. A bulk rule cannot cross source-wave or semantic-unit identity boundaries in this program. Every non-manual returned row must cite a `propagation_rule_id` present in the propagation ledger.

Examples of lawful batching:
- one Stage-1 norm contract applied to every endpoint opportunity for that exact source-wave semantic unit;
- whole endpoint sets ruled out because every endpoint has the same frozen actor contract and the norm actor is governed different;
- a norm with no expected-conduct contract propagated negative across its exact opportunity block.
