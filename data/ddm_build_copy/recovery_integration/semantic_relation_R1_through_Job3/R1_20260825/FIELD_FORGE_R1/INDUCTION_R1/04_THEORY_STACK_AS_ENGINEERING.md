# 04 — THEORY STACK AS ENGINEERING

## Rule: theory must pay rent

FIELD FORGE is not a sociology reading group. A theorist belongs in the stack only insofar as the theory improves how Rawlsing uses its existing lawful evidence, prevents a false inference, suggests a better empirical object, or exposes an observability limit.

Do not acquire new sources to satisfy theory. Do not make a concept true merely because a canonical theorist named it.

## Pierre Bourdieu — primary relational geometry

Bourdieu is the strongest current backbone because Rawlsing needs relational position, differentiated resources, field-relative valuation, and a way to avoid scalar class reduction.

Engineering primitives:

- social space rather than fixed class buckets;
- capital volume, composition, and trajectory as distinct information;
- economic, educational/credential, social, symbolic, and other capital forms only where the current source actually observes adequate proxies;
- field-specific value of a resource/disposition;
- capital conversion/devaluation across fields;
- partial field autonomy;
- doxa/common sense and symbolic consecration as interpretive candidates;
- hysteresis / position-disposition mismatch where current vs origin/trajectory evidence supports it.

Methodological fit:

- MCA / geometric data analysis for categorical relational spaces;
- active vs supplementary variables;
- specific MCA/passive modalities where noninformative or routed categories must not define distance;
- MFA/block-balancing candidates where questionnaire block size would otherwise dominate geometry.

Hard cautions:

```text
AXIS 1 != ECONOMIC CAPITAL
NUMBER OF QUESTIONS IN A BLOCK != SOCIOLOGICAL IMPORTANCE
EDUCATION != FULL CULTURAL CAPITAL
INCOME != CLASS
SES SCALAR != CAPITAL COMPOSITION
CLUSTER != FIELD
```

A field is not established because respondents cluster. It requires evidence of locally differentiated positions/stakes, differential valuation, partial autonomy, and/or conversion relations strong enough for a separate admission court.

## Antonio Gramsci — hegemony, common sense, power alignment

Gramsci prevents the machine from confusing numerical prevalence with social dominance.

Engineering primitives:

- cross-position reach of a disposition complex;
- high-position alignment;
- existing political-power alignment where independently governed assets such as EPR/GeoEPR permit a lawful join;
- bounded/local dominance and subhegemony;
- counter-complex/counterhegemonic structure;
- distinction among majority prevalence, institutional/power alignment, legitimacy/defaultness, and coercive power.

Hard cautions:

```text
HEGEMONIC != MAJORITY
SUBALTERN != MINORITY
GROUP HAS POLITICAL POWER != INDIVIDUAL GROUP MEMBERS BELIEVE X
```

FIELD FORGE should initially emit a multidimensional `HEGEMONIC_ALIGNMENT_PROFILE`, not a magical scalar hegemony score.

## Stuart Hall — articulation and conjuncture

Hall is the strongest protection against universal demographic coefficients.

Engineering primitives:

- identity/resource effects are configuration-dependent;
- the same educational/resource shift can lead to different disposition-space redistributions inside different sectarian, regional, cohort, or other configurations;
- historical/conjunctural context changes how categories are articulated;
- interaction topology is an empirical candidate, not automatically a causal mechanism.

Canonical FIELD FORGE example:

```text
education-associated disposition shift
among southern Lebanese Shia
!=
education-associated disposition shift
among southern Lebanese Sunni
```

This motivates `PRESSURE_COUPLING / ARTICULATION_FOOTPRINT` rather than additive `Shia pressure + education pressure` arithmetic.

## Bernard Lahire — plural dispositions and multiple socialization

Lahire helps prevent a too-unified habitus/ideology model.

Engineering primitives:

- disposition repertoires may contain tension and contradiction;
- different contexts can activate different parts of the repertoire;
- multiple socialization histories can deposit heterogeneous dispositions;
- co-presence is not noise merely because a one-dimensional ideology model dislikes it.

For PRE-V3 this means preserve exact respondent-backed complex structure. For V3 it means realized internal relation/activation topology can differ among people who share many disposition nodes.

## Ann Swidler — cultural toolkits / repertoires

Swidler strengthens the idea that people possess repertoires usable under different conditions rather than always expressing one coherent worldview essence.

Engineering use:

- distinguish possessed repertoire from currently activated orientation;
- preserve situational availability;
- do not infer that co-possessed dispositions are all simultaneously governing action;
- useful conceptual bridge from PRE-V3 repertoire distributions to V3 situational activation.

## Michèle Lamont — symbolic and moral boundaries

Lamont is especially useful for Rawlsing's interest in intermediate-position morality, dignity, respectability, deservingness, and status distinctions that are not reducible to economic wealth.

Engineering primitives:

- moral/symbolic boundary complexes as disposition-space objects;
- comparison of boundary complexes across relational position space;
- distinction between economic hierarchy and criteria of worth/dignity;
- cross-context variation in which boundaries are salient.

Do not start by asserting a universal `middle-class morality`. Compute whether a recurrent moral/boundary complex actually concentrates in an intermediate relational position, then compare it with dominated and dominant-position repertoires.

## Raymond Williams / Karl Mannheim — historical-temporal structure

Useful for:

- residual/dominant/emergent cultural formations;
- cohort/generation differentiation;
- temporal lag and changing common sense;
- historical conjuncture.

Engineering restriction:

No temporal semantic claim without lawful measurement comparability/portability and scope continuity. Wave differences alone do not establish persistence, rupture, or generational effect.

## Thornton / Eckert / Willis / Lareau and related local-field/socialization theory

These theorists matter as ontology constraints even though FIELD FORGE is not acquiring youth-specific data.

They demonstrate that:

- capital is locally valued;
- peer/local fields can have their own status systems;
- local dominance can convert poorly or negatively into another field;
- family/education/socialization shape trajectories and available dispositions;
- resistance can be structurally patterned without being politically emancipatory.

User example to preserve as a hostile ontology test:

A person can be the most culturally consecrated pop-punk heart-throb in a suburban high-school field and become embarrassing/low-value in an elite international-school cultural field. The same resource/disposition bundle changes symbolic value by field/context/time.

Do NOT infer that current adult surveys can directly reconstruct fine-grained adolescent peer capital. Treat that as an observability boundary. V3 may represent concrete lived local fields from its own development/encounter machinery; PRE-V3 must not fake them from absent survey evidence.

## The combined stack

A useful shorthand is:

```text
BOURDIEU: where are relational positions and what capital counts here?
GRAMSCI: what becomes common sense / socially dominant and how does it align with power?
HALL: how are identities/resources articulated differently by conjuncture/configuration?
LAHIRE + SWIDLER: what heterogeneous repertoires coexist and activate differently?
LAMONT: what moral/symbolic boundaries organize worth and distinction?
WILLIAMS/MANNHEIM: how do these structures vary historically/cohort-wise when comparison is lawful?
LOCAL-FIELD/SOCIALIZATION THEORY: do not assume capital value or socialization geometry is globally uniform.
```

The machine should compute patterns first and name sociology afterward. Theory constrains what questions to ask and what shortcuts are invalid; it does not get to counterfeit empirical support.
