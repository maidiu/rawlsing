# 07 — STRUCTURAL INTERPRETATION: HEGEMONY, FIELD, CAPITAL, BOUNDARY

## Grammar before categories

FIELD FORGE currently recommends freezing a small analytical grammar before freezing substantive universal categories.

Candidate grammar:

- relational position;
- resource/capital candidate;
- field/formation candidate;
- disposition/repertoire;
- pressure footprint;
- articulation/coupling;
- hegemony/subhegemony/counter-complex evidence;
- symbolic/moral boundary;
- capital conversion/devaluation;
- temporal/context scope.

Do NOT freeze:

- exactly N strata;
- exactly N universal fields;
- “religion belongs in stratum X”;
- “education always means cultural capital”;
- universal class bins;
- universal demographic coefficients.

The data should be able to surprise theory about how these grammar elements are instantiated.

## Strata are demoted

`Strata` originated as a useful image of nested social layers, but real pressures cut across and overlap nested institutions.

Nestedness can remain a relation:

```text
local religious movement PART_OF broader evangelical field
family embedded in church community
local community embedded in national political economy
```

But music scenes, race, political media, schooling, class position, religion, and transnational culture can cross-cut these containers.

Therefore:

`STRATA = possible derived/lossy projection of a richer relational topology`

unless later empirical/theoretical court proves a stronger role.

## Capital as field-relative value

Current core formulation:

```text
resource / disposition R
has value V
within field F
at historical moment T
```

Capital conversion may be positive, weak, zero, or negative across fields.

The pop-punk hostile test:

A disposition/resource bundle can have enormous symbolic value inside a suburban youth scene and low or negative value in an elite international-school cultural field. The point is not the bands; the point is that capital is not a universal scalar property of the person.

Therefore Rawlsing must distinguish:

- possession of a resource/disposition;
- its local valuation;
- the field/context in which it converts;
- its exchange/conversion into another field.

## Field candidacy — cluster is insufficient

A candidate field should require evidence such as some combination of:

1. internally differentiated positions;
2. recurring stakes/dispositions;
3. resources/dispositions whose value changes inside vs outside the arena;
4. stable local relations between position and disposition;
5. partial autonomy from broader geometry;
6. identifiable conversion/devaluation into another arena;
7. bounded scope and historical context.

The result may remain `FIELD_CANDIDATE_UNRESOLVED` when surveys cannot observe enough.

Do not invent missing field structure merely because Bourdieu would expect it.

## Middle-class morality without arbitrary class bins

Do not define `middle class = income percentile 35–75` and then discover its morality.

Instead:

1. construct local relational position geometry from lawfully admitted indicators;
2. determine whether an empirically meaningful intermediate region exists;
3. separately identify moral/symbolic-boundary complexes in disposition space;
4. examine whether those complexes concentrate in the intermediate region;
5. compare them with dominated and actually observed high-capital/dominant-position regions;
6. where subjective status/aspiration evidence exists, compare self-placement or status imaginary with actual high-position disposition structure.

Use a neutral descriptive object first:

`INTERMEDIATE_POSITION ↔ DOMINANT_POSITION DISPOSITION_MISMATCH`

Only a later theory court may decide whether the pattern deserves labels such as misrecognition, petit-bourgeois morality, aspiration, respectability politics, or another concept.

## Hegemony as a profile, not a scalar

Raw prevalence is not hegemony.

Candidate `HEGEMONIC_ALIGNMENT_PROFILE` dimensions:

- population prevalence;
- cross-position reach;
- positional concentration;
- alignment with high-capital regions actually observed in local social space;
- political-power alignment where independent current authority exists;
- institutional alignment where current evidence supports it;
- geographic reach;
- temporal persistence where comparison is lawful;
- symbolic/defaultness evidence if actually observable;
- counter-complex structure;
- subhegemonic/local bounded dominance;
- uncertainty / unobserved dimensions.

A view can be widespread but not power-aligned; narrow but concentrated in powerful positions; locally hegemonic but weakly convertible into a wider field.

### Subhegemony

A useful candidate definition:

> high local symbolic/common-sense dominance inside a bounded social field plus limited/negative conversion into a more dominant surrounding field.

This captures churches, local scenes, provincial elite structures, communal institutions, and other bounded formations better than “minority counterculture.”

Still: `subhegemony` remains a semantic admission, not something a cluster detector may announce.

## Moral/symbolic boundaries

Lamont-style boundary objects can be extremely useful because surveys often observe judgments of:

- deservingness;
- responsibility;
- authority;
- work discipline;
- family morality;
- tolerance/exclusion;
- national/religious worth;
- trustworthiness;
- social status/respectability.

The machine can discover recurrent boundary complexes and map them across position space.

It must not assume every distinction is a class boundary or every morality complex is hegemonic.

## Pressure footprint vs mechanism

A survey can reveal the footprint of structural pressure without solving the ultimate sociological why.

For persona-generation goals this is often sufficient.

```text
PRESSURE FOOTPRINT
= reproducible conditional probability redistribution / constraint

PRESSURE MECHANISM
= explanatory proposition about why the footprint exists
```

The first can be directly empirical or model-estimated with explicit provenance.
The second requires separate structural evidence/interpretation.

Do not require Rawlsing to solve the big sociology before it can model plausible people. Most persons themselves do not possess a correct causal account of the structures shaping them; ideology often fills precisely that gap.

## Interpretation court law

Structural interpretation receives an empirical motif atlas. It does not receive permission to overwrite empirical objects.

For every proposed label ask:

- What exact empirical pattern is being named?
- What alternative concepts could name the same pattern?
- What evidence would distinguish them?
- Is the necessary evidence in our fixed corpus?
- If not, should the object remain neutral/descriptive/unresolved?
- Does this concept improve persona/world-model generation materially, or merely make the output sound sociological?

Core rule:

`COMPUTE THE PATTERN BEFORE NAMING THE SOCIOLOGY.`
