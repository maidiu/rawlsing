# FFIF002 — POSITION ROLE AUTHORITY BOUNDARY R1

## Jurisdictional rule

FFIF002 does not adjudicate Position Role Authority (PRA). It defines the dependency seam that prevents PRA from becoming the accidental owner of basic measurement semantics.

## Measurement / transform authority owns

Before PRA can act, the lawful semantic substrate must already determine, where needed:

- source-native variable/state identity and meaning;
- source-native response-option decoding;
- special-response-state semantics;
- whether states have an authorized order;
- whether a project crosswalk is semantic identity, claim-scoped equivalence, transport equivalence, noncomparability, or unresolved;
- any claim-scoped collapse, partition, binning, rank/reference frame, or other transformation consumed by the proposed role assertion;
- information loss, ancestry, replay, and scope of those transformations.

These are measurement/equivalence/transform propositions. They do not become PRA propositions merely because the eventual analysis concerns social position.

## PRA begins here

PRA begins when Rawlsing asks whether a lawfully represented measurement/state functions as a **social-position analytic role** under a specified project claim/representation.

Examples:

### Education
A. `education response states may be ordered as a credential hierarchy`  
Owner: source/project measurement + order authority.

B. `credential hierarchy functions as a social-position dimension relevant to claim C`  
Owner: PRA.

PRA may reject B even if A is true. FFIF002 may authorize A without asserting B.

### Sect
A. `these sect categories are distinct source-native semantic states`  
Owner: source-native semantic authority.

B. `for claim C, these states may be transported/crosswalked across contexts for invariant I`  
Owner: claim-scoped transform/transport authority.

C. `sect operates as a position-bearing axis / conditioning role in representation R`  
Owner: PRA.

Being used only as a conditioning variable does not erase the semantic distinctions in A, and PRA does not own their decoding.

### Occupation
A. `source categories X/Y/Z correspond to these employment states under crosswalk W`  
Owner: project/claim transform authority.

B. `occupation is a positional proxy for class structure in claim C`  
Owner: PRA.

## Prohibited PRA capture

PRA must not be used to authorize:

- a source code mapping because a role model needs it;
- ordinal order because a social-position representation expects a gradient;
- category collapse because a positional geometry is unstable;
- cross-context equivalence because a role comparison requires common labels;
- missing-state treatment;
- respondent/person identity;
- project semantic identity.

If the only reason a basic semantic transform appears warranted is that the desired social-position representation requires it, the dependency direction is reversed and the transform is unauthorized.

## Prohibited TTAR overreach

The inverse boundary also matters. A TTAR may authorize state equivalence/order/transport but cannot assert:

- that the authorized state is actually social position;
- that its order is a hierarchy of domination/resources/prestige;
- that a crosswalk preserves the social mechanism PRA cares about;
- that a representation's positional interpretation is substantively warranted.

Those remain PRA questions.

## Dependency contract

`SOURCE/MEASUREMENT SEMANTICS -> TRANSFORM/EQUIVALENCE AUTHORITY -> PRA -> REPRESENTATION CONSUMPTION`

The chain need not instantiate every layer for every variable. But no later layer may back-authorize an earlier one.

## Exact boundary sentence

**FFIF002 owns what an empirical measurement/state is, how its states may be ordered/mapped/grouped/transported, and the scope/loss/provenance of those permissions; PRA owns the later proposition that such a lawfully represented empirical state functions as social position.**
