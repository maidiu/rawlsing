# FFIF003 — LOCAL-FIRST TRANSPORT ARCHITECTURE

## 1. Governing law

`LOCAL CONSTRUCTION PRECEDES CROSS-CONTEXT ALIGNMENT`.

The architecture must make it impossible for a global comparison model to create the local structure that it later cites as evidence of recurrence.

This does not prohibit pooled models as separate analytical objects. It prohibits circular proof: **alignment manufactured by a joint representation is not evidence that independently established local structure recurs.**

## 2. Formal object

For each context `c`:

`R_c = f_c(O_c; A_c)`

where:

- `O_c` is the governed local observation/source-topology substrate;
- `A_c` is the local authority bundle: measurement identity, role authority, routing/risk set, estimand, weighting, representation recipe;
- `R_c` is one claim-scoped local representation.

After `R_c` is frozen:

`I_c = g_c(R_c; q)`

extracts the named invariant for claim `q`.

Cross-context transport is then a test, not a representation generator:

`T_ab(q) = test[P_q(I_a, phi_ab(I_b)); phi_ab in H_q]`

where:

- `H_q` is the predeclared admissible correspondence family;
- `phi_ab` is the selected correspondence with authority and selection rule;
- `P_q` is the exact preservation predicate;
- the decision rule includes nulls, alternative mappings, and representation perturbations.

## 3. Six-stage pipeline

### Stage 0 — jurisdiction and authority freeze
Freeze context identity, source/wave/module/scope, measurement refs, routing/eligibility/exposure authority where available, sample/weight target, local estimand, and permitted representation family. Any unavailable authority becomes an explicit ceiling.

### Stage 1 — independent local construction
Construct each local view without using cross-context outcomes, a pooled axis, global category frequency, or destination-context labels to choose local states.

Allowed local views include exact configurations, conditional/dependence objects, local geometry, source topology, graphs with explicit node/edge identity, and model-derived motifs — each under its own FFIF001-style invariant/loss contract.

### Stage 2 — invariant extraction and freeze
Record:

- local object hash/version;
- invariant definition;
- local support/uncertainty;
- representation dependencies;
- known instability/degeneracy conditions.

The invariant must be defined before correspondence selection can inspect whether a target context “matches.”

### Stage 3 — correspondence registration
A transport claim registers:

- origin and destination local roles/objects;
- mapping authority refs;
- admissible map family `H_q`;
- mapping-selection rule;
- symmetry/asymmetry;
- allowed transform;
- forbidden inferences.

Partial, many-to-one, and directional maps are admissible only when they are substantively authorized and explicit. A bidirectional claim requires independent validation of the reverse direction; invertibility may not be assumed.

### Stage 4 — preservation test
Compute or adjudicate only the declared preservation predicate. Do not search across many invariants and report whichever one happens to match unless multiplicity is itself governed.

### Stage 5 — falsification and map-discretion audit
Run the applicable null suite from `09_NULL_AND_FALSIFICATION_CONTRACT.md`, including semantic-map permutations, type-constrained alternative maps, unrelated blocks, holdout contexts, and local representation perturbations.

A critical test is **map multiplicity**: if many semantically admissible correspondences yield the same claimed recurrence, the signature is not discriminative enough to support the asserted interpretation.

### Stage 6 — bounded adjudication
Emit one of:

- exact/order/rank/topology/conditional-shape/motif/signature transport;
- partial recurrence;
- reversal;
- `NO_TRANSPORT`.

Always emit a `claim_ceiling` stating the strongest inference forbidden by the evidence.

## 4. Independence law

The following architecture cannot prove recurrence:

`R*_a, R*_b = F(O_a, O_b)`

followed by:

`R*_a looks like R*_b therefore structure recurs`.

Examples include common MCA axes, pooled factor loading structures, universal latent classes, or graph embeddings trained jointly across contexts when the induced common representation is itself the evidence.

A joint model can still answer a different question — e.g. whether a particular common encoding predicts held-out observations — but it must not back-propagate that success into a claim that its common coordinates were already latent empirical facts.

## 5. Source-native replay law

Every analytic state used in transport must retain a reversible path to source-native identity. Cross-context labels such as `EDUCATION_HIGH`, `RELIGIOUS_MINORITY`, or `ECONOMIC_PRECARITY` cannot replace their local source identities.

An analytic correspondence receipt must therefore preserve both:

- `source_native_identity`; and
- `transport_role_identity`.

This is the cross-context extension of FFIF001's warning against `EQUIVALENCE_BEFORE_METHOD`.

## 6. Risk-set and estimand non-substitution

A transport map acts on a structural invariant. It does not merge local populations.

Each endpoint keeps its own:

- eligibility/exposure law;
- sample design;
- weight purpose;
- target population;
- conditioning support;
- estimand.

If the proposed comparison requires these to be treated as the same object, then the transport claim depends on a separate portability/estimand authority and cannot be closed here.

## 7. Representation disagreement is evidence

If an order transport survives under direct conditionals but not under local geometry, or a motif survives one lawful graph construction but not another, the architecture records `REPRESENTATION_DEPENDENT_TRANSPORT` rather than selecting the representation that tells the more satisfying story.

No majority vote among methods resolves this. The correct object is the typed disagreement plus its invariant/loss contracts.

## 8. No-transport certificate

A lawful `NO_TRANSPORT` result should identify at least one of:

- missing semantic correspondence;
- map family too unconstrained;
- risk-set/estimand incompatibility;
- insufficient support;
- representation instability;
- null failure;
- signature non-identifiability;
- local object absence.

This prevents “no result” from being silently replaced by researcher intuition.
