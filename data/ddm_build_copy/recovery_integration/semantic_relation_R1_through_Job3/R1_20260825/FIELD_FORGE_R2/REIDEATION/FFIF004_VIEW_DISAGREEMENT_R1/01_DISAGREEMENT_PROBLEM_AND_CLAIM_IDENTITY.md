# FFIF004 — DISAGREEMENT PROBLEM AND CLAIM IDENTITY

## 1. Disagreement is not visual difference

Two lawful views may look radically different without disagreeing.

An exact configuration view may be fragmented while a local geometry is smooth. A graph may be disconnected while a conditional table remains perfectly estimable within each component. A motif inventory may expose recurrence that a low-dimensional embedding does not encode.

None of these pairs is a contradiction until both outputs are mapped to the **same frozen empirical proposition**.

Core law:

`DIFFERENT_PICTURES != DISAGREEMENT`

and:

`DISAGREEMENT_REQUIRES_COMPARABLE_ASSERTION_SLOTS`.

## 2. Four claim-identity states

### `SAME_CLAIM_SAME_INVARIANT`

Both outputs address the same proposition, domain, estimand, target invariant, quantifier, comparison baseline, and decision tolerance.

Only here can direct agreement/disagreement be adjudicated.

### `SAME_BROAD_QUESTION_DIFFERENT_INVARIANT`

The outputs answer related parts of one substantive inquiry but preserve different empirical invariants.

Example: exact minority conjunction versus neighborhood ordering.

This is not contradiction. It may become `DIFFERENT_INVARIANTS_PRESERVED`, `INCREMENTAL_VALUE`, or, if both invariants are co-required by a frozen claim, a multi-view sufficiency question.

### `DIFFERENT_CLAIM`

The outputs differ in estimand, risk set, conditioning set, scale, target population, quantifier, semantic transform, or proposition.

No disagreement receipt may treat them as competing answers.

### `AMBIGUOUS_CLAIM_IDENTITY`

The comparison cannot determine whether the outputs address the same proposition because a required claim coordinate is unresolved or post-hoc.

Action: `ABSTAIN__CLAIM_IDENTITY_UNRESOLVED`.

## 3. Minimum claim identity manifest

The court rejects both extremes: `claim_id` alone is too weak; freezing every implementation detail as claim identity makes cross-view comparison impossible.

A `CLAIM_IDENTITY_MANIFEST` must bind at minimum:

- `claim_id` and version;
- normalized empirical proposition;
- claim scope/domain;
- empirical-unit/risk-set reference;
- estimand and weight target;
- semantic-input / TTAR manifest hash;
- target invariant or declared invariant bundle;
- quantifier (`EXISTS`, `PREVALENT`, `ORDERED`, `CONDITIONAL`, `GLOBAL`, `LOCAL`, etc.);
- conditioning/baseline relation where constitutive;
- uncertainty/support regime;
- decision or materiality tolerance;
- source-native ancestry ceiling;
- special-state law;
- allowed representation family set if the claim itself restricts representation.

Representation-family identity is **not normally constitutive of the claim**. Otherwise every view would define its own incomparable claim and cross-view testing would become impossible.

## 4. Common input is adjacent to, not identical with, claim identity

Before claim identity is compared, both views must attest to the same lawful common empirical input where the experiment requires sameness:

- observation-event substrate hash;
- governed empirical-unit universe;
- actual exposure/risk set where respondent claims are made;
- source-native measurement/state ancestry;
- exact TTAR sequence and outputs;
- special-state treatment;
- estimand/weight target;
- design/holdout partition where relevant;
- ancestry exclusions / leakage firewall.

An intentional semantic difference creates a different comparison object and must be typed as such. An unintentional difference invalidates the proposed disagreement.

## 5. Normalized assertion slots

Each claim defines one or more representation-neutral assertion slots. Examples:

- `EXISTS(configuration X in risk set R)`;
- `P(D|C,R)` within tolerance;
- `ORDER(A,B)`;
- `NEIGHBORHOOD_ORDER(i,j,k)`;
- `CONNECTED(component a,b)`;
- `RECURRENCE(signature s under map phi)`;
- `MINORITY_BRANCH_MASS >= t`;
- `GLOBAL_DIRECTION = positive`;
- `CONDITIONAL_DIRECTION(g) = negative`.

A representation may be incapable of answering a slot. That is `UNDEFINED_FOR_VIEW`, not automatically a negative answer.

The comparator must distinguish:

`FALSE`
from
`UNDEFINED_FOR_VIEW`
from
`INSUFFICIENT_SUPPORT`
from
`NOT_IDENTIFIED`.

## 6. Direct contradiction versus non-substitutability

Direct contradiction is narrow:

> two admissible views map to the same assertion slot and emit mutually incompatible truth/interval/status claims beyond tolerance.

Non-substitutability is different:

> two admissible views answer different co-required slots, and neither can recover the other's required invariant without prohibited loss.

The second is the route toward multi-view necessity. It need not contain any logical contradiction.

## 7. Claim-splitting rule

If a broad query such as “is there separation?” decomposes into materially different invariants—exact configuration separation, neighborhood separation, conditional subgroup redistribution, topology, motif recurrence—the query must be split before disagreement adjudication.

`BROAD_PROSE_QUESTION` is not a sufficient comparison key.

The court may later recombine split claims only through an explicit invariant bundle stating which subclaims are jointly required.

## 8. Forbidden claim laundering

A claim may not remain nominally identical while changing:

- global to subgroup;
- unconditional to conditional;
- sample-descriptive to population-weighted;
- exact state identity to merged state identity;
- cardinal to ordinal;
- local to pooled/common-space;
- existence to prevalence;
- descriptive association to causal mechanism;
- within-context relation to transported recurrence.

Any such change is `CLAIM_MISMATCH` or `SEMANTIC_INPUT_DIVERGENCE`, not healthy pluralism.

## 9. Claim identity and structural interpretation

A claim identity may name empirical invariants. It may not bake in `FIELD`, `CLASS`, `CAPITAL`, `HEGEMONY`, `HOMOLOGY`, `PRESSURE`, `MECHANISM`, `PERSONALITY`, or `WORLDVIEW` merely to force view convergence around a theoretical noun.

Those remain downstream admission questions.
