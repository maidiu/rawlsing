## 1. Three kinds of computational consequence, not one giant merged country table

A society is not a vector of survey means and country indicators. The external estate should make three qualitatively different operations possible:

- **Population calibration:** a distribution of materially differentiated positions or capacities supports plausible alternatives for world/person construction. It is not a position occupied by this person.
- **Relational qualification:** a dated relationship between particular groups, institutions, roles or resources can change what a position means, whose control is implicated, and which practical alternatives exist. It is not a disposition.
- **Rule/affordance grounding:** a relevant independently sourced provision, together with actual role, scope and a separately specified world rule, can change what a particular situated activity is allowed to attempt, what it costs or what dependencies it requires. It is not a predicted action or psychological effect.

A fourth use connects all three to existing empirical inquiry: **comparative research** into the changing distributions and co-travel of dispositions across materially differentiated positions and contexts. That inquiry may change the next structural question, and vice versa. Keep all independently warranted source observations even when no strong explanatory proposition is yet possible. Do not force every source through FF01, I04, N6 or C11; use only the owner appropriate to the requested operation.
