## 3. The mass-intersubjective substrate: groups-amongst-groups-within-groups

The important object is not a collection of independent demographic groups, nor a set of concentric national/local strata. It is overlapping social formations that participate in one another's reproduction. A person can be at once an employee, parent, daughter, migrant, member of a religious community, patient, renter, activist, neighbor; each role can activate different material dependence, recognition and norm obligations. Institutions themselves have internally differentiated formations (manager/worker, clergy/congregants, teachers/pupils, officials/clients). A religious formation may be nationally subordinated but locally authoritative, internally class-differentiated, and transnationally connected. An employer may be institutionally powerful locally yet subordinate to another firm. A family can reproduce a church, and the church can organize access to care, which in turn conditions the family's labor. No single fixed rank.

### 3.1 Keep four relations distinct even when they concern the same proposition

1. **Response repertoire:** a source-observed answer or joint, semantically situated; even this is a response in a survey activity, not transparent private belief.
2. **Second-order expectation:** what a person or group expects others to approve, condemn or do; use direct items where actually measured, otherwise model only as hypothesis/learned expectation. Do not compute this as prevalence by fiat.
3. **Public enactment:** observable speech, repeated ritual, social recognition, sanction, institutional performance and shared artifacts; may be strategic, ambivalent or discrepant from responses.
4. **Reproductive force:** who can maintain or withdraw the goods/resources/status/access that make a practice consequential. A norm can dominate practical life despite low expressed assent; many may reproduce it without believing it. A privately prevalent dissent may be publicly inaccessible; its social effect depends on actual carrier/activity.

Add actual actor appropriation as a fifth and later relation, not another population statistic. For a given theme, preserve plausible coherent configurations (including contradictory ones) and their conditional distribution by source-local position. Preserve whether each public act or expectation is observed, independently evidenced, fictionalized or unknown. The source repertoire makes a modeled public utterance plausible; it does not issue that utterance. Public defaultness arises through repeated, accessible, institutionally supported enactment, not a threshold on survey percentages. For strong historical HEGEMONY still distinguish actual reproduction/authority evidence and issuer; for fictional world, explicitly constructed institutions can realize hegemonic/subhegemonic relations without pretending to have proved them in the actual country.

### 3.2 Represent nested/overlapping scope using relations when demanded, not universal group hierarchy

Conceptual position axes: (a) material participation/command/dependence; (b) institutional membership/eligibility; (c) geography and mobility; (d) source-measured identity and configuration; (e) public recognition/classification by particular Others; (f) actual ties/participation/history. Some are not respondent-measured and belong only to the world. Do not infer one from another. Distinguish self-identification, assigned classification, institutional membership, geographic co-presence, and source group definition. A person can be excluded from a group they identify with, or publicly treated as a group they do not identify with. Multiple overlapping memberships must be supported by source joint or a coherent fictional construction; no independent multiplication of group marginals. A subset relation requires evidence, not a label. Preserve genuine overlap and unknown intersection.

At a selected life situation, ask which arena is consequential through an actual reachable relation. Relevant arenas are those providing resources, authoritative classifications, opportunities, obligations, accessible Others or cultural practices; relevance changes with life activity, role, time and contradiction. A child entering school acquires exposure opportunities different from home; a parent's job loss can increase dependence on relatives or church, changing the child's surroundings without a change in national attitude prevalence. An institution may change the *meaning and practical worth* of a resource: a credential is valued at one workplace, devalued at another. Don't attach a universal static pressure weight to each stratum. Use the existing world owner's actual dependencies, applicability and contacts to determine what is available; use qualified context to inform the next relational/empirical query. A missing context only holds the affected claim, not the entire life.

## 6. The actual social-world construction interface: generative sparsity rather than synthetic national population

We do not need to generate every inhabitant, every group membership or every institution in advance. The expensive useful object is the **differentiated structure of possible social formations and their relevant realizations**, with source provenance and modeled consistency. Source query can supply a D2 conditional configuration when a particular role/encounter is demanded; world owners select and persist a particular independently acting Other or institutional act. Persist already instantiated Others, ties, material roles, recurring practices and relevant history so later encounters continue those individuals and institutions rather than regenerate them from stereotype. A previously uninstantiated counterpart can be introduced under actual world reachability and role constraints. Selection is not a source-measured contact likelihood. Background population may remain as conditional offers/qualified aggregate context, rather than millions of fake individual agents.

Two sufficient routes already demanded by SEAM/29; choose by the question, not universal pipeline:

**Other-first route:** distinct eligible Other position D2 → qualified source-backed repertoire alternatives → competent modeled selection of an independent Other in an actual reachable role → that Other's acquired organization and agency → actual expression/activity → focal actor's accessible experience. Example: two plausible workmates share the same employment category but have opposing observed configurations; one modeled workplace coactor is instantiated, the other remains possible. Changing D2 alone changes candidate information only, not an existing person.

**Institution/practice-first route:** independently specified public practice, rule, artifact or social institution → actual actor-independent material/public act → accessible or inaccessible consequence → focal acquisition where it occurs. Example: an admissions rule excludes a qualified applicant even if no surveyed administrator was sampled; a public school ceremony can express a norm independently of the particular teacher's private answer. Both routes can interact. A public rule can change an actor's material life without being known, and an articulated belief can be experienced without the corresponding material event. Remote macro causes can propagate through either route.

**Public world and shared reality:** preserve the institution or public artifact as independently existing world material. Different co-present actors may perceive different aspects, attribute different motives, privately disagree, strategically perform assent, or act in conflict. World event ownership and access matter; no omniscient LLM telling each character all meanings. A collective norm can be reproduced by a plurality of actors, institutions and material dependencies; one character's opinion does not rewrite it. Conversely repeated collective refusal/cooperation can change the institution through actual rules and resource channels. This is the material/intersubjective feedback loop, not an inert setting.
