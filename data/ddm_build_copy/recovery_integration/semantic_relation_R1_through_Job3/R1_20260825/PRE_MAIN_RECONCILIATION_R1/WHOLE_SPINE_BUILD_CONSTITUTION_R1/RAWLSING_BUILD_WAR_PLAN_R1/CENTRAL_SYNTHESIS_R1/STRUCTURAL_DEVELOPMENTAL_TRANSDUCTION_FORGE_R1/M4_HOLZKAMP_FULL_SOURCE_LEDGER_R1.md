## 2. Highest-yield source locations

### 2.1 Chapter 1, pp. 23–58 — level discipline and historical paradigm

**Status:** `METHODOLOGICAL_CLAIM / CONCEPTUAL_DEFINITION`.

Holzkamp separates:

```text
PHILOSOPHICAL LEVEL
!= SOCIETAL-THEORETICAL LEVEL
!= CATEGORIAL-METHODOLOGICAL LEVEL
!= SINGLE-THEORY / CURRENT-EMPIRICAL LEVEL
```

At pp. 27–31 he argues that categorial concepts define what the psychological object is and constrain the kinds of empirical claims that can later be made. At pp. 41–57 the “historical” paradigm is framed against ahistorical categories that treat a socially produced world as a natural environment. At pp. 48–57 the functional-historical procedure reconstructs the “present historicity” of categories.

**M4 consequence:** a category such as `Handlungsfähigkeit`, `Prämisse`, or `subjektiver Möglichkeitsraum` is not itself an empirical effect law. Rawlsing must not turn the category into a direct update operator merely because it is central to the theory.

### 2.2 Chapters 2–4, pp. 59–158 — psychophylogenetic prehistory

**Status:** `HISTORICAL_ANTHROPOLOGICAL_RECONSTRUCTION / THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

The book reconstructs sensitivity, orientation, emotional valuation, social coordination, learning and developmental plasticity before the fully societal-historical level. These chapters explain how later specifically human capacities have natural-historical preconditions.

**M4 boundary:** none of the following may be imported directly as a modern actor-level social rule:

```text
phylogenetic functional transition
animal social structure
species-typical need structure
signal learning
biological “control” requirement
```

The key book-internal discontinuity comes in Chapter 5: after the dominance shift (`Dominanzumschlag`) to societal-historical development, the specific psychological form can no longer be derived merely by continuing phylogenetic functional analysis.

### 2.3 Chapter 5, pp. 189–193 — objective social reality and mediation

**Status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION / CONCEPTUAL_DEFINITION`.

At p. 191 the socially produced objective world is explicitly characterized as an objective-material reality with objective economic structures and social relations. It is **not** itself a primary psychic fact. The individual finds these relations as supra-individual objective facts and realizes possibilities/necessities embodied in them in personal life activity, thereby also participating in reproduction and change of the social relations.

At pp. 191–192 Holzkamp requires a level crossing:

```text
OBJECTIVE SOCIETAL RELATION
→ INDIVIDUAL-SCIENCE MEDIATION CATEGORY
→ PSYCHIC / SUBJECTIVE FORM
```

The individual-science categories are expressly called mediation categories between objective material-economic and psychic determinations of the human-world relation.

At pp. 192–193 the basic relation is the overall societal mediation of individual existence (`gesamtgesellschaftliche Vermitteltheit individueller Existenz`): the individual’s conditions of life are produced socially, and individual contributions and individual existence-maintenance are linked through the social division of labor rather than by immediate identity.

**M4 consequence:** this is direct support for a seam. It is not support for `STRUCTURE -> PSYCHOLOGY`.

### 2.4 Chapter 5, pp. 195–203 — position and historical specificity

**Status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`; concrete formations require social theory/current empirical work.

At pp. 195–196 the general societal mediation must be concretized by historically specific `Lage`/`Position`. A person does not relate to “society as a whole” directly; the relation is mediated through a position in the division of labor. Position mediates type of contribution, possibilities of existence-maintenance, and the degree/form of influence on the social process. Holzkamp explicitly notes possible collective contribution to changing the structures that define the position.

At pp. 198–203 the same categorical relation must be concretized by formation-specific historical relations. Bourgeois forms are historical, not natural-human essences.

**M4 consequence:** D1’s exact-position requirement is strongly supported; demographic/class labels cannot substitute for the actual position/relation.

### 2.5 Chapter 6, pp. 229–239 — objective `Bedeutungen` as action possibilities

**Status:** `CONCEPTUAL_DEFINITION / THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

At pp. 233–234 Holzkamp differentiates societal `Arbeit` as an objective-economic category from individual `Handlungen` as individual life activities under socially produced conditions.

At pp. 233–238 societal meaning structures (`gesellschaftliche Bedeutungsstrukturen`) synthesize objectively produced action relations. For an individual, these meanings are **primarily societal action possibilities** (`gesellschaftliche Handlungsmöglichkeiten`). They do not dictate concrete action. Even severe restrictions are restrictions/deformations of possibilities; the individual’s relation to meanings remains a relation of possibility (`Möglichkeitsbeziehung`).

At p. 237 conscious `Verhalten-Zu` — a conscious relation-to the meanings/action possibilities — marks the specifically human subject relation.

At p. 238 collective consciousness or a supra-individual subject must not be hypostatized. Social collectives may form through shared interests and coordination, but this does not create a literal collective mind.

**M4 consequence:**

```text
OBJECTIVE MEANING / POSSIBILITY
!= SUBJECTIVE REALIZATION
!= ACTION
```

This is one of the strongest original-text supports for the Rawlsing firewall.

### 2.6 Chapter 6, pp. 239–243 — `Handlungsfähigkeit` and needs

**Status:** `CONCEPTUAL_DEFINITION / THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

At pp. 239–241 personal `Handlungsfähigkeit` is defined through participation in disposal/control over the social production/reproduction process on which one’s own relevant conditions of life depend. Its actual form is multiply mediated and refracted by historically specific life situation and position.

At pp. 242–243 Holzkamp adopts Osterkamp’s `produktive Bedürfnisse` / `sinnlich-vitale Bedürfnisse` distinction but explicitly blocks two readings:

1. productive needs are not a freestanding personal “need to produce,” grow, or self-actualize;
2. the genealogy from “control” does not license a generic individual `Kontrollbedürfnis`.

Participation in control/disposal over social life conditions is treated as a quality of human need satisfaction, not a scalar psychological control drive.

**M4 consequence:** Rawlsing should not mint a `CONTROL_NEED` or `AGENCY_SCORE` from Holzkamp.

### 2.7 Chapter 7, pp. 315–324 — motivation is neither objective-interest readout nor action guarantee

**Status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION / CONCEPTUAL_DEFINITION`.

At pp. 315–322 objective social thought forms and goals are cognitive/action possibilities. A social goal can become motivated for the individual only through a conjunction including an objective relation to anticipatory life security/quality, an adequate representation of that relation in societal thought forms, and adequate individual cognition of it. Each link may fail.

A person may act on ideologically distorted relations or reject a recognized goal. At pp. 321–323 the individual may refuse a goal even when its general/personal relevance is cognitively grasped. Conversely, action can occur under coercion (`Zwang`) without motivated adoption.

**M4 consequence:**

```text
OBJECTIVE INTEREST
!= MOTIVE
RECOGNIZED POSSIBILITY
!= INTENTION
REASON
!= ACTION
ACTION
!= SUCCESS
```

### 2.8 Chapter 7, pp. 342–356 — `Bedingtheit`, `Prämissen`, `Begründetheit`

**Status:** `CONCEPTUAL_DEFINITION / THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

At pp. 344–345 Holzkamp argues that no matter how precisely societal life conditions are described, one never thereby obtains total determination of the individual’s action. This is the explicit rejection of environmental/structural determinism.

At pp. 348–349 he introduces `subjektive Handlungsgründe` as mediation category. Grounds arise **in the medium** of objective meaning/action structures and their thought/language forms. Human action is neither merely externally conditioned nor arbitrary private meaning-creation; it is grounded in life conditions.

At pp. 349–351 a consciously grounded action is subjectively functional relative to the subject’s experienced needs/life interests. This does **not** make it objectively successful or objectively interest-congruent. Reasons carry an intersubjective intelligibility claim.

At pp. 351–352 “unintelligibility” means the premises are unknown/hidden to the interpreter; it does not erase the distinction between reasons for actions and conditions of events. Holzkamp’s brick/dog example explicitly reserves `Bedingungen` for events where a reason relation is not the appropriate explanatory mode.

At pp. 352–353 historically specific external and personal life conditions count as `Prämissen`, but **not as conditions “as such.”** They enter reason contexts in their phenomenal aspect (`phänomenaler Aspekt`): how they are experienced as the subject’s situation, personal capabilities, past/future orientation, emotionally evaluated and enacted. Real and phenomenal aspects may diverge.

At pp. 353–355 subjective freedom is not voluntarism. The dimensions and degree of available expansion are objectively historically conditioned, including by possibilities for organized association. Objective determination therefore has primacy in delimiting the available field. Holzkamp nevertheless makes the stronger categorial claim that a “second possibility” of expanding control cannot be reduced to zero without eliminating human subjectivity.

**M4 ceiling:** the latter is a high-level theoretical/categorial claim. Rawlsing must **not** operationalize it as a rule that every actor at every instant has a nonzero executable structural alternative.

### 2.9 Chapter 7, pp. 356–369 — five levels, `Infrastrukturen`, subjective possibility spaces

**Status:** `CONCEPTUAL_DEFINITION / THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`; concrete institutional contents explicitly deferred to current-empirical work.

At p. 356 Holzkamp summarizes five levels:

```text
1. historically specific objective life conditions — societal theory
2. meanings/action relations/thought forms — first individual-science mediation
3. subjective grounds with objective life conditions mediated as premises — second, explicitly subject-science mediation
4. psychic dimensions/aspects of grounded action-capacity/condition
5. biological-physiological unspecific bases
```

At pp. 358–359 analysis from the subject standpoint starts from immediate life situation/practice (`Lebenslage/Lebenspraxis`) while retaining the real primacy of societal production. Societal meaning structures differentiate into life-situation-specific `Infrastrukturen`, facing individuals as objective action/thought possibilities.

The p. 359 footnote is decisive: the meaning analysis is extracting only objectively given action/thought possibilities, **not their subjective realization in conscious relation-to**.

At pp. 360–367 Holzkamp sketches work, nonwork, institutions, media, legal/educational/support institutions and other domains, then explicitly states that exact institutional/positional action possibilities and their restrictions must be determined by single-theory/current-empirical investigation beyond this book.

At pp. 367–369 the first mediation level is position-specific meaning/action structures; the second is subjectively functional reason contexts. The “double possibility” must be specified by formation-specific life situation/position and **only in this specification can it become premises**. Holzkamp calls the resulting first-person action field a `subjektiver Möglichkeitsraum`.

Crucially, this subjective possibility space does **not** depend directly on objective life position. Position-specific meanings determine it only as they are situational-personally given in their phenomenal aspect. Prior realizations/nonrealizations and biography also shape the personal functional basis. A subject may lag behind objective possibilities or misjudge them.

**M4 consequence:** D1 cannot lawfully emit `Prämisse` or `subjektiver Möglichkeitsraum` merely from PRE-V3 applicability.

### 2.10 Chapter 7, pp. 370–401 — restrictive/generalized `Handlungsfähigkeit`, collectivity and reciprocal effects

**Status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION / CONCEPTUAL_DEFINITION`; bourgeois-form content is historically specific and partly programmatic.

At p. 370 Holzkamp explicitly warns that the choice between restrictive and generalized action-capacity characterizes situations/relations of action, not persons or personality traits. The alternative arises anew in relevant threat/constraint situations.

At pp. 371–375 restrictive action-capacity (`restriktive Handlungsfähigkeit`) is subjectively grounded where the actor treats existing power conditions as effectively unchangeable and seeks security/control within them. Generalized action-capacity (`verallgemeinerte Handlungsfähigkeit`) concerns expansion of shared disposal over conditions and depends on the practical possibility of association/cooperation/counter-power. Formal abstract possibility is insufficient; the route must be meaningful within the subject’s actual possibility space.

At pp. 374–375 intersubjectivity/instrumentalization are likewise not person constants.

At pp. 377–378 Holzkamp gives a genuine reciprocal temporal mechanism: instrumentalizing/isolating others may yield short-term control while reducing the actor’s long-term basis for joint control and narrowing the future subjective possibility space. Action can therefore reproduce or modify later conditions of action.

At pp. 383–401 `Deuten` and `Begreifen` are cognitive moments associated with restrictive/generalized direction. They are not stable cognitive levels or person types. Around pp. 395–401 Holzkamp stresses that movement toward `Begreifen`/generalized action-capacity requires practical association with others, means, alliance base and time perspective; the actor is a passive-active part of historical relations, shaped by and jointly contributing to their change.

He also says the exact relation between these thought forms and concrete positions needs special investigations beyond the book.

### 2.11 Chapter 8, pp. 417–425 — development: categorial logic, not stage schedule

**Status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`, explicitly developmental-logical/categorial.

At pp. 417–421 Holzkamp warns that ontogenetic categories must not contain claims that properly require current empirical evidence. Humans are reconstructed as effectively lifelong capable of learning/development; restrictions of that capacity are treated as social rather than a fixed phylogenetic phase program.

He rejects a biologically preprogrammed universal sequence of human developmental phases. Instead the book reconstructs minimum ontogenetic prerequisites of developed action-capacity “backwards” (`entwicklungslogisch`) from the developed form.

The current person carries historical depth: prior developmental results may persist as development, stagnation, regression, and biographical configuration.

**M4 consequence:** Chapter 8 licenses history sensitivity and developmental process distinctions, not a fixed `AGE -> UPDATE` table.

### 2.12 Chapter 8, pp. 428–501 — meaning-generalization, transcendence of immediacy, institutions

**Status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`; exact chronologies and concrete institutional effects explicitly current-empirical.

Two reconstructed developmental directions are central:

- meaning-generalization (`Bedeutungsverallgemeinerung`) toward cooperative-societal control;
- transcendence of immediacy (`Unmittelbarkeitsüberschreitung`) toward participation in socially mediated existence beyond the immediate household/support frame.

At pp. 473–490 movement through multiple social centers and institutions is used to show how new position/meaning configurations can change the child/adolescent’s practical possibilities and first-person relation to the world. Holzkamp explicitly says exact chronologies, where possible at all, belong to current empirical work.

At pp. 490–501 restrictive/generalized alternatives are reintroduced under institutionally and historically specific developmental constraints. The book does not provide a universal empirical law that a given institution produces a given psychological outcome.

### 2.13 Chapter 9, pp. 509–545 — anti-shortcut method

**Status:** `METHODOLOGICAL_CLAIM / PROGRAMMATIC_CLAIM`.

At pp. 522–533 the experimental-statistical variable model is criticized for setting a short-circuit relation from externally set conditions to activities/reactions, thereby eliminating the subject-side mediation levels. Holzkamp labels the contrast `kontrollwissenschaftlich` versus `subjektwissenschaftlich`.

At pp. 533–545 he argues that preserving subjectivity/intersubjectivity does not require abandoning objective scientific standards.

**M4 consequence:** Rawlsing’s explicit separation of structural possibility, actor-relative qualification, subject uptake, grounds and action is much closer to the book’s required mediation grammar than a condition→response shortcut.

### 2.14 Chapter 9, pp. 568–573 — bounded role for condition/statistical models

**Status:** `METHODOLOGICAL_CLAIM`.

Holzkamp does not simply ban condition models. Where actors face factual limits beyond present control, conditionedness may be a valid partial description and statistical models may sometimes be adequate. But he rejects universalizing that constrained surface as the nature of human action.

**M4 consequence:** objective constraints and probabilistic evidence remain admissible PRE-V3 facts. They cannot by themselves replace premise/ground/action mediation.

## 3. Whole-book causal reconstruction

The strongest book-supported sequence is:

```text
OBJECTIVE SOCIETAL PRODUCTION / REPRODUCTION RELATIONS
  [gesellschaftstheoretische Ebene]
        ↓
OBJECTIVE SOCIETAL MEANING / ACTION / THOUGHT STRUCTURES
  (Bedeutungsstrukturen; Bedeutungs-/Handlungszusammenhänge; Denkformen)
        ↓
HISTORICALLY SPECIFIC LIFE-SITUATION / POSITION SPECIFICATION
  (Lebenslage / Position)
        ↓
LIFE-SITUATION-SPECIFIC “INFRASTRUCTURES” OF MEANING
  (Infrastrukturen; Bedeutungskonstellationen)
        ↓
OBJECTIVE SOCIETAL ACTION / THOUGHT POSSIBILITIES AND RESTRICTIONS
  (gesellschaftliche Handlungsmöglichkeiten / Denkmöglichkeiten)
        ↓
SUBJECT'S SITUATIONAL-PERSONAL / PHENOMENAL GIVENNESS
+ BIOGRAPHICAL/PERSONAL CONDITIONS
        ↓
SUBJECTIVE POSSIBILITY SPACE
  (subjektiver Möglichkeitsraum)
        ↓
PREMISES OF A SUBJECTIVE GROUND/REASON NEXUS
  (Prämissen)
        ↓
SUBJECTIVE GROUNDS / REASONS
  (subjektive Handlungsgründe; Begründungszusammenhang)
        ↓
MOTIVATED / COERCED ORIENTATION, DECISION, ACCEPTANCE OR REFUSAL
        ↓
ACTION
  (Handlung)
        ↓
REALIZED PARTICIPATION / INTERACTION / COOPERATION / INSTRUMENTALIZATION
        ↓
POSSIBLE REPRODUCTION, NARROWING OR EXPANSION OF LATER CONDITIONS
        ↓
BIOGRAPHICAL / DEVELOPMENTAL CONSEQUENCE ONLY IF SEPARATELY EARNED
```

No arrow in this sequence is licensed as a deterministic one-to-one function.

## 4. What the book proves for M4 versus what it leaves open

### Strongly source-supported

1. Objective social relations are not psychic states.
2. Objective societal meanings are action/thought possibilities, not realized subjective premises.
3. Position and life situation mediate social totality for the individual.
4. Concrete objective possibilities must be specified by position/context.
5. Actual subjective possibility space depends on situational-personal phenomenal givenness and biography.
6. Premises and grounds are subject-side mediation; objective conditions are not grounds “as such.”
7. Grounds do not determine action mechanically.
8. Motivation, coercion, refusal and action must be distinguished.
9. Restrictive/generalized action-capacity is a situational relational alternative, not a person type.
10. Collective/associated action can be required to expand control over conditions.
11. Actions can reproduce/narrow/alter later social possibilities.
12. Development requires historical/biographical process and cannot be read directly from structure.

### Explicitly left to further theory/current empirical work

1. the exact action possibilities in a particular institution, occupation, household, media environment or legal relation;
2. exact empirical distributions of restrictive/generalized action forms;
3. exact institutional success/failure gates for collective action;
4. exact chronological developmental stages;
5. exact causal magnitudes/effect sizes;
6. a universal typed registry covering all resource/network/gate/support mechanisms;
7. a technical data/provenance protocol for reciprocal world updates.

## 5. Source-integrity conclusion

The supplied full book materially strengthens the earlier T2 finding but also narrows what may be attributed to Holzkamp. The source supplies a **categorial mediation grammar** and several concrete causal directions. It does not supply a ready-made general-purpose social-ecology engine, a deterministic psychology, or a complete empirical theory of every institutional route.

The deepest source-supported firewall is:

```text
OBJECTIVE LIFE CONDITION
!= OBJECTIVE MEANING / ACTION POSSIBILITY
!= SUBJECTIVE POSSIBILITY SPACE
!= PREMISE
!= GROUND / REASON
!= ACTION
!= REALIZED EFFECT
!= DURABLE DEVELOPMENT
```

This ledger is the source basis for the remaining M4 courts and mapping files.
