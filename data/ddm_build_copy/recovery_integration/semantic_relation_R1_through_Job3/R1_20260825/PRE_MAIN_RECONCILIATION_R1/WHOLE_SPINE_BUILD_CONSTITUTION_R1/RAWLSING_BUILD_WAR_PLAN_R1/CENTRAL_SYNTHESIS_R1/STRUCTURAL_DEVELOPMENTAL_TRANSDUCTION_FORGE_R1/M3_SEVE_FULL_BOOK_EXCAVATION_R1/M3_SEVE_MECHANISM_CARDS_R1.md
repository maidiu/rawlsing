## M3-M01 — SOCIAL RELATION / FORM OF INDIVIDUALITY → ACTIVITY MATRIX

**SOURCE LOCATION:** Ch. II pp. 90–110, 130–156; Ch. III pp. 178–208, 253–275; Third-edition Postscript pp. 445–454, 489–493.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `PRIMARY_THEORETICAL_ARGUMENT`, with later clarification.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** historically specific relations of production, division of labour, wage/property relations, familial/interpersonal relations, institutions and other objective social relations actually bearing on the person's life.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** the relation does not inject a trait. It organizes objective positions and matrices of possible/required activity, products, exchanges, dependencies and temporal constraints. A concrete person's activity system is formed through actual insertion in those relations.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** a structured set of activities, obligations, dependencies, products, counterparts and time requirements that differs by actual relation/position rather than by class label alone.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the relation must be historically operative and the person must actually occupy/participate in the relevant position; nominal class/occupation/family membership is insufficient.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** concrete activity, capacity, prior biography, contingency and later action determine how the matrix is lived and transformed; no unique psychological outcome follows.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** none directly. The output is the organized life/activity ecology that can feed actual acts and later V3 development.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `RELATION + CONDITION + RECURRENT_ACTIVITY + TRAJECTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person ↔ work/household/institution/class relation.

**RECIPROCITY:** persons reproduce relations through activity and may alter them through socially organized action; not by direct psychological write-back.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** historical mode, actual position, other simultaneous relations, finite time, prior capacities, contingency.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `SOCIAL_FORM != CONCRETE_PERSONALITY`; `CLASS != ACTIVITY_HISTORY`; `CLASS != TRAIT`.

**D1/D2/D3 TEST:** strongly supports D1; requires D2-compatible persistence/recurrence; only qualified support for D3 through social action and external change.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 supplies actor-independent structural possibility; D1/generalized binding must derive objective actor-relative activity/social conditions; V3 owns psychological consequences.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`.

## M3-M02 — ACT AS SOCIALLY MEDIATED BIOGRAPHICAL UNIT

**SOURCE LOCATION:** Ch. III pp. 178–212; Ch. IV pp. 304–313.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `PRIMARY_THEORETICAL_ARGUMENT + INDICATIVE_HYPOTHESIS`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** an actor in a concrete social relation, current capacities, objective task/action situation, socially mediated consequences.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Sève's “act” joins an episode of behavior to its place in the singular biography and to objective social products/consequences. It is therefore not a bare motor event.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** a realized action with typed relation to the actor's prior capacities and later possibilities/history.

**REALIZATION LAW:** an act must actually occur; available action or social role is not an act. Its biographical significance depends on objective consequence and later integration, not occurrence alone.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** the actor performs the act using present capacities; motives/consciousness may matter but are not reducible to structural category.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** realized history input; potential capacity/practice/motive consequences only downstream and conditionally.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `EVENT` embedded in `TRAJECTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person ↔ task/counterpart/institution/social relation.

**RECIPROCITY:** the act can reproduce or contribute to altering relations, but a separate external/social response path is required.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** actual occurrence, social mediation, current capacity, consequence, historical position in biography.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `ACTION POSSIBILITY != ACT`; `ACT != DURABLE_CHANGE`; `ACT != PERSONALITY TYPE`.

**D1/D2/D3 TEST:** D1 supplies conditions; D2/history preserves sequence/recurrence; D3 only if the act is externally admitted as world-changing.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** discrete acts should reuse competent event/action occurrence surfaces; biographical persistence belongs existing V3 history.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE + GENERALIZE`.

## M3-M03 — CAPACITY → ACT / ACTS → CAPACITY DEVELOPMENT

**SOURCE LOCATION:** Ch. IV pp. 313–315; pp. 356–369; Third-edition Postscript pp. 456–460.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `INDICATIVE_HYPOTHESIS`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** existing capacities plus access to actual acts/activity over time.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** capacities enable acts; many capacities are themselves produced or developed by sets of acts. Sève analytically distinguishes capacity-forming activity (sector I) from activity mainly using existing capacities (sector II), while explicitly acknowledging overlap.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** possible development, maintenance, under-use or atrophy of specific capacities, depending on actual activity composition and history.

**REALIZATION LAW:** capacity development requires actual capacity-relevant activity and cannot be inferred from repetition alone. The same act may partly form and partly use capacity; quality/content matters.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** prior capacities alter which acts are possible and how they are performed; later capacity changes alter future feasible activity.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** skill/capacity repertoire.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `RECURRENT_ACTIVITY + CUMULATIVE_PROCESS + TRAJECTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person ↔ socially organized activity/task.

**RECIPROCITY:** strong within person/activity loop: capacity shapes act; acts may reshape capacity; changed capacity changes future activity possibilities.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** relevance, duration, quality, opportunity to use new capacity, competing activity/time demands, biological support.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `REPETITION != LEARNING`; `CAPACITY_USE != CAPACITY_GROWTH`; sector I/II is not an empirically validated partition.

**D1/D2/D3 TEST:** D1/D2 must preserve the activity/time history that can become developmental input; actual capacity mutation belongs downstream V3, not the seam. D3 not required for within-person capacity update.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** upstream seam = organized activity opportunity/requirement + recurrence/time; existing V3 learning/practice/memory/development = capacity change.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE_V3 + GENERALIZE_SEAM_INPUT`.

## M3-M04 — CAPACITY GROWTH → REQUIRED ACTIVITY/USE-TIME REORGANIZATION

**SOURCE LOCATION:** Ch. IV pp. 315, 340–341, 356–369; Third-edition Postscript pp. 458–460.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `INDICATIVE_HYPOTHESIS`, explicitly awaiting validation.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** increased capacity level, existing activity composition, finite time, socially constrained opportunities for using/developing capacities.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Sève proposes that changed capacities tend to require changed activity forms and use-time if they are to be exercised and further developed. Social arrangements can block that reorganization.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** under-use, stagnation, reallocation, new learning/use balance, or pressure toward changing activity.

**REALIZATION LAW:** capacity growth alone does not produce a new life structure. A compatible activity opportunity and actual reallocation/use must occur; external social constraints may make the internal developmental “requirement” unrealizable.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** actor strategy matters within objective limits, but will cannot freely rewrite use-time.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** later capacity trajectory and activity-system organization.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `CONDITION -> TRANSITION/REBINDING -> TRAJECTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person ↔ activity system ↔ institution/social relation.

**RECIPROCITY:** changed person capacities alter the set of feasible/desired activities; social gates determine whether reorganization becomes real.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** availability, role/institutional gate, schedule, resources, life conditions, biological constraints.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `CAPACITY_CHANGE != POSITION_CHANGE`; `INTERNAL_REQUIREMENT != EXTERNAL_POSSIBILITY`.

**D1/D2/D3 TEST:** supports D2 as a requalification/rebinding requirement but not as a free-standing psychological owner; D1 rederives current ecology after changed inputs. D3 needed only if actor action changes external facts through a real gate/response.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** generalize actor/context/activity binding and history; preserve existing V3 capacity owner.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`.

## M3-M05 — ACTIVITY-PRODUCED NEEDS / NEED AS RELATIVE MOTOR

**SOURCE LOCATION:** Ch. I pp. 33–36; Ch. IV pp. 315–331; Third-edition Postscript pp. 457–458, 495–497.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `INDICATIVE_HYPOTHESIS + LATER_THEORETICAL_CLARIFICATION`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** biological requirements, prior activity, socially available products/objects, current activity system.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** biological needs can be starting conditions, but developed human needs are also products of social activity/history. A need may function as a relative starting point for a concrete episode while itself requiring explanation by prior activity.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** activity-relevant demand/motive pressure only after the person's prior life/activity has produced or recruited it.

**REALIZATION LAW:** neither objective dependence nor subjective felt need is sufficient to infer motive/action. The same structural condition may be represented or felt differently.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** central; needs as experienced and motives belong the person's current organization, not the structural seam.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** motivational/need organization if a competent downstream owner earns it.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `CONDITION + TRAJECTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person ↔ products/objects/activity/social relations.

**RECIPROCITY:** prior activity may create/transform needs; needs may then orient later activity.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** biological substrate, social products, prior activity, awareness, ideology, access.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `STRUCTURAL_DEPENDENCY != SUBJECTIVE_NEED`; `FELT_NEED != OBJECTIVE_REQUIREMENT`; `NEED != ACTION`.

**D1/D2/D3 TEST:** D1 may emit objective dependency/resource/time conditions, not needs. D2/history supplies persistence. D3 irrelevant unless action alters external conditions.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** preserve CAN-010/other competent V3 motivational jurisdiction; reject an upstream generic need engine.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE_DOWNSTREAM + REJECT_UPSTREAM_NEED_SHORTCUT`.

## M3-M06 — FINITE USE-TIME / ACTIVITY ALLOCATION CROWD-OUT

**SOURCE LOCATION:** Ch. IV pp. 331–346, especially pp. 333–341; note p. 503 n.90.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `INDICATIVE_HYPOTHESIS`, with explicit simplification caveat.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** finite actor time; socially required/available activity categories; schedules, obligations and activity opportunities; current capacities.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** actual temporal relations among objective activity categories constrain which acts can be performed, for how long, and in what sequence. Under Sève's explicitly simplified constant-conditions example, more time devoted to learning/capacity formation crowds out time for capacity-using activity.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** differential duration/frequency/sequence of learning, work, care, rest, personal activity and other acts; foreclosed or delayed capacity-forming opportunities.

**REALIZATION LAW:** structural time requirement must bind the actor and actual allocation must occur. Ideal schedule, stated preference, and clock-time totals alone are insufficient.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** actor can plan/resist/reallocate within constraints; capacity changes can create pressure for reallocation.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** indirect via actual acts, recovery, practice and later V3 development.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `CONDITION + RECURRENT_ACTIVITY + CUMULATIVE_PROCESS + TRAJECTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person across work/household/institutional domains.

**RECIPROCITY:** changed capacities/action may alter future allocation only through feasible changes in roles/relations/schedules.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** simultaneity, non-substitutability, scheduling authority, resource/care constraints, duration, life stage, current capacity.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `CLOCK_TIME != USE_TIME`; `TIME_BUDGET_CATEGORY != SOCIAL_MECHANISM`; `MORE_REPETITION != BETTER_DEVELOPMENT`.

**D1/D2/D3 TEST:** strongly supports D1 time-allocation output plus D2 interval/recurrence composition; does not prove D4. D3 only for actual external schedule/rule change.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** require D1/generalized ecology facts to carry time/schedule/activity constraints and D2-compatible interval/recurrence semantics; capacity effects remain V3.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE_EXISTING_D1_D2_COMPOSITION`.

**REALIZATION LAW:** external relation must actually constrain the person's activity; internal capacity change must actually bear on feasible activity; chance and nonmodal paths remain possible.

**RECIPROCITY:** actor activity can seek change, but external transformation requires social response/collective/institutional mechanisms.

**D1/D2/D3 TEST:** D1 derives actor-relative constraints; D2 preserves their interval/sequence and rebinds after actual changes; D3 only through lawful social feedback.

## M3-M08 — BIOGRAPHY AS ORDERED SINGULAR ACTIVITY HISTORY

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** a finite life course of socially organized activities, relations, acts, capacity changes, interruptions and changing positions.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** personality singularity is attributed to the historically ordered organization of a person's concrete activity. Earlier material can be reworked by later activity; sequence, timing and contingencies matter.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** path-dependent current activity/capacity organization without reducing the person to a social type.

**REALIZATION LAW:** biography is constituted by actual realized history, not by membership or a latent trajectory class. A later interpretation cannot fabricate unoccurred history.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** the person acts within and on life conditions; current capacities/activity reorganize the meaning/effect of prior history.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** whole-person trajectory as an explanatory history, not a single state variable.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** individual across multiple social domains over life.

**RECIPROCITY:** biography affects later action; socially mediated action affects later biography and sometimes external relations.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** reuse V3 history/biography/path/trajectory carriers; no new SDTF biography store required.

## Card-corpus terminal

Sève's strongest transferable mechanism is not a Sève-specific personality topology. It is the staged requirement:

```text
EXACT SOCIAL RELATION / POSITION
-> OBJECTIVE ACTIVITY + TIME ECOLOGY
-> ACTUAL ACTS / RECURRENT ACTIVITY
-> EXISTING V3 CAPACITY / NEED / MOTIVE / MEMORY / PRACTICE DEVELOPMENT IF EARNED
-> CHANGED FUTURE ACTION POSSIBILITY
```

No card licenses `CLASS -> PERSONALITY`.
