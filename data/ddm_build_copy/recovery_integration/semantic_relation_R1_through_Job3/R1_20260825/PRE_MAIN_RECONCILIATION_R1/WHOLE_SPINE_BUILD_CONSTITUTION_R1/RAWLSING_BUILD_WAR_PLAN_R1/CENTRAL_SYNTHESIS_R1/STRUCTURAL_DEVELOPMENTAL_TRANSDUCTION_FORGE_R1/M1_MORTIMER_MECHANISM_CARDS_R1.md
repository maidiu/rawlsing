## MC-M1-01 — INSTITUTIONALLY GATED ADOLESCENT WORK ACCESS

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 1, pp. 1–28; Ch. 3, pp. 54–56, Table 3.1.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
THEORETICAL/HISTORICAL CLAIM + DESCRIPTIVE AGE/RULE ASSOCIATION.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
School organization, labor-law age rules, employer opportunities, family economy, local labor market.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Institutional rules and counterpart employers define which jobs, schedules and hours are available to a given adolescent at a given age.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Eligibility, schedule, hours ceiling, job opportunity set, counterpart set, income opportunity and time constraints.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
A structural opening becomes a work condition only if an employer/job counterpart admits the adolescent and the adolescent actually occupies the position/schedule.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Search, willingness, motives, acceptance/refusal, school commitment.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
None automatically. Downstream only through realized work ecology and V3 uptake.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
CONDITION + TRANSITION into/out of employment.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Institution ↔ employer ↔ adolescent; school/family/labor-market interface.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Later person state can change search/selection, but new position still requires external admission.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
Historically and locally variable; Minnesota age rules visibly alter hours opportunity around age 16.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`PERSON WANTS WORK != PERSON HAS WORK`; `EMPLOYED != DEVELOPMENT`.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D1 `SUPPORTED`; D2 `SUPPORTED`; D3 `SUPPORTED AS GATE REQUIREMENT`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
PRE-V3 structural rule/affordance/topology → actor/phase/context binding → D1 eligibility/gate ecology; OBL058/059/056 provide narrow reusable applicability/admission pattern.

**DISPOSITION**  
`GENERALIZE` existing gate/applicability pattern; no new owner.

## MC-M1-02 — TYPED MIXED WORK ECOLOGY

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 3, pp. 44–80; especially Table 3.5, p. 73.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
DESCRIPTIVE RESULT / HETEROGENEOUS CONDITION BUNDLE.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Job type, organization, supervision, schedule, pay structure, task allocation and advancement structure.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
A job generates multiple simultaneous actor-relative conditions: learning opportunity, specific learning, advancement, security, earnings, pay appraisal, supervisory support, psychological engagement, stressors, school compatibility and work-derived status.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Resources + obligations + activities + status + time + exposure + strain coexist.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
Each dimension must be separately realized/measured. Job membership alone cannot mint all dimensions, and one positive dimension cannot set global job valence.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Perceived meaning, engagement, status and school compatibility are actor-relative; some job features are closer to objective conditions.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
Outcome-specific: efficacy, values, depressed affect, drinking/problem behavior, educational action.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
CONDITION + RELATION + RECURRENT ACTIVITY.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Job organization ↔ supervisor/coworkers/customers ↔ actor.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Prior actor state affects selection into quality bundles; quality then predicts some later actor states.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
Table 3.5 shows high investment combines more learning/engagement/advancement with more stress. Security, “good pay,” and supervisory support do not move monotonically.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`JOB == GOOD/BAD` is rejected. `HOURS == QUALITY` is rejected.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D1 `STRONGLY SUPPORTED + NARROW`: D1 must be vector-valued. D2 `SUPPORTED` where dimensions persist/recur. D3 `SUPPORTED` only when downstream effects feed later gated positions.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
D1 exact target: actor-relative resource/dependency/authority/obligation/activity/eligibility/counterpart/information/support/sanction/time/condition ecology. Existing atlas families `WORK_QUALITY`, `TIME_ALLOCATION`, `ROLE_OBLIGATION`, `STRESS_PROCESS` are composable; do not create a work-object scalar.

**DISPOSITION**  
`GENERALIZE D1 INTERNAL TYPING`; no D4.

## MC-M1-03 — DURATION × INTENSITY WORK-HISTORY PATTERN

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 3, pp. 71–74, Tables 3.4–3.5; Ch. 7, pp. 188–196, Tables 7.1–7.2.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
DESCRIPTIVE TRAJECTORY CONSTRUCTION + PROSPECTIVE ASSOCIATION.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Availability of jobs and schedules across repeated months/waves.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Two independent temporal dimensions—duration and weekly intensity—generate distinct histories: occasional, sporadic, steady, most invested.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Different recurrence patterns despite similar snapshot employment or similar cumulative hours.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
Work history requires interval-specific observed employment and hours; a current employment flag or three annual snapshots are inadequate. Mortimer explicitly shows serial snapshots misclassify cumulative patterns.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Selection and persistence are partly actor-mediated but externally gated by job retention/opportunities and school constraints.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
Educational attainment, early earnings, family transitions, problem behavior.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
RECURRENT ACTIVITY + TRAJECTORY.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Actor across repeated employer/job episodes and school years.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Prior educational promise predicts pattern; pattern predicts later outcomes.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
Same cumulative exposure can arise from short/high-intensity versus long/moderate-intensity histories and need not have same implications.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`TOTAL HOURS == HISTORY` false; `CURRENT EMPLOYMENT == TRAJECTORY` false.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D2 `VERY STRONGLY SUPPORTED`; D1 `SUPPORTED`; D3 `COMPATIBLE`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
Directly fits `RECURRENCE(activity/practice)` + `ORDERED_HISTORY(trajectory)` from temporal court and D2 interval/recurrence grammar.

**DISPOSITION**  
`GENERALIZE D2` to preserve duration, intensity and pattern identity explicitly.

## MC-M1-04 — CROSS-DOMAIN TIME-BUDGET REBINDING

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 4, pp. 81–110; Fig. 4.2, pp. 97–98; Tables 4.2–4.4, pp. 99–104.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
DESCRIPTIVE RESULT / HETEROGENEOUS TIME-USE CONFIGURATION.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Paid-work schedule + school schedule + family obligations + extracurricular/volunteer opportunity structure.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Adding paid work changes finite time allocation, but displacement is not uniform: family labor declines at aggregate level while homework, activities and volunteering can remain similar; actor-centered clusters show multiple compensations/substitutions.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Time, obligations, activities, peer/family contact and school participation are rebound as a configuration.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
A work-hours increase yields a cross-domain effect only when it actually displaces or reorganizes another activity interval.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Prior commitments, priorities and schedule choices shape which domain yields time.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
Indirect: school engagement/achievement, family participation, extracurricular/civic activity.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
CONDITION + RECURRENT ACTIVITY + TRANSITION/REBINDING.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Work ↔ school ↔ household ↔ extracurricular/peer contexts.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Changes in school/family commitments can later alter feasible work schedules.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
No universal crowd-out. “Active workers” and “active nonworkers” can be similar outside work.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`MORE PAID WORK -> LESS HOMEWORK/ACTIVITY/VOLUNTEERING` not generally supported.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D1 `SUPPORTED`; D2 `STRONGLY SUPPORTED`; D3 `COMPATIBLE`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
Coverage matrix already identifies `time/use-time allocation` as generalize-existing and `cross-domain propagation` as missing connective semantics. D1+D2 supply the right implementation grain.

**DISPOSITION**  
`GENERALIZE D1+D2`; no standalone time-budget carrier.

## MC-M1-05 — SELECTION INTO WORK INVESTMENT AND QUALITY

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 5, pp. 111–139; Tables 5.1–5.4.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
PROSPECTIVE SELECTION EFFECT.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Family socioeconomic resources, school opportunity/achievement, local jobs, employer gate, gendered labor-market sorting, peer context.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Ninth-grade motivation, grades, aspirations, problem behavior, peer orientation, gender and background predict later work investment and job-quality dimensions.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Differential probability of high/low intensity, persistence, learning opportunity, extrinsic rewards, stress and school-compatible work.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
Person state may alter search, preference, persistence or eligibility, but position/quality requires an external employer/job/schedule gate. The data do not license a direct person→position mutation.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Motives for consumption, saving, experience and skill; school commitment; peer orientation.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
None at selection edge; it changes later exposure ecology.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
TRAJECTORY SELECTION / TRANSITION.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Actor ↔ family/school/peer context ↔ employer/labor market.

**RECIPROCITY**  
This is the person→later-position half of reciprocal dynamics, contingent on external gate.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
Observed covariates do not exhaust unobserved heterogeneity.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`WORKER DIFFERENCE AFTER EMPLOYMENT == WORK EFFECT` false unless prior selection addressed. `PERSON -> POSITION` without gate false.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D3 `STRONGLY SUPPORTS GATED FEEDBACK FORM`; D1 `SUPPORTED` because selection is to typed ecology, not employment alone.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
D3 already requires actor action/person change → external gate/response → admitted position/rule/resource change → new actor ecology.

**DISPOSITION**  
`REUSE D3 GATE LAW`; no new selection owner.

## MC-M1-06 — DEVELOPMENTAL-PHASE QUALIFICATION OF AUTONOMY/CONTROL

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 6, pp. 159–160; compared by Mortimer with adult work/self-direction literature.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
PROSPECTIVE/SELECTION-CONTROLLED HETEROGENEOUS EFFECT + AUTHOR INTERPRETATION.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Job decision authority/control, work intensity, role clarity/support.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
For novice adolescent boys, decision authority/control can impose responsibility before sufficient knowledge/problem-solving capacity and structure are available; high hours can amplify this strain.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Experienced responsibility/control burden, ambiguity and stress differ from the nominal formal autonomy dimension.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
Formal authority becomes developmental exposure only through actually exercised responsibility under a phase/history-specific competence/support context.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Prior experience, perceived competence, interpretation of responsibility, coping.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
Depressed mood/distress.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
CONDITION qualified by developmental phase/history.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Actor ↔ supervisor/job organization.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Experience accumulated over time may change how later autonomy is taken up.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
Adolescent male finding; later gender differences diminish; not a universal autonomy-harms rule.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`AUTONOMY ALWAYS BENEFICIAL` false; `FORMAL AUTONOMY == EXERCISED AUTONOMY` false; adolescent effect != adult effect.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D1 `NARROW/GENERALIZE` to distinguish formal condition from enacted experience. D2 `STRONGLY SUPPORTED` because active phase/history qualifies transduction. D3 not required for the immediate effect.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
Existing ADP-008/Stage-A active phase + D1 typed ecology + D2 history binding + V3 response. Coverage matrix already marks historical/age/timing qualification as `GENERALIZE_EXISTING_OWNER`.

**DISPOSITION**  
`REUSE/GENERALIZE`; proposed D4 `REJECT_REDUNDANT`.

## MC-M1-07 — WORK-QUALITY-SPECIFIC DEVELOPMENTAL TARGETING

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 6, pp. 159–170, Tables 6.1–6.2.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
PROSPECTIVE/LONGITUDINAL ASSOCIATIONS WITH PRIOR-STATE/BACKGROUND CONTROLS; some mechanisms remain author interpretations.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Learning opportunities, advancement/reward, stressors, autonomy/responsibility, work-school compatibility, peer status.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Different work dimensions provide different repeated experiences: learning/practice, success/reward, strain/conflict, adultlike status.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Learning exposure, efficacy evidence, conflict/strain, status and responsibility.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
A work-quality dimension must be present in actual experience and then be taken up through target-specific V3 processes; job title/hours alone do not entail it.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Perceived compatibility, efficacy, meaning/status, coping; alternative interpretations sometimes unresolved.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
Economic efficacy, intrinsic/people/extrinsic work values, depressive affect, alcohol use/problem behavior, educational plans/actions.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
RECURRENT ACTIVITY/CONDITION → TRANSITION in V3 state if earned.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Workplace + school + peer status contexts.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Efficacy subsequently predicts plans/actions, which can affect later institutional transitions.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
Effect signs and persistence differ by outcome, gender/age and work intensity.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
No generic `WORK QUALITY -> PERSONALITY`; no inference from work to durable trait without target-specific persistence evidence.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D1 `VERY STRONGLY SUPPORTED`; D2 `SUPPORTED`; D3 `SUPPORTED IN PARTIAL SEQUENCE`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
D1 produces typed realized ecology; V3 TD-R05/CAN-008/010/011/ADP-016/017 receive event/activity inputs. Seam cannot mint the psychological outcome.

**DISPOSITION**  
`REUSE V3 + GENERALIZE D1`.

## MC-M1-08 — TRANSIENT PROBLEM-BEHAVIOR EFFECT

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 6, pp. 162–164; Table 6.2; Ch. 8, pp. 221–222.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
PROSPECTIVE ASSOCIATION + LONGER-TERM NULL.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
High-intensity work and selected quality dimensions.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Possible stress-reduction, adultlike status and peer-status pathways; source cannot adjudicate among them.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
High hours, responsibility, status, school conflict.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
The exposure association is contemporaneous/near-term; durable development would require persistence after work pattern changes and later phases.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Coping/status meaning and peer context.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
Alcohol use and school problem behavior.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
CONDITION/RECURRENT ACTIVITY → TRANSIENT STATE/BEHAVIOR.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Work ↔ peer/school behavior.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Selection into intensive work/problem behavior is controlled only partially; full reciprocity not established.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
School problem behavior elevation only in tenth grade; work-pattern alcohol differences disappear four years after high school.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`CONTEMPORANEOUS DIFFERENCE == DURABLE CHANGE` false.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D1 `SUPPORTED`; D2 `STRONGLY SUPPORTED` because persistence must be adjudicated; D3 `NOT PROVED` by this result.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
Temporal court already separates condition/recurrence from persistent V3 change.

**DISPOSITION**  
`REUSE D2/V3 PERSISTENCE FIREWALL`.

PROSPECTIVE ASSOCIATION WITH EXTENSIVE BACKGROUND/PRIOR-PROMISE CONTROLS; not randomized.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Sustained low/moderate-intensity employment alongside school.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Potential compatibility of sustained work with schooling, plus skill/efficacy/resource gains; exact mediation not fully identified by the table.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Stable work exposure without high-intensity time burden; earnings/experience alongside continued educational participation.

**REALIZATION LAW**

Work ↔ school/postsecondary institutions.

**RECIPROCITY**

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**

Does not establish that steady work itself causally produces the B.A.; residual selection may remain.

## MC-M1-10 — NORMATIVELY TIMED FAMILY-ROLE TRANSITION

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 7, pp. 196–200, Figs. 7.2–7.3, Table 7.3; Ch. 8, pp. 221–222.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
PROSPECTIVE AGE/TIMING HETEROGENEITY.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Prior work-investment history, earnings and learning potential plus adult relationship/family opportunity context.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
More intensive work history is associated with faster movement into marriage/cohabitation/parenthood, but not at age 21–22; differences emerge by 24–25 when transitions are more normative.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
New partner/parent relations, obligations, household resources/time and role ecology.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
The transition exists only when relationship/family formation actually occurs. Its developmental significance arises from post-transition ecology rebinding, not from the label `adult`.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Plans, economic readiness, partner selection and role expectations; not all measured end-to-end.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
No automatic psychological target; social-position/ecology transition first.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
TRANSITION → REBINDING → TRAJECTORY.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Actor ↔ partner/child/household/institutions.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Prior work trajectory predicts later social transition; transition then changes later resources/obligations and subsequent work/family ecology.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
No accelerated family transition at 21–22; effects emerge at 24–25. Table 7.3 shows learning potential reduces parenthood probability; steady pattern is less likely than most-invested to predict parenthood.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`HIGH-INTENSITY ADOLESCENT WORK -> PRECOCIOUS FAMILY FORMATION` not supported.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D2 `STRONGLY SUPPORTED`; D3 `SUPPORTED AS REBINDING/FEEDBACK FORM`; D1 `SUPPORTED` for post-transition ecology.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
Temporal court transition law already says causal importance comes from rebinding roles/resources/obligations/schedules/institutions/counterparts/activities/opportunities.

**DISPOSITION**  
`REUSE/GENERALIZE D2+D3`; no transition ontology expansion.

## MC-M1-11 — EARLY VOCATIONAL PAYOFF WITH LATER CONVERGENCE

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 7, pp. 195–196, Table 7.2; Ch. 8, p. 221.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
PROSPECTIVE ASSOCIATION + LATER ATTENUATION/NULL.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
High-school work intensity/history and later labor-market participation.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Intensive workers accumulate early experience/earnings and enter labor force faster; educational investment among steady/lower-intensity youth can later offset early earnings differences.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Early income/employment advantage versus later education/credential opportunity.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
A short-run advantage cannot be marked durable until later observations preserve it under changing postsecondary and labor-market ecology.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Education choices, job search, labor-force attachment.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
Occupational/earnings trajectory, not personality.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
TRAJECTORY / CUMULATIVE PROCESS with possible reversal/convergence.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Actor ↔ education system ↔ labor market/employers.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Early work changes credentials/experience; later institutional decisions alter subsequent jobs and earnings.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
By six years after high school earnings profiles of work-investment groups are very similar; nonworkers remain disadvantaged.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
`EARLY ADVANTAGE == CUMULATIVE ADVANTAGE` false.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D2 `SUPPORTED`; D3 `SUPPORTED`; generic cumulative scalar `REJECT`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
Matches temporal court iterative-update law where shocks/education/other gates can interrupt/reverse trajectories.

**DISPOSITION**  
`REUSE_TYPED_ITERATIVE_FEEDBACK`; reject monotonic accumulation.

## MC-M1-12 — PARTIAL RECIPROCAL WORK→EFFICACY→ACTION→GATED TRANSITION SEQUENCE

**SOURCE**  
Ch. 5 selection results; Ch. 6, pp. 165–170; Ch. 7 attainment analyses.

**CLAIM STATUS**  
COMPOSITE OF PROSPECTIVE ASSOCIATIONS; NOT A SINGLE END-TO-END CAUSAL ESTIMATE.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT**  
Available job-quality bundle + school/postsecondary gate structure.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM**  
Prior person state predicts gated work selection; positive work reward/advancement and learning experiences predict efficacy/values; economic efficacy predicts educational plans and concrete application actions; schools/colleges then gate later attainment.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE**  
Changed efficacy, plans/actions, application/contact with institutions, later opportunity/credential ecology.

**REALIZATION LAW**  
Every edge must occur separately: job condition must be realized; V3 change must be earned; actor must act; institution must respond/admit; only then may a later social fact/position be updated.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION**  
Efficacy, plans, motives, actions.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET**  
Economic/work efficacy and educational orientation/action; later attainment is an external social outcome.

**TEMPORAL FORM**  
RECURRENT ACTIVITY → V3 CHANGE → ACTION → GATED TRANSITION → NEW TRAJECTORY.

**RELATIONAL SCALE**  
Workplace ↔ actor ↔ educational institution.

**RECIPROCITY**  
Yes in architecture; Mortimer provides component links but not a single fully identified closed loop.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS**  
Residual selection/unobserved heterogeneity; multiple mediators; institution responses not modeled as a complete causal system.

**NEGATIVE CEILING**  
Do not claim Mortimer proves a self-executing person↔structure feedback law.

**D1 / D2 / D3 TEST**  
D3 `STRENGTHENED BUT EMPIRICALLY PARTIAL`; D1/D2 supply ecology and history inputs.

**RAWLSING MAPPING**  
Exactly matches frozen D3 form: `V3/actor action -> realized external action -> institutional/counterpart response/gate -> admitted change in position/resource/rule/network -> new social fact with provenance -> later actor-ecology derivation`.

**DISPOSITION**  
`REUSE D3`; no D4.
