## 2. Chapter 1 — Bhattacharya, Introduction

**Theoretical argument.** Bhattacharya begins from the question of how a worker is made ready to arrive at work: food, sleep, household labor, school, transport, parks/libraries and other life-maintaining arrangements (`SRC:L230-L248`). She distinguishes Marx's use of social reproduction for reproduction of the capitalist system as a whole from narrower feminist usage around maintenance/reproduction of life and labor power (`SRC:L285-L350`). She rejects a simple spatial split between production and reproduction and emphasizes production-time structuring of reproductive time (`SRC:L306-L332`).

**Historical interpretation / cited evidence.** The chapter synthesizes Marx, Vogel, Laslett/Brenner, Luxton, Weeks, intersectionality debates, and examples developed more fully by later contributors. The notes show the argument is overwhelmingly theoretical and secondary-literature based (`SRC:L2157-L2219`).

**Political/strategic argument.** Struggles around living conditions outside the workplace can be class struggle because wages and extra-workplace provisioning are materially linked (`SRC:L387-L406`).

**Rawlsing mechanism yield.** (i) wage/property dependence -> resources/exit constraints; (ii) paid-work time -> unpaid-work/recovery/education scheduling constraints; (iii) public provision -> resource/time/access ecology; (iv) household/care allocation -> obligations/support; (v) migration/slavery -> labor-force replacement at historical-system scale, not individual psychology.

**Under-specified claims.** “Oppressions are coproduced with surplus value” and “organic totality” do not specify the institutional classification, allocation, treatment, or activity mechanism. Those statements can orient search but cannot execute.

## 3. Chapter 2 — Fraser

**Theoretical argument.** Fraser types a “care crisis” as pressure on concrete capacities for childrearing, household maintenance, care and social connection (`SRC:L425-L431`) and proposes a systemic production/reproduction contradiction (`SRC:L442-L452`).

**Historical interpretation.** She distinguishes liberal-competitive, state-managed and financialized regimes (`SRC:L461-L469`), then narrates protective legislation, colonial expropriation, welfare provision, racial exclusions, two-earner households, austerity, debt and global care chains (`SRC:L475-L561`).

**Cited secondary evidence.** The chapter draws on histories of protective labor law, slavery, Indigenous boarding schools, welfare-state scholarship, debt/finance, care-work and migration studies (`SRC:L2220-L2294`).

**Rawlsing mechanism yield.** Policy/service provision and withdrawal; working-hours/wage/resource effects on care capacity; debt/austerity gates; migration/care outsourcing; legal exclusions; gendered allocation only where a rule, labor-market relation, household dependency or service structure actually mediates it.

**Under-specified claims.** The universal “contradiction” is too coarse for Rawlsing. It must be rewritten as changed wage, time, service, debt, care obligation, migration, or eligibility relations.

## 4. Chapter 3 — Mohandesi & Teitelman

**Theoretical/historical argument.** The authors define social reproduction primarily as activity creating, maintaining and restoring labor power and show households as both sites of reproduction and sites of pooled resources, domination and mutual aid (`SRC:L596-L606`). They explicitly acknowledge at least three levels in the notes: individual labor power, total workforce, capitalist system (`SRC:L2295-L2525`, note 4).

**Historical evidence base.** The chapter is a synthesis of historical scholarship on enclosure, Native dispossession, sharecropping/credit, domestic service, consumer struggle, social welfare, New Deal/Great Society policy, racial exclusions, household technology, incarceration and welfare retrenchment. It often supplies concrete institutional rules and dates but generally through secondary sources.

**Rawlsing mechanism yield.** Especially strong: loss of subsistence resources -> wage/credit dependence (`SRC:L611-L626`); wage dependence + separate work/home -> gendered bargaining/allocation (`SRC:L628-L642`); prices/rents -> household budgeting strain and organized response (`SRC:L648-L663`); Ford job sanctions -> domestic surveillance/routines (`SRC:L680-L685`); Depression -> wage collapse -> resource/housing/care reorganization -> collective action -> policy response (`SRC:L698-L737`); New Deal eligibility/provision -> resource/infrastructure changes plus racial/gender exclusions (`SRC:L728-L740`); neoliberal welfare conditionality -> paid-work compulsion/service-sector labor (`SRC:L803-L812`).

**Under-specified claims.** “Class recomposition” is not a causal primitive. The useful evidence is the sequence of network, organization, mobilization, state response, and altered provision.

## 5. Chapter 4 — Bhattacharya, How Not to Skip Class

**Theoretical argument.** Class is a relation grounded in dispossession and wage dependence, not a job label (`SRC:L875-L954`, `L999-L1004`). Reproduction of labor power includes household and public institutions (`SRC:L942-L958`). Production and reproduction are distinct forms within one process, not independent systems (`SRC:L962-L986`).

**Rawlsing mechanism yield.** Property separation -> wage dependence; wages/public goods -> means of subsistence; workday/schedule -> time available for self-maintenance/development; differential access to provisioning -> heterogeneous class life; extra-workplace institutions and costs -> class-relevant ecology.

**Political/strategic argument.** Bhattacharya broadens class struggle to include water, housing, education and other reproduction struggles and argues for wider working-class organization (`SRC:L1076-L1212`).

**Under-specified claims.** The strategic definition of all working-class life struggles as class struggle does not prove organization, solidarity, action, or structural effect in a given case. The “self-development” circuit is theoretical and does not license a V3 psychological outcome.

## 6. Chapter 5 — McNally

**Theoretical/philosophical argument.** McNally criticizes atomistic “axes” and argues that race, gender and class relations are historically co-constituting within a concrete totality (`SRC:L1242-L1348`). He uses Hegelian reproduction and retrodetermination to frame systems as temporal relational processes.

**Rawlsing mechanism yield.** Primarily negative/architectural: do not treat demographic categories as independent causal atoms; preserve typed relations and their historical composition. A relation can condition another relation only through concrete links.

**Under-specified claims.** “Each relation is inside the others,” “the whole determines the parts,” and retrodetermination are not implementation rules. Rawlsing must preserve later reinterpretation or consequence without rewriting earlier provenance. No `CO_CONSTITUTION` operator is licensed.

## 7. Chapter 6 — Ferguson

**Theoretical/historical argument.** Ferguson treats children as participants in socially organized life, not simply passive future workers. Capitalist productive relations structure terrain and constraints without mechanically determining childhood (`SRC:L1358-L1489`).

**Rawlsing mechanism yield.** Household income/care need -> child paid/unpaid labor requirement -> school attendance/time tradeoff; compulsory-schooling rules -> recurrent institutional activity; school/work discipline -> schedules, sanctions, permitted play/activity; play and collective child practices -> possible action, not guaranteed development or structural change.

**Evidence ceiling.** The chapter uses cited histories and developmental theory. It cannot support a universal `SCHOOL -> CAPACITY` or `CHILD LABOR -> PSYCHOLOGICAL TRAIT` edge. Development requires downstream evidence about actual activity, instruction, practice and uptake.

## 8. Chapter 7 — Teeple Hopkins

**Empirical evidence.** This is the clearest original micro-empirical chapter. The study involved 28 interviews with precarious women workers/activists; four were paid domestic workers; two Filipina former live-in caregivers, Marie and Dawn, supply the central vignettes (`SRC:L1502-L1510`, `L1610-L1652`). The author also cites a separate Montreal survey of 148 live-in caregivers showing long hours and frequent unpaid overtime (`SRC:L1630-L1637`).

**Mechanisms.** LCP rules linked residence/employment/time toward permanent residence (`SRC:L1578-L1591`); employer control over live-in space enabled time encroachment (`SRC:L1620-L1637`); full-time work + twenty hours/week schooling + church/rest created a finite time budget (`SRC:L1643-L1652`); church ties supplied concrete job referrals, food, housing, material help and emotional support (`SRC:L1640-L1660`).

**Evidence ceiling.** The two vignettes richly identify mechanisms but cannot establish prevalence or average effects. The chapter's slavery analogy is historical/interpretive. Faith community must be represented as actual ties, copresence, transfers and referrals—not as `RELIGION -> SUPPORT`.

## 9. Chapter 8 — Oran

**Theoretical/historical argument.** Oran places pensions in generational reproduction of the working class and distinguishes deferred-wage, savings, need-based and kinship/dependency forms (`SRC:L1699-L1715`). She traces historical pension provision, labor struggle, public PAYG systems and later financialization (`SRC:L1725-L1857`).

**Mechanisms.** eligibility/contribution/indexation rules -> old-age income; pension income -> household resource flows/elder-care burden; employment history/citizenship/kinship -> benefit access; funded schemes -> market-risk exposure; reform -> higher retirement age/longer contribution period/lower replacement rate (`SRC:L1798-L1822`).

**Evidence ceiling.** Oran's claims that all pensions derive from surplus value, workers cannot ultimately pay tax, or pension reform is necessarily a unitary capitalist-class attack are theoretical political-economy theses, not actor-level causal identification. Rawlsing can represent the explicit rule/resource changes without adopting those premises.

## 10. Chapter 9 — Sears

**Theoretical/historical argument.** Sears situates sexuality within work, household, leisure and community relations and argues that capitalist “double freedom” combines bodily self-ownership with material compulsion from dispossession (`SRC:L1879-L1955`).

**Mechanism yield.** wage/resource dependency; care allocation/devaluation; unequal labor and household relations; migration/legal vulnerability; violence/coercion; schedule and childcare constraints; movement participation as a site of changed activity/counterpart relations (`SRC:L2017-L2108`).

**Evidence ceiling.** Claims linking forms of work to “embodied agency,” masculine/feminine ways of knowing, or sexuality remain theoretically suggestive and secondary-evidence based. Rawlsing must stop at role/activity/power/exposure unless a competent V3 owner and appropriate evidence earn a psychological consequence.

## 11. Chapter 10 — Arruzza

**Historical/political evidence.** Arruzza reports the 2016 Polish women’s strike and the stopping of an abortion-ban bill, plus international movement diffusion; she cites US unionization/strike-day statistics and describes Women’s Strike organization (`SRC:L2124-L2153`).

**Mechanism yield.** organization/network -> mobilization/work stoppage -> identifiable external political/institutional response -> possible rule outcome. The Polish case is the cleanest D3-shaped episode in the chapter, but even there the chapter is not a causal-identification study isolating the strike from all other political causes.

**Evidence ceiling.** “Women’s strike as political translation of SRT,” “solidarity as the only way,” and claims about lived inseparability are political/theoretical propositions. They do not license direct identity->mobilization or mobilization->transformation.

## 12. Cross-volume evidentiary verdict

The volume is strong for **mechanism discovery, historical specification and causal decomposition**. It is much weaker for **effect-size estimation, counterfactual identification, psychological developmental law, and universal macro-to-micro causation**. The only original actor-level qualitative evidence in the volume is narrow and should remain narrow. Most other empirical content is historical or secondary evidence.
