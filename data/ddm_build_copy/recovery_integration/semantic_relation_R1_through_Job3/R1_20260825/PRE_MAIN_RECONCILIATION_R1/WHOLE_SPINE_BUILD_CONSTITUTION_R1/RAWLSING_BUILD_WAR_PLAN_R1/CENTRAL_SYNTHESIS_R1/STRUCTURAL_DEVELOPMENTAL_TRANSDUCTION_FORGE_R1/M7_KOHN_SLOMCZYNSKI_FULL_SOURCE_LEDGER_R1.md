## 1. What the book actually tries to establish

The book is a cross-system test of an interpretation developed first in US work. The proposed chain is not `COUNTRY/CLASS -> PERSONALITY`. It is:

```text
SOCIAL-STRUCTURAL POSITION
-> SYSTEMATICALLY DIFFERENTIATED PROXIMATE CONDITIONS OF LIFE
-> RECURRENT EXPERIENCE
-> PSYCHOLOGICAL FUNCTIONING
```

The empirical core narrows this to:

```text
CLASS / STRATIFICATION
-> OCCUPATIONAL SELF-DIRECTION
   = substantive complexity of work
   + freedom from close supervision
   + freedom from routinization
-> parental valuation of self-direction
-> intellectual/ideational flexibility
-> self-directedness of orientation
```

with reciprocal selection/molding effects running back from psychological functioning into occupational self-direction and, more weakly, later class/status placement.

The book itself repeatedly treats broad social categories as analytically insufficient unless unpacked into concrete conditions of life (Introduction, pp. 3-7; chs. 5-6; ch. 9, pp. 255-62).

## 2. Comparative settings and historical period

### United States

- 1964 baseline: national area-probability sample of `3,101` men, age 16+, employed in civilian occupations at least 25 hours/week; women, military personnel and the currently unemployed were excluded (ch. 2, pp. 20-22).
- 1974 follow-up: representative subsample of `687` men still under age 66, selected from baseline men younger than 56; wives and the originally referenced children were also sought (pp. 23-24).
- Fieldwork: NORC, spring/summer 1964; median baseline interview about 2.5 hours; response rate 76 percent. Follow-up response among selected men 78 percent.
- Known sample distortion: underrepresentation of larger metropolitan areas; otherwise close correspondence to the 1960 Census population defined by the study's eligibility criteria.

### Poland

- 1978 baseline: probability sample of `1,557` urban men, age 19-65, employed full-time in civilian occupations (pp. 25-26).
- Population explicitly excludes the rural peasantry; farmers near urban centers could enter the sample. This was done partly to make the Polish population more comparable to the US employed-male population.
- Three-stage probability design: urban centers -> electoral districts -> voter registers, with eligibility screening and an auxiliary replacement sample.
- 1979-80 family follow-up: mothers and children then aged 13-17, `N=177`, interviewed about 1.5-2 years after fathers.
- Polish Academy of Sciences conducted and quality-controlled the fieldwork.

### Historical configuration is not background noise

The Polish data describe a highly centralized state-socialist economy circa 1978, after post-war industrialization, managerial/technical expansion, and recent labor-law changes. The US baseline precedes the Polish baseline by fourteen years. The authors explicitly recognize that these are different historical moments and later use cohort analysis to test whether some relationships remain stable across historically distinct cohorts (ch. 9, pp. 238-41).

## 3. Cross-national comparability discipline

The book's methodological premise is `MEANINGFUL COMPARABILITY`, not mechanical identity.

The Polish instrument was designed as a replication of the US instrument, but translation was connotative rather than merely literal. Alternate translations were judged by linguistic experts, culturally impossible situations were replaced by functionally analogous situations, the schedule was piloted, unclear items were revised, and coding/verification procedures were audited (ch. 2, pp. 18-30).

The authors' strongest comparative rule is:

```text
SAME CONCEPTUAL TARGET
+ VALID WITHIN EACH SYSTEM
+ EMPIRICALLY TESTED MEASUREMENT RELATION
!= IDENTICAL SURFACE ITEMS REQUIRED
```

This logic governs class, stratification, occupational self-direction, values, intellectual flexibility and orientations.

## 4. Social class: same abstract relation, system-specific operationalization

Class is nominal/relational, not a single rank. It is defined by relations to ownership/control of means of production and control over others' labor power, with secondary attention to employment situation (ch. 3, pp. 31-42).

### US class structure

Six categories are operationalized from actual work relations (pp. 32-37):

1. `Employers`: owners employing four or more non-family workers.
2. `Self-employed`: owners employing no more than three non-family workers.
3. `Managers`: employees owning less than 20 percent of the enterprise and with at least two hierarchical levels beneath them.
4. `First-line supervisors`: employees with direct supervisory authority over roughly three or more workers and only nonsupervisory workers below.
5. `Nonmanual workers`: nonsupervisory employees doing predominantly nonmanual work.
6. `Manual workers`: nonsupervisory employees doing predominantly manual work.

Crucial negative finding: the authors refuse to define class using autonomy/occupational self-direction, because that would build the proposed mediator into the class category and make the mediation test circular (pp. 33-35).

### Polish class structure

The same abstract class logic cannot be instantiated by copying the US categories. In a centralized nationalized economy, the authors instead make concrete institutional location part of the class map (pp. 38-42):

1. `Managers`: top decision positions in state/cooperative enterprises, sufficiently extensive formal supervision, or multiple subordinate levels.
2. `First-line supervisors`: direct authority over 2-24 workers with no supervisors below.
3. `Nonmanual workers`: nonsupervisory professionals, technicians and office workers.
4. `Production workers`: manual workers in large-scale manufacturing/extractive enterprises of the centralized core.
5. `Nonproduction workers`: manual workers in secondary/supportive industry and service.
6. `Self-employed`: private owners and family members employed in their enterprises.

There is no employer class comparable to the US employer class. Polish class construction explicitly depends on central planning, state control, hierarchical authority, the manual/nonmanual division, core/periphery location inside the nationalized economy, and the constrained private sector.

**M7 consequence:** class is not a portable actor property. A class label is an output of a system-specific relational configuration.

## 5. Social stratification: a different structural axis

Stratification is treated as a continuous hierarchy of power, privilege and prestige, indexed by three components in both countries (ch. 3, pp. 42-49):

- educational attainment;
- occupational status/prestige;
- job income.

Confirmatory factor models show broadly comparable latent structure but different relative weights: education and occupational status are roughly co-dominant in the US model; occupational status is more dominant in Poland, with education secondary; income is least powerful in both. Some of the difference is attributable to US longitudinal versus Polish cross-sectional measurement.

Class and stratification overlap but do not collapse into one another. They have independent effects, although the overlap is larger in Poland. The book directly rejects treating occupation/status as the whole content of occupational position.

## 6. Psychological functioning: exact construct decomposition

The book does not offer a general theory of personality and explicitly lacks behavioral measures other than behavior during interview (Introduction, pp. 3-4). Its empirical psychological targets are narrower.

### 6.1 Parental valuation of self-direction

Parents rank desirable characteristics for children. The latent contrast is self-direction versus conformity to external authority (ch. 4, pp. 56-69). The same general measurement strategy is used in both countries after intensive Polish pretesting.

### 6.2 Intellectual flexibility

The causal analyses focus on the ideational component of intellectual flexibility, using performance-based and interviewer-based indicators, including cognitive problems whose surface wording is culturally adapted where necessary (pp. 69-74). The Polish model is a cross-sectional analogue of the US longitudinal model; one perceptual/ideational relationship differs, but the authors judge the ideational construct comparable.

### 6.3 Orientations to self and society

Seven first-order dimensions are indexed in Poland and in the US comparison (pp. 75-88):

- authoritarian conservatism;
- standards of morality;
- trustfulness;
- idea-conformity;
- self-confidence;
- self-deprecation;
- anxiety.

US models additionally include fatalism; an adequate Polish fatalism measure could not be built. At second order the authors distinguish:

- `SELF_DIRECTEDNESS_OF_ORIENTATION`;
- `DISTRESS / WELL_BEING`.

The second-order structure itself exposes an important contextual divergence: in the US, self-directedness tends to covary with well-being; in Poland, self-directedness tends to covary with distress. This is not treated as a measurement failure but as a substantive difference requiring explanation.

## 7. Occupational self-direction: exact disaggregation

Occupational self-direction means use of initiative, thought and independent judgment in work (ch. 5, pp. 110-13). It is a second-order construct with three first-order components:

### 7.1 Substantive complexity of work

How much the work itself requires thought and independent judgment. It is measured from detailed descriptions of work with things, data/ideas and people, including overall complexity and time spent in those activities.

### 7.2 Closeness of supervision

Subjective/relational indicators include freedom to disagree with a supervisor, how closely one is supervised, whether the supervisor tells rather than discusses, and the importance of doing what one is told.

### 7.3 Routinization

Repetition/predictability and task variability. The US and Polish measures are not surface-identical: predictability adds no explanatory power in Poland, so the Polish index is narrower.

The second-order models fit both countries and have similar parameters. Substantive complexity is by far the strongest component in both. The same observed construct therefore translates comparatively at the level necessary for the book's causal analysis.

But `SAME CONSTRUCT != SAME MECHANISM MAGNITUDE`: substantive complexity is more psychologically potent in the US; closeness of supervision is relatively more important in Poland.

## 8. Main cross-system mechanism that survives

The strongest replicated chain is:

```text
CLASS / STRATIFICATION
-> DIFFERENTIAL OPPORTUNITY FOR OCCUPATIONAL SELF-DIRECTION
-> RECURRENT SELF-DIRECTED OR CONSTRAINED WORK EXPERIENCE
-> VALUES / IDEATIONAL FLEXIBILITY / SELF-DIRECTED ORIENTATION
```

Evidence:

- class is very strongly associated with occupational self-direction in both countries (ch. 5, pp. 113-18);
- controlling occupational self-direction substantially reduces class associations with parental values, intellectual flexibility and self-directed orientation in both countries (pp. 118-26);
- analogous structural-equation models show much of stratification's effect mediated by occupational self-direction (pp. 126-49);
- reciprocal-effects models support an actual effect of occupational self-direction on these outcomes, while also allowing personality to affect later occupational self-direction (ch. 6, pp. 156-67).

This is a genuine portability result, but only at the typed-mechanism level. The concrete institutions that produce supervision, complexity, status allocation and class placement differ.

## 9. Reciprocal directionality: what is and is not shown

The US longitudinal data support reciprocal relations:

```text
OWNERSHIP / HIERARCHICAL POSITION
<-> OCCUPATIONAL SELF-DIRECTION
<-> IDEATIONAL FLEXIBILITY / SELF-DIRECTED ORIENTATION / VALUES
```

with different temporal directions depending on relation (ch. 6, pp. 153-70). Occupational position affects self-direction; self-direction can affect later occupational position/class placement. Intellectual flexibility can affect later work complexity; self-directed orientation can affect later supervision. Direct personality effects on ownership/hierarchical placement are weak; much of the return path runs through occupational self-direction.

For Poland, this reciprocal causal court is materially weaker because there is no comparable panel. The authors build quasi-longitudinal models from retrospective work information plus assumptions partly calibrated from US longitudinal data and subject these assumptions to sensitivity analysis.

**Ceiling:** this supports reciprocal person-job selection/molding, not actor-caused institutional/world-rule transformation.

## 10. System difference: education and allocation

Polish men more often continued formal education into their occupational careers. The authors tie this to post-war reindustrialization, shortages of credentialed technical/managerial labor, political job assignment, and education's formal role as an allocation criterion in the planned economy (ch. 8, pp. 203-4).

The mechanism is therefore not:

```text
POLAND -> MORE EDUCATION -> PSYCHOLOGY
```

but:

```text
POSTWAR INDUSTRIALIZATION + CENTRALIZED JOB ALLOCATION + CREDENTIAL RULES
-> ENTRY INTO POSITIONS BEFORE FULL CREDENTIALING
-> CONTINUED FORMAL EDUCATION DURING CAREER
-> OCCUPATIONAL SELF-DIRECTION / PSYCHOLOGICAL PATHS
```

The eventual psychological relation of education is nonetheless broadly similar across countries.

## 11. System difference: authority organization

The authors find that substantive complexity matters relatively more in the US while closeness of supervision matters relatively more in Poland (ch. 8, pp. 204-7).

They tentatively interpret the Polish supervision result through a historically specific authority configuration: more centralized workplace organization, supervisor authority extending beyond workplace/workday, and wider social salience of authority. The state bureaucracy and church are discussed as institutions reinforcing obedience, while US power is described as comparatively de-emphasizing visible authority.

This is not strong enough to universalize as a general law, but it is exactly the form M7 requires:

```text
INSTITUTIONAL AUTHORITY CONFIGURATION
-> ACTOR'S SUPERVISION RELATION
-> RECURRENT EXPERIENCE OF CONTROL / PERMISSION
-> AUTHORITARIAN OR SELF-DIRECTED ORIENTATION
```

with `TENTATIVE` evidence status for the system-level explanatory link.

## 12. System difference: distress breaks the simple portability claim

Distress is the decisive negative case.

For US men, higher class/stratification and greater occupational self-direction generally correspond to less distress. For Polish men, the class ordering is nearly reversed and occupational self-direction fails to ameliorate distress (chs. 4-6; ch. 8, pp. 208-31).

The authors test and reject a simple measurement-artifact explanation. They then widen the concrete conditions of life beyond self-direction.

### US measured mechanism

```text
HIGHER OCCUPATIONAL POSITION
-> MORE OCCUPATIONAL SELF-DIRECTION -> LESS DISTRESS
AND
-> FEWER JOB PROTECTIONS -> MORE DISTRESS
```

Job protections include seniority/contract/union/civil-service protection, grievance procedures and paid sick leave. The countervailing paths explain why broad class/status relations with distress are weaker than a one-mediator model predicts.

### Poland historically grounded but incompletely measured mechanism

At the time of the Polish survey, employees of the nationalized sector had legal protections including employment security, sick leave and guaranteed vacations. Late-1960s/early-1970s labor-law changes extended protections formerly associated with nonmanual workers to manual workers; full implementation was recent before the 1978 survey. Production/manual workers also had high labor demand and system-specific benefits such as housing access, workplace health care and advantages for children's higher education (ch. 8, pp. 216-23).

These facts plausibly help explain low manual-worker distress and high nonmanual-worker distress, but the Polish survey lacks individual job-protection measures sufficient to test the mediation. The book itself marks the explanation as partial.

For Polish managers, non-party managers are exceptionally distressed; the authors infer greater uncertainty/risk in the centralized planning system, but the cell is small and the finding is not definitive.

**M7 disposition:** structural distress reversals are not portable outcomes of `class` or `stratification`. They are `UNEXPLAINED_RESIDUAL` except where concrete job-security/authority/institutional mechanisms are actually evidenced, and even there Polish mediation remains only partially tested.

## 13. Family and linked-life mechanisms

The family chapter extends the structure-person chain intergenerationally (ch. 7, pp. 171-201):

```text
FAMILY CLASS / STRATIFICATION
-> PARENTS' OCCUPATIONAL SELF-DIRECTION
-> PARENTS' VALUES
-> OFFSPRING VALUES
```

The chain is found in both countries. But the parent carrying the transmission differs:

- US: fathers play at least as large a role as mothers.
- Poland: mothers play the predominant role; fathers' values do not significantly carry into children's values in the final model.

The authors tentatively connect this to historically rooted division of family labor, rural-to-urban cultural inheritance, church/state reinforcement of maternal socialization responsibility, and father roles emphasizing control/punishment (ch. 8, pp. 207-8).

Again, this is `SAME MECHANISM FAMILY, DIFFERENT COUNTERPART/ROLE CONFIGURATION`, not `COUNTRY -> CHILD VALUE`.

The chapter also exposes a subject-mediation ceiling: the data show parent-value effects even where children's accurate perception of fathers' values is not necessary, but they do not observe the intra-family microprocess that carries the influence. Environmental structuring is suggested, not demonstrated.

## 14. Non-work structural conditions

The source contains three levels of non-work evidence.

### Direct family evidence

Strong enough to retain: parental values and family social-structural position shape offspring values, with system-specific father/mother pathways.

### US educational self-direction evidence

Adolescent/young-adult educational self-direction, especially substantive complexity of schoolwork and freedom from close supervision, predicts valuation of self-direction (chs. 7 and 9). This supports a broader `STRUCTURAL IMPERATIVE -> SELF-DIRECTED ACTIVITY -> OUTCOME` family, but the direct comparative Polish test is absent because the Polish data lack the necessary schoolwork-complexity measure.

### US housework evidence summarized in ch. 9

For women, substantively complex housework has effects analogous to complex paid work. The effect depends on whether the activity functions as an actual structural imperative. For men, the same housework measures do not operate identically. This is relevant to portability: an activity form is not portable merely because its surface label is the same; obligation/demand structure matters.

### Unmeasured possibilities

Family structure, religion, rural origin and national culture are explicitly considered as possible contributors to distress differences but cannot be tested with the available data (ch. 8, p. 228). They remain `EVIDENCE_INCONCLUSIVE`, not mechanisms.

## 15. Temporality and developmental ceiling

The book is stronger than a simple cross-sectional comparison but materially weaker than a full developmental event-history design.

- US men: two-wave panel, 1964-1974.
- Poland: cross-sectional 1978 baseline plus retrospective prior-job information and simulated earlier psychological variables.
- Families: US partly longitudinal; Polish father data precede mother/child data by 1.5-2 years but are often modeled as contemporaneous.
- Cohort comparisons test stability across historically distinct cohorts but cannot cleanly separate age, cohort, career and life-course.

The authors explicitly state that a two-wave ten-year design cannot establish when effects begin, whether effects are continuous/discontinuous, taper, accumulate, oscillate, or depend on thresholds (ch. 9, pp. 245-47). They also concede that they do not model career sequences adequately (pp. 250-51).

Therefore M7 must not infer:

```text
BETWEEN-PERSON DIFFERENCE
-> WITHIN-PERSON DEVELOPMENT
```

or

```text
TWO-WAVE NET EFFECT
-> KNOWN MICRO-TIMING / CUMULATIVE PATH
```

## 16. Subject mediation: what this tradition earns and what it does not

### What it earns

- Objective/relational work conditions matter.
- Recurrent task structure can causally affect values, cognition and orientations.
- Psychological functioning can in turn affect later job conditions/selection.
- Parent values can affect offspring values.
- Same structural construct can have system-specific consequences because institutions alter the concrete conditions attached to it.

### What it does not earn

- no direct observation of moment-to-moment uptake;
- no adequate specification of the microprocess by which job experience is learned and generalized;
- no general measure of actor meaning or motive;
- no behavioral outcome measure beyond interview behavior;
- no proof that actors correctly perceive objective conditions;
- no proof that a structural condition necessarily becomes a motive, belief, practice or durable capacity;
- no direct evidence of actor action changing institutional rules or collective structure.

Chapter 9 explicitly identifies the microdynamics of learning/generalization as an unresolved gap and suggests observational, experimental or more frequent longitudinal designs would be required.

## 17. External comparative evidence discussed by the book

The book reviews studies from multiple industrialized settings and treats the Japanese study as the strongest third-country replication. Japan supports class/stratification comparability, occupational self-direction -> values/intellectual flexibility/self-directedness, and mediation by occupational self-direction. The book also notes weaker/fragmentary support in Italy, Canada, Ireland, Germany, Norway and elsewhere (ch. 9, pp. 251-55).

The authors explicitly do **not** claim the theory for nonindustrialized/partially industrialized societies; Taiwan/Peru evidence is discussed as negative or methodologically uncertain.

Thus the source's own portability scope is bounded roughly to industrialized institutional formations, not humanity in general.

## 18. Full-book source verdict

The source strongly supports a portable **form**:

```text
SYSTEM / INSTITUTIONAL CONFIGURATION
-> ACTOR'S RELATIONAL POSITION
-> CONCRETE, RECURRENT CONDITION OF LIFE / ACTIVITY
-> OUTCOME
```

It rejects portable category shortcuts. What generalizes most strongly is not the meaning of `class`, `manager`, `manual worker`, `higher status`, or even the psychological effect of every dimension of self-direction. What generalizes is the requirement to derive concrete actor-relative conditions from institutionally situated relations and then test what recurrent experience does.

The source therefore strengthens D1, partially strengthens the recurrence/interval half of D2, and does **not** independently validate D3's strong reciprocal social-world-update semantics.
