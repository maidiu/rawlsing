## 2. Negative ceilings from Chapters 1 and 9 — method

### NC-01 — no ahistorical naturalization of the social world

**Source:** Ch. 1 pp. 41 ff; Ch. 5 pp. 193–195.

Traditional categories that represent socially produced life conditions as a natural “environment” erase the constitutive societal mediation.

**Rawlsing ceiling:** do not represent historically/socially generated constraints as generic environment stimuli when the producing relation matters.

### NC-02 — no condition→reaction short circuit

**Source:** Ch. 9 pp. 522–533.

Holzkamp criticizes the variable model for setting a short-circuit relation between conditions and activities/reactions and thereby eliminating human subject mediation.

**Rawlsing ceiling:** association, exposure or condition is not a sufficient action mechanism.

### NC-03 — no universal rejection of condition/statistical models either

**Source:** Ch. 9 pp. 568–573.

Where factual constraints lie outside current disposal/control, conditionedness models may describe bounded surface relations.

**Rawlsing ceiling:** M4 does not invalidate PRE-V3 statistical/structural evidence; it only blocks treating such evidence as the full person-level causal explanation.

### NC-04 — category != empirical effect law

**Source:** Ch. 1; Ch. 8 pp. 417–425; Ch. 9 pp. 509 ff.

Categorial definitions open an object for investigation; they do not automatically prove a current-empirical regularity.

**Rawlsing ceiling:** do not compile a philosophical/categorial category into a deterministic runtime transition.

## 3. Negative ceilings on `Bedeutung`, possibility and premise

### NC-05 — `Bedeutung` is not subjective meaning

**Source:** Ch. 6 pp. 229–238; Ch. 7 p. 359 note.

Objective societal meanings are action/thought possibilities. Their subjective realization is a later step.

### NC-06 — action possibility is not realized experience

**Source:** Ch. 6 pp. 233–238.

The actor may not use a possibility or may act differently.

### NC-07 — actor-relative objective applicability is not `subjektiver Möglichkeitsraum`

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 367–369.

The subjective possibility space depends on situational-personal phenomenal givenness and biography, not directly on objective position.

### NC-08 — PRE-V3 applicability is not `Prämisse`

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 352–353, 367–369.

External/personal conditions are premises only in their subject-relative phenomenal form inside a reason nexus.

### NC-09 — premise does not require explicit propositional articulation

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 351–353.

Premises may be hidden/unknown to the interpreter; phenomenal givenness includes situation, capabilities, temporal orientation and emotional evaluation.

**Rawlsing ceiling:** do not reduce premise status to a boolean `ACTOR_KNOWS_PROPOSITION`.

### NC-10 — objective opportunity not encountered or not accessible remains objective possibility only

**Source:** action possibility logic throughout Ch. 6–7.

**Rawlsing ceiling:** opportunity must not be counted as subject experience merely because structure permits it.

## 4. Negative ceilings on reasons, motivation and action

### NC-11 — `Bedingtheit != Begründetheit`

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 344–356.

External conditions and subjective grounds are distinct explanatory relations.

### NC-12 — ground/reason is not causal compulsion

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 348–355.

A reason makes an action intelligible for the subject under premises; it does not mechanically cause the action.

### NC-13 — subjective functionality is not objective correctness

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 349–350.

A consciously grounded action can conflict with objective life interests while remaining subjectively functional relative to how interests/situation are experienced.

### NC-14 — objective interest is not motive

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 315–324.

A societal goal can objectively serve an interest without being adequately represented or grasped and without being adopted.

### NC-15 — recognized goal is not adopted goal

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 321–323.

The subject can refuse even a recognized goal.

### NC-16 — coercion is not motivation

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 322–324.

Action may occur under coercion to avoid immediate threat without motivated agreement with the goal.

### NC-17 — action is not successful effect

**Source:** reciprocal/collective passages Ch. 5–7.

Other actors, institutions, material resources and power relations mediate outcomes.

### NC-18 — effect is not development

**Source:** Ch. 8.

A realized event/effect needs historical/developmental preservation to become durable change.

## 5. Negative ceilings on structural determinism and voluntarism

### NC-19 — objective conditions never directly determine the full subject action relation

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 344–345, 367–369.

Even precise societal description does not by itself yield concrete action/condition of the subject.

### NC-20 — subject freedom is not arbitrary voluntarism

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 349, 353–355.

The dimensions and extent of options are objectively historically conditioned.

### NC-21 — “second possibility never zero” is not an executable runtime guarantee

**Source:** p. 355.

Holzkamp’s claim is categorial/philosophical about irreducible human subjectivity.

**Rawlsing ceiling:** do not force every actor/timepoint to have an explicitly enumerable, materially realizable alternative route because of this passage.

## 6. Negative ceilings on restrictive/generalized action-capacity

### NC-22 — restrictive/generalized action-capacity is not a personality typology

**Source:** Ch. 7 p. 370 ff.

The alternative characterizes action in concrete situations, not persons.

### NC-23 — not a class/group typology

No class, race, gender, political group or demographic category is licensed to inherit a default restrictive/generalized psychological status.

### NC-24 — not a moral score

Restrictive action is analyzed as subjectively grounded under concrete dependency/threat, not as a moral defect.

### NC-25 — not an ideology classifier

A person’s political vocabulary does not by itself establish which action relation is being realized.

### NC-26 — `Deuten/Begreifen` are not fixed cognitive levels

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 383–402.

They are directionally related modes within situated action-capacity, not IQ bands or permanent developmental stages.

## 7. Negative ceilings on needs/control

### NC-27 — no generic need-to-produce

**Source:** Ch. 6 pp. 242–243.

`produktive Bedürfnisse` does not mean a freestanding need to be productive, grow or self-actualize.

### NC-28 — no generic control need

**Source:** Ch. 6 pp. 242–243.

The earlier reconstructed `Kontrollbedarf` is transformed at the societal-human level; participation in control over life conditions is a quality of human need satisfaction, not a scalar personal control drive.

### NC-29 — Handlungsfähigkeit is not an internal agency quantity

It is relational, position-sensitive and tied to practical control over conditions.

## 8. Negative ceilings on collectivity and reciprocity

### NC-30 — no collective mind

**Source:** Ch. 6 p. 238.

Collective subjectivity must not be hypostatized as a supra-individual psychological entity.

### NC-31 — shared condition != collective organization

Common interests/constraints do not automatically form coordination, organization or counter-power.

### NC-32 — collective organization != successful structural effect

Holzkamp gives no universal success law for association.

### NC-33 — person/action cannot directly rewrite structure

Source reciprocity is mediated by realized social action/relations. Rawlsing D3 must require external effect and causal admission.

### NC-34 — intention != world update

Desired change is not evidence that a rule, resource, relation or institution changed.

### NC-35 — provenance-preserving update is a Rawlsing discipline, not a Holzkamp concept

Do not attribute the technical transaction protocol itself to the source.

## 9. Negative ceilings on history and development

### NC-36 — phylogenetic reconstruction != modern social-causal execution

**Source:** Ch. 2–5.

Natural-historical origin arguments are not present-day actor update rules.

### NC-37 — `gesellschaftliche Natur` != a psychological essence of a demographic group

The phrase concerns species-level historically formed human capacities and social mediation, not fixed personality content.

### NC-38 — ontogenetic process type != universal age stage

**Source:** Ch. 8 pp. 417–425.

Holzkamp explicitly rejects biologically preprogrammed human developmental phase sequences.

### NC-39 — developmental-logical sequence != observed chronology

Exact timings/chronologies belong to current empirical research.

### NC-40 — institution entry != automatic psychological outcome

**Source:** Ch. 8 pp. 473–493.

Institutions change meaning/action environments; their actual developmental effects require empirical analysis and subject mediation.

### NC-41 — biography != cumulative scalar

Prior realizations/nonrealizations shape later possibilities, but the book provides no generic one-dimensional accumulation score.

### NC-42 — development may include stagnation/regression/reinterpretation

**Source:** Ch. 8 pp. 420–421.

Do not model development as monotonic progress by default.

## 12. Positive implication of the negative method

These ceilings do not leave Rawlsing without a positive account. They force the positive chain to remain explicit:

```text
OBJECTIVE STRUCTURE
-> TYPED ACTOR-RELATIVE OBJECTIVE POSSIBILITY
-> SUBJECTIVE ACCESS / PHENOMENAL GIVenness
-> PREMISE / REASON
-> ACTION
-> EXTERNAL EFFECT
-> UPDATED SOCIAL FACT
-> LATER DEVELOPMENT ONLY THROUGH HISTORY
```

That grammar preserves objective constraint **and** subject mediation without making either structurally deterministic or voluntarist.
