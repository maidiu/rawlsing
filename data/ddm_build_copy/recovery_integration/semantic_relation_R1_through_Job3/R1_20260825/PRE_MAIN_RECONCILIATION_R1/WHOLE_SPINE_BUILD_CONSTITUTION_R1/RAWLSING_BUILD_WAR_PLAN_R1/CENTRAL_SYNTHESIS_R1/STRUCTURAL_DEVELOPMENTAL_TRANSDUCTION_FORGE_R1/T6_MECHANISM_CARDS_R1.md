## Card T6-M01 — FOCUS-ORGANIZED COUNTERPART ECOLOGY

**SOURCE:** Scott L. Feld, “The Focused Organization of Social Ties,” *AJS* 86(5), 1981, pp. 1015–1035; especially p. 1016, p. 1021, p. 1026.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical mechanism with observational illustration.  
**GAP SOLVED:** Rawlsing Phase 0 contains interaction-topology possibility and contact/encounter realization, but does not yet prove how organized settings/activities positively generate the actor’s likely counterpart set.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** membership, assignment, residence, institution, activity setting, or other extra-network “focus” organizing joint activity.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** shared foci organize recurrent joint activity and interaction; repeated co-presence creates conditions for tie formation and clustered relations.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** a delimited counterpart set; altered contact frequency; recurring activity partners; access to some people and practical isolation from others; possible future tie opportunities.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** a focus does not itself create a relationship. The actor must actually participate/co-occur/interact sufficiently for a tie or repeated relation to be realized.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** actors can pursue, avoid, deepen, weaken, or reorganize ties within the opportunity field; already-existing ties can also generate new shared foci.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** only downstream if realized relations/activities are admitted and metabolized; potentially relationship history, practice, information exposure, support, expectation, memory.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** CONDITION + RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE + RELATION; can alter TRAJECTORY through repeated setting membership.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** group / organization / neighborhood / institution / network.  
**RECIPROCITY:** yes; formed ties can create new foci and alter later activity organization.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** actual participation, schedule overlap, size/composition of focus, competing foci, institutional sorting, mobility, actor choice.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `SHARED_SETTING != TIE`; `POSSIBLE_COUNTERPART != ENCOUNTER`; `ENCOUNTER != UPTAKE`; no direct setting→trait inference.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 interaction-topology possibility + structural affordance/applicability can represent the eligible setting/topology; actor/context binding and contact/encounter/OEE can carry realization. Missing connective function candidate: `FOCUS_ORGANIZED_ACTIVITY_TO_ACTOR_COUNTERPART_SET_DERIVATION`. Exact V3 sub-owner remains `NAMED_IN_CAMPAIGN__R_LANE_TO_VERIFY`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` as a positive connective function; do not create a theory-name object.

## Card T6-M02 — BRIDGING-TIE INFORMATION / MOBILITY ACCESS

**SOURCE:** Mark S. Granovetter, “The Strength of Weak Ties,” *AJS* 78(6), 1973, pp. 1360–1380; bridge caveat p. 1364; different-information argument p. 1371; mobility opportunities around p. 1373.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + empirical illustration.  
**GAP SOLVED:** topology alone does not explain why some realized ties expose an actor to information/opportunities unavailable in a dense local circle.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** network segmentation plus an actor tie spanning otherwise weakly connected circles.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** bridging connection links the actor to a nonredundant social circle and therefore to different information/influence channels.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** earlier/different vacancy information, referrals, knowledge, contacts, and mobility opportunities; broadened counterpart/information ecology.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** the tie must actually function as a bridge, information must traverse it, and the actor must receive/use it before biography changes.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** attention, trust, interpretation, pursuit of the lead, and willingness/ability to act.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** principally information exposure and later opportunity/trajectory; psychological development only through realized downstream histories.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** RELATION + EVENT (information transfer) + possible TRANSITION/TRAJECTORY if opportunity is taken.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** dyad embedded in network/community/organization.  
**RECIPROCITY:** yes; mobility and new ties alter later network position.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** weak ties are not automatically bridges; information value and institutional eligibility vary; homophily and segregation can eliminate bridge function.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `WEAK_TIE != BRIDGE`; `BRIDGE != INFORMATION_TRANSFER`; `INFORMATION != OPPORTUNITY_TAKEN`; no direct network-position→personality claim.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 mediation-route and interaction-topology possibility can host possible channels; actor contact/encounter realizes the tie/event; opportunity/constraint can host discovered option. Missing connective function candidate: `NETWORK_BRIDGE_TO_NONREDUNDANT_INFORMATION_OPPORTUNITY_ECOLOGY`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE` existing topology/route/opportunity machinery with bridge-conditioned information semantics.

## Card T6-M03 — BROKERAGE / STRUCTURAL-HOLE INFORMATION ARBITRAGE

**SOURCE:** Ronald S. Burt, “Structural Holes and Good Ideas,” *AJS* 110(2), 2004, pp. 349–399; mechanism pp. 353–355.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** organizational observational study + theory.  
**GAP SOLVED:** Granovetter identifies bridging; Burt sharpens what the actor can do with cross-group position: compare, translate, transfer, analogize, and synthesize heterogeneous information.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** actor network position spanning separated groups with heterogeneous knowledge/practice.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** brokerage supplies early/broad access plus translation and information arbitrage across groups.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** visibility of otherwise unseen options; access to heterogeneous practices; chance to transfer or synthesize information; differentiated evaluation/promotion opportunities in the studied organization.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** network position is context only; the actor must actually communicate, translate, propose, coordinate, or otherwise act across the gap.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** skill, judgment, motive, interpretation, credibility, strategic action.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** knowledge/practice exposure, competence through recurrent brokerage, expectations, relationship history; durable change is downstream and contingent.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** RELATION + RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE + EVENT; potentially cumulative career TRAJECTORY.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** network / organization / intergroup.  
**RECIPROCITY:** strong; successful brokerage can alter network structure and organizational coordination.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** opportunity to broker, heterogeneous groups, incentive/permission to coordinate, credibility, information quality, organizational hierarchy.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** Burt explicitly warns that networks do not act; `BROKERAGE_POSITION != BROKERAGE_ACTION != GOOD_OUTCOME`.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** topology + mediation-route possibility can encode cross-group location; actor activation/contact can encode actual cross-boundary action. Candidate missing semantics: `BROKER_POSITION_TO_ACTOR_INFORMATION_TRANSLATION_ACTION_OPPORTUNITY`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE` rather than add a new module.

## Card T6-M04 — CLOSURE-CONDITIONED OBLIGATION / SANCTION / INFORMATION SUPPORT

**SOURCE:** James S. Coleman, “Social Capital in the Creation of Human Capital,” *AJS* 94 Suppl., 1988, pp. S95–S120; information channels p. S104; norms/effective sanctions pp. S104–S105; closure p. S107; appropriable organization p. S108.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** theory + observational application.  
**GAP SOLVED:** a network can alter not only who is connected but also whether obligations are enforceable, information circulates, norms are sanctionable, and organized collective support is available.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** closed/interconnected network, repeated relations, reputational visibility, pre-existing organization.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** closure raises observability and collective sanction capacity; repeated relations create obligations/expectations; ties act as information channels; organizations can be repurposed for collective support.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** concrete debts/obligations, access to information, support, enforcement pressure, sanctions, trust-relevant expectations, collective capacity.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** closure only makes sanction/support/obligation mechanisms feasible; particular actors must owe, know, enforce, communicate, organize or receive support in realized relations.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** compliance, resistance, reciprocation, free-riding, organizing, selective trust, refusal.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** relationship expectations, recurrent practices, support/strain histories, knowledge exposure, mastery-relevant conditions only via realized history.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** CONDITION + RELATION + RECURRENT PRACTICE + CUMULATIVE PROCESS.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** household / peer network / community / organization.  
**RECIPROCITY:** yes; actors maintain, violate, repair, repurpose or exit relations/organizations.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** network closure can support beneficial or coercive norms; sanctions depend on observability/power; obligations may be asymmetric.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** no scalar `SOCIAL_CAPITAL -> DEVELOPMENT`; closure can constrain as well as support; information availability is not uptake.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** structural topology/affordance can encode possible closure and support routes; opportunity/constraint and realized relationship/contact machinery can carry actor-specific obligations/support. Candidate missing semantics: `NETWORK_CONFIGURATION_TO_OBLIGATION_SANCTION_SUPPORT_ECOLOGY`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`; generic social-capital stock `REJECT`.

## Card T6-M05 — ROLE-SET EXPECTATION / SANCTION ECOLOGY

**SOURCE:** Robert K. Merton, “The Role-Set: Problems in Sociological Theory,” *BJS* 8(2), 1957, pp. 106–120; expectation conflict and autonomy mechanisms around pp. 113–117; recap pp. 117–118.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** theory.  
**GAP SOLVED:** “role” is otherwise easily overcompressed into a direct behavioral or personality prior. Merton supplies a relational decomposition into multiple counterpart-specific expectations and powers.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** occupancy of a social status embedded in an organized role-set.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** different members of the role-set hold expectations backed by unequal authority, observability and sanctions; conflicting expectations can create room for autonomy or organized coping.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** obligations, incompatible demands, monitoring, sanctions, insulation, peer support, bargaining space, ability to abridge some relations.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** status occupancy must place the actor in concrete relations; expectations must be communicated/enforced/anticipated through real interaction before they alter biography.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** interpretation, prioritization, compliance, resistance, concealment, coalition, exit.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** recurrent practice, tension, expectation, relationship history, coping/mediator formation only if repeated and metabolized.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** RELATION + CONDITION + RECURRENT PRACTICE; possible TURNING POINT on role entry/exit.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** role-set / organization / institution.  
**RECIPROCITY:** yes; actors can negotiate expectations, organize peers, abridge relations, or change role arrangements.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** sanctioning power, observability, competing expectations, exit costs, organizational support.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `STATUS != ROLE_BEHAVIOR`; `ROLE_EXPECTATION != COMPLIANCE`; `ROLE != TRAIT`.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 structural relation/topology can host status-linked possible counterpart relations; actor-specific opportunity/constraint/relationship/event machinery can host realized expectation/sanction episodes. Candidate missing semantics: `STATUS_TO_COUNTERPART_SPECIFIC_EXPECTATION_OBLIGATION_SANCTION_SET`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` connective relation grammar candidate.

## Card T6-M06 — CLASSIFICATION-CUED INSTITUTIONAL GATE TREATMENT

**SOURCE:** Marianne Bertrand & Sendhil Mullainathan, *AER* 94(4), 2004, pp. 991–1013, DOI `10.1257/0002828042002561`.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** randomized field experiment.  
**GAP SOLVED:** structural classification/exclusion must be realized through an institutional decision mechanism rather than encoded as direct actor deficit.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** institutional hiring gate plus application materials carrying a randomized name cue associated by employers with race.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** gatekeeper receives/classifies the application cue and differentially advances otherwise similar résumés.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** altered probability of interview callback; differential access to employment opportunity despite equivalent résumé content.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** cue must be present, recognized/used in a gate decision, and a callback/no-callback outcome must occur. Race category alone is not the realized treatment.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** on the applicant side, later pursuit remains open; the studied gate outcome is produced by employer decision rather than applicant psychology.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** none directly. Possible downstream economic/activity/trajectory effects require realized job-search histories and V3 processing.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** EVENT at each gate; recurrent applications can form a CUMULATIVE PROCESS / TRAJECTORY.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** organization / labor market institution.  
**RECIPROCITY:** limited within single gate; actors can alter search strategy, credentials, networks, organizations or collective rules later.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** study occupations/cities/time; cue interpretation; employer decision process; labor-market context.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `RACE_CLASSIFICATION != TRAIT`; `CLASSIFICATION != DISCRIMINATION_EVENT`; only realized differential treatment at a gate changes opportunity.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** positioned-subject empirical possibility may represent population evidence; structural affordance/applicability can encode institutional eligibility/gate possibility; contact/event realization can encode application/decision; opportunity/constraint can encode callback access. Candidate missing semantics: `CLASSIFICATION_OR_CUE_TO_REALIZED_GATEKEEPER_TREATMENT`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` positive institutional-treatment connective function candidate.

## Card T6-M07 — INSTITUTION-PRODUCED MARK → LATER GATE PENALTY

**SOURCE:** Devah Pager, “The Mark of a Criminal Record,” *AJS* 108(5), 2003, pp. 937–975, DOI `10.1086/374403`; design p. 937; results/figures in empirical section including material around p. 957.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** matched field audit experiment.  
**GAP SOLVED:** one institution can generate a durable record/classification that conditions treatment by another institution, producing path-dependent opportunity loss.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** prior criminal-justice institutional history represented as criminal-record status plus race and hiring context.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** employer gatekeeper treats record status as a negative hiring signal; race conditions the realized effect.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** reduced callback/employment opportunity; compounded institutional exclusion across sequential gates.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** the prior record must be carried into/known at the later gate and used in decision; institutional history is not itself the later employment outcome.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** applicant behavior can matter downstream, but the audit design isolates a gate treatment mechanism at the first stage.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** no direct psychological target; downstream activity, income, strain, network and biography consequences only through repeated realized outcomes.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** TRANSITION/HISTORY STATE → EVENT → possible cumulative TRAJECTORY.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** inter-institutional sequence (criminal justice → labor market organization).  
**RECIPROCITY:** later action can alter exposure but prior record creates path dependence; institutional policy can change the gate rule.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** legal disclosure regimes, employer knowledge, labor market, race, study population and historical context.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `RECORD != ESSENCE`; `PRIOR_INSTITUTIONAL_HISTORY != GUARANTEED_EXCLUSION`; no criminal-record→trait shortcut.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 can represent structural/empirical applicability of a record within a gate; actor history/V3 carries prior realized institutional history; opportunity/constraint/contact machinery can carry later gate realization. Candidate connective function: `PRIOR_INSTITUTIONAL_MARK_TO_LATER_GATE_APPLICABILITY_AND_TREATMENT`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE` gate-treatment semantics with explicit path-dependent institutional mark.

## Card T6-M08 — STATUS EXPECTATION → EVALUATION / SANCTION AT GATE

**SOURCE:** Shelley J. Correll, Stephen Benard & In Paik, “Getting a Job: Is There a Motherhood Penalty?” *AJS* 112(5), 2007, pp. 1297–1338, DOI `10.1086/511799`.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** laboratory experiment + field audit.  
**GAP SOLVED:** shows a role/status classification can operate through evaluator expectations and concrete gate outcomes rather than through assumed trait differences.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** hiring/evaluation setting plus parenthood-status cue and gendered status expectations.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** evaluators infer differential competence/commitment and translate those judgments into salary/hiring recommendations; actual employers also produce differential callbacks.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** lower evaluation, lower recommended pay, lower hiring access for mothers in the studied settings.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** classification cue must be perceived, expectation invoked, evaluation made, and gate consequence issued.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** applicant psychology is not required for the gate effect; applicant response begins after treatment.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** only downstream via realized employment/strain/activity histories.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** EVENT; repeated gates can accumulate into TRAJECTORY/CUMULATIVE PROCESS.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** organization / labor market / role-status relation.  
**RECIPROCITY:** policies, evaluator practices, collective action and actor search strategy can alter future gates.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** gender/parenthood norms, evaluator context, occupation, time/place.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `PARENTHOOD_STATUS != COMPETENCE`; evaluator inference is a social mechanism, not truth about the actor.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** same gate carrier family as T6-M06; adds expectation-mediated evaluator interpretation between classification and decision. Candidate missing connective function: `ROLE_STATUS_CUE_TO_EVALUATOR_EXPECTATION_TO_GATE_TREATMENT`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`; evidence strengthens M06 rather than licensing a motherhood-specific object.

## Card T6-M09 — CROSS-SETTING / EXOSYSTEM INDIRECT CAUSAL CHAIN

**SOURCE:** Urie Bronfenbrenner, *The Ecology of Human Development* (1979), Ch. 9 pp. 209–236; Ch. 10 pp. 237–257; Ch. 11 pp. 258–292. Ch. 10 explicitly requires external-setting event → process in actor’s microsystem → developmental change. Corroborating review: Bronfenbrenner 1986, *Developmental Psychology* 22(6):723–742.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + review.  
**GAP SOLVED:** Phase-0 bookends permit structural world and actor history, but a setting that does not contain the actor can still alter the actor’s ecology indirectly through linked settings/people.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** event/condition in an external setting connected to an actor’s immediate setting through another person, institution, schedule, policy or resource flow.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** cross-setting linkage changes the immediate microsystem’s activities, resources, relations, schedules or interaction patterns; only then can development be affected.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** changed caregiver availability, work schedule, school-family coordination, neighborhood access, peer/contact ecology, resources or recurrent activity.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** at least two causal links must be realized: external setting → immediate actor ecology, and immediate ecology → actor experience/development. A macrosystem label alone does nothing.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** actor and linked others interpret/respond within the changed microsystem; reciprocal effects can travel back across settings.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** depends on downstream realized practices/relations/events; may include relationship, memory, skill, expectation, affective tendency only after V3 metabolism.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** EVENT / CONDITION / TRANSITION / TRAJECTORY depending on the linkage; chronosystem adds timing/history but not automatic effect.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** household ↔ organization ↔ institution ↔ neighborhood/community.  
**RECIPROCITY:** yes; activity in one setting can alter linked settings.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** actual linkage strength, timing, duration, intermediary actor, institutional policy, family/setting organization.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `EXOSYSTEM != EXPOSURE`; `MACROSYSTEM != ACTOR_STATE`; ecological labels are not mechanisms.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 structural possibility + mediation-route possibility can represent possible cross-setting routes; actor/context binding plus realized condition/contact/event carriers can realize the route. Missing connective function candidate: `CROSS_SETTING_DEPENDENCY_PROPAGATION_TO_ACTOR_RELATIVE_ECOLOGY`.  
**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` / `GENERALIZE` candidate depending lane-R owner sufficiency.

## Card T6-M10 — TEMPORALLY EMBEDDED AGENCY UNDER CONSTRAINT

**SOURCE:** Mustafa Emirbayer & Ann Mische, “What Is Agency?” *AJS* 103(4), 1998, pp. 962–1023; triad pp. 970–972.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** theory.  
**GAP SOLVED:** upstream social ecology must not mechanically determine action; actor action can be oriented differently by past routines, projected futures, and present contingencies.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** current relational/institutional context plus actor’s prior patterns and available future possibilities.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** concrete action contains varying mixtures of iteration (selective reactivation of past patterns), projection (possible future trajectories), and practical evaluation (judgment under present contingencies).  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** different action selection among structurally available possibilities; maintenance, avoidance, resistance, improvisation, alliance, exit, or pursuit.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** agency is exercised only in actual action/choice within a context; a capacity label does not itself transform conditions.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** this card is the subject-mediation grammar itself.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** action/practice history can later alter expectations, mediators, relationships, memory and paths if repeated and metabolized.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** EVENT + RECURRENT PRACTICE + TRANSITION/TURNING POINT depending on action sequence.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** dyad through institution/collective action.  
**RECIPROCITY:** central; action may reproduce or redirect later relations and possibilities.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** available alternatives, resources, knowledge, uncertainty, sanctions, time pressure, prior routines.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `AGENCY != UNCONSTRAINED CHOICE`; analytic dimensions are not stable traits; projection does not guarantee achievable opportunity.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** V3-side needs/expectations/strivings, mediators/tensions/practices and actor response machinery are named campaign owners likely to host subject mediation; T6 does not credit exact sub-owner semantics without lane R. Candidate function is not a new object but a normalization rule: action selection must remain history-, future- and present-context-sensitive.  
**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE/GENERALIZE` existing V3 subject-mediation machinery; no new agency module.

## Card T6-M11 — RESOURCE-ENABLED SCHEMA TRANSPOSITION / STRUCTURAL RECIPROCITY

**SOURCE:** William H. Sewell Jr., “A Theory of Structure: Duality, Agency, and Transformation,” *AJS* 98(1), 1992, pp. 1–29; especially pp. 16–21, agency discussion p. 19.  
**CLAIM STATUS:** theory.  
**GAP SOLVED:** Rawlsing needs a non-voluntarist reciprocal path by which actors/collectives can sometimes alter the relations that conditioned them.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** multiple intersecting structures understood operationally here as uneven distributions of human/nonhuman resources plus learned/available schemas.  
**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** actors mobilize resources and transpose/reinterpret schemas across contexts; because resources are uneven and structures intersect, some actions can reproduce while others alter relations.  
**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** differential capacity to mobilize people/materials, reinterpret rules, coordinate collective action, challenge a gate, or create a new relation/opportunity.  
**REALIZATION LAW:** resource access and schema knowledge must be instantiated in actual mobilization/action; potential capacity alone is not structural transformation.  
**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** interpretation, strategic mobilization, coalition, innovation, resistance.  
**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** principally reciprocal environment/position change; downstream personal development only through the new realized history.  
**TEMPORAL FORM:** RECURRENT PRACTICE / TRANSITION / TURNING POINT / TRAJECTORY.  
**RELATIONAL SCALE:** network / organization / institution / collective / class relation.  
**RECIPROCITY:** explicit and central.  
**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** unequal resources, institutional sanctions, coalition availability, transposability of schemas, competing structures, historical conjuncture.  
**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `SCHEMA != TRAIT`; `RESOURCE != POWER_REALIZED`; agency is unequally enabled and not guaranteed to transform structure.  
**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 structural relations/affordances represent possible resource/constraint configurations; V3 action machinery can represent actor mobilization; later PRE-V3/V3 worlds would need lawful update through existing world/history authorities. Candidate connective function: `REALIZED_ACTOR_OR_COLLECTIVE_ACTION_TO_LATER_RELATION_POSITION_EXPOSURE_UPDATE`, subject to existing world-update jurisdiction.  
**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`; do not import Sewell’s full ontology.

## 12. Card-level terminal synthesis

The cards support a finite connective corpus rather than a generic sociology layer:

1. `ORGANIZED_ACTIVITY/SETTING -> COUNTERPART SET`
2. `NETWORK BRIDGE/BROKERAGE -> NONREDUNDANT INFORMATION/OPPORTUNITY`
3. `NETWORK CLOSURE -> OBLIGATION/SANCTION/SUPPORT FEASIBILITY`
4. `STATUS -> COUNTERPART-SPECIFIC EXPECTATION/SANCTION SET`
5. `CLASSIFICATION/CUE + INSTITUTIONAL GATE -> REALIZED DIFFERENTIAL TREATMENT`
6. `PRIOR INSTITUTIONAL HISTORY -> LATER GATE APPLICABILITY/TREATMENT`
7. `EXTERNAL SETTING -> LINKED IMMEDIATE ECOLOGY -> REALIZED ACTOR HISTORY`
8. `ACTOR HISTORY + POSSIBLE FUTURES + PRESENT CONTINGENCY -> ACTION SELECTION`
9. `RESOURCE/SCHEMA MOBILIZATION -> POSSIBLE RECIPROCAL RELATION CHANGE`

No card licenses structure→trait, demographic→personality, possibility→experience, or exposure→durable-change shortcuts.
