## 2. First major distinction: persistence is a family of temporal phenomena

A shallow system would represent all temporal carryover as:

`state[t+1] = state[t] * decay`

This collapses several different phenomena.

At minimum distinguish conceptually:

### 2.1 Continuation

The same live process remains active across time.

Example:

An unresolved argument remains consciously active into the next turn.

### 2.2 Inertia

A state tends to continue because the system does not instantly reorganize when the eliciting cue ends.

Example:

A remains angry after B stops provoking.

### 2.3 Residue

The focal state has weakened or left awareness, but some consequence remains capable of altering subsequent processing.

Example:

A no longer consciously feels embarrassed, but becomes unusually sensitive to further teasing.

### 2.4 Priming / readiness

A prior event increases the ease with which related material is recruited again.

Example:

After one ambiguous rejection cue, later ambiguous cues more readily recruit rejection-related organization.

### 2.5 Sensitization

Repeated or sufficiently strong recruitment increases responsiveness to subsequent related cues.

This is distinct from the original activation remaining continuously high.

### 2.6 Habituation / attenuation

Repeated exposure may reduce responsiveness under some conditions.

This is not the same as forgetting, suppression, or loss of durable significance.

### 2.7 Suppression maintenance

An activation remains causally relevant because another process continuously inhibits awareness or expression.

The hidden process may therefore persist as **control demand** rather than overt conscious content.

### 2.8 Rebound / return

A previously inhibited or reduced activation reappears after the suppressing condition weakens or a related trigger occurs.

### 2.9 Refractory / depleted response

A process may become temporarily harder to recruit after strong activation or regulation.

This is conceptually different from habituation and from durable change.

### 2.10 Accumulation

Several individually modest events leave residues that jointly alter a later transition.

### 2.11 Dormancy

A configuration is not currently active but remains conditionally reactivatable through already-lawful underlying history/organization.

PFF must be careful here: durable dormant organization itself may already be V3-owned. The PFF-specific phenomenon may instead be temporary lowered threshold/readiness around that organization.

### 2.12 Hysteresis / path dependence

The current state depends partly on the route by which the system arrived there, not only the current instantaneous input.

These distinctions are phenomenal requirements, not adopted state fields.

## 3. Activation persistence and altered transition propensity are non-equivalent

One of the most important distinctions is:

`SAME_STATE_CONTINUES`

versus

`STATE_ENDED_BUT NEXT_TRANSITION_LANDSCAPE CHANGED`.

Example A — continuation:

A remains consciously angry from turn 5 to turn 6.

Example B — changed propensity:

A has calmed, but after the argument a neutral remark from B is more readily interpreted as criticism.

The second case cannot be faithfully represented by simply retaining `anger = 0.4`.

The relevant carryover may concern:

- recruitment readiness;
- interpretive bias;
- attention allocation;
- defensive readiness;
- target salience;
- memory accessibility;
- expected consequence;
- threshold for re-entry into a prior configuration.

Therefore:

`PERSISTED_EFFECT != PERSISTED_CONTENT`.

This is likely central to psychologically realistic continuity.

## 4. Hysteresis: reversing the cue need not reverse the psyche

A system exhibits path dependence when the input needed to enter a state differs from the input needed to leave it, or when removing the initiating cue does not restore the earlier configuration.

PFF examples:

### 4.1 Trust/safety-like local opening

A initially requires repeated reassuring interaction before vulnerability becomes locally accessible.

Once open, one neutral turn does not immediately restore the prior guarded state.

### 4.2 Shame/exposure capture

A becomes highly self-conscious after believing B has seen through a defense.

Even after B changes topic, A remains guarded because the fact of believed exposure is now part of the live trajectory.

### 4.3 Escalation

A and B enter a conflict loop.

Returning wording to neutral may be insufficient because each participant now interprets neutral behavior through the escalated context.

### 4.4 Flirtation / playful mode

A playful dyadic mode may continue after the original initiating cue because each subsequent move is interpreted inside the already-established local mode.

### 4.5 Suppression failure

A hidden motive crosses into awareness after sustained pressure.

Removing one cue may not make the actor instantly unable to recognize what has now become conscious.

Therefore:

`ENTRY_CONDITION != EXIT_CONDITION = POSSIBLE`

`INPUT_REVERSAL != STATE_REVERSAL = MUST_FACTOR`

No exact hysteresis operator is adopted here.

## 5. Multiple temporal scopes must remain distinct

The zero-loss PFF vision already preserves several timescales. This examination sharpens them.

### 5.1 Intra-turn microdynamics

Sub-turn or single-decision unfolding:

- cue noticed;
- several readings compete;
- one structure recruits another;
- defense changes accessibility;
- expression pressure forms.

This may not require any persistence beyond the current computation.

### 5.2 Inter-turn live residue

The next turn begins from a live configuration altered by the prior turn rather than from a blank slate.

Examples:

- unresolved irritation;
- increased attraction salience;
- increased self-consciousness;
- active suspicion;
- lingering playfulness;
- defensive effort;
- recent metaperception.

### 5.3 Session-live continuity

A local interaction mode/configuration can remain relevant across many turns in the same session.

This does not become V3 relationship truth merely because it lasts an hour.

### 5.4 Relationship-episode continuity

A meaningful episode may span a break, interruption, or several related interactions.

Whether PFF requires a distinct lawful persistence class here remains unresolved.

### 5.5 Durable generated-person / relationship history

When an occurrence is causally admitted and produces durable consequence, V3 owns the durable result.

Examples include, subject to exact V3 authority:

- memory formation/change;
- durable expectation change;
- enduring relationship development;
- persistent self-model change;
- attractor reinforcement/destabilization;
- developmental middle-state change.

Hard law:

`LONG_PFF_DURATION != AUTOMATIC_V3_DURABILITY`.

## 6. Relationship-live state is still an unresolved semantic remainder

Prior chemistry work identified a possible need for pair/relationship-live continuity beyond one turn.

This examination applies the no-gadget test.

Before admitting a new `relationship_live_state`, attempt to reconstruct the needed continuity from:

1. current PFF actor-local residues;
2. lawful objective V3 relationship history/current relationship facts;
3. actor-local V3 memory/expectation/accessibility;
4. session transcript/provenance/context where noncausal continuity is sufficient;
5. currently active interaction trajectory derived from recent moves;
6. fresh re-recruitment on re-entry into the interaction.

Possible outcome A:

No new persistence class is needed. A recurring interaction can be regenerated from V3 history + actor-local organization + recent lawful context.

Possible outcome B:

A narrow intermediate class is required because a live relational configuration remains causally relevant across a bounded interruption but does not yet qualify as durable V3 change.

Current disposition:

`RELATIONSHIP_EPISODE_RESIDUE = PLAUSIBLE PHENOMENON`

`DEDICATED RELATIONSHIP_LIVE STORAGE = NOT ADMITTED`

The semantic remainder must be demonstrated before architecture.

## 7. Persistence can be target-specific

An actor may be calm globally while remaining highly sensitized around one counterpart/topic.

Examples:

- ordinary conversation resumes, but mention of one rival instantly reactivates conflict;
- general mood is good, but a specific criticism remains shame-charged;
- actor is relaxed with others but still guarded with B after a prior exposure;
- attraction toward B remains highly recruitable while unrelated attention is normal.

Therefore:

`GLOBAL_MOOD != TARGET_SPECIFIC_RESIDUE`

`SESSION_STATE != ALL_TARGETS_EQUAL`

Any future architecture must preserve target/scope binding.

## 8. Persistence can be domain-specific within one relationship

A and B may recover humor quickly while trust-related disclosure remains inhibited.

A conflict may be resolved practically while status resentment remains active.

Sexual/romantic charge may persist while emotional safety decreases.

Thus:

`RELATIONSHIP_RESIDUE != ONE_CLOSENESS_VALUE`.

This reinforces the chemistry conclusion that pair dynamics are mode/domain-specific.

## 9. Persistence can be hidden from consciousness

A causally important residue need not remain in the conscious field.

Example:

1. A experiences humiliation.
2. conscious distress subsides.
3. A sincerely believes the issue is over.
4. later B makes a similar joke.
5. irritation rises unusually fast.

The prior event may have altered readiness/recruitment without remaining consciously accessible as an active concern.

PFF must therefore allow:

`LIVE_RESIDUE != CURRENT_CONSCIOUS_CONTENT`.

At the same time, if the relevant long-term memory/accessibility change is already V3-owned, PFF must consume rather than duplicate it.

## 10. Conscious persistence and causal persistence can diverge

Four cases must remain possible:

### 10.1 Both persist

A remains consciously and causally angry.

### 10.2 Causal persistence without conscious persistence

A no longer feels consciously angry but continues interpreting B defensively.

### 10.3 Conscious persistence with changed causal basis

A remains preoccupied with an issue even though the original motive has shifted and the preoccupation is now maintained by shame, self-model threat, or anticipated consequence.

### 10.4 Neither persists but reactivation threshold is altered

The issue appears gone until a small related cue produces rapid re-entry.

Therefore:

`CONSCIOUS_DURATION != CAUSAL_DURATION != REACTIVATION_READINESS`.

## 11. Suppression/rebound must be represented as conditional, not universal

The defense examination identified rebound as a required possible phenomenon.

External research is useful but does not justify a universal law. Meta-analytic work has found rebound effects after thought suppression in some paradigms, while newer reviews emphasize that suppression can also be effective and adaptive depending on method, context, individual differences, and target material.

Rawlsing implication:

`SUPPRESSION -> REBOUND` is **possible**, not guaranteed.

PFF must preserve at least these alternatives:

- suppression succeeds with little later residue;
- suppression succeeds while requiring ongoing control;
- suppression reduces awareness but increases later accessibility;
- suppression fails under load;
- suppression causes compensatory strategy without thought rebound;
- suppression initially works but sensitizes related cues;
- suppression has no meaningful later effect.

No canonical suppression-decay/rebound formula is justified.

## 12. Affective inertia is not emotional stability

External affective-dynamics research distinguishes emotional inertia—the temporal persistence/dependency of emotional states—from broader stability or low variability. Recent reviews explicitly warn against conflating these constructs.

Rawlsing implication:

A person can:

- have generally stable affect but low inertia after specific events;
- be emotionally variable while individual states still show strong carryover;
- recover quickly from some domains and slowly from others;
- exhibit high relationship-specific inertia without globally persistent mood.

Thus:

`LOW_VARIABILITY != HIGH_INERTIA`

`HIGH_INERTIA != GENERALLY_STABLE_PERSONALITY`.

This reinforces the need for process-specific temporal semantics rather than one global `emotional_stability` quantity.

## 13. Sensitization and attractor reinforcement must not be collapsed

PFF may need temporary sensitization:

> after recent activation, a related cue more easily recruits the same organization.

V3 may own durable attractor reinforcement:

> repeated admitted lived history changes the person's longer-run developmental dynamics.

These are not the same.

`PFF_TEMPORARY_SENSITIZATION != V3_ATTRACTOR_REINFORCEMENT`

A useful architecture later must explain exactly when repeated temporary sensitization becomes evidence/input for durable V3 change, if at all, through lawful admission.

PFF cannot infer that threshold by narrative plausibility alone.

## 14. Accumulation need not be arithmetic accumulation

Repeated interactions may matter because they:

- repeatedly reactivate the same organization;
- reduce recovery time;
- broaden trigger classes;
- establish a predictive expectation;
- recruit additional defenses;
- change conscious interpretation;
- create a dyadic role pattern;
- eventually produce an admitted V3 event/effect.

This should not be reduced automatically to:

`residue += x`.

Accumulation can instead mean a changing **structure of readiness and relation among processes**.

## 15. Recovery is itself a dynamic process

Returning toward baseline may involve:

- spontaneous decay;
- corrective evidence;
- partner reassurance;
- successful repair;
- reappraisal;
- attention shift;
- restored self-model coherence;
- expression/discharge;
- fatigue/depletion;
- explicit boundary restoration;
- time without further recruitment.

Different routes may produce different later susceptibility.

Example:

A calms because B apologizes versus A calms because B leaves.

Observable activation may be equally low afterward, but the future relationship dynamics may differ.

Therefore:

`SAME_CURRENT_INTENSITY != SAME_HISTORY / SAME_NEXT_TRANSITION`.

## 16. Repair should be distinguished from simple dampening

A partner response may lower immediate activation without resolving the underlying relational meaning.

Examples:

- changing subject reduces conflict but leaves resentment;
- reassurance reduces anxiety but reinforces reassurance dependence;
- apology both reduces activation and revises interpretation;
- humor lowers tension while leaving status injury unresolved;
- silence ends escalation but increases perceived rejection.

Thus:

`DEESCALATION != REPAIR`

`LOWER_ACTIVATION != RESTORED_RELATIONSHIP_CONFIGURATION`.

This is important for dyadic chemistry and future synthetic-life tests.

## 17. Persistence can concern unresolved possibility, not just emotion

Some of the most human live residues are prospective:

- `did she mean that?`;
- `I think he might ask me out`;
- `I may have embarrassed myself`;
- `we still need to discuss that`;
- `I wonder whether she noticed`;
- `he did not answer the question`.

An unresolved social hypothesis can persist and shape later attention even without a stable emotion.

This links persistence directly to metaperception and anticipation.

## 18. Persistence can concern a control policy rather than content

After a difficult moment, the actor may adopt a temporary local policy:

- avoid this topic;
- monitor B's reactions closely;
- do not reveal more;
- stay playful;
- repair before changing subject;
- maintain formal distance;
- test whether B is still upset.

Such a policy may be consciously strategic, defensively generated, or mixed.

It is not necessarily a durable personality disposition.

This is another reason not to model persistence solely as lingering affect.

## 19. Self-output creates temporal continuity even before counterpart response

The actor's own expression becomes part of the next moment.

Examples:

- saying something vulnerable increases self-consciousness;
- making a cruel remark produces immediate regret or defensive doubling-down;
- accidentally revealing an interest changes what the actor believes B can now see;
- telling a story makes an associated memory more accessible;
- hearing oneself state a belief strengthens or destabilizes one's conscious self-interpretation.

Therefore:

`OUTPUT[t]` can be an input to `PFF[t+1]` even before B replies.

This strongly motivates the later self-output feedback examination.

## 20. Relationship breaks and session breaks must not be treated identically

A technical session boundary does not necessarily correspond to a psychic boundary.

Conversely, a resumed chat does not automatically justify restoring every prior live residue.

Future architecture must distinguish:

- application/session lifecycle;
- generated-world elapsed time;
- relationship episode continuity;
- whether an intervening V3 event occurred;
- whether prior live material remains relevant/accessible;
- whether the actor remembers/cares about the prior episode.

Hard negative:

`SESSION_ID_CONTINUITY != PSYCHIC_CONTINUITY`

and

`NEW_SESSION_ID != PSYCHIC_RESET`.

This is a major seam with V2/session infrastructure.

## 21. Generated-world time matters more than wall-clock time

PFF persistence must eventually be grounded in generated causal/interaction time, not hidden server delay.

A five-minute real-world pause may correspond to:

- no generated-world elapsed time;
- five generated minutes;
- several generated days;
- a resumed transcript with no causal-life continuity.

Therefore:

`WALL_CLOCK_GAP != GENERATED_PSYCHIC_ELAPSED_TIME`.

No decay/recovery law may silently use infrastructure time as person-time without explicit authority.

## 23. No universal baseline is justified

A system should not assume every actor returns to a fixed neutral value after enough turns.

Why:

- durable V3 personhood establishes actor-specific organization;
- relationship history establishes target-specific expectations;
- current situation may sustain pressure;
- some configurations settle into a new local equilibrium;
- some processes oscillate;
- some residues disappear;
- some convert into another form.

Therefore:

`DECAY_TOWARD_GLOBAL_ZERO = NOT A GENERAL PSYCHOLOGICAL LAW`.

- one universal decay coefficient;
- `mood *= 0.9` as general psyche persistence;
- session duration used as generated-person elapsed time;
- transcript recency used as memory/accessibility truth;
- every lingering state promoted to V3 memory;
- every repeated cue treated as attractor reinforcement;
- every suppression attempt assumed to rebound;
- every interaction assumed to begin from prior-session PFF state;
- new session assumed to reset generated psyche;
- one global relationship residue scalar;
- affective inertia treated as personality stability;
- low current activation treated as full recovery;
- simple de-escalation treated as repair;
- current calm treated as loss of sensitization;
- actor forgetting a topic treated as causal state deletion;
- hidden wall clock used to decay generated state;
- arbitrary fixed half-lives for motives, shame, attraction, fear, trust, or relationship charge.
