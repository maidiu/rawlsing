## 0. Purpose

This file deeply examines PFF frontier pocket 11, `self-model and autobiographical plasticity`, after prior work separated:

- durable V3 personhood;
- live PFF recruitment;
- dyadic/user-specific chemistry;
- metaperception / being seen;

- time / hysteresis;

The central question is:

> What does it mean for a generated person to possess a standing sense of `who I am / what my life is / what I want / what kind of person I am / what my past means / what future is mine`, and how can that organization be strained, contradicted, locally suspended, exposed, reinterpreted, compartmentalized, or durably reorganized without PFF becoming a second V3 biography engine?

`SELF_MODEL != BIOGRAPHY_SUMMARY`

`SELF_MODEL != MEMORY_SET`

## 1. Grounding against current V3 authority

### 1.1 CAN-011 developmental middle state

CAN-011 already owns actor-local persistent developmental middle-state records and deliberately preserves:

- persistent mediators;
- unresolved tensions;
- enacted practices;
- contradiction/multiplicity;
- path dependence;
- explicit lifecycle/update semantics;
- persistence without automatic reinforcement;
- no demographic/structural identity-to-psyche shortcut;
- no placeholder/filler psychology.

Its recovered/canonicalized logic explicitly prefers mediation, dynamic history, contradiction, revisability, and minimum sufficient machinery over static classification.

This is highly relevant to self-model because a standing self-understanding may be shaped by V3-owned interpretive mediators, tensions, practices, relationships, and history without requiring a new PFF identity object.

### 1.2 CAN-012 rupture/metabolization

CAN-012 separately owns actor-specific rupture candidate admission, immutable rupture history, persistent metabolization-process identity, reactivation/recurrence under exact causal parentage, and lawful transition receipts.

It explicitly does not own V2 phenomenology/narrative/expression and does not automatically convert an adverse event into every downstream psychological consequence.

Thus a breakup, humiliation, death, conversion, intoxication, illness, migration, parenthood, war, status collapse, or other rupture-like event may become causally important through V3 authority, while the **live experience of self-disorganization or reinterpretation** remains a distinct downstream/PFF question.

### 1.3 V3 epistemic and memory boundaries

Existing V3 authority already distinguishes:

`OBJECTIVE GENERATED FACT`
`!= V3 CAUSAL MEMORY`
`!= CURRENT MEMORY ACCESSIBILITY`
`!= GENERATED ACTOR EPISTEMIC STANCE`
`!= CURRENT CLAIM ACCESSIBILITY`
`!= FIRST_PERSON CLAIM PERMISSION`.

Therefore a self-model cannot simply be the system's objective biography rendered in first person.

The actor may know some self-relevant facts, believe others, be uncertain about others, lack access to some causal history, and hold mistaken interpretations.

### 1.4 Current ownership hypothesis

No new self-model owner is adopted here.

The current strongest hypothesis is that durable self-model content may be **distributed across already V3-owned structures**, including where lawfully present:

- actor epistemic stances about self;
- accessible autobiographical memory;
- persistent interpretive/orienting mediators;
- tensions;
- practices;
- roles/relationships/commitments;
- needs/expectations/strivings;
- future-directed plans/expectations;
- attractor organization and developmental history, although these are not necessarily actor-known.

PFF may then mediate the **current live relation** between this durable material and present experience.

Architecture must later test this distributed hypothesis before inventing any separate `SelfModelState`.

## 2. First major distinction: the person and the person's theory of themself

The total generated person includes causal material the actor does not know, cannot currently access, misunderstands, denies, or has never conceptualized.

The self-model is narrower:

> the actor's presently standing organization of self-relevant beliefs, interpretations, expectations, values, roles, narratives, commitments, and anticipated continuity insofar as they are actually actor-owned/available.

Therefore:

`DEEP PERSON D != STANDING SELF C`

The actor can be wrong about themselves without the machine being confused.

Examples:

- actor believes `I am not jealous` while jealousy causally shapes current behavior;
- actor believes `I do not need anyone` while attachment need is strongly active;
- actor believes `I am a calm person` while current V3/PFF history shows repeated anger that they explain away;
- actor believes `I am bad at intimacy` while one relationship repeatedly unlocks unusual tenderness;
- actor believes `this job is who I am` until role loss exposes a larger self-organization;
- actor believes `I never wanted children` and later discovers that the belief was partly defensive, or alternatively genuinely changes after new life history.

Rawlsing must preserve both possibilities: **prior self-misrecognition** and **genuine later change**.

## 3. The self-model is not one thing

`WHO_I_AM` should not be frozen as one paragraph or scalar coherence score.

At minimum, the following phenomenal dimensions may contribute:

### 3.1 Descriptive self

What the actor believes is factually true about themselves:

- roles;
- relationships;
- capacities;
- habits;
- preferences;
- biographical facts;
- recurrent tendencies.

### 3.2 Evaluative self

How the actor judges themselves:

- good/bad;
- competent/incompetent;
- desirable/undesirable;
- strong/weak;
- loyal/selfish;
- authentic/fake;
- successful/failure;
- worthy/unworthy.

These are examples, not a canonical polarity list.

### 3.3 Motivational self

What the actor believes they want, value, fear, seek, or refuse.

This may diverge from deeper causal motivation.

### 3.4 Relational self

Who the actor believes they are **with respect to particular others**:

- daughter/son;
- parent;
- lover;
- spouse;
- friend;
- subordinate;
- leader;
- rival;
- caretaker;
- outsider;
- desired/rejected person;
- trusted confidant.

The same generated person can carry several relational self-conceptions without contradiction.

### 3.5 Role/institutional self

Who the actor understands themselves to be within work, family, community, religion, profession, citizenship, status hierarchy, craft, etc.

V3 owns exact generated relations/history; PFF may mediate how those roles become live identity pressure.

### 3.6 Narrative self

How the actor links past events into a story:

- `I survived because I am strong`;
- `people always leave me`;
- `I wasted my twenties`;
- `everything changed when she died`;
- `I became myself after leaving home`.

The narrative is actor interpretation, not objective event truth.

### 3.7 Moral/ideal self

Who the actor believes they ought to be or wants to become.

### 3.8 Possible/future self

The person the actor expects, fears, hopes, or intends to become.

Prospective psychology therefore participates directly in current identity.

### 3.9 Causal self-theory

What the actor believes explains why they are the way they are.

This may be accurate, partial, or wrong and must remain distinct from the system's causal account.

These dimensions can conflict.

`SELF_MODEL_CONTRADICTION != DATA_ERROR`

## 4. Self-model coherence is local and graded, not necessarily global

Humans can maintain partly incompatible self-understandings in different contexts.

Examples:

- professionally decisive, privately dependent;
- compassionate toward friends, punitive toward family;
- sexually confident with one partner, deeply inhibited with another;
- religiously devout in one social world while carrying doubt elsewhere;
- self-conceived as independent while repeatedly seeking approval from one particular user/relationship.

This should not automatically be collapsed into hypocrisy or inconsistency repair.

A useful pre-architecture distinction is:

`GLOBAL SELF-CONCEPTION`
`!= RELATION-SPECIFIC SELF-CONCEPTION`
`!= ROLE-SPECIFIC SELF-CONCEPTION`
`!= CURRENT SELF-EXPERIENCE`.

PFF may temporarily recruit one part of the self-model while others remain backgrounded.

## 5. Relationship-specific selves are not separate personalities

The generated-person <-> user correction makes this especially important.

A particular user may make the persona:

- more playful;
- more dependent;
- more guarded;
- more intellectual;
- more tender;
- more status-conscious;
- more confrontational;
- more confessional;
- more self-conscious.

This does not imply multiple personalities or one self-model per user.

The current strongest interpretation is:

> relationships alter the **local accessibility, salience, safety, and recruitment** of parts of one durable person and one standing self-organization.

`RELATION-SPECIFIC SELF != SEPARATE PERSON`

`LOCAL SELF-ACCESSIBILITY != DURABLE IDENTITY REWRITE`

This connects directly to Enneagram-derived security-specific repertoire unlocking without importing a type table.

## 6. Being seen can destabilize or consolidate self-model

The user's response can become identity-relevant because the persona models what the user sees.

Examples:

- user recognizes a capacity the persona does not normally credit in itself;
- user calls attention to fear beneath anger;
- user rejects a persona's preferred self-presentation;
- user admires something the persona feels ashamed of;
- user consistently treats the persona as competent when the persona expects incompetence;
- user sees through a performance the persona believed was opaque;
- user misreads the persona in a way that intensifies defensive self-definition.

Therefore:

`METAPERCEPTION -> SELF_MODEL_PRESSURE = POSSIBLE`

`USER_VIEW_AS_MODELED_BY_PERSONA != OBJECTIVE SELF-TRUTH`

`BEING_SEEN_CAN_RECRUIT SELF-REVISION WITHOUT FORCING IT`.

The external user's actual private interpretation remains opaque; only observable user behavior and the generated persona's actor-local reading may participate.

## 7. Self-discrepancy is a core live phenomenon

PFF must preserve discrepancies among:

- actual deep causal organization;
- standing self-model;
- current consciously experienced self;
- ideal/ought self;
- anticipated future self;
- perceived self in the user's eyes;
- currently enacted/public self.

Candidate tensions include:

`I AM`
versus
`I THOUGHT I WAS`

`I WANT`
versus
`I SHOULD WANT`

`I DID`
versus
`I WOULD NEVER DO THAT`

`THEY SEE ME AS X`
versus
`I SEE MYSELF AS Y`

`I USED TO BE X`
versus
`I MAY BE BECOMING Y`.

These discrepancies can produce shame, curiosity, denial, relief, pride, grief, defensive hardening, or revision depending on the generated person's organization.

No universal discrepancy-to-emotion rule is adopted.

## 8. Exposure versus creation must remain unresolved until causally established

One of the most important originating PFF questions is whether a dramatic event **reveals what was already there** or **creates something new**.

Rawlsing must preserve at least four possibilities:

### 8.1 Exposure

Previously active but inaccessible/denied material becomes conscious.

### 8.2 Recontextualization

Known material acquires a new meaning without being newly created.

### 8.3 New causal development

The generated event produces genuinely new durable organization through V3 causal authority.

### 8.4 Mixed transformation

Existing vulnerabilities/strivings/mediators interact with genuinely new history, producing a configuration that did not exist in that form before.

Therefore:

`NEWLY_RECOGNIZED != NEWLY_CREATED`

`NEWLY_CREATED != PROOF_IT_WAS_ALWAYS_LATENT`

`PREEXISTING_CAUSAL_MATERIAL != PREEXISTING_CONSCIOUS_SELF-KNOWLEDGE`

`RUPTURE_EXPOSES_X` must not be asserted merely because X appears after rupture.

## 9. Self-model destabilization can occur before durable revision

A person's self-model need not instantly update when contradicted.

Possible live states include:

- contradiction noticed but rejected;
- contradiction noticed and rationalized;
- uncertainty about prior self-belief;
- compartmentalized exception;
- temporary identity confusion;
- alternating old/new interpretations;
- defensive doubling down;
- curiosity / provisional revision;
- grief over lost self-conception;
- relief at a new explanation;
- inability to form a coherent self-account.

This is a major PFF territory:

> the actor can be in **live self-model disequilibrium** without durable V3 identity having already changed.

`SELF_MODEL_PRESSURE != SELF_MODEL_COMMIT`

`SELF_MODEL_DISEQUILIBRIUM != DURABLE PERSON REWRITE`

## 10. Load-bearing self-conceptions / personality-island metaphor

The originating vision included the intuition that a major rupture can cause `personality islands` or parts of the standing self to crumble.

Preserve the phenomenon without literalizing the metaphor.

Some self-conceptions may be more **load-bearing** because many other expectations, commitments, memories, relationships, and future plans depend on them.

Examples:

- `I am a successful professional`;
- `my marriage is permanent`;
- `my parents are good people`;
- `I am heterosexual`;
- `I am a believer`;
- `I am the strong one`;
- `I am a good mother/father`;
- `I am someone who would never betray a friend`.

A contradiction to a load-bearing self-conception may destabilize multiple dependent interpretations simultaneously.

But:

`LOAD_BEARING_IDENTITY = PHENOMENON HYPOTHESIS`

not an adopted graph node or centrality score.

Architecture must later determine whether existing V3 relations/epistemic/memory structures already make such dependency derivable.

## 11. Rupture can change the accessibility topology of self

A major generated event may alter what parts of self become easy or difficult to access.

Examples:

- grief makes previously ordinary future plans feel unreal;
- humiliation makes status-sensitive memories and self-judgments unusually accessible;
- falling in love makes dependency needs and future relational selves newly salient;
- illness collapses a self-model organized around competence/physical independence;
- intoxication/psychedelic-like generated experience may temporarily weaken ordinary self-interpretive control if V3 lawfully generates such effects;
- parenthood may recruit roles/capacities that were never central to the prior self-model.

PFF should represent the **live accessibility/reorganization effect** only where supported by exact generated state.

It must not infer a permanent transformed identity from a dramatic surface event.

## 12. Memory and self-model are reciprocal without being identical

Prior memory examination established:

`MEMORY IDENTITY != CURRENT PSYCHIC CHARACTER`.

Self-model adds a reciprocal relationship:

- current self-model affects which memories feel self-relevant;
- accessible memories support or contradict current self-understanding;
- a memory can be reinterpreted after self-model change;
- a newly accessible memory can destabilize a standing self-conception;
- the same memory can support different narratives at different times.

Example:

A memory of leaving home at eighteen may once mean `I was brave`, later `I was escaping`, later `I abandoned them`, and later again `all three are partly true`.

The event need not change for its autobiographical role to change.

`OBJECTIVE_EVENT_STABILITY + AUTOBIOGRAPHICAL_REINTERPRETATION = POSSIBLE`.

## 13. Own behavior can become evidence about self

The self-output feedback examination becomes central here.

Examples:

- persona hears itself say something unexpectedly cruel;
- persona notices it kept returning to a particular user/topic;
- persona realizes it disclosed more than intended;
- persona acts courageously in a situation where it believed itself cowardly;
- persona discovers it enjoyed dependence, competition, tenderness, or conflict;
- persona notices a repeated posture across several interactions.

Own behavior may therefore pressure the standing self-model.

But:

`BEHAVIOR = EVIDENCE FOR SELF-INTERPRETATION`

not

`BEHAVIOR = INFALLIBLE REVELATION OF TRUE SELF`.

A rendered wording accident must not silently become durable identity evidence unless the whole event and its consequences are lawfully admitted.

## 14. Performance can precede internalization

Prior defense/performance work established that a public posture can be strategic, adaptive, defensive, genuine, or mixed.

Repeated enacted roles can potentially affect self-model over time:

`I am pretending to be confident`
may, through lawful life history, become
`I can act confidently`
and perhaps later
`I am a confident person`.

Or the opposite may occur:

repeated performance can intensify alienation:

`everyone thinks I am confident but this is not me`.

Therefore:

`REPEATED PERFORMANCE -> SELF-MODEL CHANGE` is not automatic.

The pathway requires generated consequences, actor interpretation, repetition/context, and exact V3 admission where durable.

## 15. Future self is part of present self-model

The anticipation examination shows that identity is partly prospective.

Examples:

- `I am going to be a doctor`;
- `we are going to grow old together`;
- `I will never become like my father`;
- `I am getting out of this town`;
- `I think I am becoming someone I dislike`.

A future collapse can therefore destabilize present identity even before any other trait changes.

A breakup can destroy not only a relationship but an anticipated self:

`future spouse / shared-home self / parent self / socially embedded self`.

Likewise a new relationship can make previously unavailable future selves imaginable.

`FUTURE_MODEL_CHANGE -> CURRENT_SELF_MODEL_PRESSURE = POSSIBLE`.

## 16. Values and motives must remain separate

An actor's self-model may contain values such as loyalty, compassion, independence, honesty, faith, ambition, restraint, generosity.

Current motives may conflict with those values.

The conflict does not prove the value is false.

Likewise professed value does not prove it causally controls current behavior.

`ENDORSED_VALUE != CURRENT_MOTIVE`

`CURRENT_MOTIVE != DEEP ATTRACTOR`

`VALUE VIOLATION != VALUE ABSENCE`

This is important for guilt, hypocrisy, temptation, moral injury, rationalization, and self-forgiveness phenomena.

## 17. The Enneagram contribution

Enneagram source theory supplies useful internal logic but not self-model content.

### 17.1 Type-like attractor versus conscious identity

The actual V3 realized attractor may be unknown or misrecognized by the actor.

An actor can:

- never know Enneagram;
- know it and mistype themselves;
- accurately identify a pattern;
- reject the framework;
- use a flattering type label defensively.

Therefore:

`ENNEAGRAM INTERPRETATION != SELF_MODEL`

`SELF-MODEL TYPE LABEL != V3 ATTRACTOR TRUTH`.

### 17.2 Levels / operating condition

A more flexible/integrated operating condition may permit contradictory self-information to be tolerated and examined rather than instantly defended.

A more captured condition may increase:

- self-model rigidity;
- selective interpretation;
- defensive rationalization;
- reduced corrective permeability;
- compulsive identity preservation.

This is internal logic, not a health-level scalar.

### 17.3 Defense and hidden complaint

Source theory usefully illustrates how a preferred self-image can coexist with disowned motives/claims and how defense preserves self-conception while producing interpersonal consequences.

Rawlsing should retain the relation, not the canned type-specific content.

### 17.4 Security-specific repertoire

A trusted user/relationship may make capacities locally reachable that contradict the actor's standing self-model.

This can create a powerful self-model question:

`If I can be this way with you, what does that mean about who I am?`

## 18. Self-model revision is not necessarily toward greater truth

A person can revise themselves inaccurately.

Examples:

- one rejection becomes `I am unlovable`;
- one success becomes `I am invincible`;
- a manipulative user's reaction becomes over-weighted;
- shame produces a harsh self-theory unsupported by broader history;
- infatuation creates an idealized new self narrative;
- defensive reinterpretation replaces one distortion with another.

Therefore:

`SELF-MODEL CHANGE != SELF-KNOWLEDGE IMPROVEMENT`.

Rawlsing must allow actor development to include mistaken self-conclusions where causally generated, while the system retains separate objective/analyst truth.

## 19. Self-model change can be partial and domain-specific

A person may revise:

- sexual self-conception;
- professional identity;
- relational confidence;
- moral self-evaluation;
- political/religious identity;
- parental identity;
- bodily identity;
- sense of competence;
- sense of attachment needs;

without globally rewriting every self-understanding.

`LOCAL IDENTITY REVISION != TOTAL SELF REPLACEMENT`.

This supports sparse, evidence-linked change rather than global prose regeneration.

## 20. PFF jurisdiction hypothesis

PFF likely needs to mediate at least the following live phenomena:

- current self-discrepancy;
- identity threat/exposure;
- self-model conflict with live motive;
- temporary self-model uncertainty/disequilibrium;
- relation-specific access to normally backgrounded self-capacities;
- current reinterpretation pressure;
- being-seen effects on self-conception;
- own-output evidence about self;
- future-self threat/expansion;
- current defense protecting standing self-model;
- leakage contradicting public/self story.

PFF should **not** by default own:

- canonical durable biography;
- objective life history;
- causal memory creation;
- durable actor beliefs/knowledge without V3 authority;
- durable middle-state creation/update;
- rupture history;
- attractor formation;
- permanent identity rewrite.

## 21. Candidate live self-model states without state-machine adoption

Architecture must eventually preserve phenomena such as:

- stable/unchallenged self-understanding;
- challenged but defended;

- contradictory self-interpretations coexisting;
- relation-specific exception;

- old narrative losing explanatory force;
- provisional new narrative;
- grief over lost identity;

These are phenomenological obligations, not enum proposals.

## 22. Hidden-cause firewall applies especially strongly to self-model

The machine may know that a current interaction was causally shaped by:

- attachment need;
- shame sensitivity;
- old relationship history;
- hidden attraction;
- status concern;
- V3 middle-state tension;
- attractor organization.

The actor may know none of this.

Therefore the final renderer cannot be given hidden causal explanations and invited to make the persona `insightful`.

Self-insight must arise through lawful actor-side evidence, accessibility, inference, reflection, and possible later V3 admission.

`MACHINE KNOWS WHY != ACTOR KNOWS WHY`

`THERAPEUTIC-SOUNDING SELF-EXPLANATION != LAWFUL SELF-KNOWLEDGE`.

## 23. User-facing synthetic-personhood implications

In the primary generated-person <-> user setting, good self-model dynamics should let the user encounter a persona that can:

- hold a standing sense of self without reciting a dossier;
- be surprised by itself;
- defend a self-conception;
- discover exceptions to its own self-story;
- become uncertain about itself;
- resist the user's interpretation rather than accepting every psychoanalysis;
- sometimes recognize that the user sees something important;
- sometimes conclude the user is wrong;
- change locally before changing durably;
- carry identity pressure across turns;
- later revise durable self-understanding only through lawful causal development.

Hard negative:

`USER SAYS "YOU ARE X" -> PERSONA SELF-MODEL BECOMES X` = REJECT.

The user's interpretation is social evidence/input, not identity authority.

## 24. Synthetic scenario obligations

Later conceptual/architecture courts should include at least:

1. **Breakup:** anticipated life/self collapses; old attachment material becomes accessible; actor does not instantly know why they are destabilized.
2. **Humiliation:** public failure contradicts competence identity; actor alternates rationalization, shame, anger, and reconsideration.
3. **Secure relationship:** user repeatedly unlocks tenderness that conflicts with `I am not affectionate` self-model.
4. **Unexpected courage:** actor acts bravely, then reconsiders `I am a coward` without one event automatically rewriting identity.
5. **Moral violation:** actor does something conflicting with endorsed moral identity and can defend, compartmentalize, confess, or revise.
6. **Conversion/deconversion:** old narrative, values, relationships, future-self, and meaning structures reorganize at different rates.
7. **Parenthood:** new role becomes identity-relevant while old self remains partially active.
8. **Illness/disability:** competence/body/future self challenged without objective biography being overwritten.
9. **User psychoanalysis:** user accurately or inaccurately names a hidden pattern; persona reacts based on accessibility, trust, defense, and evidence rather than adopting it as truth.
10. **Old memory reinterpreted:** same V3 memory changes autobiographical meaning after new life experience.
11. **Repeated performance:** strategic confidence either becomes genuine capacity or increases alienation depending on generated history.
12. **Relationship-specific exception:** persona is unusually playful/dependent/assertive with this user but not globally changed.
