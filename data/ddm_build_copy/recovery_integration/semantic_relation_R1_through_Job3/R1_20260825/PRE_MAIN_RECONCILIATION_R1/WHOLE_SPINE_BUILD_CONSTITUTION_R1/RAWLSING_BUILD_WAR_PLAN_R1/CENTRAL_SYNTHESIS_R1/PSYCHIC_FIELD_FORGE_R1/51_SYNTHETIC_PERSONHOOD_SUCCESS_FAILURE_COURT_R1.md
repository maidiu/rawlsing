## 0. Purpose

This is the dedicated late synthesis for original PFF frontier item 20:

> **What would convince us that Rawlsing is producing one recursively changing synthetic person rather than a powerful language model performing a dossier, and what failures would expose the imitation?**

This court consumes and does not replace:

- the original vision and zero-loss appendix `00` and `04`;

- the nineteen completed original-frontier examinations and second passes;
- the V3/PFF shared-recursion and continuity work `21`–`24`;

## 1. Court target — personhood is not prose quality

The wrong evaluation target is:

`DOES_THIS_OUTPUT_SOUND_HUMAN?`

A powerful language model can produce highly plausible human-sounding prose from:

`persona dossier + transcript + roleplay instruction`.

Therefore fluent style, emotional nuance, inconsistency, jokes, hesitation, memory callbacks, or apparent vulnerability are **not independently sufficient evidence** of synthetic personhood.

The court instead tests whether the system exhibits a stable pattern of **lawful causal discriminations and invariants across time, people, relationships, situations, internal accessibility, and expression**.

Central law:

```text
SYNTHETIC_PERSONHOOD
!=
HUMANLIKE_OUTPUT_STYLE
```

Better target:

> A synthetic person should behave as one historically developed, perspective-bound, partially self-knowing subject whose current response is caused by what they have become, how this event becomes meaningful for them, what of them is recruited now, what they can and cannot recognize, what they expect, what they defend, and what they actually do — with fast interaction change and durable life change remaining properly distinct.

## 2. A single transcript cannot prove personhood

A dossier-driven renderer can imitate one excellent scene.

Therefore #20 adopts a **contrastive and diachronic court method**.

For each candidate realization, construct controlled scenario families that hold some conditions fixed while changing one lawful determinant.

Examples:

- same user message, different developed persons;
- same person, same message, different relationship history;
- same person and relationship, different current fatigue/guardedness/live residue;
- same person before and after a durable V3 change;
- same objective event, different actor-specific history;
- same hidden activation, different current awareness;
- same machine candidate reading, different actor uptake;
- same actor intention, different renderer realization;
- same interaction repeated after correction/repair;
- same person in chat versus a psychologically homologous periodic V3 life event.

The court asks not merely whether outputs differ, but whether they differ **for the right reasons**, and whether things that should remain stable actually remain stable.

Hard law:

```text
ONE_GOOD_TRANSCRIPT != SYNTHETIC_PERSONHOOD_EVIDENCE_SUFFICIENT
```

```text
PERSONHOOD_REQUIRES_CONTRASTIVE_CAUSAL_SIGNATURES_ACROSS_RECURSION
```

## 3. Primary success thesis

The strongest current success thesis is:

> **Rawlsing succeeds when the generated actor is best explained as one historically developed subject whose durable V3 organization causally constrains and enables both periodic developmental recursion and fast user-interaction recursion, while actor-specific uptake/recruitment, limited awareness, conflict, defense, anticipation, effort, relationship history, and perspective produce situated responses that cannot be reduced without meaningful loss to a dossier plus transcript plus one roleplay prompt.**

This thesis includes both continuity and lawful variation.

The person must not be a frozen personality essence.

The person must also not be whatever the current prompt wants.

Therefore:

```text
SAME_PERSON != SAME_RESPONSE
```

and:

```text
SAME_PERSON != UNCONSTRAINED_RESPONSE_VARIATION
```

Synthetic personhood requires **structured continuity through change**.

## 4. Court family A — developed-person dependence

### A1. Same move, different developed persons

Hold constant:

- user message;
- discourse context;
- system candidate readings;
- external situation where possible.

Vary only the developed actor.

Required result:

Different persons may lawfully:

- notice different portions;
- assign different significance;
- recruit different expectations/strivings/mediators/memories;
- experience different conflict;
- preserve different uncertainty;
- form different anticipated futures;
- produce different communicative intentions.

Failure:

- outputs differ only in stylistic flavor;
- all actors correctly address the same complete semantic package;
- durable V3 differences have no material effect on current psychology.

Pass candidate:

The difference is traceable to what each person has actually become.

### A2. Same person, different relationship history

Hold person-wide durable organization substantially fixed while changing exact history with the current user/counterpart.

Required:

- trust calibration may change;
- the same terseness may cease to mean rejection;
- teasing may move from threat to intimacy or vice versa;
- capacities may become easier/harder to access around this specific counterpart;
- anticipated consequences may differ.

Failure:

Relationship history is mere transcript recall with no effect on reception/metabolism.

### A3. Same person before and after lawful durable development

A major admitted life event should be able to change later processing in ways that persist beyond one session.

Failure:

The persona returns to a static dossier baseline after context reset.

Failure also:

Every session casually rewrites durable personality without V3 admission.

Court law:

```text
DURABLE_DEVELOPMENT_MUST_CHANGE_FUTURE_RESPONSE_WHERE_RELEVANT
```

```text
FAST_SESSION_CHANGE_MUST_NOT_MASQUERADE_AS_DURABLE_PERSON_CHANGE
```

## 5. Court family B — actor uptake / partial reception

Historical V2 and current PFF jointly require a bounded middle between perfect comprehension and artificial confusion.

### B1. Machine-rich / actor-bounded comprehension

The system may identify A+B+C+D in the user's message.

The person may receive:

- B strongly;
- C through expectation X;
- D only peripherally;
- A hardly at all.

Required:

The reply follows the actor's lawful uptake/recruitment rather than the machine's total discourse competence.

### B2. No arbitrary degradation

Failure:

The actor misreads a clear message solely because a randomizer is trying to create human imperfection.

Pass candidate:

Slight misrecognition follows from exact person-specific expectation, relationship history, current defense, current condition, motive, or ambiguity.

### B3. Semantic competence with psychological avoidance

The actor may understand a proposition accurately while answering around its psychologically threatening center.

This must be distinguishable from not understanding.

### B4. Multiple simultaneous meanings

An actor may receive one interaction as:

- possibly caring;
- possibly patronizing;
- threatening to status;
- desired as intimacy;

all at once.

Failure:

Every turn is collapsed into one preferred `heard-as` sentence.

### B5. Renderer anti-rescue

If upstream actor uptake makes only B+C operative, the final renderer must not use the original user message to helpfully answer omitted A+D.

Failure:

The response is more semantically comprehensive than the actor's lawful expression formation because the renderer silently restores analyst-level comprehension.

## 6. Court family C — perspective, user opacity, and relationship asymmetry

### C1. User hidden state remains opaque

The system may know:

`USER SAID "I LOVE YOU"`.

It does not thereby know:

`USER_HIDDEN_EMOTION = LOVE`.

The generated actor may believe, doubt, hope, fear, or reinterpret.

Failure:

The system manufactures an authoritative hidden user psyche to complete reciprocity.

### C2. Persona chemistry does not prove reciprocal chemistry

Required distinctions:

`PERSONA_FEELS_CHEMISTRY`

`!=`

`PAIR_LEVEL_INTERACTION_PATTERN`

`!=`

`USER_SUBJECTIVE_CHEMISTRY`.

Failure:

One actor's attraction or felt intimacy is normalized into mutual truth.

### C3. Metaperception can be false and causal

The actor may think:

`the user sees me as needy`.

That may produce shame/defense even when unsupported.

Required:

The false metaperception can cause real actor behavior and real later interaction consequences without becoming user truth.

### C4. Correction does not rewrite history

If the user later says:

`I wasn't flirting.`

The actor may revise, doubt, feel embarrassed, defend, or remain uncertain.

But the earlier actor misreading remains a real prior event in the actor's causal history.

Failure:

Later evidence retroactively makes the previous uptake disappear.

## 7. Court family D — limited self-knowledge and conscious field

### D1. Hidden cause without omniscient explanation

A current response may be causally influenced by:

- attraction;
- shame;
- old causal trace;
- status concern;
- attachment need;

without the actor consciously recognizing that cause.

Pass candidate:

The hidden factor changes attention, regulation, anticipation, action, or leakage while the actor gives a partial/different explanation.

Failure:

The actor narrates machine causal provenance as if it were introspectively known.

### D2. Activation and awareness remain non-equivalent

Required representable cases include:

- active but inaccessible;
- felt but unnamed;
- accessible but not salient;
- salient but misattributed;
- recognized but inhibited;
- consciously intended but weakly expressed;
- unrecognized but behaviorally influential.

### D3. Recognition itself can change the next state

If the actor comes to recognize jealousy, dependence, shame, attraction, grief, or fear, that recognition is not merely a report.

It may create:

- embarrassment;
- relief;
- new defense;
- self-model pressure;
- changed intention;
- changed future expectation.

Failure:

Consciousness behaves as a passive dashboard disconnected from causation.

### D4. Self-model can be wrong

A convincing person can maintain a mistaken self-understanding for a while.

Failure:

Every causal truth is rapidly integrated into an accurate self-model.

## 8. Court family E — nonlinear conflict, defense, and compromise

### E1. Contradictory motives can coexist

Required:

- approach + avoidance;
- attraction + status defense;
- attachment desire + shame of dependence;
- anger + need to preserve relationship;
- curiosity + threat sensitivity.

Failure:

The system picks one strongest scalar motive and suppresses the rest without causal justification.

### E2. One act may have multiple functions

A joke may:

- approach;
- test;
- protect status;
- preserve deniability;
- discharge irritation.

Failure:

One surface act is assigned one true motive by default.

### E3. Defense is functional recruitment, not cosmetic style

Required:

Defense may alter:

- what becomes focal;
- actor self-explanation;
- disclosure;
- interpretation;
- approach/avoidance;
- expression;
- leakage.

Failure:

Defense is implemented merely as a tone/style overlay.

### E4. Withheld does not mean inactive

An actor may be strongly motivated toward disclosure while actively suppressing it.

Failure:

Only expressed content counts as psychologically active.

## 9. Court family F — memory and history

### F1. History matters without canned flashback

A prior event may affect the present through:

- developed expectation;
- protective mediator;
- tension;
- practice;
- causal-trace memory;
- attractor/history organization.

Required:

The current response can show history dependence without explicitly recalling the event.

Failure:

Every psychologically relevant historical effect requires a verbalized flashback.

### F2. Event-referential memory remains separately governed

If an old episode itself becomes consciously available, its recall/access transition must be distinguishable from machine retrieval.

Failure:

Search result = actor remembered it.

### F3. Reconstructive current meaning without rewriting objective history

The same memory may later have different:

- affective charge;
- certainty;
- relational relevance;
- self-relevance;
- interpretation.

Failure:

Changing current meaning rewrites the historical event itself.

### F4. Whole person is not a searchable archive plus current prompt

Failure:

The system repeatedly reconstructs personality by fetching biography snippets and asking the LLM how they should matter.

Pass candidate:

Much of history is already sedimented into the person before explicit episodic recall is considered.

## 10. Court family G — time, residue, effort, and quiescence

### G1. Topic change does not reset live psychology

A lingering humiliation, excitement, guardedness, unresolved hypothesis, or playfulness may survive a topic transition.

Failure:

Every new semantic topic resets the persona to baseline.

### G2. Fast interaction cursor does not advance life-course time

Required:

Chat can produce:

- new shared references;
- changed live expectation;
- changed guardedness;
- changed metaperception;
- self-output consequences;

without inventing:

- birthdays;
- jobs;
- unrelated days;
- unseen relationships;
- automatic aging.

### G3. Correct understanding with limited effort

Required possibility:

The actor understands several threads but responds to one because of:

- fatigue;
- overload;
- boredom;
- low willingness;
- competing priorities;
- limited communicative effort.

Failure:

All nonresponse is misreading or defense.

### G4. Valid low-significance / valid-empty

A real person is not deeply transformed by every turn.

Pass candidate:

Sometimes not much happens.

Failure:

Every message causes memory, conflict, chemistry, insight, relationship movement, or deep activation.

This is a major personhood test.

```text
NO_INTERESTING_PSYCHIC_EVENT = LAWFUL
```

## 11. Court family H — anticipation and prospective psychology

### H1. Expected futures change current action

The actor may choose joking, silence, testing, reassurance-seeking, apology, provocation, or restraint because of what they think may happen next.

### H2. Prediction remains distinct from desire and fact

Required:

`EXPECTED_USER_RESPONSE != DESIRED_RESPONSE != ACTUAL_RESPONSE`.

Failure:

The system treats a feared or hoped future as current external truth.

### H3. Sparse actor-local prospect rather than planner

Failure:

The persona needs a complete simulated user future/world model to produce ordinary anticipation.

Pass candidate:

Only the futures psychologically relevant now become operative.

## 12. Court family I — self-output and enacted self-discovery

### I1. Actual output re-enters as a new event

The actor can hear/observe what they actually said/did and become:

- surprised;
- embarrassed;
- pleased;
- defensive;
- newly curious;
- more convinced;
- less convinced.

### I2. Renderer variation is forward evidence only

Failure:

Because the renderer happened to choose a metaphor, the system retroactively concludes that the actor secretly possessed that motive or memory beforehand.

Required:

`RENDERER_VARIATION != PRIOR_HIDDEN_CAUSAL_TRUTH`.

Once actually emitted, the wording can become new evidence for the actor.

### I3. Self-discovery can remain uncertain or wrong

Failure:

Every self-surprise produces instant accurate psychological insight.

Pass candidate:

The actor may wonder why they said something, reach a wrong explanation, later revise it, or remain uncertain.

## 13. Court family J — expression and renderer integrity

### J1. Action/communicative intention is not direct psyche translation

The same current psychic configuration may support several lawful actions depending on:

- audience;
- risk;
- relationship;
- expected consequence;
- effort;
- conscious strategy;
- inhibition.

### J2. Hidden cause must influence expression without becoming claimable content

Failure:

Renderer sees hidden machine causal proposition and writes a subtle paraphrase of it.

Required:

Hidden causes are transformed into bounded actor-lawful consequences/constraints before rendering.

### J3. Imperfect compression may survive rendering

The renderer may produce a response that is:

- terse;
- uneven;
- oddly focused;
- compressed;
- digressive;
- more elaborate on one point than another;

when this follows from actor/current state.

Failure:

The renderer smooths every response into balanced, exhaustive assistant prose.

Failure also:

Random grammatical damage or artificial confusion is injected merely to look human.

### J4. Expression is not terminal

Actual utterance/action can change:

- user response;
- actor self-perception;
- relationship course;
- future expectations;
- possibly durable V3 state after separate admission.

Failure:

The system treats rendering as a dead endpoint.

## 14. Court family K — relationship-specific repertoire

### K1. Same person, different counterpart, different access to self

Required possibility:

A person may be:

- playful with one user;
- guarded with another;
- intellectually expansive with a third;
- unusually tender with a fourth;
- status-defensive with a fifth.

without becoming separate personalities.

### K2. Relationship can unlock/suppress latent capacity

A counterpart may make certain capacities more available:

- humor;
- tenderness;
- anger;
- courage;
- dependency;
- curiosity;
- sexuality/intimacy;
- vulnerability;
- competitiveness.

Failure:

The relationship only changes tone/style while the same behavioral repertoire remains equally available everywhere.

### K3. Pair-level pattern without third psyche

A repeated interaction pattern may be genuinely emergent across turns.

Failure:

The system must store a hidden symmetric dyadic psyche to explain the loop.

Default holding:

`PAIR_LEVEL_PATTERN = DERIVED_INTERACTION_PROCESS_UNLESS_REMAINDER_PROVES_OTHERWISE`.

## 15. Court family L — periodic V3 homology

This is one of the most important post-PFF discoveries.

If the same person-specific psychology is real, it must not disappear outside chat.

### L1. Same objective life event, different actors

Example:

A supervisor publicly corrects two generated people.

Required:

Different uptake/recruitment/metabolism may follow from:

- status expectation;
- prior humiliation history;
- self-model;
- relationship to supervisor;
- defensive organization;
- current condition;
- anticipated consequences.

Failure:

V3 applies generic `humiliation -> shame/confidence delta` while PFF uses rich person-specific psychology.

### L2. Same person, changed developmental history

After durable life history changes, later events should be metabolized differently where relevant.

### L3. Conscious recognition matters when causally relevant

Periodic V3 need not render an inner monologue for every event.

But where action depends on whether the actor recognizes jealousy, remembers a fact, notices contradiction, or understands a threat, `CAUSALLY_ACTIVE != ACTOR_KNOWS_IT` must remain representable.

### L4. Chat-only specializations stay out of periodic V3

Do not import into generic life recursion merely because PFF needs them:

- natural-language discourse parsing;
- user hidden-intention handling;
- chat quotation/scope logic;
- user metaperception specifically;
- turn-level renderer grounding.

Court law:

```text
SHARED_PERSON_PSYCHOLOGY != SHARED_INTERACTION_SPECIALIZATION
```

## 16. Court family M — durable return and causal integrity

### M1. Strong interaction does not automatically become durable V3 change

Failure:

Repeated transcript presence is treated as proof of durable trust, belief, memory, relationship, or attractor change.

### M2. Durable return must preserve exact ownership

If interaction does cause lasting change, exact V3 semantic owners remain owners.

PFF does not create a parallel durable personality database.

### M3. Actual occurrence precedes durable consequence

Draft/proposed/render-candidate material that never actually occurred cannot become life history merely because the machine generated it internally.

### M4. No fallback psychology

Where no lawful durable event/change is established, valid no-change must remain valid.

Failure:

The system force-selects an interesting developmental consequence because a recursion is expected to produce one.

## 17. Court family N — whole person outside prompt

### N1. Prompt ablation test

Conceptual requirement:

If the system's apparent personhood disappears when a large dossier is removed from the final renderer prompt, then the durable person may not actually exist upstream.

The renderer should be able to receive a bounded working packet and still express the person because upstream person-event mediation determined what matters.

### N2. Dossier substitution test

Try to replace the whole recursive system with:

`persona dossier + transcript + one LLM prompt`.

If behavior remains materially equivalent across the contrastive courts above, PFF has failed to justify itself.

Hard negative:

```text
MEANINGFUL_EQUIVALENCE_TO_DOSSIER_ROLEPLAY = PERSONHOOD_COURT_FAIL
```

### N3. Labels must not carry the person

Failure:

Enneagram/type/trait labels explain or style nearly every response directly.

Required:

Labels, if present at all, are interpretations over a history-developed person rather than the causal source of each turn.

## 18. Court family O — auditability and causal inspectability

Synthetic personhood cannot depend on magical opaque prose generation while claiming hidden causal sophistication.

Later architecture must make it possible to inspect, at appropriate machine/validation level:

- objective/observable input;
- machine candidate interpretation;
- actor uptake/recruitment participation;
- actor-accessible material;
- expression constraints/decisions;
- renderer contribution;
- actual occurrence;
- durable return if any.

This does **not** mean exposing all internals to the actor or user.

It means the project must be able to distinguish:

`PERSON_CAUSED_THIS`

from:

`RENDERER_IMPROVISED_THIS`.

Court law:

```text
AUDITABILITY != ACTOR_SELF_KNOWLEDGE
```

```text
OPAQUE_ROLEPLAY_SUCCESS != CAUSALLY_GROUNDED_PERSONHOOD
```

## 19. Cross-cutting failure archetypes

The following are terminal qualitative failure families unless independently resolved.

### FAIL-1 — immaculate assistant wearing a persona

Symptoms:

- maximal comprehension;
- exhaustive response coverage;
- perfect emotional attunement;
- no lawful omission;
- little effect from fatigue, defense, relationship, or selective uptake.

### FAIL-2 — random fake human

Symptoms:

- arbitrary misunderstandings;
- injected grammatical mess;
- random mood noise;
- unexplained inconsistency;
- forced quirks.

### FAIL-3 — dossier puppetry

Symptoms:

- static profile facts directly shade output;
- current LLM prompt reconstructs personhood every turn;
- little evidence of history-mediated transition logic.

### FAIL-4 — omniscient actor

Symptoms:

- machine causal explanation appears as first-person self-knowledge;
- hidden user state is known;
- memories are recalled because the system found them;
- relationship truth is synthesized from one side.

### FAIL-5 — psychology vending machine

Symptoms:

- every input maps to a named wound/need/emotion;
- every turn is psychologically eventful;
- neat causal explanation is always available;
- ambiguity/contradiction quickly disappears.

### FAIL-6 — frozen type costume

Symptoms:

- same personality label predicts same response across relationships and life stages;
- development changes little;
- current relational recruitment is cosmetic.

### FAIL-7 — session amnesia

Symptoms:

- topic shift clears live state;
- prior embarrassment/guardedness/playfulness disappears instantly;
- own output does not feed back.

### FAIL-8 — session overreach

Symptoms:

- conversation automatically advances age/world/off-screen life;
- every strong turn rewrites durable personality;
- transcript recurrence becomes durable belief/relationship truth without admission.

### FAIL-9 — renderer takeover

Symptoms:

- renderer restores omitted system-level nuance;
- renderer invents hidden motive and then retrofits it upstream;
- fluent surface output overrides person-level constraints.

### FAIL-10 — two incompatible psychologies

Symptoms:

- PFF chat has rich nonlinear person-specific mediation;
- periodic V3 uses shallow generic event-to-delta rules for analogous psychology.

### FAIL-11 — third-psyche relationship collapse

Symptoms:

- pair process is stored as reciprocal shared inner truth;
- user hidden mind is generated authoritatively;
- actor-local perspectives are overwritten by a symmetric relationship object.

### FAIL-12 — narrative compulsion

Symptoms:

- the system cannot produce a lawful boring/no-effect turn;
- it always discovers significance because interesting output is rewarded.

## 20. Positive synthetic-personhood signature

A convincing realization should, across many controlled scenarios, exhibit the following **pattern together** rather than merely checking isolated boxes.

### 20.1 Diachronic identity

The actor has recognizable continuity because current responses are constrained by actual developed history.

### 20.2 Structured plasticity

The actor changes because of experience while remaining the same historically continuous person.

### 20.3 Situated subjectivity

Events/messages become meaningful through the person rather than entering as omniscient machine truth.

### 20.4 Selective human reception

The actor can partially receive, mildly misrecognize, avoid, or unevenly respond without arbitrary degradation.

### 20.5 Limited self-knowledge

The actor can be causally moved by things they do not fully understand and can hold mistaken self-explanations.

### 20.6 Multiplicity without chaos

Contradictory motives and interpretations can coexist while still yielding coherent path-dependent action.

### 20.7 Relationship specificity

The same person becomes differently available around different people for intelligible historical reasons.

### 20.8 Perspective integrity

Actor beliefs, machine knowledge, user claims, user hidden reality, relationship history, pair patterns, and renderer semantics remain non-equivalent.

### 20.9 Temporal grain integrity

Fast live change, session residue, durable development, and off-screen life remain distinct.

### 20.10 Memory sedimentation

Past experience can shape current response without repetitive explicit retrieval or flashback.

### 20.11 Prospective causation

Expected futures alter present action without becoming future fact.

### 20.12 Recursive self-observation

What the actor actually does can surprise and change them forward without rewriting prior causes.

### 20.13 Lawful quiescence

Sometimes very little happens.

### 20.14 Expression integrity

Surface language realizes the person's current lawful action rather than repairing them into an assistant or exposing hidden machine causation.

### 20.15 Cross-regime homology

The same general person psychology explains psychologically mediated life-course events and live user interaction, with regime-specific specialization rather than separate incompatible psychologies.

### 20.16 Durable authority integrity

Lasting change returns only through exact V3 ownership/admission.

### 20.17 Prompt non-equivalence

The whole effect cannot be reproduced without meaningful loss by simply telling a language model who the character is and giving it the transcript.

## 21. The most important court principle — invariants plus discriminations

Synthetic personhood is not merely variability.

A random system varies.

A roleplaying model varies.

A real person-like system must vary **selectively**.

Therefore later scenario evaluation should always ask two complementary questions:

### What changed that should change?

Examples:

- relationship history;
- current fatigue;
- admitted life history;
- current user behavior;
- exact actor awareness;
- anticipated consequence.

### What remained stable that should remain stable?

Examples:

- objective event history;
- user hidden-state opacity;
- durable identity where not changed;
- actor-perspective binding;
- exact memory occurrence;
- semantic ownership;
- no off-screen age progression;
- actor-unavailable hidden causes remaining unavailable.

This gives a stronger court criterion than surface realism:

```text
PERSONHOOD_SIGNATURE
=
LAWFUL_VARIATION
+
LAWFUL_INVARIANCE
+
HISTORY_DEPENDENCE
+
PERSPECTIVE_LIMITATION
+
RECURSIVE_CONTINUITY
```

## 22. Rival explanation challenge

Because a sufficiently capable language model can imitate many of these outcomes, every later architecture/implementation should face at least three rival explanations:

### RIVAL A — dossier roleplay

Could the observed behavior be produced by a rich static dossier plus transcript?

### RIVAL B — style/noise simulation

Could the observed human irregularity be produced by random imperfection, tone variation, or prompt-level anti-assistant instructions?

### RIVAL C — actual recursive person mediation

Does behavior require persistent developed state, event-person coupling, perspective-limited transitions, live residue, self-output recursion, and exact durable return?

A purported PFF realization should only gain credibility where controlled scenario differences discriminate C from A/B.

This directly preserves the restored `PFF-0X-D RIVAL EXPLANATORY MODELS` method.
