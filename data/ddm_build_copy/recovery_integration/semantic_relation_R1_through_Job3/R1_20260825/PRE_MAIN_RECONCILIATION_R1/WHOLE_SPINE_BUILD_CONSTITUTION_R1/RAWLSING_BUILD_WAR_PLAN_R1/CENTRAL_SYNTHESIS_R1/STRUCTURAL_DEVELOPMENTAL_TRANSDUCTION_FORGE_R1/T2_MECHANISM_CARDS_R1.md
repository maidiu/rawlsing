## M1 — SOCIETAL MEANING → SUBJECT-RELATIVE PREMISE → REASON → ACTION

**SOURCE:** Klaus Holzkamp, *Psychology from the Standpoint of the Subject* (2013), “What Could a Psychology from the Standpoint of the Subject Be?”, pp. 47–48; “Psychology: Social Self-Understanding on the Reasons for Action in the Conduct of Everyday Life,” pp. 286–287. Corroboration: Holzkamp (1990), pp. 35, 40–41.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL / METHODOLOGICAL`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** objective societal meaning structures, action possibilities, constraints, rules, relations, or historically organized circumstances available independently of whether this actor acts on them.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** determinate aspects of the objective situation become action-relevant only insofar as the subject takes/experiences them as premises in a first-person reason relation. Reasons connect those premises with the subject’s perceived life interests; they ground but do not mechanically produce action.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** a subset/configuration of objective possibilities and constraints becomes actor-relevant as actionable opportunity, limitation, obligation, information, dependency, or possible route. The consequence at this stage is **premise relevance**, not a trait and not yet an event or action.

**REALIZATION LAW:** `OBJECTIVE POSSIBILITY` must be available/applicable; the actor must be situated so that it can become a premise; it must enter a reason relation; and an action must actually occur before an action enters biography. No step licenses skipping the next.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** first-person standpoint, perceived life interests, interpretation of circumstances, and selection among grounded alternatives. Different subjects—or the same subject at different moments—may form different premise/reason constellations from overlapping objective conditions.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** none directly. Realized action and its consequences may later become practice, memory, relationship history, learned expectation, mediator, tension, or other V3 developmental material if separately earned.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `EVENT` when a premise/reason constellation concerns an episode; `CONDITION` when premises persist; premise constellations may also change across `TRAJECTORY` or `TURNING POINT` but M1 itself does not supply a quantitative change threshold.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** from dyad/local practice through organization/institution to broader societal relations, provided the structural meaning is concretely actor-relevant rather than inferred directly from category membership.

**RECIPROCITY:** indirect in M1: realized action can affect later conditions, but the structural-update mechanism is not contained in M1 and is represented separately in M4/M9.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** actual actor standpoint is required for claims about premises/reasons; observer-assigned structural significance is not automatically the subject’s premise. The inspected theory is strongest for reason-mediated action and is not a full account of preverbal/embodied orientation.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** no `STRUCTURE → ACTION` determinism; no `CATEGORY → TRAIT`; no inference that an objectively available possibility was perceived, adopted, or used; no inference that a reason is a hidden deterministic variable; no claim that grounded action is objectively correct, beneficial, or morally justified.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** structural pressure / structural affordance-applicability; positioned-subject empirical possibility where relevant; mediation-route possibility.
- **V3 owner:** actor-indexed uptake/recruitment/response and downstream memory/learning/biography owners can receive realized history; the Phase-0 seam map does not prove an exact owner for subject-relative premises as a distinct seam state.
- **missing connective function:** `OBJECTIVE_SOCIAL_MEANING_TO_SUBJECT_RELATIVE_PREMISE_BINDING`, preserving `possibility != premise != reason != action`.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` as explicit connective semantics; no new object is adopted here.

## M2 — POSITION-SPECIFIC PREMISE / SCOPE FORMATION

**SOURCE:** Holzkamp (1990), especially pp. 40–41; Dreier (1999), pp. 6 and 14.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL / PRACTICE-ANALYTIC`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** historically specific social relations plus the actor’s concrete location/position in one or more practices.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** the structured world is encountered from a position. Position-specific slices of societal meanings, available relations, obligations, dependencies, and scopes become the local material from which subject-relative premises can be formed.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** differential opportunity/constraint sets, obligations, counterpart relations, information access, influence, exposure, and possible courses of action—without converting position into a psychological essence.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the subject must actually occupy/participate in the relevant position/context at the relevant time. Structural availability at population/world level is insufficient; local applicability and participation must be established before actor-relative scope is claimed.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** subjects interpret, prioritize, resist, combine, or bypass position-relevant possibilities; a position structures a scope but does not uniquely determine which possibility becomes premise or action.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** only downstream via realized participation/activity: practices, knowledge, expectations, relationship histories, memories, capacities, tensions, and other earned developmental changes.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** primarily `CONDITION` and `RELATION`; can recur as `RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE` and change along `TRANSITION` or `TRAJECTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** local practice, household, organization, institution, network, and broader relation as instantiated in concrete positions.

**RECIPROCITY:** participation can reproduce or alter the practice/position and thereby change later scopes for self or others, but exact update law is separate.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** position must be represented concretely enough to identify actual scopes/obligations; demographic or class labels alone do not specify the local ecology.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `POSITION != PERSONALITY`; `POSITION != PREMISE`; `POSITION != ACTION`; no assumption that all holders of a nominally similar position encounter the same household, institution, network, or counterpart ecology.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** structural affordance/applicability, structural pressure, interaction topology, mediation-route possibility.
- **V3 owner:** actor/context binding and actor-indexed opportunity/constraint realization as reconstructed in Phase 0; downstream biography owners after realization.
- **missing connective function:** `POSITION_AND_LOCAL_PRACTICE_TO_ACTOR_RELATIVE_SCOPE_OBLIGATION_AND_COUNTERPART_ECOLOGY`.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE` existing actor/context binding and opportunity/constraint semantics; Phase-0 explicitly says binding alone does not derive this ecology.

## M3 — RESTRICTIVE / GENERALIZED AGENCY AS A SITUATIONAL ACTION ALTERNATIVE

**SOURCE:** Holzkamp (1990), pp. 35–40; Holzkamp (2013), pp. 47–48, 56–58; Osterkamp (2017), pp. 29–30.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** dependency, power asymmetry, threatened/limited agency, sanctions, or a situation in which maintaining local security may conflict with expanding influence over relevant conditions.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** the subject can have grounds for actions that secure or stabilize a position within existing relations (`restrictive`) or grounds for actions aimed at expanding control/influence over relevant conditions (`generalized`). The distinction concerns possible modes of action in concrete situations, not fixed types of people.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** different uses of available resources, alliances, compliance/resistance, risk acceptance, dependency management, and participation in reproduction or change of the surrounding relation.

**REALIZATION LAW:** there must be an actual dilemma/threat/constraint, actor-relative premises, grounded alternatives, and a realized action. A structurally likely route or an observer’s judgment cannot substitute for the subject’s reason relation or the realized act.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** central. The subject can preserve, reinterpret, resist, bypass, or challenge conditions. Apparent accommodation may be subjectively grounded in security/dependency; apparent resistance is not automatic or universally available.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** recurrent practice, expectations, relationship patterns, learned strategies, tensions, self-understanding, and other durable changes only after repetition/history and downstream metabolism.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `EVENT` in a discrete dilemma; `CONDITION` under persistent dependency; `RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE` when a mode of action recurs; possibly `TRAJECTORY` or `CUMULATIVE PROCESS` when historically stabilized.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** dyad, household, organization, institution, class/power relation, collective setting.

**RECIPROCITY:** strong in principle: actions may reproduce the relation, alter one’s position, recruit allies, or contribute to changing the conditions.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** feasibility matters. A generalized action possibility must exist and may require resources/allies; collective transformation is not inferred merely because a subject desires it.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** never label a person/group/class as inherently “restrictive” or “generalized”; no `oppressed → resistant personality`; no `dominant position → restrictive psychology`; no normative scoring of actors from outside; no automatic translation of structural threat into one action.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** structural pressure and affordance/applicability establish objective pressures and possibilities.
- **V3 owner:** uptake/recruitment/response and biography/history can carry realized actor action and its consequences.
- **missing connective function:** explicit `SUBJECT_GROUNDED_ACTION_ALTERNATIVE_UNDER_DEPENDENCY` semantics if Rawlsing needs to distinguish actor-grounded preservation/reproduction from actor-grounded expansion/change rather than only storing generic response.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`; likely composable with existing response machinery, but the premise/reason distinction is not proven as an existing exact owner.

## M4 — COLLECTIVE CONDITION TRANSFORMATION

**SOURCE:** Osterkamp (2017), pp. 29–30; corroborating theoretical support in Dreier (1999), p. 6.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL / PROGRAMMATIC`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** a condition, relation, dependency, or power arrangement that constrains individual action and cannot be materially altered by isolated action alone.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** subjects communicate overlapping problems/interests, coordinate action, combine capacities/resources, and act jointly so that collective action can increase effective capacity to alter the relation or condition itself.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** new allies/counterparts, pooled information/resources, altered sanctions or bargaining capacity, access to new action routes, and—if successful—changed objective conditions for later action.

**REALIZATION LAW:** commonality must be recognized/communicated enough for coordination; organization must actually occur; collective action must be realized; and it must causally modify a condition/relation before later structural ecology is updated. Insight or generalized possibility alone is not success.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** choice to coordinate, competing interests, conflict, interpretation, solidarity, withdrawal, and strategy remain open. Collective capacity is produced through action, not attributed automatically to category membership.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** indirect: changed relations/conditions create new later experiences and practices; collective participation itself may also enter biography and downstream learning/relationship history.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** usually `RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE`, `TRANSITION`, or `CUMULATIVE PROCESS`; a decisive collective success/failure can function as a `TURNING POINT` but no general threshold is supplied.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** group, network, organization, institution, community, class relation.

**RECIPROCITY:** constitutive. Realized action can alter later structural possibilities/constraints for self and others.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** organization costs, sanctions, resource asymmetry, conflicting interests, institutional closure, and coordination failure can block transformation.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `COLLECTIVE ACTION POSSIBILITY != ORGANIZATION != SUCCESS`; no automatic solidarity; no assumption that a shared category creates shared interests or action; no guarantee that changed conditions produce a specific psychological outcome.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** structural possibility/pressure can represent the condition and possible routes.
- **V3 owner:** actor response/biography can carry realized participation.
- **missing connective function:** `REALIZED_COLLECTIVE_ACTION_TO_UPDATED_STRUCTURAL_CONDITION_AND_LATER_ACTOR_ECOLOGY`. Phase 0 explicitly lists collective action → transformed structural conditions as a research question rather than a proved owner.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING`.

## M5 — PARTICIPATION POSITION → SCOPE / OBLIGATION / INFORMATION / INFLUENCE

**SOURCE:** Dreier (1999), pp. 6, 10, 14.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL / PRACTICE-ANALYTIC`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** a social practice/context organized through differentiated positions, relations, standards, rules, responsibilities, and distributions of influence.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** actual participation in a position gives the subject a particular, partial relation to the practice: different concerns, obligations, information, counterpart relations, access, influence, and limits. Conduct remains interpretive rather than a mechanical execution of rules.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** concrete responsibilities, action scopes, counterpart set, information exposure, authority/dependency, available routes, and practical concerns.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the person must actually participate in the context/position at the relevant time. A formal rule or structural position is insufficient if the person never encounters or enacts the relevant relation.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** the subject adopts stances toward rules, standards, commitments and other participants; may comply, reinterpret, negotiate, avoid, or challenge them.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** recurrent practices, practical knowledge, expectations, capacities, relationship histories and other downstream changes when participation is repeated/metabolized.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `RELATION`, `CONDITION`, and `RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE`; participation can be one-off or long-duration.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** dyad/team, household, organization, institution, community practice.

**RECIPROCITY:** participation contributes to reproducing the practice and can also modify local arrangements, relations, rules or later position.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** formal position may not equal enacted position; access, duration, co-participants, local exceptions, and cross-context commitments change what is actually realized.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `FORMAL POSITION != ACTUAL PARTICIPATION`; `RULE != ACTION`; `OBLIGATION != COMPLIANCE`; no inference from position directly to disposition/personality.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** interaction topology, structural applicability, mediation routes.
- **V3 owner:** actor activation/context binding, opportunity/constraint, realized encounter/participation and downstream learning/relationship history as reconstructed in Phase 0.
- **missing connective function:** `STRUCTURED_PRACTICE_POSITION_TO_ACTOR_SPECIFIC_PARTICIPATION_SCOPE_INFORMATION_OBLIGATION_ECOLOGY`.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`.

## M6 — CROSS-CONTEXT CONDUCT-OF-LIFE COORDINATION

**SOURCE:** Dreier (1999), pp. 10 and 14. Supporting successor synthesis: Højholt & Schraube (2016), pp. 1–14 (partial inspection only).

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL / PRACTICE-ANALYTIC`; successor support `THEORETICAL / SYNTHETIC`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** multiple social contexts with differentiated positions, commitments, rules, relations, rhythms, resources, demands and possible contradictions.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** persons conduct everyday life by composing and coordinating participations across contexts rather than being exhausted by any one setting. They prioritize commitments, establish routines, carry stances/premises across contexts, and resolve or live with conflicts among demands.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** time allocation, scheduling pressure, obligations, cross-context conflict, support dependencies, information carried between settings, accessible routes, and an actor-specific activity ecology distributed across places and relations.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the actor must actually participate across the contexts; cross-context demands must occur in overlapping biography; coordination/routine/prioritization must be realized. Membership in several contexts does not by itself establish the actor’s conduct pattern.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** prioritization, interpretation, planning, avoidance, negotiation, stance-taking, and modification of routines; actors may preserve one context by sacrificing another or seek to change arrangements.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** recurrent practice, expectations, coping strategies, relational patterns, knowledge, capacities, tensions and biography if downstream learning/memory stabilizes consequences.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** centrally `RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE` and `CONDITION`; often organized as `TRAJECTORY` across changing participation configurations.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** cross-context: household, school, work, care, network, institutional and community contexts.

**RECIPROCITY:** conduct-of-life arrangements can change local relations/routines and later access; coordinated action with others can alter conditions.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** context multiplicity, temporal overlap, dependencies, transport/spatial constraints, linked persons, institutional schedules, and resource limitations determine which coordination problems are real.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** no “context effect” may be treated as whole-person determination; no assumption that repeated events are an adequate ontology for persistent cross-context obligations; no inference that an actor has harmonized contradictory demands.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** phase/world/context binding plus structural topology/applicability can provide context-specific possibility material.
- **V3 owner:** biography/path dependence and recurrent realized history can carry cross-context sequence.
- **missing connective function:** `MULTI_CONTEXT_PARTICIPATION_TO_CROSS_CONTEXT_ACTIVITY_OBLIGATION_TIME_AND_RELATION_ECOLOGY`; Phase 0 proves only generic actor/context binding and marks recurrent-practice semantics as underproved.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING`.

## M7 — INSTITUTIONALLY ARRANGED PATHWAY → PERSONALLY REALIZED TRANSITION / TRAJECTORY

**SOURCE:** Dreier (1999), especially p. 20; supporting Dreier (2009) abstract only.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL / PRACTICE-ANALYTIC`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** institutionally organized sequences, positions, age/track arrangements, eligibility rules, expected transitions, and context access.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** institutions prearrange possible pathways and transition points, while subjects develop stances and projections toward them, reappropriate institutional arrangements as vehicles, follow or diverge from expected routes, and move through changing participation configurations.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** differentiated access, expected/required transitions, new obligations/counterparts, temporal sequencing, changes in position, and changing context participation.

**REALIZATION LAW:** an institutional route must exist and be applicable; the person must reach/encounter its transition conditions; the transition must actually occur; and its later developmental consequence must be separately metabolized. A projected or normatively expected transition is not a realized trajectory.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** anticipation, stance, aspiration, interpretation, negotiation, refusal, redirection, and reliance on others’ guidance; subject action can diverge from institutional molds where feasible.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** biography, expectations, practices, relationships, knowledge/capabilities and path dependence after realized transitions.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** explicitly `TRANSITION` and `TRAJECTORY`; a particular transition may become a `TURNING POINT` only if later history establishes that role.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** institution plus linked contexts and relations.

**RECIPROCITY:** subjects may select into later contexts, alter local paths for themselves, or contribute to practice/institutional change; this remains conditional.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** eligibility, historical context, guidance, resources, linked lives, institutional discretion, alternative routes and timing all moderate realization.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `INSTITUTIONAL PATHWAY != ACTOR TRAJECTORY`; expected transition is not actual transition; group differences in access do not license demographic psychological priors; “turning point” cannot be assigned from event magnitude alone.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** structural applicability, mediation-route possibility, interaction topology, context/phase binding.
- **V3 owner:** biography/path dependence can record realized sequence and downstream developmental change.
- **missing connective function:** `INSTITUTIONALLY_ORDERED_ROUTE_TO_ACTOR_SPECIFIC_TRANSITION_AND_TRAJECTORY_REALIZATION`; Phase 0 marks transition as fragment and trajectory structural semantics as underproved.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`.

## M8 — DEVELOPMENTAL REORGANIZATION OF PREMISE–REASON CONSTELLATIONS

**SOURCE:** Holzkamp (2013), pp. 56–58.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL / METHODOLOGICAL`; no universal empirical stage law claimed.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** ongoing dilemmas/dependencies plus currently unrecognized, unavailable, or difficult broader action possibilities.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** reflection, inquiry, dialogue, and practical engagement may change how the subject identifies premises and understands the relation between actions and life conditions, producing a changed reason constellation and possibly broader courses of action.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** altered interpretation of opportunities/constraints, newly actionable routes, revised priorities/relationships, or abandonment of previously locally securing action patterns.

**REALIZATION LAW:** changed understanding alone is insufficient. The premise/reason constellation must actually change in a way that supports different action, different action must be realized, and downstream history must stabilize any durable developmental consequence.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** constitutive; the process is first-person and potentially intersubjective rather than an imposed observer-side correction.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** self-understanding, learned expectations, mediators, practice, relationship, memory, epistemic state and other downstream V3 constructs if separately earned.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `TRANSITION`, possible `TURNING POINT`, `TRAJECTORY`, or `CUMULATIVE PROCESS` depending on realized history.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** subject in social practice; often intersubjective inquiry within relationships/groups.

**RECIPROCITY:** changed action may alter later conditions, but the feedback law is not supplied by M8 alone.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** feasibility of broader action, availability of information/support, actual contradictions in current practice, and ability to communicate/reflect. Articulated reasons may underdescribe preverbal/bodily action.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** no therapeutic/educational intervention is guaranteed to produce generalized agency; no universal developmental stage sequence; no claim that observer-defined “better insight” equals actor change; no direct inference from discourse to durable development.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** provides objective possibility/constraint material only.
- **V3 owner:** learning, memory, uptake/response, biography/path dependence are the closest proven downstream families in the Phase-0 seam map.
- **missing connective function:** exact premise/reason state representation is not proved by the seam map; however this mechanism is downstream of the campaign’s principal missing upstream ecology derivation.

**DISPOSITION:** `DEFER` to V3 normalization unless a later coverage court proves an exact representational loss.

## M9 — ACTION → REPRODUCTION OR MODIFICATION OF LATER CONDITIONS

**SOURCE:** Holzkamp (2013), pp. 56–58; Osterkamp (2017), pp. 29–30; Dreier (1999), p. 6.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** an existing social relation/practice with dependencies, constraints, rules, opportunities and possible alternatives.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** realized individual or collective participation can help reproduce the existing practice/condition or contribute to changing it. Locally securing action may maintain relations that continue to constrain later action; transformative action may alter later possibility/constraint ecology if causally effective.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** later position, dependency, sanction, relation, counterpart access, opportunity/constraint and exposure ecology may change—or remain stabilized—because of prior action.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the action must actually occur, must have a causal path to the relevant relation/condition, and the structural update must be realized before later actor ecology is recomputed. Mere intention, interpretation, or action possibility cannot update the world.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** reasons govern why the subject participates as they do, but structural effects need not coincide with intention; an action may reproduce or alter conditions in ways the subject did not fully foresee.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** indirect and recursive: updated conditions change subsequent experiences and actions, which may later feed V3 development.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE`, `CUMULATIVE PROCESS`, `TRANSITION`; potentially `TURNING POINT` when a realized action changes subsequent trajectory.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** dyad through institution/class relation, including collective settings.

**RECIPROCITY:** this card is the reciprocity mechanism itself.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** action efficacy, institutional rules, power asymmetry, other actors’ responses, path dependence, timing and scale. Not every accommodating act reproduces a macrostructure; not every resistant act changes it.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** no intention→structural-effect shortcut; no observer-side moral classification of action; no assumption that reproduction/change at one scale propagates automatically to another; no automatic developmental consequence from a structural update.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:**
- **PRE-V3 owner:** can represent the structured possibility world before and after lawful update, but Phase 0 does not prove the update producer.
- **V3 owner:** actor action/response and biography can carry realized actor history.
- **missing connective function:** `REALIZED_ACTOR_OR_COLLECTIVE_ACTION_TO_LATER_POSITION_RELATION_EXPOSURE_UPDATE`. Phase 0 explicitly lists prior person/action → later selection/position/exposure and collective action → transformed conditions as unresolved candidate subproblems.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING`.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORETICAL / METHODOLOGICAL`; `PARTIAL_INSPECTION_ONLY`.

## 11. Cross-card terminal separations

The T2 corpus requires the synthesis to preserve all of the following:

```text
GENERALIZED SOCIETAL ACTION POSSIBILITY != ACTUAL SUBJECT ACTION
FORMAL POSITION != ENACTED PARTICIPATION
INSTITUTIONAL PATHWAY != REALIZED TRAJECTORY
SHARED CONDITION != COLLECTIVE ORGANIZATION
COLLECTIVE ORGANIZATION != SUCCESSFUL STRUCTURAL CHANGE
REASON FOR ACTION != DETERMINISTIC CAUSE OF ACTION
ACTION != INTENDED STRUCTURAL EFFECT
STRUCTURAL EFFECT != DURABLE PSYCHOLOGICAL CHANGE
```

These separations are the main protection against both structural determinism and voluntarism. Structure matters because it organizes real scopes, relations, obligations, dependencies and possible routes; agency matters because subjects relate to, select among, coordinate across, reproduce, resist and sometimes transform those conditions; voluntarism is blocked because every action remains bounded by actually available scopes, relations, resources, sanctions and collective capacities.
