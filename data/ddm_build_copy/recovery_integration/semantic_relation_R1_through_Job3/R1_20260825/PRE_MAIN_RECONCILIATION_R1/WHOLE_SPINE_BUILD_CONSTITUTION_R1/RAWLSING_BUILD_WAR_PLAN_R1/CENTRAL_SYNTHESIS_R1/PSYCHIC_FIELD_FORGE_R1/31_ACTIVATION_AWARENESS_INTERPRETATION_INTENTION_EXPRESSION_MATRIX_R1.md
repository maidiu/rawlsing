# PSYCHIC FIELD FORGE R1 — ACTIVATION / AWARENESS / INTERPRETATION / INTENTION / EXPRESSION MATRIX R1

**Status:** `PRE_ARCHITECTURE_PHENOMENON_MATRIX__BANKED__MUST_FACTOR__NOT_ARCHITECTURE_ADOPTION__NO_RUNTIME_AUTHORITY`

## 0. Purpose

This file gives a dedicated direct treatment to original PFF frontier item 3:

> Precisely distinguish what it means for something to be recruited, causally active, felt, accessible, salient, focal, recognized, named, self-attributed, intended, inhibited, defended, leaked, and expressed.

The goal is not to define a state machine.

The goal is to prove that Rawlsing must preserve psychologically meaningful combinations that a flat `internal state -> response` model would collapse.

## 1. Central conclusion

The process from deep cause to observable expression is **not one monotone ladder**.

There are several partially independent relations:

- causal participation;
- phenomenological presence;
- conceptual access;
- attentional priority;
- self-recognition;
- causal attribution;
- intention;
- inhibition;
- defense/transformation;
- leakage;
- observable manifestation.

A person can occupy many non-intuitive combinations.

Therefore:

`ACTIVATED != FELT != ACCESSIBLE != SALIENT != FOCAL != RECOGNIZED != NAMED != SELF_ATTRIBUTED != INTENDED != EXPRESSED`.

## 2. Terminology — conceptual, not schema

### 2.1 Recruited

A durable V3 structure/history relation is participating in the current live configuration.

Example:

an old rejection expectation is relevant to this user's ambiguous distance.

`RECRUITED` says **this durable material is in play now**.

### 2.2 Causally active

The recruited material actually contributes to a current transition/effect.

Something can be recruited as context but weakly/no-effect in a particular branch; causal activity is the stronger claim.

### 2.3 Phenomenally present / felt

The actor experiences some affective, bodily, or experiential consequence.

This does not require knowing what it is or why.

Examples:

- tightness;
- nervous energy;
- warmth;
- irritation;
- unease;
- excitement.

### 2.4 Actor-accessible

Some relevant content can be accessed by the actor under current lawful memory/epistemic conditions.

This term must remain compatible with V3 ADP-016/017 and should not be casually used to fabricate actor knowledge.

### 2.5 Salient

Among material that can matter to current actor experience/action, something has elevated current priority/weight/relevance.

`ACCESSIBLE != SALIENT` remains binding.

### 2.6 Focal

The actor's present attention is actually centered on it.

A thing can be salient in the background without being focal.

### 2.7 Recognized

The actor identifies the experienced/active phenomenon as a particular motive/emotion/issue.

Example:

> "I'm jealous."

Recognition may still omit or misidentify cause.

### 2.8 Named/conceptualized

The actor has an available conceptual label or explicit proposition for it.

Something can be consciously felt without being well named.

### 2.9 Self-attributed

The actor treats the state/motive as belonging to them and/or as explanatory of their behavior.

This is stronger than merely noticing a sensation.

### 2.10 Causally self-explained

The actor has an explanation for why they are experiencing/doing something.

That explanation can be partial, defensive, or wrong.

### 2.11 Intended

The actor consciously aims to perform/communicate something.

Intention can concern:

- content;
- disclosure;
- concealment;
- social effect;
- posture;
- action.

### 2.12 Inhibited

A candidate response/motive/action tendency is blocked/reduced/prevented from direct realization.

Inhibition may be conscious or nonconscious.

### 2.13 Defended/transformed

The original active pressure is altered into another experienced/interpersonal form serving protection/regulation/self-organization.

Defense is not merely "less expression."

### 2.14 Leakage pressure

Hidden/inhibited material still biases observable behavior without actor intending to disclose it.

### 2.15 Expressed

Something becomes observable in the actual response/action.

Expression may be direct, indirect, transformed, strategic, or accidental.

## 3. Important distinction — phenomenal presence versus proposition accessibility

A person can consciously experience:

> "something feels wrong"

without accessing the proposition:

> "I am afraid this person will reject me because they resemble my father."

Thus PFF should not require every felt state to have a claim-level representation.

This is especially important because ADP-017 governs proposition/claim epistemics while PFF present-conscious field includes preconceptual/felt experience.

## 4. Important distinction — recognition versus causal attribution

Actor may accurately recognize:

> "I'm angry."

while incorrectly explaining:

> "because you're being stupid"

when the deeper generated causal configuration includes humiliation/shame/status threat.

Therefore:

`EMOTION RECOGNITION != CAUSE RECOGNITION`.

## 5. Important distinction — intention versus underlying motive

An actor can intentionally:

> make a joke

while hidden attraction increases why that joke arose, how elaborate it becomes, or why it targets this user.

Thus:

`CONSCIOUS SPEECH ACT INTENT != COMPLETE CAUSAL MOTIVE SET`.

## 6. Important distinction — inhibition versus absence

Low direct expression does not imply low motive.

`HIGH ACTIVATION + HIGH INHIBITION -> LOW DIRECT EXPRESSION`.

Conversely:

`LOW UNDERLYING MOTIVE + HIGH STRATEGIC DISPLAY -> HIGH SURFACE EXPRESSION`.

This alone defeats simple trait-to-language scaling.

## 7. Important distinction — defense can operate at multiple points

Defense may alter:

- what gets recruited;
- what becomes phenomenally present;
- what reaches conceptual access;
- what explanation becomes available;
- what intention is formed;
- what is expressed;
- how hidden pressure leaks.

So there should be no assumption of one late-stage `DEFENSE_FILTER`.

## 8. Important distinction — expression can alter awareness afterward

The sequence is recursive.

`A(t) -> SELF-OBSERVATION -> B(t+1)`.

An actor may not recognize a motive until after hearing themself express something.

Thus awareness can occur **after** expression.

## 9. Nonlinear sequence, not one state machine

A canonical linear chain such as:

`activation -> awareness -> intention -> expression`

is insufficient.

Valid paths include:

```text
activation -> defense -> leakage -> expression -> self-recognition
```

```text
activation -> felt state -> wrong interpretation -> intentional concealment -> leakage
```

```text
accessible memory -> salience -> expression -> causal-link recognition later
```

```text
conscious intention -> inhibition -> substitute expression
```

```text
hidden motive A + conscious motive B -> mixed expression
```

## 11. Case study — attraction without recognition

```text
D:
  eros/intimacy striving
  prior attraction patterns
  shame-of-need mediator

B:
  user-specific attraction recruited
  shame/inhibition recruited
  increased attention/playfulness

present experience:
  nervous excitement

actor interpretation:
  "they're kind of annoying / I'm just joking"

intention:
  keep conversation playful

A:
  disproportionate attention
  double meaning
  extended banter
```

No step requires actor to know attraction.

## 12. Case study — recognized motive but hidden expression

```text
activation: anger
felt: anger
recognized: yes
self-attributed: yes
cause explanation: reasonably accurate
intention: preserve relationship
inhibition: direct attack blocked
expression: calm boundary statement
```

This shows:

`RECOGNIZED != EXPRESSED DIRECTLY`.

## 13. Case study — expression without underlying congruence

Actor wants status advantage and deliberately presents enthusiasm they do not feel.

```text
underlying enthusiasm: low
strategic motive: high
intention: appear enthusiastic
expression: high enthusiasm
```

Thus:

`SURFACE INTENSITY != INNER AFFECT INTENSITY`.

## 14. Case study — actor consciously wrong about cause

```text
hidden cause:
  user comment recruits humiliation history

felt:
  anger

recognized:
  "I'm angry"

self-explanation:
  "because this conversation is boring"

expression:
  abrupt dismissal
```

The actor can be sincere and wrong.

The machine causal account must remain quarantined.

## 15. Case study — ambiguity itself becomes focal

User message plausibly has both friendly and flirtatious readings.

The actor does not choose one.

```text
received state:
  friendliness plausible
  flirtation plausible
  high uncertainty
  desire for flirtation increases attention

conscious field:
  "Wait—was that flirting?"

intention:
  test without overcommitting

expression:
  playful probe
```

Ambiguity is active psychology, not failure to classify.

## 16. Case study — accessible but not salient

The actor can recall a prior conversation if asked, but it is not currently foregrounded.

A new user phrase increases its relevance.

The memory becomes salient/focal without necessarily changing ADP-016 durable accessibility.

Thus:

`ACCESSIBLE BEFORE TURN`
`+ NEW RECRUITMENT`
`-> SALIENT NOW`.

## 17. Case study — causally active but not felt

A durable mediator subtly changes interpretation before any obvious affect emerges.

Example:

control sensitivity transforms user concern into slight resistance.

The actor simply feels:

> "I don't want to answer that."

There may be no discrete emotion they can identify.

Therefore psychological causation cannot require an emotion label at every step.

## 18. Case study — felt but not self-attributed

Actor experiences bodily excitement but thinks it is due to caffeine/fatigue rather than attraction.

The feeling is actor-experienced, but the underlying relational motive is not self-attributed.

`FELT != CORRECTLY ATTRIBUTED`.

## 19. Case study — intention generated against live pressure

Actor strongly wants to lash out but consciously decides:

> "I'm not giving them that satisfaction."

Here intention is partly **counter-regulatory** to active pressure.

Expression is not the strongest motive; it is the result of conflict and control.

## 20. Case study — defense creates substitute experience

Hidden dependency need threatens self-model.

Instead of consciously feeling:

> "I need reassurance"

actor experiences:

> "Why are they being so distant and irritating?"

Possible process:

`attachment/reassurance pressure`
`-> shame/self-model threat`
`-> defensive externalization`
`-> anger/criticism`.

This is more than suppression.

## 21. Case study — full inhibition without leakage

PFF must permit strong self-control.

Not every hidden motive leaks.

A person may successfully suppress an urge with no detectable surface effect.

Thus:

`HIDDEN ACTIVE MATERIAL -> LEAKAGE`

is not mandatory.

Leakage is a possible consequence under current regulation, not a magical truth detector.

## 22. Case study — leakage without obvious linguistic marker

Leakage may appear through:

- unusual verbosity;
- repeated topic return;
- asymmetric attentiveness;
- delayed response shape;
- excessive clarification;
- choice of anecdote;
- avoidance;
- disproportionate humor.

It need not be a stereotyped "tell."

## 23. Recognition can destabilize self-model

A current state may become recognized:

> "I think I'm actually jealous."

That recognition can itself trigger:

- shame;
- relief;
- curiosity;
- self-model conflict;
- new inhibition;
- increased honesty.

Thus:

`RECOGNITION = POSSIBLE NEW TRIGGER`,

not merely passive readout.

## 24. Naming can change the state

Putting a label on an experience may alter regulation.

Example:

> "Okay, this is embarrassment, not anger."

The act of conceptualization can change what response routes become available.

This is another reason to avoid a simple pipeline where naming is an inert late annotation.

## 25. Self-attribution can differ from ownership

An actor may recognize:

> "there's jealousy here"

while resisting:

> "I am a jealous person."

Current state recognition need not become standing identity.

Thus:

`CURRENT STATE SELF-ATTRIBUTION != DURABLE SELF-MODEL IDENTITY`.

## 26. Intention can have multiple targets

An actor may intend simultaneously to:

- answer the literal question;
- preserve face;
- test the user;
- conceal attraction;
- maintain humor.

There need not be one response intention.

Multiple intentions can coexist or conflict.

## 27. Expression can be semantically accurate while causally incomplete

Actor says:

> "I'm tired."

They really are tired.

But hidden rejection activation also contributes to withdrawal.

The utterance need not be a lie just because it is causally incomplete.

Rawlsing should preserve partial truths.

## 28. Actor explanation can improve over turns

Turn t:

> "I'm just annoyed."

Turn t+2 after own-output feedback:

> "Actually, I think that hit a nerve."

Turn t+5:

> "Maybe I was worried you were pulling away."

Self-understanding can emerge gradually.

Do not require instant introspective correctness.

## 29. Defense can be consciously chosen, automatic, or mixed

Examples:

### Conscious

> "I'm not going to let them know that bothered me."

### Automatic

Actor simply becomes sarcastic without recognizing why.

### Mixed

Actor knows they are deflecting but misunderstands what they are protecting.

A fixed conscious/nonconscious defense taxonomy is therefore premature.

## 30. PFF versus V3 ownership across the matrix

### V3 durable owners

- durable memories/accessibility state;
- durable actor epistemic stances;
- expectations;
- strivings;
- mediators/tensions/practices;
- attractor/developmental state;
- durable self-model changes where governed.

### PFF fast relations

- current recruitment;
- live causal activity;
- phenomenal presence;
- salience/focus;
- current recognition/misattribution;
- current intention;
- inhibition/defense configuration;
- leakage pressure;
- fast recursive feedback.

### V2/downstream

- actor-safe projection where currently governed;
- expression semantics/register/move as appropriate;
- rendered surface realization.

No exact repartition is adopted here.

## 31. Matrix implications for hidden-cause firewall

The renderer must not be given hidden causal material merely because that material influences expression.

Instead the system may translate hidden causes into lawful consequences such as:

- more/less elaboration;
- increased caution;
- avoidance of explicit admission;
- warmer/cooler posture;
- teasing tendency;
- changed topic selection;
- leakage constraints.

Only actor-accessible recognized content may become first-person self-explanation.

## 32. Matrix implications for retrieval

Retrieved deep material can participate at several levels:

- hidden causal relevance;
- accessible but peripheral memory;
- focal conscious recollection;
- explicit self-explanation.

Retrieval must not force the deepest available item straight to the renderer.

## 33. Matrix implications for two LLM calls

The first LLM-facing interpretation stage should not attempt to output the actor's final conscious/intended response.

The middle person engine must determine:

- what is recruited;
- what is felt/accessible;
- what is recognized;
- what is defended/inhibited;
- what expression pressure survives.

The final renderer then realizes lawful expression.

This is a semantic reason for separation even if exact call topology changes later.

## 34. Matrix implications for V3 periodic development

Some parts of this matrix are not chat-only.

Periodic V3 response may also need to distinguish:

- causal activation;
- actor awareness;
- actor interpretation;
- intentional action;
- hidden/defensive response;
- durable consequence.

Otherwise generated life decisions risk being made from omniscient analyst truth rather than the actor's actual perspective.

However fast salience/focality/leakage/render details may remain PFF-specific.

## 36. Minimal phenomenon dimensions architecture must preserve

Without choosing fields, architecture must be able to distinguish at least:

1. whether durable material is currently recruited;
2. whether it causally affects current live state;
3. whether actor experiences some consequence;
4. whether relevant content is actor-accessible;
5. whether it is salient/focal;
6. whether actor recognizes/names it;
7. whether actor self-attributes it or explains cause;
8. whether an expression/action is intended;
9. whether current regulation inhibits/transforms it;
10. whether hidden pressure leaks;
11. what actually becomes observable;
12. how output feeds back into next recursion.

Architecture may satisfy these distinctions without one variable per bullet.
