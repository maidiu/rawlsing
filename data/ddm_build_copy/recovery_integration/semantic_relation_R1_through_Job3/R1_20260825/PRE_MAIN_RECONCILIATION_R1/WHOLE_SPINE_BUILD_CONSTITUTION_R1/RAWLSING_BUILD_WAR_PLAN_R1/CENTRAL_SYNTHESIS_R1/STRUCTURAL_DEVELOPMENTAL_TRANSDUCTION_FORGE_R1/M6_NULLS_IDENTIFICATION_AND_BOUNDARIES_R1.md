# M6 NULLS, IDENTIFICATION, AND BOUNDARIES R1

STATUS: `NEGATIVE_EVIDENCE_MINED__IDENTIFICATION_CEILINGS_BOUND__GENERALIZATION_LIMITS_PRESERVED`

SOURCE: Kohn & Schooler et al., *Work and Personality* (1983), entire book with special weight on Chapters 5-13 and Appendices A-D.

## 0. Governing rule

The book contains unusually strong observational evidence, but the source's own unresolved issues and nulls forbid converting its structural-equation models into a universal causal machine.

```text
STATISTICAL SIGNIFICANCE != MECHANISM COMPLETENESS
LONGITUDINAL SEM != RANDOMIZED IDENTIFICATION
LATENT FACTOR != CAUSAL PROCESS
NONSIGNIFICANT PATH != PROVED NONEXISTENCE
AVERAGE EFFECT != UNIVERSAL ACTOR EFFECT
```

## 1. Longitudinal identification limits

### 1.1 Two-wave limitation

The core male panel observes 1964 and 1974, with pre-1964 substantive complexity available for the strongest Chapter 5 model. For most job conditions and psychological targets there are only two principal observation waves separated by ten years.

Consequences:

- onset is unobserved;
- exact duration before change is unobserved;
- intervening jobs are incompletely represented;
- daily/weekly recurrence is not continuously recorded;
- contemporaneous model paths can represent processes occurring at any point within the long interval;
- direct person -> later job associations do not expose the external gate.

### 1.2 Exclusion restrictions

Identification depends on assumptions that some earlier variables can affect later analogues but not other later variables directly. Chapter 5 explicitly acknowledges that even with three measurements of substantive complexity, identification remains plausible rather than certain.

M6 ceiling:

`IDENTIFYING_RESTRICTION != OBSERVED NATURAL EXPERIMENT`.

### 1.3 Chapter 6 parameter constraint

The broad reciprocal system cannot estimate all contemporaneous and lagged paths simultaneously. The authors prioritize contemporaneous job-condition relations and test lagged alternatives selectively.

A nonsignificant contemporaneous path is therefore not evidence that a lagged effect is absent. Conversely, a fitted contemporaneous path does not prove literal instantaneous causation.

### 1.4 Residual confounding

The longitudinal models control substantial background information and prior states, but cannot eliminate:

- time-varying health;
- family transitions;
- labor-market shocks;
- unmeasured employer practices;
- changing local opportunities;
- unmeasured motivation/preferences;
- intervening education/training;
- network effects;
- macroeconomic change.

The source does not claim random assignment.

## 2. Measurement nulls and failures

### 2.1 Routinization indicator failure

The proposed second indicator, based on the worker's “unit of work,” cross-loaded on substantive complexity and supervision. The final longitudinal model therefore relies heavily on a single routinization/predictability indicator.

Consequence:

`ROUTINIZATION` is theoretically important but empirically less secure than substantive complexity.

### 2.2 Housework supervision analogue failed

Chapter 10 could not construct a satisfactory household analogue of closeness of supervision from spouse comments/directions. This is a direct measurement failure.

Consequence:

The paid-work supervision mechanism should not be generalized automatically to all activity contexts merely by analogy.

### 2.3 Interview reports are not direct observation

The authors strive to characterize objective conditions, but most measures ultimately depend on respondents' descriptions. Chapter 13 explicitly calls for research jointly measuring objective conditions and subjective appraisals.

Consequence:

`REPORTED_OBJECTIVE_CONDITION != DIRECTLY_OBSERVED_CONDITION`, although the detailed work-content interview is substantially better than an occupation-title proxy.

## 3. Reciprocal nulls

Not every psychological outcome predicts later occupational self-direction.

Important weak/nonsignificant reverse paths include:

- valuation of self-direction for oneself -> OSD: approximately `-.05`, nonsignificant;
- distress -> OSD: approximately `-.04`, nonsignificant;
- trustfulness -> OSD: approximately `.01`, nonsignificant;
- anxiety -> OSD: approximately `-.02`, nonsignificant.

Routinization reverse paths are also weaker and less consistent than flexibility -> complexity.

M6 conclusion:

`PERSON_TO_WORK_RECIPROCITY_IS_TYPED_AND_SELECTIVE`, not generic.

## 4. Substantive-complexity timing null

In Chapter 5, the direct 1964 substantive-complexity -> 1974 ideational-flexibility cross-lag is small (about `.05`) and nonsignificant, while current complexity -> current flexibility is about `.17-.18` and significant.

This is not a failure of the work-effect thesis; it is an important timing result. It undermines a simplistic delayed-only model and supports an ongoing/current exposure interpretation.

Rawlsing implication:

Do not encode the effect as `JOB_EVENT_1964 -> PERSON_CHANGE_1974`. Preserve an interval/recurrent activity history.

## 5. Rejected universal cognitive-mediator hypothesis

Chapter 7 tests whether ideational flexibility generally mediates the effect of occupational self-direction on values/orientations. It does not. The expected mediating pattern fails for several outcomes; self-directed orientation is more central in some models.

Reject:

```text
COMPLEX_WORK
-> IDEATIONAL_FLEXIBILITY
-> ALL VALUES / ORIENTATIONS / DISTRESS
```

Different downstream targets require their own V3 causal histories.

## 6. Work/nonwork anomalies and compensation boundaries

Chapter 9 contains several specification-sensitive findings that should be retained rather than cleaned away:

- closeness of supervision has a small positive direct effect on leisure intellectuality while its indirect effect through low complexity is negative; total effect is slightly negative. The authors treat this as possible modest compensation, not the dominant process;
- physically heavy work predicts somewhat greater leisure intellectuality while dirty work predicts less;
- the contemporaneous direct education effect on 1974 leisure intellectuality is small, negative and nonsignificant; the model is improved by treating education through earlier leisure intellectuality;
- ownership and bureaucratization show anomalous negative lagged effects in one formulation.

These results block an over-neat single-factor account of “good work.”

## 7. Sex/gender and sample boundaries

### 7.1 Male baseline panel

The 1964 national sample contains `3,101` employed civilian men age 16+ working at least 25 hours/week. Women, unemployed men, military personnel and people working fewer hours are excluded.

The sample underrepresents the largest metropolitan areas and, because of selection criteria, is somewhat older, better educated and more often married than the Census category of employed males.

### 7.2 Follow-up

The follow-up frame is limited to baseline men younger than 55 in 1964 so they would generally remain in the labor force ten years later. `687` men were reinterviewed, about `78%` of those selected.

The reinterviewed group shows some baseline psychological differences, though major occupational/stratification relationships replicate.

Boundaries:

- retirement transitions are underrepresented;
- unemployment histories are not the center of the design;
- very old workers are excluded from follow-up by design;
- panel attrition remains possible selection.

### 7.3 Women

The women's evidence comes from wives interviewed in 1974; `555` wives were interviewed and about `269` employed women enter the paid-work analysis. They do not have equivalent 1964 personality measures.

Thus:

`WOMEN_CROSS_SECTIONAL_EXTENSION != MEN_LONGITUDINAL_PANEL`.

The book supports the claim that women's actual job conditions matter psychologically, but it cannot establish equivalent reciprocal timing with the same evidentiary strength.

### 7.4 Gender is not a psychological prior

Observed sex differences in path patterns do not license `SEX/GENDER -> TRAIT`. They point to different work histories, role obligations, task conditions and measurement contexts requiring explicit mediation.

## 8. Historical cohort and period boundary

The principal U.S. data were collected in 1964 and 1974. Chapter 12 reviews cross-national replications, but the book itself cannot prove time-invariance across later labor-market restructuring, digitalization, gig work, remote work or other later institutional forms.

The authors argue that the structural relationships may be general even when the distribution of jobs changes, but Chapter 13 leaves historical generalization open.

Rawlsing ceiling:

Use the mechanism as a typed hypothesis supported in specified contexts; do not freeze 1960s-70s U.S. occupational distributions as universal priors.

## 9. Cross-national boundaries

Chapter 12 finds substantial cross-national support for self-direction/flexibility/value relations, but weaker universality for self-conception and distress. Japanese/Polish findings are not identical to U.S. results.

Therefore:

- complexity/self-direction mechanisms have stronger generality;
- distress and some self-conception effects require greater context qualification;
- cultural/national category itself is never an actor trait prior.

## 10. Occupational-title and aggregate-proxy boundary

Chapter 12 explicitly notes that occupation-level Dictionary of Occupational Titles measures can approximate complexity when individual job content is unavailable, but substantially attenuate relationships compared with person-specific work descriptions.

This is a hard null against title essentialism:

```text
SAME OCCUPATIONAL TITLE
CAN CONTAIN
DIFFERENT ACTOR-SPECIFIC TASK COMPLEXITY / SUPERVISION / ROUTINIZATION.
```

M6 recommends binding invariant:

`OCCUPATION_TITLE != WORK_CONTENT`.

## 11. Routinization boundary

Do not equate “repetition” with developmental impoverishment. The source's mechanism concerns repetition of fixed/predictable low-choice activity. Repeated complex problem solving is also repetition in a temporal sense but has the opposite hypothesized developmental consequence.

Required distinction:

```text
RECURRENCE_FREQUENCY
!=
ACTIVITY_QUALITY / COMPLEXITY / CONTROL
```

D2 must preserve both.

## 12. Supervision boundary

Freedom from close supervision is not sufficient for self-direction. A worker can be unsupervised while executing a mechanically determined routine. Conversely, some complex work can occur within formal hierarchy.

Required distinction:

`FORMAL_OR_RELATIONAL_CONTROL -> ACTUAL DISCRETION CONDITIONS -> EXERCISED JUDGMENT`.

## 13. Distress boundary

Distress is not interchangeable with self-direction, cognitive flexibility, alienation or anxiety. Its work-condition predictors differ, reverse paths are weak, and cross-national generality is less robust.

Reject a generic `WORK_QUALITY -> PERSONALITY_WELLBEING` scalar.

## 14. Family/intergenerational boundary

The book's parental-value work and the serfdom-legacy chapter do not establish direct cultural inheritance.

Chapter 13 acknowledges modest parent-child value correlations and multiple social contexts. Chapter 11 lacks direct observations of the chain from historic regime through family practice to present actor psychology.

Reject:

```text
CLASS_OF_PARENT -> CHILD_TRAIT
ETHNIC/HISTORIC_ORIGIN -> ACTOR_TRAIT
PARENT_VALUE -> CHILD_VALUE AUTOMATICALLY
```

Any intergenerational use requires realized family practices, schooling, interaction, opportunity and later actor uptake.

## 15. Learning-generalization boundary

The authors favor direct learning-generalization, and Chapter 9 provides behavioral evidence outside work. But the source does not directly observe the microprocess by which a work episode changes later off-job conduct.

It also finds limited evidence of compensation in selected relationships.

Verdict:

`LEARNING_GENERALIZATION = WELL_SUPPORTED_INTERPRETATION__NOT_A_SINGLE_DIRECTLY_MEASURED UNIVERSAL OPERATOR`.

Rawlsing should route realized activities to existing V3 learning/practice owners rather than mint `GENERALIZATION()` as a magical seam transform.

## 16. Duration/dose boundary

Chapter 13 explicitly leaves unresolved:

- how fast effects begin;
- whether they accumulate continuously;
- whether thresholds exist;
- whether habituation weakens some effects;
- whether prolonged exposure strengthens others;
- whether anticipation of a condition matters.

Thus D2 needs duration/recurrence representation, but M6 cannot provide universal numeric parameters for it.

## 17. Deliberate job-redesign boundary

The observational finding that job conditions correlate reciprocally with psychological functioning does not prove how deliberate institutional interventions will alter work or person outcomes. Chapter 13 separates explanation of naturally occurring job conditions from successful organizational redesign.

No implementation policy should be inferred automatically.

## 19. Positive boundary-preserving verdict

The source is strongest when Rawlsing keeps the chain decomposed:

```text
STRUCTURAL POSITION
-> CONCRETE JOB CONDITION
-> REALIZED/RECURRENT ACTIVITY OR PERSISTENT CONDITION
-> EXISTING V3 UPTAKE/LEARNING/DEVELOPMENT
-> PERSON-DEPENDENT ACTION
-> EXTERNAL GATE
-> LATER JOB CONDITION
```

M6 has found no null or identification failure that defeats this architecture. The nulls instead specify its required firewalls.
