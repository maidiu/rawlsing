## 0. Purpose

This file deeply examines PFF frontier pocket 18, `anticipation and prospective psychology`.

The prior PFF examinations have largely described how past/deep personhood becomes live in a current interaction. But psychologically realistic action also depends on **expected futures**.

A person may:

- avoid saying something because they expect rejection;
- disclose because they hope for reassurance;
- flirt indirectly to test reciprocation;
- apologize to prevent relationship damage;
- act tough because they expect exploitation if weakness is visible;
- withhold anger because they fear abandonment;
- provoke someone to see whether they care;
- make a promise because of a desired future identity;
- stay silent because they expect escalation;
- overexplain because they expect negative evaluation.

The central questions are:

1. What kinds of expected future consequences must PFF preserve?
2. How do current social readings, V3 expectations/strivings, and live dyadic state generate prospective pressure?
3. How does anticipation differ from downstream response-move selection?
4. How can an actor act on a predicted counterpart response without turning the prediction into fact?
5. How should testing, strategic ambiguity, preemptive defense, hope, dread, and imagined future self fit without introducing a planning gadget?

Current conclusions:

`CURRENT_PSYCHIC_STATE != ONLY_REACTION_TO_PAST/PRESENT`

`EXPECTED_FUTURE_CAN_CAUSE_CURRENT_RECRUITMENT / EXPRESSION = MUST_FACTOR`

`PREDICTED_COUNTERPART_RESPONSE != COUNTERPART_ACTUAL_RESPONSE`

`ANTICIPATION != RESPONSE_MOVE`

`GOAL / STRIVING != PREDICTED_OUTCOME`

`PREDICTED_OUTCOME != DESIRED_OUTCOME`

`FUTURE_SELF / RELATIONSHIP CONSEQUENCE = MUST_FACTOR`

`TESTING / REASSURANCE-SEEKING / PREEMPTIVE DEFENSE = MUST_FACTOR AS PHENOMENA`

`ONE_GENERAL_PLANNING_ENGINE = NOT ADMITTED`

## 1. Governing V3/PFF/V2 boundaries

V3 already owns durable needs, expectations, strivings, relationship history, actor epistemic state, and durable causal consequences.

PFF is examining how those structures become current, relationally specific **prospective pressures**.

V2 CAN-018 separately owns generic response-operation selection over lawful inputs.

ADP-022/023 and CAN-019 separately own uncertainty/disclosure, boundary/face-risk/effort, and relationship-expression constraints.

Therefore PFF should not become a monolithic conversational planner.

Hard distinction:

> PFF may represent why an actor currently expects/fears/hopes for certain consequences and how those expectations shape live psyche; downstream V2 still owns the lawful expression operation and realization.

## 2. First major distinction: expectation, prediction, desire, intention

These are non-equivalent.

### 2.1 Durable expectation

A relatively persistent learned orientation from V3 history.

Example:

`When I show need, people withdraw.`

### 2.2 Current prediction

A situation-specific forecast.

Example:

`If I ask B for reassurance now, B will probably think I am needy.`

### 2.3 Desired outcome

What the actor wants to happen.

Example:

`I want B to reassure me.`

### 2.4 Feared outcome

What the actor wants to avoid.

Example:

`I do not want B to lose respect for me.`

### 2.5 Intention / current chosen aim

What the actor is presently trying to do.

Example:

`I will joke rather than ask directly.`

These can conflict.

`EXPECT X` does not mean `WANT X`.

`WANT X` does not mean `INTEND DIRECTLY TO PURSUE X`.

`PREDICT X` does not mean `KNOW X`.

## 3. Anticipation can recruit psyche before any counterpart response

Example:

A is about to disclose attraction.

Before saying anything:

- A predicts B may reject them;
- predicted rejection recruits shame;
- shame recruits self-concealment;
- attraction remains active;
- A chooses an indirect joke.

The feared event has not occurred.

Yet the imagined/anticipated event has real current causal effect.

Thus:

`PREDICTED_FUTURE_EVENT -> CURRENT_PSYCHIC_EFFECT` is possible without the event being real.

## 4. Anticipation is actor-local and epistemically uncertain

A may confidently predict B will be angry and be completely wrong.

The prediction can still shape A's behavior.

Therefore:

`A_PREDICTION_OF_B != B_FUTURE_STATE`.

`A_CONFIDENCE_IN_PREDICTION != PREDICTION_ACCURACY`.

This inherits the metaperception/actor-opacity law.

## 5. Expected consequences can be social, material, identity, or internal

Prospective psychology is broader than predicting what the counterpart will say.

Possible anticipated consequence domains include:

### 5.1 Counterpart reaction

approval, rejection, anger, amusement, attraction, withdrawal, reassurance, retaliation.

### 5.2 Relationship trajectory

closeness, rupture, commitment, awkwardness, dependency, status shift, repair, future contact.

### 5.3 Material/practical consequence

job loss, resource gain, punishment, opportunity, safety, exposure, obligation.

### 5.4 Social/audience consequence

reputation, embarrassment, status, group belonging, coalition response.

### 5.5 Self-image consequence

`If I ask, I become the kind of person who needs them.`

### 5.6 Future-self consequence

`If I say yes, I may regret it.`

`If I leave now, I will think of myself as a coward.`

### 5.7 Internal affective consequence

`If I let myself think about this, I may fall apart.`

`If I confront him, I will feel guilty.`

These can combine and conflict.

## 6. Prospect can be appetitive, aversive, or mixed

A future state may simultaneously attract and threaten.

Examples:

- intimacy promises connection and exposure;
- promotion promises status and pressure;
- confession promises relief and shame;
- confrontation promises justice and relationship risk;
- moving away promises autonomy and grief.

Thus:

`EXPECTED_FUTURE_VALUE != ONE_VALENCE`.

Prospective conflict can reproduce the same nonlinear approach/avoidance structure already banked for current states.

## 7. Anticipation can alter current perception

Expected outcomes can bias how ambiguous present cues are interpreted.

Example:

A expects rejection.

B gives a short answer.

A's forecast makes `B is pulling away` more recruitable than `B is busy`.

Thus:

`DURABLE EXPECTATION + CURRENT FUTURE MODEL -> RECEIVER TRANSFORM`.

Anticipation is not only downstream planning; it can reach back into perception/recruitment.

## 8. Testing behavior is a core prospective phenomenon

An actor may take an action partly to obtain information about the counterpart.

Examples:

- tease to see whether B reciprocates;
- withdraw to see whether B pursues;
- disclose a small vulnerability to test safety;
- mention a rival to test jealousy;
- set a boundary to test respect;
- ask an indirect question to test interest;
- make a status challenge to test submission/resistance.

Testing involves:

`UNCERTAINTY`
+
`DESIRE TO LEARN`
+
`ACTION CHOSEN TO PROVOKE DIAGNOSTIC RESPONSE`.

This does not mean every ambiguous behavior is secretly a test.

`TESTING = POSSIBLE FUNCTION`, not a universal explanation.

## 9. Tests can contaminate the evidence they seek

A critical dyadic point:

The testing act can change B's response.

Example:

A fears abandonment and withdraws to see if B cares.

B interprets withdrawal as disinterest and gives space.

A reads the lack of pursuit as proof B does not care.

Thus:

`TEST -> COUNTERPART EFFECT -> APPARENT CONFIRMATION`.

The result is real but not clean evidence of the original hypothesis.

This links anticipation to self-confirming dyadic loops.

## 10. Reassurance seeking is more than desire for reassurance

A may seek reassurance because:

- current fear is high;
- durable expectation predicts abandonment;
- B's answer is expected to reduce uncertainty;
- the actor hopes reassurance will regulate them;
- the actor also feels shame about needing reassurance.

Repeated reassurance can temporarily soothe while potentially reinforcing the future need to seek it, depending on actual dynamics.

No universal reassurance law is adopted.

The key is the prospective loop:

`fear -> prediction -> reassurance-seeking -> response -> update / shame / temporary regulation`.

## 11. Preemptive defense is future-oriented defense

Defense need not wait for actual injury.

Examples:

- reject before being rejected;
- mock before being mocked;
- dominate before being controlled;
- withdraw before dependence forms;
- confess a flaw first to control how it is exposed;
- minimize success before others can envy/criticize it;
- become angry before vulnerability is visible.

Thus:

`ANTICIPATED_THREAT -> DEFENSE_BEFORE_THREAT_OCCURS`.

This is particularly important in relationship chemistry.

## 12. Impression management is prospective social regulation

Performance/self-presentation often depends on the desired future model in another person's mind.

Examples:

`I want B to think I am competent.`

`I do not want B to see I care.`

`I want the group to perceive me as calm.`

`I want her to realize I am desirable without seeming needy.`

This requires:

- actor model of counterpart/audience;
- metaperception;
- desired future perception;
- action designed to influence that perception.

PFF must preserve this causal logic without treating predicted audience perception as actual audience state.

## 13. Strategic ambiguity is fundamentally prospective

Ambiguity can preserve multiple future branches.

A flirtatious joke may:

- invite reciprocation;
- permit retreat if rejected;
- protect status;
- gather information;
- sustain chemistry.

The actor may prefer ambiguity not because they are confused, but because it preserves optionality.

`AMBIGUITY_CAN_BE_INTENTIONALLY PROSPECTIVE`.

This is distinct from uncertainty-induced ambiguity.

## 14. Delayed consequences matter

Actors often act against immediate gratification because of longer-range expectations.

Examples:

- withhold anger to protect a valued relationship;
- leave an attractive situation to preserve fidelity;
- tell painful truth to preserve future trust;
- endure embarrassment for career opportunity;
- refuse reassurance-seeking to reduce dependency;
- apologize despite immediate pride injury.

Therefore:

`CURRENT_STRONGEST_AFFECT != ACTION_CONTROLLER`.

Future-valued commitments/strivings can dominate immediate pressure.

## 15. Future self is a psychologically meaningful object without needing a literal sub-agent

Actors can imagine:

- who they want to become;
- what they will regret;
- what future version of themselves would think;
- whether current action fits their life trajectory.

Examples:

`I don't want to become someone who cheats.`

`I will hate myself tomorrow if I say this.`

`This is the kind of risk I said I wanted to take.`

This is a prospective relation to standing self-model and goals, not proof of a separate “future self” component.

## 16. Hope and dread can persist without a concrete prediction

Some anticipation is diffuse:

- hope something develops;
- dread an impending conversation;
- sense that a relationship is changing;
- fear unspecified exposure;
- expect “something bad” without a precise proposition.

PFF must not require every prospective state to be a fully articulated proposition.

`PROSPECTIVE_ORIENTATION != ALWAYS EXPLICIT FORECAST`.

## 17. Uncertainty can increase prospective activation

An unknown future can be more activating than a known one.

Examples:

- uncertain reciprocation sustains attention;
- waiting for a diagnosis/interview result dominates thought;
- ambiguous silence maintains vigilance;
- uncertain status threat leads to monitoring/testing.

Thus:

`MORE_UNCERTAINTY` does not always mean `LESS_EFFECT`.

Again, confidence and intensity remain separate.

## 18. Anticipation can be domain-specific and target-specific

A trusts B professionally but expects romantic rejection.

A expects a parent to forgive anger but not sexual disclosure.

A expects a boss to reward competence but punish disagreement.

Therefore:

`GLOBAL_EXPECTATION_OF_PERSON = TOO_COARSE`.

Prospective models must preserve target/domain/context.

## 19. Anticipation can itself become a live residue

After an unresolved turn, the actor may carry:

- `B may answer later`;
- `I still expect an apology`;
- `I think she may leave`;
- `I need to find out whether he noticed`.

This is not merely lingering affect; it is an **open future branch** that organizes attention and later interpretation.

This supports the time/persistence finding that unresolved possibility is a legitimate live residue.

## 20. Actual outcome should update but need not instantly erase prior expectation

If A expects rejection and B responds warmly, several outcomes are possible:

- expectation updates quickly;
- A distrusts the warmth;
- A feels relief;
- A feels shame at having expected rejection;
- A reinterprets B's warmth as manipulation;
- A temporarily opens but remains sensitized.

Thus:

`DISCONFIRMING_EVIDENCE != AUTOMATIC EXPECTATION RESET`.

Operating condition, history, and defensive organization matter.

Durable expectation update remains V3-bound.

## 21. Anticipatory control can become self-defeating

Examples:

- overpreparing makes A seem stiff;
- concealment makes B suspicious;
- reassurance testing pushes B away;
- preemptive dominance elicits resistance;
- excessive apology lowers perceived confidence;
- avoiding conflict prevents repair.

The strategy can produce the future it was trying to prevent.

This is another dyadic self-confirmation pathway.

## 22. Enneagram contribution: pursuit strategy and frustration response

The Enneagram source theory is useful here as internal logic.

Hornevian-like pursuit patterns can be reinterpreted as recurring ways of trying to move toward a desired future:

- press/assert;
- earn/accommodate;
- withdraw/disengage.

Harmonic-like frustration patterns can be reinterpreted as characteristic responses when the expected/desired future fails:

- positive reframing;
- competency/control;
- emotional/reactive intensification.

Rawlsing should not preserve the labels as type law.

The valuable question is:

> How does this actor characteristically attempt to cause desired outcomes, and what happens when the anticipated pathway fails?

This can likely stitch onto V3 strivings + PFF live transition dynamics without new machinery.

## 23. Anticipation must not become unrestricted simulation of the counterpart

PFF does not need to enumerate all futures or run a complete agent simulation for every turn.

The social-model examination already favored sparse relevance-bound modeling.

The same principle applies prospectively:

> Model those future consequences that are causally relevant to the actor's current motives, fears, obligations, self-model, and relationship.

`PROSPECTIVE_MODEL != WORLD SIMULATOR`.

`MORE FUTURE BRANCHES != MORE PSYCHOLOGICAL REALISM`.

## 24. Prediction may be explicit or implicit

An actor may consciously think:

`If I tell him, he'll leave.`

Or may simply become inhibited without articulating the forecast.

Thus:

`ANTICIPATORY_CAUSATION != NECESSARILY CONSCIOUS PREDICTION`.

Durable learned expectations may shape current transition propensity below full conscious awareness.

This must preserve actor opacity.

## 25. Expression formation consumes anticipation but downstream V2 still selects expression

PFF may establish live facts such as:

- actor fears B will interpret disclosure as neediness;
- actor hopes continued engagement will elicit reassurance;
- actor wants to test reciprocation while preserving deniability;
- actor anticipates public face loss.

These become bounded reasons/constraints approaching V2.

PFF should not directly output:

`RESPONSE_MOVE = DEFLECT`

unless future architecture explicitly assigns such ownership.

Existing V2 response/disclosure/boundary owners remain separate.

## 26. Anticipation and actor epistemic stance must remain separate

A can predict:

`B will reject me`

while knowing that this is only a prediction.

PFF must not turn forecast into actor knowledge of B's future mental state.

`PREDICTION != KNOWLEDGE`.

`HIGH_CONFIDENCE_FORECAST != FUTURE FACT`.

The expression firewall must preserve this distinction.

## 27. Self-output feedback and anticipation form a tight loop

A says something.

Immediately:

- A predicts B's response;
- that forecast changes embarrassment, regret, hope, or defense;
- A may prepare repair/doubling-down before B answers;
- B's actual response later updates the forecast.

Thus:

`SELF_OUTPUT -> PROSPECT -> LIVE PFF UPDATE -> COUNTERPART RESPONSE -> NEW PROSPECT`.

This is a central mechanism of conversational continuity.

## 28. No-gadget semantic remainder test

Before architecture introduces a dedicated prospective-planning subsystem, attempt to preserve the required phenomena through:

1. V3 durable expectations/strivings/goals/obligations;
2. actor-local current social reading/metaperception;
3. PFF current conflict/defense state;
4. current dyadic coupling/history;
5. current live candidate predictions/hopes/fears;
6. expression constraints handed to existing V2 owners;
7. actual outcome returning as new input;
8. V3 admission for durable expectation/goal/self-model change.

Only a surviving semantic remainder may justify new machinery.

## 29. Hard negatives

Reject unless independently justified:

- prediction treated as counterpart fact;
- one expected-value scalar controlling all action;
- PFF replacing CAN-018 response-move authority;
- every behavior assigned a strategic future goal;
- every indirect statement treated as testing;
- uncertainty treated as weak causation;
- all future reasoning required to be conscious proposition-level thought;
- unrestricted world simulation per turn;
- type label -> fixed strategy table;
- future self implemented as a separate pseudo-agent by default;
- failed prediction instantly erased from durable expectation;
- reassurance always interpreted as healthy regulation;
- public statement automatically treated as commitment belief;
- anticipated relationship outcome promoted to relationship truth;
- current avoidance automatically inferred to be fear of future harm.

Any successful PFF architecture must eventually preserve:
