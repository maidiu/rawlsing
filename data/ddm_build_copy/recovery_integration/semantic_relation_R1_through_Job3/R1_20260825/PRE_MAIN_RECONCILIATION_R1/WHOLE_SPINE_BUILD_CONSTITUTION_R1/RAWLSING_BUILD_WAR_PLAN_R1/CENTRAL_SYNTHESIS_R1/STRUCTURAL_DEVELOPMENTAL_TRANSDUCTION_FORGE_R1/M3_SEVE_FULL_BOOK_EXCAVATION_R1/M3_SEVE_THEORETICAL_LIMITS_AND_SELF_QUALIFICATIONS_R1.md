# M3 SÈVE THEORETICAL LIMITS AND SELF-QUALIFICATIONS R1

STATUS: `COMPLETE__SOURCE_INTERNAL_LIMITS_COURT_R1`

This file treats contradiction, tentativeness and admitted gaps as first-class evidence rather than apologetic footnotes.

## 1. Chapter IV is not established science

**pp. 297–303:** Sève announces Chapter IV as “indicative hypotheses.” He says the experimental value remains to be demonstrated in practice by specialists using tested scientific procedures. He describes the method as theoretical conjecture plus a semi-empirical route, not deduction from historical materialism.

**Binding M3 ceiling:** every acts/capacities, use-time, P/N, reproduction, progress-rate and crisis claim in Ch. IV remains `INDICATIVE_HYPOTHESIS` unless separately supported; this lane supplies no such external validation.

## 2. Later postscripts do not remove the ceiling

**Second-edition Postscript pp. 421–438:** the hypothetical/programmatic status remains.

**Third-edition Postscript pp. 456–460:** Sève restates several Chapter IV hypotheses but again says experimental validation is required and that the scientific theory of personality substantially belongs to the future.

**pp. 487–488:** he accepts the description of the book as a philosopher's preface and says the hypotheses in the last chapter were purposely indicative; concrete scientific representation must go far beyond them.

## 3. Use-time is explicitly underdeveloped

**pp. 333–348:** use-time is introduced as a hypothesis; capitalism-specific topology is called schematic/hypothetical; the diagrams are not a typology.

**p. 503 n.90:** Sève warns that use-time is deceptively simple and would lose scientific meaning if applied merely as an empirical time-budget concept without deeper theoretical structure.

**M3 disposition:** retain finite-time/allocation/provenance requirements; reject a Sève use-time scalar/module.

## 4. Sector I / sector II is not a clean empirical partition

**pp. 313–315:** Sève says acts can belong to both sectors and that in a sense all activity includes both learning and knowing. Concrete classification difficulties are acknowledged at inception.

**M3 disposition:** retain functional distinction “capacity-forming contribution vs capacity-using contribution”; reject hard two-sector ontology or automatic learning inference.

## 5. Economic analogies are high-risk

The book compares capacities to fixed capital, simple/expanded reproduction, organic composition and a falling rate of progress.

**pp. 486–487:** reported GDR criticism questions whether these economic/psychological parallelisms have well-founded content; surplus-value analogy is specifically criticized, and even the more plausible analogies require qualification.

**p. 495:** Sève denies that he intends mechanical parallelism, which itself confirms the danger of literal transfer.

**M3 disposition:**

- `FIXED_CAPITAL_OF_PERSONALITY` — `REJECT_AS_RAWLSING_OBJECT`.
- `P/N` — `DEFER/REJECT_AS_UNVALIDATED_SCALAR`.
- `FALLING_RATE_OF_PROGRESS` — `REJECT_AS_GENERAL_LAW`.
- simple/expanded reproduction — retain only as loose schema for maintenance/development vs no net change.

## 6. Personality topology is not a validated architecture

**pp. 264–275:** topology is relational/processual, not a substantial model, and is historically specific.

**pp. 336–348:** no universal topology; four-cycle capitalism diagram explicitly schematic/hypothetical and explicitly not a four-term typology.

**pp. 490–493:** Sève admits vacillation between a restricted social-relations kernel and a global concept of the total individual.

**M3 disposition:** extract process/temporal constraints only; do not create Sève-specific modules or personality classes.

## 7. Reciprocity is narrower than a dialectical slogan

**pp. 465–468:** Sève rejects the easy formula that because social relations determine the individual, the individual determines social relations “vice versa.” He distinguishes psychological individuality from socially situated historical action. People can transform relations as producers, citizens, union/party members, classes, etc., in determinate conditions.

**M3 disposition:** this is strong negative evidence against a direct V3-person-state → structural-state edge. Any D3 path must include realized action, external/institutional response, competent social/world authority and provenance.

## 8. Biography/personality terminology remains unsettled

**pp. 489–493; p. 503 n.84:** Sève acknowledges a real ambiguity between a restricted notion of personality (socially specific kernel) and a global notion (whole concrete individual). He floats “science of biography” for global knowledge but admits the naming problem remains.

**M3 disposition:** do not mint a new `BIOGRAPHY_SCIENCE` runtime owner from terminology. Reuse ordered history/trajectory semantics.

## 9. Underdeveloped domains admitted by Sève

**p. 488:** Sève acknowledges insufficient concrete knowledge of socialist conditions, limited access to socialist psychological literature, and disproportionate focus on infrastructure while consciousness/superstructures are underdeveloped.

**M3 disposition:** no attempt to fill those gaps from model knowledge. In particular, do not infer a complete theory of motive, consciousness, defense, meaning, or ideology from this book.

## 10. Strong rhetoric that must not become Rawlsing law

Several passages move from theoretical mechanism to broad political/generalizing claims. The most important M3 example is around pp. 374–375, where Sève links militant life, work, and supposedly “superior” development in class-political terms.

This is not experimentally established in the book and sits uneasily with his own anti-typological and singular-biography commitments.

**Binding rejection:**

```text
WORKER / PROLETARIAN / MILITANT / CLASS POSITION
!= PSYCHOLOGICAL SUPERIORITY
!= DEVELOPMENTAL PRIOR
```

## 11. Needs theory remains conceptually delicate

Sève rejects need as the sole basic motor and insists developed needs are produced through activity; later he also warns that felt/declared needs and objective developmental requirements may diverge.

**M3 disposition:** do not import “need” as an upstream social-ecology output. Preserve objective resource/dependency/activity conditions upstream and let competent V3 motivational owners adjudicate person-state consequences.

## 12. Biological/social articulation remains incomplete

**pp. 486–493:** critics question biological-social articulation; Sève agrees global personality analysis includes biological determinations and says the concrete science still must be constructed.

**M3 disposition:** no purely social explanation is allowed to erase biological competence/constraint owners, and this source does not license Rawlsing to redesign them.

## 13. Methodological ambition exceeds evidence

Sève's strongest enduring contribution for M3 is **problem decomposition**, not validated parameterization:

- social forms are not personalities;
- objective relations must become concrete activity;
- actual activity occurs in finite time;
- acts and capacities are recursively related in theory;
- biography is singular ordered history;
- social change by persons is mediated through socially situated action.

The book does not establish quantitative update rules, thresholds, universal stage laws, or causal effect sizes.
