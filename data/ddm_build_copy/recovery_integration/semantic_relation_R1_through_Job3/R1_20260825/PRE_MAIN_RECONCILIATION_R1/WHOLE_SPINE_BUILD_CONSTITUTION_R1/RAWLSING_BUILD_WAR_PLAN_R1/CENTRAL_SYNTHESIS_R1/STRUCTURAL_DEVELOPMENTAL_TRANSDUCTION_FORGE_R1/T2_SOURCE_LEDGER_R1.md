## 1. Primary sources inspected in substantive detail

### S1 — Klaus Holzkamp (1990)

**Citation:** Klaus Holzkamp, “Worauf bezieht sich das Begriffspaar »restriktive/verallgemeinerte Handlungsfähigkeit«? Zu Maretzkys vorstehenden »Anmerkungen«,” *Forum Kritische Psychologie* 26 (1990), 35–45.

**Access inspected:** primary-host journal PDF at `kritische-psychologie.de`; article landing page and PDF.

**Exact inspected locations and retained claims:**

- **p. 35:** the restrictive/generalized distinction is located on the mediation level of **subjective reasons for action**; “restrictive agency” is a subjectively grounded alternative of coping with limitations without expanding control over relevant life conditions, within existing possibilities and power relations.
- **pp. 35–36:** the distinction is formulated from the generalized first-person standpoint (“je meinem” / each-my-own), not as a third-person sorting of people.
- **pp. 36–38:** social-structural categories such as rulers/oppressed/capitalists are not licensed as direct psychological classifications. Restrictive/generalized action alternatives concern concrete subject situations, not fixed kinds of persons.
- **pp. 38–40:** the process is an alternative in individual life practice/biography under threatened or limited agency, not a typology of opportunists versus resisters and not itself a law of historical movement.
- **pp. 40–41:** historically specific, position-specific slices of societal meaning structures may enter the subject’s reasons as premises; the historical world is therefore mediated into action through subject-relative premise constellations rather than directly determining action.

**Claim status:** `THEORETICAL / CONCEPTUAL`; not treated as experimental or population-causal evidence.

**Use in T2:** primary authority for cards M1–M3 and M9.

### S2 — Klaus Holzkamp (2013 English selected writings)

**Citation:** Klaus Holzkamp, *Psychology from the Standpoint of the Subject: Selected Writings of Klaus Holzkamp*, ed. Ernst Schraube and Ute Osterkamp (Palgrave Macmillan, 2013), DOI `10.1057/9781137296436`.

**Inspected sections:**

- “What Could a Psychology from the Standpoint of the Subject Be?”, pp. 46–59, especially **pp. 47–48** and **pp. 56–58**.
- “Psychology: Social Self-Understanding on the Reasons for Action in the Conduct of Everyday Life,” pp. 233–341, especially **pp. 286–287**.

**Retained claims:**

- **pp. 47–48:** actions are not directly determined by circumstances. Societal meaning structures become action-relevant when the subject takes determinate aspects as **premises** for reasons. Subjective reasons therefore mediate between societal meanings and individual life activity.
- **pp. 47–48:** which action is grounded depends on the subject’s standpoint and perceived life interests, including maintaining or extending influence over life conditions and life quality; this is not an observer-side probability assignment.
- **pp. 56–58:** premise–reason relations can themselves change over time through the subject’s engagement with dilemmas, dependencies, and broader action possibilities; this is a theoretical-developmental proposal, not a demonstrated universal stage law.
- **pp. 286–287:** societal meaning structures designate possibilities for action, but an actually performed action cannot be inferred from their existence. Real action requires that relevant meanings become premises in a reason relation and that the subject act.

**Claim status:** `THEORETICAL / METHODOLOGICAL`.

**Use in T2:** primary authority for cards M1, M3 and M8; reinforces the required separation `OBJECTIVE POSSIBILITY != PREMISE != REASON != ACTION`.

### S3 — Ute Osterkamp (2017)

**Citation:** Ute Osterkamp, “Widerspruch zwischen Anspruch und Wirklichkeit in der Kritischen Psychologie,” *Forum Kritische Psychologie* 59 (2017), 27–45.

**Access inspected:** primary-host journal PDF at `kritische-psychologie.de`.

**Exact inspected locations and retained claims:**

- **p. 27:** persons do not merely conform to given life circumstances; within historically available scope they may participate in shaping them, while needs and understandings themselves develop through this process.
- **p. 28:** a direct individual–society determination model misses the subject’s conscious relation to and possible influence on life conditions.
- **p. 29:** action is neither determined by personal traits nor simply by external conditions or the interests of dominant others. The specifically human possibility is to relate to conditions and, within limits, act upon them.
- **p. 29:** meaningful expansion of influence over conditions is frequently supra-individual: it requires action with others and is a task constrained by objective and subjective resistances, not an automatic consequence of insight.
- **pp. 29–30:** restrictive/generalized agency is a double possibility: securing one’s position within existing relations versus acting toward greater common influence over the conditions that determine action.
- **p. 30:** self-determination is linked to participation in determining the conditions that determine action; individual experiences and capacities may become communicable bases for coordinated change, but coordination and success must still be achieved.

**Claim status:** `THEORETICAL / PROGRAMMATIC`.

**Use in T2:** primary authority for cards M3, M4 and M9.

### S4 — Ole Dreier (1999)

**Citation:** Ole Dreier, “Personal Trajectories of Participation across Contexts of Social Practice,” *Outlines. Critical Practice Studies* 1(1) (1999), 5–32, DOI `10.7146/ocps.v1i1.3841`.

**Access inspected:** primary journal PDF.

**Exact inspected locations and retained claims:**

- **p. 6:** subjects encounter and realize societal possibilities in concrete contexts of participation. Positions differ in scopes of possibilities, concerns, obligations, and influence. Participation contains a duality between acting within extant limits and contributing to their change.
- **p. 10:** participation varies by duration and recurrence and across contexts. Contexts contain differentiated positions, relations, scopes, concerns, standards and rules; conduct is not mere rule execution because subjects interpret and may depart from standards.
- **p. 14:** persons compose complex personal practices across relations, commitments, places and rhythms; they develop and adopt stances and find premises of action that connect participations across times and places. Dreier distinguishes a location from the social position occupied within it.
- **p. 20:** institutions may prearrange trajectories and transitions (for example, age- or track-ordered educational pathways), but persons develop positions and stances toward them, reappropriate institutional landscapes, and may follow, redirect, or fail to realize projected transitions. Trajectories are configurations of changing participation rather than mechanically executed institutional scripts.

**Claim status:** `THEORETICAL / PRACTICE-ANALYTIC`; examples are not treated here as experimental causal estimates.

**Use in T2:** primary authority for cards M2 and M5–M7.
