# M4_COLLECTIVE_AGENCY_AND_RECIPROCITY_COURT_R1

STATUS: `M4_D3_SOURCE_COURT_COMPLETE_R1`

QUESTION:

> Does *Grundlegung der Psychologie* provide original-text support for subjects acting, often with others, to alter the conditions of action, and what gates must intervene before changed social conditions may be asserted?

PRIMARY SOURCE: Ch. 5 pp. 191–203; Ch. 6 p. 238; Ch. 7 pp. 353–355, 370–401; Ch. 8 relevant institutional passages.

---

## 1. Court holding

**D3 survives.**

Holzkamp repeatedly treats individuals as both:

1. confronted by objective societal relations and conditions; and
2. participants in their reproduction and possible change.

However, the source does **not** license:

```text
SUBJECT WANTS CHANGE
-> STRUCTURE CHANGED
```

nor

```text
COLLECTIVE EXISTS
-> STRUCTURAL EFFECT SUCCEEDS
```

The strongest source-supported normalized chain is:

```text
SUBJECTIVE GROUNDS / ACTION PROJECT
        ↓
REALIZED ACTION
        ↓
COORDINATION / ASSOCIATION WITH OTHERS WHERE REQUIRED
        ↓
ORGANIZED PRACTICE / COUNTER-POWER / USE OF REAL MEANS
        ↓
ACTUAL RELATION WITH COUNTERPARTS / INSTITUTIONS / POWER HOLDERS
        ↓
REALIZED EXTERNAL RESPONSE AND EFFECT
        ↓
REPRODUCED / MODIFIED SOCIAL RELATION OR CONDITION
        ↓
LATER POSITION / POSSIBILITY ECOLOGY CHANGES
        ↓
SUBJECTIVE CONSEQUENCES ONLY THROUGH NEW MEDIATION
```

The middle “external response/effect” gates are indispensable Rawlsing architecture, even though Holzkamp does not formulate them as a software transaction protocol.

## 2. Original-text bases for reciprocity

### 2.1 Individual activity participates in reproduction/change

**Source:** Ch. 5 p. 191.

The individual finds societal relations as supra-individual objective facts, realizes embodied possibilities/necessities in personal life activity, and thereby participates in the reproduction and change of societal relations.

**Source status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

**Court significance:** reciprocal direction is not imported from a secondary school summary; it is internal to the book’s basic mediation conception.

### 2.2 Position conditions capacity to influence/change structures

**Source:** Ch. 5 pp. 195–198.

Holzkamp states that historically specific social positions affect how and to what degree individuals can gain influence over the social process and their own conditions and may contribute collectively to changing the structures that define the position.

**Source status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

**Court significance:** reciprocal potential is position- and formation-specific. Generic personal “agency” is insufficient.

### 2.3 Organized association is part of actual freedom

**Source:** Ch. 7 p. 354.

The dimensions and extent of the actor’s possibility to expand control depend on objective life conditions, including possibilities of organized association (`organisierter Zusammenschluß`) for expansion of collective self-determination.

**Source status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

**Court significance:** the route from individual action to condition change may require organized capacities external to the single person.

### 2.4 Restrictive/generalized alternative turns on collective practical possibility

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 370–375.

Generalized action-capacity (`verallgemeinerte Handlungsfähigkeit`) cannot be reduced to an attitude of wanting broad change. Under concrete danger/dependency, it becomes subjectively functional only where a path of cooperation/association/counter-power can be experienced as practically possible. Where such a route is absent from the actor’s real subjective possibility space, restrictive action-capacity may be the subjectively grounded alternative.

**Source status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

**Court significance:** a collective route has both objective and subject-relative prerequisites.

### 2.5 Restrictive action can recursively narrow later possibilities

**Source:** Ch. 7 pp. 377–378.

This is the strongest concrete reciprocity mechanism in the book. Instrumentalizing others may temporarily secure individual control but reciprocally produces isolation and reduces the actor’s future basis for cooperative control. Holzkamp explicitly describes the future possibility space as narrowed by the realized action strategy.

**Source status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION`.

**Court significance:**

```text
ACTION
-> RELATIONAL CONSEQUENCE
-> LATER POSSIBILITY SPACE
```

is source-supported.

### 2.6 `Begreifen` / generalized action includes practical historical participation

**Source:** Ch. 7 roughly pp. 395–401.

Holzkamp frames the subject as a passive-active part of historical relations: conditioned by them, yet needing to help determine them jointly with others if the conditions of human life are to be expanded. The question becomes not only whether expansion is possible but how — by what means, alliances and time perspective.

**Source status:** `THEORETICAL_DERIVATION / PROGRAMMATIC_CLAIM`.

**Court significance:** collective action requires practical means and alliance base; cognition alone does not update the world.

## 3. Collective subject prohibition

**Source:** Ch. 6 p. 238 and surrounding discussion of intersubjectivity.

Holzkamp rejects treating collective consciousness or a collective subject as an entity with psychological subjectivity independent of individuals. Collective formations are constituted by subjects relating/acting together around objective interests and relations.

Rawlsing prohibition:

```text
COLLECTIVE_ORGANIZATION
!= COLLECTIVE_MIND
```

D3 must therefore represent at least:

- participating actors;
- organizational/coordination relations;
- objective shared/overlapping interests where relevant;
- resources and means;
- counterpart/institutional relations;
- realized external acts/responses;
- resulting social facts.

It must not create a supra-individual motive/belief state unless another lawful owner separately defines such an organizational state without psychologizing the collectivity.

## 4. D3 gate court

The frozen candidate D3 requires:

```text
V3/actor action
-> realized external action/event
-> institutional/counterpart/collective response/gate
-> admitted change in relation/position/rule/resource/network
-> new social fact with provenance
-> later ecology derivation
```

M4 tests each link.

### Gate G1 — subject action must become an externalized act

**Source support:** strong.

Holzkamp distinguishes reasons, action and conditions. A thought/reason does not alter social conditions without action/practice.

**Verdict:** `REQUIRED`.

### Gate G2 — other persons/collective coordination where causal mechanism requires it

**Source support:** strong for generalized condition-control; pp. 354, 370–401.

**Verdict:** `REQUIRED_WHERE_RELATIONAL_OR_COLLECTIVE`.

### Gate G3 — real means/resources/organization

**Source support:** strong at theoretical/programmatic level: means, alliance base, counter-power, objective scope of possibilities.

**Verdict:** `REQUIRED`.

### Gate G4 — counterpart/institutional response

**Source support:** indirect but necessary. Holzkamp’s theory is relational and power-sensitive, but *Grundlegung* does not provide a generic institutional response calculus.

**Verdict:** `RAWLSING_INFERENCE_STRONGLY_REQUIRED_BY_NONVOLUNTARISM`.

### Gate G5 — causal admission of changed external condition

**Source support:** the book distinguishes action from conditions and does not equate intention with effect.

**Verdict:** `REQUIRED`.

### Gate G6 — provenance preservation

**Source support:** no technical provenance formalism in Holzkamp.

**Verdict:** `RAWLSING_ARCHITECTURAL_DISCIPLINE`, compatible with and required by the frozen Rawlsing evidence regime, but not a Holzkamp term.

### Gate G7 — later actor ecology must be rederived from new social facts

**Source support:** strong in general reciprocity and p. 377–378 example.

**Verdict:** `REQUIRED`.

## 5. Strongest positive D3 grammar

```text
ACTOR A:
  premise/ground/action project
  + objective position/resources/constraints
        ↓
  realized action A1
        ↓
  [if collective route]
  association + coordination + organization + means
        ↓
  counterpart/institution/power-holder encounters the action
        ↓
  response / nonresponse / sanction / concession / cooperation / conflict
        ↓
  externally realized social effect E
        ↓
  lawfully admitted new or modified social fact F'
        ↓
  later D1/D2 derives actor-relative ecology from F'
        ↓
  V3 may receive new premise-relevant/experienced conditions later
```

Failure paths must remain first-class:

```text
ACTION WITH NO RESPONSE
ACTION BLOCKED
COLLECTIVE FAILS TO FORM
ORGANIZATION LACKS RESOURCES
INSTITUTION SANCTIONS ACTION
EFFECT OCCURS BUT DIFFERS FROM INTENTION
EFFECT CHANGES OTHERS' ECOLOGIES MORE THAN ACTOR'S
EFFECT IS TEMPORARY AND DOES NOT REBIND STRUCTURE
```

These failure paths are required to avoid voluntarism.

## 6. Restrictive action as a D3 negative-feedback exemplar

Holzkamp’s p. 377–378 mechanism can be normalized without importing the entire political-theoretical vocabulary:

```text
DEPENDENCY / THREAT
+ SUBJECTIVELY GROUNDED SHORT-RUN STRATEGY OF INDIVIDUAL CONTROL
        ↓
INSTRUMENTALIZATION / ISOLATING RELATION
        ↓
SHORT-RUN CONTROL MAY INCREASE
        ↓
RECIPROCAL TRUST/COOPERATION BASIS DECLINES
        ↓
FUTURE COLLECTIVE SUPPORT / JOINT-CONTROL POSSIBILITY NARROWS
        ↓
LATER SUBJECT POSSIBILITY SPACE NARROWS
```

This is not a universal empirical law. It is a source-derived theoretical mechanism family and a valuable D3 court case.

## 7. Generalized action as a D3 positive-feedback possibility

The positive route is more conditional:

```text
CONSTRAINT / THREAT
+ OBJECTIVE POSSIBILITY OF ASSOCIATION
+ SUBJECT-RELATIVE RECOGNITION / GROUNDS
+ PARTICIPANTS WITH OVERLAPPING INTERESTS
+ COORDINATION / ORGANIZATION / RESOURCES
+ EXTERNAL RELATIONAL GATE
        ↓
POSSIBLE EXPANSION OF SHARED CONTROL OVER CONDITIONS
```

Holzkamp does not supply a universal rule for whether this succeeds. That absence is a **ceiling**, not a defect to fill by assumption.

## 8. Historical specificity court

D3 effects are not universal functions of “agency.” The source insists on:

- historically specific power relations;
- concrete positions;
- actual life situations;
- objective possibilities of association;
- institutionally/socially available means;
- other actors and power holders.

Thus:

```text
SAME INTENTION
+ DIFFERENT POSITION / ORGANIZATION / COUNTERPART / INSTITUTION
= DIFFERENT POSSIBLE EXTERNAL EFFECT
```

No psychological essence follows from class or political relation.

- action can reproduce/change conditions;
- position shapes capacity to influence conditions;
- collective association may be necessary;
- real means and counter-power matter;
- realized action can narrow or expand later possibilities;
- new conditions feed later subject possibilities only through renewed mediation.

### What is Rawlsing-specific rather than Holzkamp-derived

- exact transaction boundary;
- provenance receipts;
- source admission protocol;
- explicit institution/counterpart response object schema;
- atomic update/rebind implementation.
