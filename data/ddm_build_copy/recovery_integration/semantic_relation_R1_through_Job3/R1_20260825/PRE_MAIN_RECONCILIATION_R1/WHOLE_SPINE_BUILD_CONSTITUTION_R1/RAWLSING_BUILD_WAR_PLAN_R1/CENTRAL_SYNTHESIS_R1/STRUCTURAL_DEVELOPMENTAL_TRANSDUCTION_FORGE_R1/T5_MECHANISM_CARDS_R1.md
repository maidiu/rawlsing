## M01 — HISTORICAL-TIME / PLACE CONDITIONING OF ACTOR ECOLOGY

**SOURCE exact author/work/location:** Elder 1994, *Time, Human Agency, and Social Change*, pp. 4-7; Elder 1998, *The Life Course as Developmental Theory*, pp. 2-5, especially p. 3 historical time/place.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORY + LONGITUDINAL SYNTHESIS`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Historically specific economic, institutional, war, labor-market, family, and place conditions.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Historical conditions alter the menu, scarcity, timing, and accessibility of roles, resources, obligations, institutional routes, counterpart relations, and exposures available to persons occupying particular ages/positions.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** Different opportunity/constraint/contact/activity ecologies for actors located in different cohorts, places, or life stages during the same macrohistorical change.

**REALIZATION LAW:** Historical condition must be bound to actor + time/place + position, then realized through concrete family/institutional/activity/contact routes before it enters biography.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** Actor choice/adaptation operates within the historically available option set; no assumption of identical response.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** Indirectly any lawful V3 target reached through realized encounter/activity/relationship and downstream uptake/history; no direct target assignment from cohort.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `CONDITION + HISTORICAL CONJUNCTURE`, possibly producing `EVENTS/TRANSITIONS`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** household / institution / labor market / community / state / historical conjuncture.

**RECIPROCITY:** Limited at the individual level; actors can adapt and collectively alter conditions, but macrohistorical conditions are not reduced to actor choice.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** age/life stage, cohort, place, family position, institutional eligibility, duration of conjuncture.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `COHORT OR PERIOD != ACTOR EXPOSURE != TRAIT`. Calendar time is not a causal mechanism by itself.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3: structured-world possibility, structural pressure/affordance/applicability, route/topology possibility. Actor-indexing seam: actor activation + phase/world/context binding. V3: opportunity/constraint, encounter/OEE where discrete, biography/path dependence. **Missing connective function:** historical-context binding must preserve which structural possibilities become actor-relevant rather than treating period as a universal event.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`.

## M02 — TIMING / SEQUENCE-SENSITIVE CONSEQUENCE

**SOURCE:** Elder 1994 pp. 5-6 life-stage principle; Elder 1998 pp. 3-4 timing principle; Pavalko & Elder 1990 pp. 1213-1234; Elder 1986 pp. 233-245.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORY + LONGITUDINAL OBSERVATIONAL`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** A transition/event/condition that can occur at different ages or at different positions within an existing role sequence.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Timing changes what the event interrupts, enables, forecloses, or synchronizes with: schooling, marriage, work, dependency, caregiving, military service, etc. Earlier/later occurrence therefore changes downstream opportunity sets and subsequent transitions.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** The same nominal condition/event can yield different realized constraints/resources because the actor’s prior state and concurrent obligations differ.

**REALIZATION LAW:** Need ordered actor history plus event/transition time. Compare `prior_state -> transition -> rebound ecology`; age alone is insufficient.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** Plans, perceived options, readiness, commitments, and choice can mediate response; these are downstream actor states, not inferred from age.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** practices, expectations, role-related relationships, memory/learning, attractor movement only after V3 processing.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `EVENT / TRANSITION` composed with `TRAJECTORY HISTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** actor / household / organization / institution.

**RECIPROCITY:** Earlier choices and prior person-state affect later timing/selection; transition consequences alter later choices and exposure.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** chronological/developmental/social age, sequence position, duration, prior commitments, historical context.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `AGE != MECHANISM`; “off-time” is not inherently harmful or beneficial without the changed social ecology and downstream process.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** V3 history/path dependence can represent prior sequence; OEE can represent a discrete occurrence; state/context binding can represent time. **Missing connective function:** an explicit timing-sensitive composition rule connecting prior trajectory state to changed actor-relative ecology after the transition.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`.

## M03 — LINKED-LIFE TRANSMISSION THROUGH RESOURCE / OBLIGATION / SUPPORT / RELATION CHANGE

**SOURCE:** Elder 1994 p. 6 linked lives; Elder 1998 pp. 3-4 Depression family mechanism; Pavalko & Elder 1990 for marital sequence; Elder, Gimbel & Ivie 1991 pp. 215-231.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORY + LONGITUDINAL OBSERVATIONAL`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Another linked person’s state or transition: job loss, income loss, military entry, illness, marriage change, role change, opportunity, sanction, etc.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** The linked person’s change modifies the focal actor’s material resources, obligations, support, supervision, relationship quality, time allocation, mobility, network access, or exposure. Example form: breadwinner income loss -> household scarcity/parental strain -> changed parenting/work obligations -> child activity/exposure ecology.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** Focal actor faces changed resource, support, obligation, counterpart, time, or exposure conditions even though the initiating event occurred to someone else.

**REALIZATION LAW:** There must be a real relation/binding with a transmissive pathway. Mere kinship/category membership is insufficient. The other person’s transition must change a quantity or relation relevant to the focal actor.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** Both actors may adapt, resist, compensate, share, withdraw, or mobilize resources; linked-life influence is not one-way deterministic transfer.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** relationship state, practice, stress/tension, expectation, memory/learning, support-dependent uptake where lawfully processed.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** persistent `RELATION` carrying `EVENT/TRANSITION` consequences across time; may generate recurrent activity change.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** dyad / household / kin network / close social network.

**RECIPROCITY:** Strong. Focal actor’s response may alter the linked person and the relation, recursively changing both ecologies.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** tie strength, co-residence, resource pooling, dependency, role obligations, network redundancy, support alternatives, historical/institutional context.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `SPOUSE/PARENT/CHILD STATUS != TRANSMITTED EFFECT`; another person’s state does not become the focal actor’s psychological state.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 interaction topology/route possibility can state possible relational route; V3 relationship/biography can hold realized relation history; encounter/opportunity/constraint can hold actor-relative changes. **Missing connective function:** cross-actor relational transduction that computes lawful focal-actor resource/obligation/support/time/opportunity changes from the linked person’s realized state/transition without copying internal states.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` as a general positive connective function; existing relation/topology/history owners are reusable bookends.

## M04 — RECURRENT ACTIVITY QUALITY / INTENSITY / DURATION -> DEVELOPMENTAL INPUT

**SOURCE:** Mortimer et al. 1996 *Child Development* 67:1243-1261; Mortimer et al. 1996 *Social Forces* 74:1405-1418; Mortimer, Harley & Staff 2002 *Work and Occupations* 29:166-197.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `PROSPECTIVE LONGITUDINAL OBSERVATIONAL`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Organized adolescent work with varying hours, quality, learning opportunities, stressors, autonomy, and social contacts.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Repeated participation supplies recurring practice, feedback, demands, skill-learning opportunities, adult contacts, stressors, and time displacement. Development depends on the content/quality/intensity of repeated activity, not on the structural label “employed.”

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** A temporally extended activity ecology with specific practice and exposure frequencies.

**REALIZATION LAW:** Actual repeated participation with measured quality/intensity must occur; exposure must then be processed by V3. Binary membership is insufficient.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** Worker selection into jobs, interpretation, effort, coping, and use of learning opportunities can alter effects.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** occupational values, contemporaneous depressive affect/well-being, skill/competence-relevant learning, expectations/practices where supported.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE` with duration/intensity/quality attributes.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** organization / workplace / actor-counterpart network.

**RECIPROCITY:** Person characteristics affect job selection and persistence; work experience can alter later preferences/choices.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** age, hours, work quality, learning opportunity, school demands, prior mental state, family context.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** Work status/hours do not guarantee durable psychological change; contemporaneous effects may dissipate.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** existing recurrence/history and V3 learning/memory/practice owners can carry repeated episodes. **Missing connective function:** preserve recurrent-activity identity plus quality/intensity/duration so the system does not flatten a practice into unrelated OEE events.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`; no independent “work trajectory” carrier is required if recurrence/history composition preserves these parameters.

## M05 — TRANSITION-DRIVEN ECOLOGY REBINDING / EARNED TURNING POINT

**SOURCE:** Elder 1986 pp. 233-245; Elder, Gimbel & Ivie 1991 pp. 215-231; Laub & Sampson 1993 pp. 301-325.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `LONGITUDINAL OBSERVATIONAL + QUALITATIVE + THEORY`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Entry/exit into a role or institution such as military service, marriage, employment, school, or other organized setting.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Transition changes routines, counterpart sets, supervision/control, resources, obligations, schedules, geographic location, social bonds, and opportunities. If these changed bindings persist and redirect subsequent transitions/behavior, the transition may function as a turning point.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** Rebound actor ecology after transition, potentially discontinuous with prior ecology.

**REALIZATION LAW:** `TRANSITION OCCURS -> BINDINGS ACTUALLY CHANGE -> CHANGED ACTIVITIES/RELATIONS RECUR -> DOWNSTREAM TRAJECTORY DIFFERS`. Event occurrence alone is insufficient.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** commitment, interpretation, investment in new bonds, use/resistance of new opportunities, and selection into transition.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** practices, relationships, expectations, identity/self-model only if downstream V3 law earns them; broad trajectory redirection.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `TRANSITION`; `TURNING POINT` is a derived trajectory-level property, not an atomic event type.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** household / organization / institution / network.

**RECIPROCITY:** Strong: altered person and bonds influence later positions, transitions, and exposure.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** timing, prior disadvantage, transition quality, duration, institutional resources, strength of new bonds, historical period.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** Not every transition is a turning point; no event is intrinsically transformative.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** current state/context/binding + opportunity/constraint + encounter/OEE + V3 biography/path dependence can compose most representation. **Missing connective function:** transition-triggered rebinding of actor-relative ecology; turning-point status should be derived from sustained trajectory alteration rather than stored as causal authority.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE` transition rebinding; `REJECT` a magical standalone turning-point carrier.

## M06 — ITERATIVE CUMULATIVE RISK / OPPORTUNITY FEEDBACK

**SOURCE:** Dannefer 2003 S327-S337; Ferraro & Shippee 2009 pp. 334-337/Table 1; DiPrete & Eirich 2006 pp. 271-297.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORY + REVIEW`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Initial inequalities in resources, institutional position, risk exposure, opportunity access, and historically structured conditions.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Realized advantage/disadvantage changes the probability/availability of later opportunities, risks, resources, and positions; those later realizations feed forward again. Accumulation is therefore an iterative state-transition process, possibly cross-domain, not a sum of labels.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** Path-dependent exposure/resource/opportunity ecology whose future distribution depends partly on realized prior history.

**REALIZATION LAW:** Each step requires a lawful route: `prior realized state/history -> changed future possibility ecology -> new realized exposure/opportunity -> actor response/consequence -> updated history`. Un-realized risk does not count as realized disadvantage.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** resource mobilization, choice, resistance, effort, collective action, and perception can modify paths; agency is constrained but not erased.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** any V3 target only through realized repeated histories and metabolism; the cumulative process primarily updates actor-relative social ecology and biography.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `CUMULATIVE PROCESS` realized by recurrence + history + feedback.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** actor / household / organization / institution / class/community depending domain.

**RECIPROCITY:** Essential: changed actor state/actions can influence later selection and position, while social systems continue generating differential opportunities/risks.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** onset, duration, magnitude, domain diffusion, resource availability, turning transitions, historical context.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `CUMULATIVE DISADVANTAGE != DESTINY`; no generic scalar “disadvantage score” licenses a trait; accumulation requires explicit transition mechanisms.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** V3 history/path dependence supplies a carrier for realized sequence; PRE-V3 pressure/affordance/route supplies future possibility. **Missing connective function:** feedback from realized actor history/person/action to later actor-relative structural opportunity/risk/resource ecology. This is the strongest T5 candidate for a genuinely missing seam function.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` general feedback/update law; `REJECT` a content-free cumulative-advantage operator.

## M07 — INSTITUTION × BIOGRAPHICAL HISTORY ACCUMULATION

**SOURCE:** O’Rand 1996 pp. 230-238, especially labor-market/pension opportunity structures interacting with work, employer, and job-mobility histories.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORY/REVIEW + EMPIRICAL STRATIFICATION SYNTHESIS`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Institutional allocation/reward rules in labor markets, employers, pension systems, eligibility regimes.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Rules condition rewards/coverage/resources at each step, while prior job/employer/labor-force history changes later eligibility and payoff. Long-run inequality emerges from rule × sequence interaction.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** Different security, income/wealth, eligibility, mobility, and retirement-resource ecologies among people with different realized histories under the same institutional system.

**REALIZATION LAW:** Must record actual positions/transitions/durations and apply the contemporaneous institutional rule at each relevant step.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** job mobility, labor-force exit/reentry, household strategy, and constrained choice influence sequences.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** primarily social-condition/resources; psychological/developmental consequences remain downstream and conditional.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `TRAJECTORY + CUMULATIVE PROCESS` composed from institutional state transitions.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** organization / labor market / pension institution / household.

**RECIPROCITY:** Actor decisions affect history; institutional structure constrains available options and rewards.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** gendered employment patterns, discontinuous work, employer changes, pension design, historical policy regime, duration.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** Cross-sectional late-life inequality cannot be backfilled as a direct person prior or used to infer an individual’s work history.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** structural applicability/route + actor binding + biography/history are reusable. **Missing connective function:** repeated institutional allocation against actor history, if not already supplied by domain-specific PRE-V3 rules; T5 cannot credit a specific current owner from Phase-0 evidence.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE/MISSING` depending lane-R owner recovery; mechanism semantics are required even if carrier composition already exists.

## M08 — RECIPROCAL SELECTION / CUMULATIVE CONTINUITY

**SOURCE:** Laub & Sampson 1993 pp. 301-325; Mortimer et al. 2002 pp. 166-197; Elder 1994 pp. 6-10 on agency and environment selection.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORY + LONGITUDINAL OBSERVATIONAL`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Existing person/history state plus available institutional/relationship/activity options.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Prior behavior/capacity/preferences influence selection into or exclusion from later roles, relationships, jobs, or environments; those later environments then change subsequent experience. Laub/Sampson’s cumulative-continuity logic includes prior delinquency weakening later bonds, while adult bonds can also redirect behavior.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** Later structural position/exposure is partly endogenous to prior biography without ceasing to be structurally constrained.

**REALIZATION LAW:** Need explicit actor action/selection or institution response that changes later position/binding. Correlation between trait and position is insufficient.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** central: choice, effort, avoidance, attachment, resistance; also institutional sanction/gatekeeping.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** later practices, relationships, expectations, biography, path dependence.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `CUMULATIVE PROCESS / TRAJECTORY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** actor / dyad / organization / institution.

**RECIPROCITY:** Constitutive.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** opportunity set, gatekeeping, sanctions, social bonds, historical period, available alternatives.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** Selection does not prove that outcomes are self-caused; structural constraint and differential opportunity remain part of the mechanism.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** V3 person/history owners provide prior state; PRE-V3 publishes actor-independent possibility; actor binding realizes later ecology. **Missing connective function:** lawful V3-to-future-position/exposure feedback, mediated by action plus institutional/relational response.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` as general reciprocal seam law.

## M09 — RESOURCE MOBILIZATION / INTERRUPTION OF AN UNFAVORABLE CHAIN

**SOURCE:** Ferraro & Shippee 2009 pp. 336-337/Table 1 axiom 3; Elder 1998 pp. 3-4 family adaptations; Elder 1994 agency discussion.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORY + SYNTHESIS`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Existing chain of risk/disadvantage plus available resources/support/opportunities.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Actor or linked others mobilize resources, choose among options, or enter a transition that changes exposure/resources enough to interrupt or redirect the expected chain.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** Mitigated, delayed, or redirected risk trajectory; favorable trajectories may also accelerate when resources arrive early/strongly enough.

**REALIZATION LAW:** Resource must be available, accessible, relevant, and actually mobilized/used; nominal resource possession is insufficient.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** agency, perception of possibility, help-seeking, collective/family action.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** whatever downstream state is affected by altered exposure/uptake; no direct “resilience trait” inference.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `EVENT/TRANSITION` embedded in `CUMULATIVE PROCESS`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** actor / household / network / institution.

**RECIPROCITY:** Yes; mobilization can change later opportunities and relationships.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** onset, duration, magnitude, accessibility, timing, linked-life support, institutional eligibility.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** Early adversity does not fix the trajectory; “resilience” cannot be back-inferred as a stable trait merely from a favorable outcome.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** opportunity/constraint + encounter + V3 agency/uptake/history can carry realized resource use. **Missing connective function:** resource-use transition must update later exposure/opportunity ecology inside the cumulative feedback law.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE` inside M06 feedback rather than mint a separate universal resource carrier.

## M10 — POPULATION SELECTION / ATTRITION IS AN EVIDENCE CEILING, NOT AN ACTOR MECHANISM

**SOURCE:** Ferraro & Shippee 2009 p. 337/Table 1 axiom 5.

**CLAIM STATUS:** `THEORY/METHOD`.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** Differential mortality, censoring, attrition, cohort inclusion after unequal histories.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Composition of the observed population changes nonrandomly, potentially making late-life inequality appear smaller or different from the inequality-generating process.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** None as a direct psychological mechanism; this constrains inference from population evidence.

**REALIZATION LAW:** Apply in evidence/admission/interpretation: distinguish survivor composition from within-person change.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** not applicable as a person-development operator.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** none directly.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** `POPULATION COMPOSITION PROCESS`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** cohort/population.

**RECIPROCITY:** not an actor reciprocity mechanism.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** mortality, dropout, censoring, age-restricted sampling, cohort inclusiveness.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `OBSERVED LATE-LIFE DISTRIBUTION != INDIVIDUAL TRAJECTORY LAW != DEMOGRAPHIC PRIOR`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** belongs primarily in empirical-evidence qualification / positioned-subject admission ceilings, not in V3 developmental runtime.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE` as evidence-side caution if existing PRE-V3 admission can express selection qualification; otherwise `DEFER` to the evidence-authority owner, not SDTF runtime.

```text
HISTORICALLY STRUCTURED POSSIBILITY
+ ACTOR'S CURRENT AGE / PRIOR HISTORY / RELATIONAL BINDINGS
    -> ACTOR-RELATIVE ECOLOGY AT t
    -> REALIZED ACTIVITY / RELATION / EXPOSURE / TRANSITION
    -> V3 UPTAKE / RESPONSE / HISTORY UPDATE
    -> CHANGED PERSON + CHANGED RELATIONS/POSITION/RESOURCES
    -> MODIFIED POSSIBILITY / RISK / OPPORTUNITY ECOLOGY AT t+1

```

with linked-person transitions capable of updating the focal actor’s ecology and with “turning point” earned only when a transition produces sustained trajectory redirection.
