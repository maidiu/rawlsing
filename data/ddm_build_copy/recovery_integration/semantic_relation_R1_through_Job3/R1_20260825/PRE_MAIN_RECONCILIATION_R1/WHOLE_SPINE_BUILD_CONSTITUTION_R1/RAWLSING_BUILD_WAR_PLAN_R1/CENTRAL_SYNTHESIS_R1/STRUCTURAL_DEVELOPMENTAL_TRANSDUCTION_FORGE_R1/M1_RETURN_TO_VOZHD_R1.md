## 3. Top new / confirmed mechanisms

### M1-A — typed mixed work ecology

A job is not a scalar exposure. Mortimer separately measures learning opportunity, advancement, earnings/pay, security, supervisory support, engagement, stressors, work-school compatibility, status, intensity, duration and other dimensions. Table 3.5 shows that intensive/long-duration work can combine more learning/advancement/engagement/status with more stress. Some dimensions do not move monotonically.

**Architectural implication:** D1 must output independent typed facts rather than a global `GOOD_WORK/BAD_WORK` score.

### M1-B — duration × intensity × recurrence pattern

Mortimer's `occasional`, `sporadic`, `steady`, and `most invested` patterns prove that snapshot employment, total hours and duration are not interchangeable. The `steady` long-duration/lower-intensity pattern predicts later outcomes differently from the `most invested` long-duration/high-intensity pattern.

**Architectural implication:** D2 must preserve active intervals, intensity, recurrence, gaps and ordered history.

### M1-C — cross-domain time rebinding

Paid work changes the adolescent's time ecology, but not by universal crowd-out. Workers and nonworkers can have similar homework/extracurricular/volunteer participation, and actor-centered time-use clusters show different substitutions.

**Architectural implication:** compose D1 typed time/activity ecology with D2 rebinding; do not create a universal `work_hours -> activity_loss` rule.

### M1-D — selection before socialization

Ninth-grade school orientation/performance, problem behavior, peer orientation, gender/background and motives predict later work investment/quality.

**Architectural implication:** prior person state can affect search/selection but cannot directly mint a work position. Employer/job/schedule/labor-law opportunity remains an external gate. D3 is strengthened.

### M1-E — quality-specific developmental targeting

Hours/status show weak or null relations to many adolescent outcomes, while learning opportunity, reward/advancement, stress/control and school compatibility relate differently to efficacy, values, depressed affect and problem behavior.

**Architectural implication:** D1 must preserve condition type; V3 must preserve developmental target type. Never collapse outcomes into `PERSONALITY`.

### M1-F — phase/history-qualified transduction

Teenage work has different meaning from adult work because adolescents are novice workers, usually not economically dependent on the job, and occupy a different school/family/institutional ecology. Mortimer's work-control/depressive-affect discussion is consistent with decision authority becoming burden under novice/high-intensity conditions rather than automatically producing the adult self-direction pattern.

**Architectural implication:** active phase/history is a mandatory qualifier, but existing ADP-008/Stage-A binding plus D1/D2 already compose it.

### M1-G — transition as ecology rebind

High-school work pattern has no significant bearing on residential independence/marriage/cohabitation/parenthood at 21–22, yet some associations emerge at 24–25 when these transitions are normatively timed.

**Architectural implication:** a transition matters because it changes actual relations/resources/obligations/opportunities at a specific later phase, not because `ADULT_TRANSITION` is itself a developmental cause.

### M1-H — transient versus persistent consequences

High-intensity adolescent work is linked to some near-term drinking/problem behavior, but work-pattern drinking differences disappear four years after high school. Early earnings advantages also converge substantially by later follow-up.

**Architectural implication:** repeated exposure/response does not automatically become durable V3 change.

## 4. Top nulls / boundary conditions

1. Employment status and work hours do **not** consistently predict the major mental-health indicators; Mortimer's Ch. 6 conclusion also reports no significant hours/status effect on educational achievement, vocational development, or family/peer relationships at the high-school grain.
2. Hours do not strengthen intrinsic work values; learning opportunities matter more.
3. Work investment does not produce a monotonic “good work” gradient: Table 3.5 mixes gains and stress and contains null quality dimensions.
4. Paid work does not universally crowd out homework, extracurriculars, volunteering or other activity.
5. Apparent work effects are selection-sensitive because prior person/social states predict later work pattern and quality.
6. Work-quality trajectories are only partially observed; quality reports are largely snapshots and only about half of workers have repeated quality measurements.
7. High-intensity work/problem behavior effects are modest and outcome/time-specific; school problem behavior elevation is concentrated in tenth grade.
8. Drinking differences by high-school work pattern disappear four years after high school.
9. Early earnings advantages of intensive work substantially converge later.
10. High-school work pattern has no significant family-transition effect at ages 21–22; effects emerge later at 24–25 and are normatively timed, not evidence of generic precocious maturity.
11. Gender/age heterogeneity is not a license for demographic→psychological inference; the mechanism still runs through different jobs, conditions, opportunities, histories and uptake.
12. YDS longitudinal control improves temporal/selection adjudication but is not random assignment and does not eliminate all unobserved heterogeneity.

## 8. Exact terminal questions A–I

### A. Does Mortimer strengthen D1?

**YES — strongly.** The full book requires typed, simultaneous, mixed actor-relative work conditions and makes scalar employment/job-quality representations indefensible.

### B. Does Mortimer strengthen D2?

**YES — very strongly.** Duration, intensity, recurrence, gaps, pattern identity, phase, attenuation and transition rebinding are empirically consequential.

### C. Does Mortimer strengthen D3?

**YES, with an evidence ceiling.** Selection into work, downstream efficacy/value changes, later plans/actions and institutionally gated attainment supply strong component evidence for reciprocity. Mortimer does not identify one fully closed causal loop end-to-end.

### D. Does Mortimer require narrowing or splitting any of them?

**NARROW/GENERALIZE INTERNAL CONTRACTS, BUT DO NOT SPLIT THE TOP-LEVEL THREE.** D1 needs vector-valued dimensions; D2 needs duration×intensity/interval/history parameters; D3 needs explicit selection/action/gate/update provenance.

### E. Does Mortimer reveal a genuinely missing fourth connective capability?

**NO.** Phase-qualified transduction and cross-domain time rebinding are important, but compose from existing/frozen owners plus D1/D2.

### F. Does Mortimer show that some proposed capability can instead be composed from existing Rawlsing owners?

**YES.** Developmental-phase qualification composes from ADP-008/Stage-A active phase + V3 prior/history + D1/D2. Discrete realized events can reuse ADP-019/OBL060/056/055 rather than inventing a work-specific event carrier. Psychological uptake/durable change must reuse V3 rather than be duplicated in SDTF.

### G. What is the strongest evidence in the entire book?

For the seam, the strongest single inferential result is **Table 7.1 / pp. 188–190**: after background, prior educational promise and economic efficacy are controlled, the long-duration/lower-intensity `steady` workers are about three times as likely to obtain a B.A. as the long-duration/high-intensity `most invested` workers; nonworkers are also nearly three times as likely as the most-invested reference. This is observational, not randomized, but it is exceptionally strong evidence that **temporal pattern/intensity—not employment or accumulated work alone—matters to later institutional attainment**.

The strongest direct D1 descriptive evidence is **Table 3.5 / p. 73**, where greater work investment simultaneously carries more learning/advancement/earnings/engagement/status and more stress while some job-quality dimensions are null across patterns.

### H. What is the strongest negative/null evidence in the entire book?

The strongest broad negative result is Mortimer's Ch. 6 conclusion: **neither employment status nor hours significantly influence the high-school students' mental health, educational achievement, vocational development, or family/peer relationships at the general exposure grain**. Work pattern and quality matter more. The later disappearance of work-pattern drinking differences reinforces the ceiling: an adolescent association is not automatically a persistent developmental effect.
