## T3-M01 — PROPERTY SEPARATION → WAGE-LABOUR DEPENDENCY

**SOURCE:** Karl Marx, *Capital* I, Ch. 6 “The Buying and Selling of Labour-Power”; Ch. 23 “Simple Reproduction.” Public primary text: https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch06.htm and https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch23.htm

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical / historical-materialist.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** an actor is legally able to sell labour-power but lacks sufficient independent access to means of production/subsistence.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** separation from productive means makes repeated sale of labour-power a material route to subsistence; recurrent production can reproduce that separation and return the worker to the labour market.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** dependency on wage income; employer/market counterpart set; exposure to hiring/firing, wage and schedule conditions; reduced exit options where alternatives are scarce; recurring need to seek employment.

**REALIZATION LAW:** property relation alone does not create biography. The actor must actually depend on income/resources, face a relevant labour market, enter or seek employment, and encounter concrete employment conditions.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** job search, refusal, mobility, household pooling, self-employment, organization, strike, migration and other strategies can alter whether and how dependency is realized.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** none directly. Realized work/constraint histories may later affect expectations, practices, motives, knowledge, relationships or other V3-owned targets if lawful metabolism earns the change.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** CONDITION + RELATION + RECURRENT PROCESS; may organize a TRAJECTORY.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** class/property relation → labour market → household/organization.

**RECIPROCITY:** individual/collective action can alter employment, resources or property access; collective/political action can alter institutional rules.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** welfare provision, household assets, labour-market tightness, legal status, union coverage, self-employment possibilities, geography, age, care obligations.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `WORKING_CLASS_POSITION != WAGE_DEPENDENCY_REALIZED != PERSONALITY`; property separation does not prove a particular job, hardship level, belief or trait.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 structural pressure/affordance/applicability + mediation-route possibility can represent the relation/possible routes; actor activation/context binding and opportunity/constraint surfaces can index the actor. Existing V3 owners receive only realized histories. Missing connective candidate: `PROPERTY_RESOURCE_RELATION_TO_ACTOR_DEPENDENCY_AND_MARKET_ECOLOGY`.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING` — the positive derivation is underproved even though both structural and downstream carriers exist.

## T3-M02 — WORKING-DAY CONTEST → TIME-CONTROL / RECOVERY / SOCIAL-TIME ECOLOGY

**SOURCE:** Marx, *Capital* I, Ch. 10 “The Working-Day,” especially §§1, 5–7. https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch10.htm

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + historical/observational source use.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** wage employment under an authority relation in which employer and worker have conflicting interests over duration/intensity of labour, embedded in law and collective organization.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** employment rules, schedules, enforcement and bargaining determine how much of the actor's day is committed to labour and what recovery/social time remains; legislation and collective struggle can change those bounds.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** work hours; schedule predictability; available recovery time; time for care, education, social participation and collective activity; fatigue/exposure duration; sanctions for noncompliance.

**REALIZATION LAW:** a statutory or organizational possibility matters only when applicable to the actor's job and schedule and actually governs hours or enforcement.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** workers may comply, bargain, refuse, change jobs, organize, strike or pursue legal remedies; employers and the state can respond.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** no direct target. Recurrent realized time-use/activity and exhaustion histories may enter V3 through existing event/activity/condition processing and memory/practice owners.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** RELATION + CONDITION + RECURRENT ACTIVITY; legal change can be EVENT/TRANSITION; cumulative hours can form CUMULATIVE PROCESS.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** worker-employer; organization; class organization; state.

**RECIPROCITY:** explicit—collective action and regulation can alter later working-time structure.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** employment law, enforcement, sector, bargaining power, occupation, household care load, shift system, technology.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** class position does not specify hours; long hours do not guarantee a psychological response; legal entitlement does not guarantee realized protection.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 structural pressure/applicability and route possibility → actor-bound constraint/opportunity; realized schedules/episodes must enter V3. Missing connective candidate: `AUTHORITY_RULE_TO_ACTOR_TIME_CONTROL_AND_DURATION_ECOLOGY`; temporal carrier for persistent conditions remains a coordinator question.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE` — existing opportunity/constraint machinery appears close but must preserve duration and recurrence rather than event-only packaging.

## T3-M03 — FACTORY / ORGANIZATIONAL AUTHORITY → SUPERVISION, SANCTION, PACE AND PHYSICAL EXPOSURE

**SOURCE:** Marx, *Capital* I, Ch. 15, §§3–4 “The Proximate Effects of Machinery…” and “The Factory.” https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch15.htm

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + historical/observational source use.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** production organized through machinery, hierarchy and employer control.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** machine pacing, assignment to equipment/departments, overseer hierarchy, workplace rules and penalties coordinate labour and enforce compliance; material workplace design sets sensory/safety exposure.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** task and pace; supervisory counterpart; discretion; fines/deductions or other sanctions; work-group composition; noise/heat/dust/injury exposure; schedule/intensity.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the actor must occupy the relevant job/worksite under those rules and actually perform or be subject to the organized activity/exposure.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** adaptation, informal work practice, resistance, evasion, worker organization, safety action and exit can alter realized activity.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** no direct trait. Repeated activity can potentially alter practice/capacity; repeated sanctions/exposures may affect memory, expectation, tension or relationship state only through V3-owned processes.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** RELATION + CONDITION + RECURRENT ACTIVITY; individual sanction/injury can be EVENT; long exposure can be CUMULATIVE PROCESS.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** organization/workgroup/dyad; embedded in class/property relation.

**RECIPROCITY:** worker/collective resistance, regulation and technical redesign can change rules and activity organization.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** technology, safety law, supervisory practice, occupation, collective organization, labour-market alternatives.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** machinery itself does not entail one authority regime or one psychological effect; nominal occupation does not establish actual pace/control/exposure.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 structural relation + interaction topology + route possibility can represent possible hierarchy/activity arrangements; actor-bound opportunity/constraint/contact can realize them. Missing connective candidate: `ORGANIZATIONAL_CONTROL_TO_ACTOR_ACTIVITY_SANCTION_EXPOSURE_ECOLOGY`.

## T3-M04 — MANAGEMENT KNOWLEDGE CONTROL → CONCEPTION/EXECUTION SEPARATION

**SOURCE:** Harry Braverman, *Labor and Monopoly Capital*, 25th Anniversary ed. (1998), Ch. 4 “Scientific Management,” especially pp. 78–83.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical/historical synthesis; breadth of deskilling thesis contested.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** management has authority to redesign the labour process and appropriate/codify task knowledge.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** planning/production knowledge is concentrated in management or specialist units while execution is specified for workers; method and pace can thereby be controlled independently of workers' full craft knowledge.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** reduced or altered discretion; narrower information access; prescribed methods; changed task breadth; changed opportunities to exercise/develop skill; dependence on planning/technical specialists.

**REALIZATION LAW:** a management doctrine or technology matters only if work is actually reorganized this way for the actor; local discretion, tacit knowledge and worker workaround must remain possible.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** workers can retain tacit knowledge, improvise, resist, bargain, learn outside the formal job or move into different roles.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** skill/practice/capacity only through repeated realized activity and learning, never by job title alone; epistemic/access state may also be affected through actual information restriction.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** organizational TRANSITION → persistent RELATION/CONDITION → RECURRENT ACTIVITY; possible cumulative skill-use effects.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** organization/workgroup/occupation.

**RECIPROCITY:** worker expertise/resistance and collective bargaining can constrain redesign; technical/organizational change can reverse or deepen it.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** occupation, technology, actual discretion, professional jurisdiction, union power, labour scarcity, local workflow.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `TAYLORIST_LABEL != DESKILLING_REALIZED`; no universal deskilling law; no inference from reduced task discretion directly to intelligence/personality.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 route/activity-organization possibilities can carry the structural possibility; V3 CAN-011/ADP-017 and learning/memory owners can receive realized practice/information histories. Missing connective candidate: `KNOWLEDGE_AUTHORITY_TO_ACTOR_DISCRETION_INFORMATION_SKILL_USE_ECOLOGY`.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING`.

## T3-M05 — TASK FRAGMENTATION / SKILL-COST ALLOCATION → DIFFERENTIAL ACTIVITY AND BARGAINING POSITION

**SOURCE:** Braverman, *Labor and Monopoly Capital*, Ch. 3 “The Division of Labor” and Ch. 4; especially the Babbage-principle discussion around pp. 82–83 in the 1998 edition.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical/historical synthesis; contested as universal tendency.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** employer ability to divide a complex labour process and allocate fragments to differently trained/paid workers.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** tasks are decomposed; scarce knowledge is assigned selectively; training requirements and labour costs differ across task positions.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** task repetition/breadth; skill-use opportunities; training requirement; wage bargaining leverage; interchangeability; promotion pathways and counterpart dependencies.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the actor must be assigned to a specific task bundle in an actual workflow; decomposition at organization level cannot be projected to every worker.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** workers learn across task boundaries, craft informal roles, bargain over classifications, seek credentials or move to other jobs.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** repeated practice may build some capacities and leave others unused; durable change requires V3 learning/history, not structural assignment alone.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** RELATION + RECURRENT ACTIVITY + TRAJECTORY; reorganization can be TRANSITION.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** workgroup/organization/labour market.

**RECIPROCITY:** skill scarcity, turnover, organization and worker action can force recomposition of tasks or pay.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** technology, labour supply, occupational licensing, team production, union classification rules, training institutions.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** division of labour is not equivalent to monotony, low skill or low agency in every case.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 activity/route possibility + actor-specific opportunity/constraint; V3 practice/learning owners after actual task recurrence. Missing connective candidate: `DIVISION_OF_LABOUR_TO_ACTOR_TASK_SKILL_AND_BARGAINING_ECOLOGY`.

**DISPOSITION:** `MISSING`.

## T3-M06 — WORK AS A GAME → EFFORT, PEER RULES AND SITUATED CONSENT

**SOURCE:** Michael Burawoy, *Manufacturing Consent*, Ch. 5 “The Labor Process as a Game,” pp. 77–94; mechanism cross-checked in Burawoy, “Ethnographic fallacies,” *Work, Employment and Society* 27(3) (2013): 526–536.

**CLAIM STATUS:** qualitative/ethnographic + theoretical; site- and period-bounded.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** piece-rate/production rules and a labour process allowing constrained worker discretion and informal peer organization.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** workers organize/participate in a production “game” with shared rules, quotas/challenges and peer meanings; constrained choice can make arduous work more engaging while shifting conflict horizontally or obscuring some background constraints.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** effort choices within bounds; peer counterpart set; informal rewards/status; peer sanctions; meanings attached to pace/output; locally perceived room for maneuver.

**REALIZATION LAW:** no consent is inferred from formal incentives. The actor must actually participate in the practice/game; rules must be socially maintained; nonparticipation/resistance remains possible.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** central—workers create, reinterpret, exploit, refuse or resist the game; “consent” is not structural truth about their mind.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** situated practice/meaning/expectation only if V3 processing earns it; no durable ideology object is created by T3.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** RECURRENT ACTIVITY/PRACTICE + RELATION; can become CONDITION through stabilized informal rules.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** workgroup/organization.

**RECIPROCITY:** worker practices reproduce or alter shop-floor rules; conflict can reshape management strategy.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** incentive system, peer group, supervisor behavior, employment security, production technology, local culture.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `PARTICIPATION != TRUE CONSENT`; game-like work does not prove endorsement of ownership relations or a durable belief.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 interaction topology/rule/route possibility → actor-specific counterpart/activity ecology; actual practice must be realized before CAN-008/CAN-011/ADP-015/016 can operate. Missing connective candidate: `ORGANIZATIONAL_RULE_PLUS_PEER_TOPOLOGY_TO_ACTOR_RECURRENT_PRACTICE_ECOLOGY`.

## T3-M07 — INTERNAL LABOUR MARKET → MOBILITY, SECURITY, SENIORITY AND SUPERVISOR LEVERAGE

**SOURCE:** Burawoy, *Manufacturing Consent*, Ch. 6 “The Rise of an Internal Labor Market,” pp. 95–108; author retrospective in Burawoy 2013, pp. 526–536.

**CLAIM STATUS:** qualitative/ethnographic + theoretical; historically bounded organizational case.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** organization has internal vacancy/bidding rules based on seniority/skill and differentiated jobs.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** internal allocation rules create conditional access to jobs and benefits; accumulated seniority makes staying valuable while ability to bid away from a supervisor constrains some arbitrary managerial power.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** eligible vacancies; promotion/mobility route; job security; benefit access; switching cost; supervisor dependence/leverage; incentive to remain with firm.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the actor must be covered by the rules, meet eligibility/seniority conditions, face an actual vacancy/choice, and exercise or forgo the option.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** workers can bid, stay, leave, cultivate indispensability, organize, or decline mobility.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** expectations/plans and relationship history only through realized organizational experience; no loyalty trait inferred from tenure.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** CONDITION + institutional RELATION + TRAJECTORY; bid/promotion is EVENT/TRANSITION.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** organization/workgroup.

**RECIPROCITY:** turnover, bargaining and worker use of bidding rights can change supervisory behavior or rules.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** vacancy structure, seniority rules, union contract, firm stability, external labour market, skill requirements.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** internal opportunity does not equal promotion; seniority does not equal loyalty; formal rule does not prove fair implementation.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 institutional applicability/gate possibility + actor binding → actor-relative opportunity/constraint; OEE/contact can represent specific bid/promotion events. Missing connective candidate: `INSTITUTIONAL_ALLOCATION_RULE_TO_ACTOR_ELIGIBILITY_MOBILITY_SECURITY_ECOLOGY`.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** peer group/school/household/local labour market.

**RECIPROCITY:** peer action changes local interaction ecology; later route selection changes future structural position, though macro class relation need not change.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** historical period, gender, race, locality, school tracking, labour-market demand, family/network alternatives, individual participation.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** the twelve-boy case cannot support a universal working-class developmental law; resistance is not automatically emancipatory or reproductive.

## T3-M11 — SCARCE SKILL / CREDENTIAL BARRIER → LABOUR-MARKET POWER AND ALLOCATION

**SOURCE:** Wright, *Class Counts*, Student Edition, Ch. 1, pp. 17–18, “Skills and expertise.”

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + comparative empirical class analysis.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** eligibility; wage bargaining position; training cost/time; occupational options; promotion; exclusion from protected roles; information/skill network access.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** education institution/occupation/labour market/organization.

**RECIPROCITY:** collective professional action and policy can change credential barriers; labour supply can change scarcity.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** licensing law, institutional prestige, labour demand, technology, geography, discrimination and alternative certification.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** credential != competence; education category != opportunity realized; skill asset != personality/intelligence prior.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** household/network/organization/state/transnational care chain.

**RECIPROCITY:** household behavior, migration, labour organizing and policy movements can alter institutional provision and later care allocation.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** income, age, children/dependents, disability, gendered institutions, migration status, public services, leave policy, labour hours, geography.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** population gender gap does not license a female-caregiver prior; care “crisis” does not establish any actor's actual burden; paid employment does not determine household care division.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** PRE-V3 policy/structural pressure/applicability + household/network topology; actor binding must yield actual time/resource/dependency/counterpart opportunities and constraints before realization.
