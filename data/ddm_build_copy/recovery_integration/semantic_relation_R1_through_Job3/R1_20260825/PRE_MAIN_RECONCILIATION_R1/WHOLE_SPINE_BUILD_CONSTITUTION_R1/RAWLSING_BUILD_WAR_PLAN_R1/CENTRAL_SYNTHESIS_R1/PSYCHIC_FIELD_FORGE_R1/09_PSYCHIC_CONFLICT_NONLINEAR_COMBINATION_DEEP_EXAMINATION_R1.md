## 0. Purpose

This file deeply examines PFF frontier pocket 8, `psychic conflict and nonlinear combination`, including coalition, inhibition, displacement, substitution, ambivalence, tipping, masking, rebound, cross-activation, path dependence, and compromise expression.

The central question is:

> When several durable organizations, current social readings, memories, motives, fears, self-model constraints, and defenses are simultaneously recruited, what kinds of interaction among them must PFF be able to preserve before any architecture is selected?

Current conclusion:

`PSYCHIC_COMBINATION_IS_NOT_GENERALLY_ADDITIVE`

`SIMULTANEOUS_CONTRADICTORY_RECRUITMENT = MUST_FACTOR`

`BEHAVIORAL_ABSENCE != MOTIVE_ABSENCE`

`DOMINANT_CAUSAL_INFLUENCE != CONSCIOUSLY_DOMINANT_CONTENT`

## 1. Why additive trait arithmetic is insufficient

A shallow model would compute something like:

`attraction + confidence - shame - fear = response`

This loses central human phenomena.

Examples:

- weak attraction plus weak shame may remain behaviorally quiet, but being visibly noticed by the desired person can suddenly produce strong withdrawal;
- strong attachment desire can coexist with strong counterdependence, producing coolness rather than approach;
- high anger can be behaviorally invisible because maintaining attachment is more important;
- moderate fear can increase vigilance/engagement, while excessive fear produces avoidance;
- reassurance can temporarily reduce fear but also recruit shame about needing reassurance, producing renewed distance;
- a motive may become stronger while its overt expression becomes weaker because defense intensifies.

Therefore:

`MORE_OF_CAUSE_X -> MORE_OF_OUTPUT_Y` is not generally lawful.

`SUM_OF_SIGNED_SCORES` is not an adequate pre-architecture model of psyche.

## 2. Core distinction: coactivation versus resolution

Multiple incompatible tendencies may be causally active simultaneously.

Examples:

- approach + avoidance toward the same person;
- desire for intimacy + desire for autonomy;
- desire for approval + contempt for dependence on approval;
- anger + fear of abandonment;
- attraction + shame;
- desire to confess + desire to preserve self-image;
- desire to dominate + desire to be loved;
- grief + relief;
- suspicion + longing for reassurance.

PFF must not force these into a single winner merely because surface behavior eventually becomes singular.

`COACTIVATION != INDECISION_RECORD`

`CONTRADICTION != DATA_ERROR`

`ONE_OBSERVABLE_ACTION != ONE_ACTIVE_MOTIVE`

The output may be a temporary resolution, partial compromise, oscillation, or behavior dominated by an inhibitor while the suppressed motive remains active.

## 3. Candidate classes of psychic conflict

These are phenomenon classes, not machine objects.

### 3.1 Same-object approach/avoidance

The same person, action, disclosure, memory, or future outcome is simultaneously rewarding and threatening.

### 3.2 Motive versus motive

Two positively valued goals compete, e.g. autonomy versus belonging, status versus honesty, intimacy versus loyalty elsewhere.

### 3.3 Motive versus defense

A desire becomes active while a protective organization blocks its recognition or enactment.

### 3.4 Motive versus self-model

An active desire contradicts who the actor believes they are.

Example:

`I am independent / not needy`
versus
`I strongly want reassurance from this person`.

### 3.5 Affect versus strategy

The actor feels one thing but chooses behavior based on anticipated consequence.

### 3.6 Social reading versus social reading

Several interpretations coexist:

`B may be flirting`
`B may be mocking me`
`B may simply be friendly`.

Uncertainty itself can alter psychic pressure.

### 3.7 Current need versus durable obligation/value

Immediate desire conflicts with standing commitment, role, morality, future plan, or relationship obligation.

### 3.8 Actor-local need versus relationship-preservation need

The actor suppresses or transforms a current response because the relationship matters more than immediate discharge.

## 4. Interaction operators that must remain conceptually possible

No operator below is adopted as an implementation primitive. They describe distinct phenomenal relations architecture must eventually be able to explain.

### 4.1 Amplification

One activation increases another.

Example: perceived rejection amplifies shame already active from self-doubt.

### 4.2 Inhibition

One activation suppresses access, expression, or action tendency of another.

Example: fear of abandonment inhibits anger expression.

### 4.3 Gating

A condition determines whether another activation can influence a downstream process at all.

Example: sufficient felt safety may permit grief into conscious awareness.

### 4.4 Masking

A strong underlying state is behaviorally hidden by another process without ceasing to be active.

### 4.5 Substitution

One consciously available motive/feeling stands in for another less available one.

Example: irritation may become the actor's accessible explanation for an underlying shame/attraction configuration.

### 4.6 Displacement

Activation originating around one target is redirected toward another target or context.

### 4.7 Cross-conversion

A combination changes the **form** of the resulting live state rather than merely its magnitude.

Candidate examples:

`attachment need × shame × counterdependence -> coolness / anger / denial`

`attraction × perceived exposure -> mockery / withdrawal / exaggerated indifference`

`fear × status threat -> aggression`

`grief × role obligation -> hyperfunctioning`

These examples are hypotheses, not universal laws.

### 4.8 Coalition

Several motives support the same action for different reasons.

Example: leaving a party may satisfy fatigue, social anxiety, resentment, and a desire to make someone notice the departure.

### 4.9 Threshold / tipping

Several individually weak influences jointly produce a qualitative change once a configuration becomes sufficiently strong or coherent.

### 4.10 Saturation

Increasing one influence beyond some point may no longer increase the same response and may instead switch regimes.

### 4.11 Rebound

Suppression or inhibition can increase later accessibility or intensity after the inhibitor weakens.

### 4.12 Oscillation

The system alternates among partially incompatible configurations rather than settling quickly.

### 4.13 Hysteresis / path dependence

The current state depends not only on present inputs but on the route by which the actor arrived there. Reversing the immediate cue may not restore the earlier state.

## 5. Source-theory evidence from Enneagram as internal logic

The Enneagram corpus supplies useful examples of non-additive internal organization. These examples are not adopted as type laws.

### 5.1 Six-like ambivalence/compensation

The source describes cycles in which affection recruits fear of dependency/abandonment, then suspicion, reassurance seeking, then shame or resistance to dependence, producing defensive counter-movement.

Rawlsing lesson:

> A response can recruit a counter-response to the actor's own immediately preceding state, producing oscillation and mixed signals.

### 5.2 Two-like love/aggression contradiction

The source describes positive self-image and need for love coexisting with disowned aggression/resentment, with behavior interpreted by the actor in ways that preserve the preferred self-image.

Rawlsing lesson:

> Self-model constraints can inhibit conscious access to one motive while that motive still shapes interpersonal behavior.

### 5.3 Eight-like autonomy/need conflict

The source describes dependence/need as threatening to a self-organization around strength and independence.

Rawlsing lesson:

> A desired relationship can recruit both attachment need and a defense against needing, producing paradoxical control or withdrawal.

### 5.4 Nine-like peace/anger conflict

The source describes accommodation/withdrawal intended to preserve connection and peace while anger remains suppressed and can emerge indirectly.

Rawlsing lesson:

> Suppressing overt conflict may preserve short-term equilibrium while accumulating unresolved activation that later appears through another channel.

### 5.5 Three-like functioning/feeling partition

The source describes modes where effective functioning and direct feeling become difficult to sustain simultaneously.

Rawlsing lesson:

> Some organizations may gate or compartmentalize access rather than simply reduce intensity continuously.

The value of these examples is the **internal logic of interaction among motives, self-model, defense, and behavior**, not the type labels themselves.

## 6. Operating condition alters conflict dynamics

The Levels/health material suggests that the same underlying organization can operate with differing flexibility, defensive capture, and capacity for self-observation.

PFF implication:

A durable developmental condition may alter:

- how quickly one motive monopolizes the field;
- how much contradiction can coexist without forced resolution;
- how readily corrective information is admitted;
- how strongly defenses inhibit access;
- how many response alternatives remain available;
- whether conflict produces curiosity, tension, compulsive action, shutdown, or escalation.

Thus:

`SAME_MOTIVE_CONFIGURATION + DIFFERENT_OPERATING_CONDITION -> DIFFERENT_LIVE_DYNAMICS`

This is a conceptual modifier, not an adopted health scalar.

## 7. Conflict can increase salience without determining behavior

A common modeling error would be to treat the most salient content as the eventual action cause.

But conflict itself can make both alternatives more salient while behavior is determined by a third consideration.

Example:

- attraction becomes focal;
- fear of rejection becomes focal;
- both remain consciously recognized;
- commitment to an existing partner determines non-engagement.

Therefore:

`SALIENCE != CONTROL`

`CONTROL != INTENSITY`

`INTENSITY != CONSCIOUSNESS`

`CONSCIOUSNESS != EXPRESSION`

This reinforces the PFF activation/access/salience/intention non-collapse.

## 8. Conflict can be hidden from the actor

The system/analyst may have a causal account in which several structures conflict while the actor consciously experiences only the resultant state.

Example:

`attraction + shame + perceived evaluation + inhibition`
may yield
`consciously experienced irritation`.

The actor may sincerely believe the irritation is caused by B being annoying.

PFF must permit:

`ACTUAL_CAUSAL_CONFLICT != ACTOR_SELF_EXPLANATION`

while preserving the hidden-cause firewall:

`MACHINE_CAUSAL_ACCOUNT != RENDERER_FIRST_PERSON_KNOWLEDGE`

## 9. Compromise formations / multi-motive expression

Observable behavior may simultaneously serve several active organizations.

Example: teasing can at once:

- approach a desired person;
- preserve deniability;
- test reciprocation;
- protect status;
- discharge tension;
- conceal vulnerability.

The correct explanation may therefore be plural rather than one winning motive.

`ONE_ACTION_CAN_HAVE_MULTIPLE_CAUSAL_FUNCTIONS`

This is especially important for human-like ambiguity and self-surprise.

## 10. Nonmonotonicity is a hard requirement

The relation between an input and behavior can reverse as other conditions change.

Examples:

- increasing attraction may initially increase approach, then beyond a shame/exposure threshold increase withdrawal;
- moderate anxiety may increase attention, severe anxiety may collapse engagement;
- increased trust may permit more disagreement rather than more warmth because the actor no longer needs to placate;
- stronger attachment can increase either disclosure or defensive distance depending on vulnerability and self-model.

Therefore architecture must not assume that every psychological dimension has one fixed monotonic behavioral mapping.

## 11. Ambiguity can participate in conflict, not merely precede it

Competing social readings are not just uncertainty waiting to be resolved.

The coexistence of multiple plausible interpretations can itself:

- increase vigilance;
- prevent commitment;
- sustain attraction/tension;
- produce testing behavior;
- recruit prior expectations;
- amplify defensive projection;
- preserve strategic ambiguity.

Thus:

`AMBIGUITY != MISSING_VALUE`

`UNCERTAINTY_CAN_BE_CAUSALLY_ACTIVE`

PFF must preserve multiple readings without averaging them into a bland midpoint.

## 12. Conflict is target- and scope-specific

An actor may simultaneously:

- want closeness with B;
- resent B;
- trust B professionally;
- distrust B romantically;
- admire B's competence;
- fear B's judgment.

There is no contradiction requiring global resolution.

Conflict should preserve target, domain, temporal scope, and relationship context.

`GLOBAL_TRAIT_WINNER = REJECT`

## 13. Dyadic interaction can change the conflict topology

The counterpart does not merely add activation; their response can change which conflict exists.

Example:

1. A wants reassurance but fears appearing needy.
2. B spontaneously offers reassurance.
3. attachment fear decreases.
4. shame about having been readable increases.
5. A becomes dismissive.
6. B withdraws reassurance.
7. A's attachment fear returns stronger.

The relevant state is not `neediness = 0.7` but a changing **configuration of relations among recruited processes**.

This links psychic conflict directly to dyadic chemistry and metaperception.

## 14. Regime concepts worth preserving without adopting machinery

The live psyche may occupy qualitatively different regimes:

### 14.1 Flexible coexistence

Several motives remain active and mutually informative; multiple responses remain available.

### 14.2 Ambivalent/bistable

Two incompatible configurations remain near-dominant and the system can flip between them.

### 14.3 Oscillatory

The actor repeatedly overcorrects from one pole to another.

### 14.4 Captured/locked-in

One defensive/motivational organization suppresses alternatives and narrows interpretation/action.

### 14.5 Metastable

A temporary configuration persists despite perturbation but can shift when a threshold is crossed.

These are pre-architecture phenomenological descriptors, not state-machine states.

## 15. External research as non-authoritative design evidence

Several research traditions support preserving conflict and dynamics rather than simple additive response rules:

- approach-avoidance research explicitly studies simultaneous reward and threat tendencies and shows that anxiety can reshape valuation and action selection;
- reviews note coactivation of approach and avoidance goals rather than a universal single-axis interpretation;
- conflict-monitoring research treats competing response programs as coactive and capable of recruiting additional control;
- temporal interpersonal emotion research treats emotion as dynamically unfolding within and across people;
- dynamical social research emphasizes attractors, hysteresis, sensitivity to initial conditions, and reciprocal compensation as useful concepts for interpersonal processes.

These literatures are evidence that the originating PFF intuitions correspond to real classes of problems; they do not prescribe Rawlsing implementation.

## 17. Hard negatives

Reject unless independently justified:

- personality traits summed into one response score;
- one current emotion/motive winner;
- forced resolution of contradictory motives;
- behavior treated as direct readout of strongest activation;
- suppression treated as deletion;
- lack of expression treated as lack of underlying motive;
- one universal defense coefficient;
- one linear health modifier;
- ambiguity averaged into one interpretation;
- conflict represented only as conscious indecision;
- a global actor mood used to replace target-specific conflict;
- type label -> fixed conflict formula;
- random oscillation used as a substitute for causal ambivalence.
