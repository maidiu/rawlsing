## 1. Primary synthesis — the cluster may be one feedback loop

The strongest current hypothesis is:

> A developed person does not merely have needs and traits. They repeatedly construe relational situations as presenting characteristic kinds of problems; they have learned ways of trying to secure or protect what matters; blocked pursuit recruits characteristic frustration/regulation responses; repeated responses shape consequences; those consequences can reinforce or destabilize the same expectations, mediators, practices, self-model and attractor organization; and the resulting organizational condition changes which alternatives remain accessible next time.

Abstractly:

```text
DURABLE HISTORY / ORGANIZATION
        |
        v
CURRENT RELATIONAL EVENT
        |
        v
ACTOR UPTAKE / SITUATED RECRUITMENT
        |
        v

  "what is at stake / what kind of failure or opportunity is this?"
        |
        v
PURSUIT / MANAGEMENT STRATEGY
  "what do I do to obtain, protect, restore, avoid, or control it?"
        |
        v
COUNTERPART / WORLD CONSEQUENCE
        |
        +--> expected/sufficient -> regulation / continuation / possible broadening
        |

                      |
                      v
              FRUSTRATION RESPONSE
                      |
                      v
       new behavior / defense / interpretation
                      |

                      v

                      |

```

This is a phenomenon-level causal hypothesis, not adopted runtime topology.

## 2. Relational problem-form is not a need

A need tells us **what domain of concern matters**.

A relational problem-form asks something closer to:

> **What kind of relational predicament does the actor experience themselves as having to solve, prevent, survive, secure, or manage with this particular other in this situation?**

This is a different level of description.

For example, attachment/connection may matter in all of the following, yet the experienced problem differs:

### Case A — connection-maintenance problem

> `How do I keep this person close / preserve the bond?`

### Case B — rejection-risk problem

> `How do I reach without being rejected, humiliated, depleted, or exposed?`

### Case C — autonomy/control problem

> `How do I stay connected without being controlled or swallowed?`

### Case D — recognition problem

> `How do I get this person to see me accurately / value me?`

### Case E — status/rank problem

> `How do I avoid being placed beneath them or treated as inferior?`

### Case F — trust/betrayal problem

> `Can I rely on this person without becoming vulnerable to betrayal?`

### Case G — intimacy/exposure problem

> `How much can I let them close enough to know me without losing protection?`

The same need axis may therefore participate in different problem-forms.

Hard law:

```text
NEED_DOMAIN != RELATIONAL_PROBLEM_FORM
```

Likewise:

```text
RELATIONAL_PROBLEM_FORM != OBJECTIVE_RELATIONSHIP_PROBLEM
```

It is perspective-bound. The actor may organize around a rejection problem even when the user is not rejecting them.

## 3. Problem-form is not necessarily consciously stated

The actor need not think:

> `My relational problem is rejection.`

A problem-form may be visible only through patterned uptake, anticipation, defense and behavior.

For example:

```text
USER: "You can tell me if you need anything."
```

One actor may receive this primarily as availability/connection.

Another may receive it as a dependency trap:

> `If I need them, they will own the upper hand.`

Another may receive it as pity/status threat.

Another may receive it as an invitation to intimacy but feel exposure anxiety.

The system-level causal account can recognize the organizing problem without granting the actor explicit self-knowledge of it.

Therefore:

```text
RELATIONAL_PROBLEM_FORM != CONSCIOUS_PROBLEM_STATEMENT
```

and:

```text
MACHINE_CAUSAL_DESCRIPTION_OF_PROBLEM_FORM
!=
ACTOR_SELF_EXPLANATION
```

## 4. Problem-form may be multiple and conflictual

Do not turn relational problem-form into a single category selector.

A person may simultaneously experience:

- connection opportunity;
- rejection risk;
- status threat;
- intimacy reward;
- autonomy threat.

Example:

```text
USER: "Stay. I don't want you to leave yet."
```

Possible simultaneous significance:

```text
attachment: wanted / connection available
status: gratifying evidence of value
intimacy: desired exposure
rejection: fear this closeness will later be withdrawn
control: discomfort at being asked to stay
```

The live problem may therefore be a **structured field of stakes and conflicts**, not one label.

Hard law:

```text
RELATIONAL_PROBLEM_FORM != ONE_HOT_CATEGORY
```

The term is useful only if it improves explanation of uptake and response beyond enumerating activated need domains.

## 6. Pursuit strategy — what is wanted is not how it is sought

V3 striving identifies a persistent target or pursuit object.

It does not by itself determine how the actor tries to obtain it.

Thus preserve:

```text
WHAT_IS_NEEDED
!=
WHAT_IS_EXPECTED
!=
WHAT_IS_STRIVEN_FOR
!=
HOW_THE_ACTOR_TRIES_TO_GET_OR_PROTECT_IT
```

Two people may both want closeness but pursue it differently.

Examples:

### Direct/assertive pursuit

- ask explicitly;
- initiate;
- challenge ambiguity;
- press for answer;
- compete;
- declare claim.

### Earning/accommodating pursuit

- become useful;
- adapt;
- please;
- caretake;
- prove worth;
- wait for recognition.

### Withdrawing/protective pursuit

- reduce exposure;
- make the other pursue;
- preserve autonomy;
- secure control through distance;
- seek the desired state indirectly.

These are examples, not adopted classes.

The deeper phenomenon is:

> A developed person can have **history-shaped recurrent ways of pursuing or protecting valued conditions** that are not reducible to the value of the need or striving itself.

## 8. Frustration response is a separate phenomenon

How a person tries to get something does not determine how they respond when the attempt fails.

Hard law:

```text
PURSUIT_STRATEGY != FRUSTRATION_RESPONSE
```

A person can pursue directly but react to failure by withdrawing.

A person can pursue through accommodation but react to failure with anger.

A person can pursue through distance but respond to failed distance-control with frantic reassurance-seeking.

The frustration response may include:

- reactive amplification;
- anger/protest;
- emotional truth-demand;
- procedural/problem-solving control;
- positive reframing;
- dismissal/devaluation;
- withdrawal;
- renewed pursuit;
- self-blame;
- other-blame;
- testing;
- retaliation;
- repair;
- collapse;
- humor;
- acceptance;
- flexible route-switching.

Again, these are examples rather than a final taxonomy.

## 9. What counts as “frustration” is actor-relative

An objective event is not intrinsically a frustration event.

Frustration exists relative to:

- what the actor wanted;
- what they expected;
- what strategy they were attempting;
- what outcome they thought the counterpart produced;
- whether they interpret the result as blockage, refusal, failure, ambiguity, insult, accident, or delay.

Thus:

```text
OBJECTIVE_OUTCOME != EXPERIENCED_FRUSTRATION
```

For example, the user replies:

> `I'm not ready to talk about that.`

For one actor this may be ordinary boundary information.

For another it may constitute rejection.

For another it may confirm expected emotional unavailability.

For another it may increase respect because the user set a clear boundary.

Frustration response therefore depends on the same actor-uptake relation established elsewhere.

## 11. Frustration response can alter the next relational problem

This is where the cluster becomes recursive.

Suppose:

```text
PROBLEM-FORM:
"Can I get closeness without being rejected?"

PURSUIT:
indirect bid / testing

USER RESPONSE:
misses the bid

ACTOR UPTAKE:
"they don't care"

FRUSTRATION RESPONSE:
withdraw / coolness / devaluation

USER CONSEQUENCE:
user becomes less warm
```

The actor has now helped create evidence that appears to confirm the original problem-form.

The next turn may become:

```text
"See, closeness is unsafe / unavailable."
```

This is a candidate micro-loop of attractor reinforcement.

Thus:

```text
FRUSTRATION_RESPONSE
CAN_CHANGE_THE_ENVIRONMENT_THAT_NEXT_APPRAISAL_CONSUMES
```

and:

```text
SELF_REINFORCING_RELATIONAL_LOOP
CAN_ARISE_WITHOUT_ANYONE'S_HIDDEN_STATE_BEING_FABRICATED
```

## 12. Attractor capture is not “strong personality”

The Enneagram Levels discussion suggests that an organization can become increasingly **captured by its characteristic solution**.

A useful Rawlsing-native interpretation is not `type intensity` or `health score`.

It is the degree to which a recurrent organization progressively monopolizes the person's:

- perception;
- significance assignment;
- interpretation;
- motivational competition;
- defense;
- response routes;
- self-explanation;
- willingness to absorb corrective evidence.

Candidate signs include:

- perceptual narrowing;
- faster defensive recruitment;
- motive monopolization;
- reduced contradiction tolerance;
- increasing compulsivity;
- stronger hysteresis;
- lower corrective permeability;
- fewer available alternate routes;
- reduced relational reciprocity;
- increasing self-model distortion;
- repeated environment-shaping behavior that generates confirming consequences.

This is a **configuration of dynamic properties**, not necessarily one dimension.

Hard law:

```text
ATTRACTOR_CAPTURE != ONE_HEALTH_SCALAR
```

and:

```text
ATTRACTOR_CAPTURE != CURRENT_MOOD
```

## 13. Capture may happen at multiple timescales

Distinguish at least:

### A. Momentary/live capture

A particular interaction temporarily narrows the actor's repertoire.

Example:

A normally flexible person becomes status-defensive with one critic for five turns.

### B. Relationship-local recurrent capture

The same counterpart repeatedly recruits a constricted pattern.

The person may be flexible elsewhere.

### C. Episode-scale capture

A rupture, prolonged stressor or major life period makes a constricted organization unusually dominant across contexts.

### D. Durable organizational capture / condition

The actor's longer-run developmental organization has become more rigid, self-confirming and difficult to perturb.

These are not automatically different storage objects, but they are non-collapsible timescale phenomena.

```text
LIVE_CAPTURE != RELATIONSHIP_LOCAL_CAPTURE != DURABLE_CAPTURE
```

## 14. Durable organizational condition is not attractor identity

A person's characteristic attractor/organization and the current **condition of that organization** must remain distinct.

Hard laws:

```text
ATTRACTOR_IDENTITY != ATTRACTOR_CONDITION
```

```text
DURABLE_ORGANIZATIONAL_CONDITION != CURRENT_LIVE_EXCURSION
```

```text
CURRENT_LIVE_EXCURSION != MOOD
```

The same durable organization can operate with:

- broad flexibility;
- moderate constraint;
- severe rigidity;
- different capacities for self-observation;
- different tolerance of contradiction;
- different access to alternate responses.

This is one reason a simple personality label is inadequate.

## 15. Reachable bandwidth is more promising than a “health level” scalar

The useful source-theory intuition may be:

> A person has a **range of configurations they can plausibly enter and leave without requiring durable identity change**.

Call this `reachable bandwidth` provisionally.

This does not imply a numeric interval.

It asks:

- Which alternate response organizations remain accessible?
- How much provocation is required to produce constriction?
- How readily can the actor recover?
- Can corrective evidence be metabolized?
- Can contradictory motives coexist without collapse?
- Can awareness interrupt automatic response?
- Can the actor switch pursuit route?
- Does a supportive relationship unlock capacities unavailable elsewhere?

A wider bandwidth could mean **more reachable alternatives**, not “better score.”

A narrower bandwidth could mean that the recurrent organization has fewer viable exits.

Provisional law:

```text
REACHABLE_BANDWIDTH
=
QUESTION_ABOUT_ACCESSIBLE_RESPONSE_ORGANIZATIONS_AND_TRANSITION_POSSIBILITY
NOT
ONE_VALUE_OF_WELLNESS
```

That is about:

- recurrence;
- transition propensity;
- recovery;
- residence/return pattern;
- path dependence;

It is not necessarily:

`health = 5.7`.


## 17. Live excursion can be strongly relationship-specific

A key PFF contribution is that the current user may temporarily recruit a much narrower or broader range than the actor ordinarily inhabits.

Examples:

- a normally guarded actor becomes unusually playful and open with one trusted user;
- a flexible actor becomes rigid and punitive around someone who resembles a humiliating authority figure;
- someone usually independent becomes dependency-sensitive in one attachment relationship;
- someone ordinarily status-conscious becomes relaxed with a person whose acceptance feels secure.

This gives:

```text
DURABLE_PERSON_CONDITION
!=
DYADICALLY_RECRUITED_CONDITION
```

The local excursion can be psychologically real without changing durable V3 condition.

Repeated or powerful local excursions may later become inputs to durable development, but only through V3 admission.

## 18. Lock-in is a feedback process, not a flag

A recurrent organization becomes locked in when its own operation increasingly creates conditions that make itself more likely to recur.

Generic loop:

```text
EXPECTATION / PROBLEM-FORM
        -> selective uptake
        -> characteristic pursuit
        -> defense/frustration response
        -> action
        -> counterpart/world consequence
        -> selective interpretation of consequence
        -> expectation reinforced
        -> alternate interpretations/routes become less available
        -> next event enters narrower basin
```

No single step has to be pathological or irrational.

The lock-in arises from **the recursive loop**.

Therefore:

```text
LOCK_IN != HIGH_STATE_VALUE
```

```text
LOCK_IN = CANDIDATE_PROPERTY_OF_RECURSIVE_TRANSITION_PATTERN
```

## 19. Lock-in may include epistemic narrowing

One particularly important mechanism is **corrective permeability**.

Suppose the actor expects care to become control.

User behaves kindly.

Possible broad response:

> `Maybe care can be safe.`

Possible captured response:

> `They are being nice because they want leverage.`

The second interpretation may recruit protection that causes the actor to distance, which may lead the user to stop offering care, which then appears to confirm the expectation.

Thus capture can operate partly by controlling how disconfirming evidence is interpreted.

This connects:

- actor uptake;
- expectations;
- defense;
- self-model;
- relationship history;
- epistemic uncertainty;
- future action.

Important non-collapse:

```text
CORRECTIVE_EVIDENCE_PRESENT
!=
CORRECTIVE_EVIDENCE_METABOLIZED
```

This is directly relevant to periodic V3 expectation change too.

## 20. Recovery is not simply the reverse of lock-in

Because the system is path-dependent, entering a constricted regime and leaving it need not require symmetric conditions.

Examples:

- one humiliation may trigger capture while many small reassurances fail to undo it;
- one trusted person's intervention may open an alternate route that generic evidence could not;
- conscious recognition of a pattern may help but not immediately dissolve it;
- a learned practice may preserve a route under stress;
- exhaustion may remove access to a normally effective regulatory capacity;
- repeated corrective experiences may gradually widen future uptake.

Thus:

```text
ENTRY_THRESHOLD != EXIT_THRESHOLD
```

and:

```text
RECOVERY != SIMPLE_NEGATIVE_OF_CAPTURE
```

This is a natural extension of PFF hysteresis and V3 developmental path dependence.

## 23. Pursuit failure can either deepen capture or broaden repertoire

A blocked strategy does not always reinforce the attractor.

It can also create learning.

Example:

```text
PROBLEM:
"I need approval to feel secure here."

USUAL PURSUIT:
overperform / impress

FAILURE:
user remains unimpressed
```

Possible captured transition:

```text
increase performance
attack user's competence
withdraw contemptuously
reinterpret user as hostile
```

Possible broadening transition:

```text
recognize overinvestment
try direct curiosity
tolerate nonapproval
discover relationship remains intact
```

Thus the key developmental question is not merely `did the strategy fail?` but:

> **How did the person metabolize the failure, and did the resulting path reduce or increase future response alternatives?**

This may be one of the strongest general V3/PFF connective laws discovered by the cluster.

```text
RELATIONAL_PROBLEM_FORM != FIXED_TYPE_PROPERTY
```

## 25. Interaction-time specialization

In user conversation, the cluster is realized at unusually fine temporal grain.

A single turn may contain:

```text
user cue
-> problem-form activation
-> pursuit impulse
-> anticipated response
-> defensive modification
-> expression
-> user reply
-> frustration or fulfillment
-> immediate strategy shift
-> new live condition
```

Examples include:

- reassurance-seeking that becomes irritation when reassurance is ambiguous;
- teasing as low-risk pursuit of intimacy followed by withdrawal when the user responds literally;
- status display followed by contempt when admiration is not received;
- accommodating warmth followed by resentment when appreciation is absent;
- direct vulnerability followed by defensive joking after perceived exposure.

These are not chat-specific psychological laws, but conversation makes them visible at microtime.

## 27. How this cluster interfaces with the semantic round-trip

The first LLM-facing interaction translation should describe **what was presented**, not assign the actor's relational problem-form.

For example, machine-side interaction coordinates may support:

```text
criticism intensity: moderate
status implication: possible
care/reassurance: moderate
intimacy/familiarity claim: weak
ambiguity: high
```

The developed person then makes this into something actor-specific:

```text
"They pity me and are putting me beneath them."
```

The relational problem-form and current pursuit/frustration dynamics arise **inside person-specific mediation**, not in the discourse encoder.

Therefore:

```text
INTERACTION_SCHEMA != RELATIONAL_PROBLEM_FORM
```

```text
ENCODER_MAY_MEASURE_STATUS_RELEVANT_CUE
BUT_MAY_NOT_DECIDE_ACTOR_STATUS_THREAT
```

and:

```text
ENCODER_MAY_MEASURE_INTIMACY_BID
BUT_MAY_NOT_DECIDE_ACTOR_ATTACHMENT_ACTIVATION
```

This preserves the round-trip boundary constituted in `53`.

## 28. Relationship-specific pattern does not create a third psyche

A pursuit/frustration loop can become genuinely dyadic in process:

```text
A tests indirectly
-> user misses test
-> A withdraws
-> user becomes cautious
-> A reads caution as rejection
-> A tests more strongly
```

There is a real pair-level pattern.

But no shared hidden pair psyche is required.

The pattern can be derived from:

- A's actor-local states;
- user's observable responses;
- objective interaction history;
- recursive coupling.

Thus:

```text
DYADIC_SELF_REINFORCING_LOOP = POSSIBLE
```

while:

```text
DYADIC_SHARED_HIDDEN_PSYCHIC_STATE = NOT_REQUIRED
```

## 29. Rival explanatory models to carry forward

The restored rival-model method should compare at least these explanations.

### Model A — need-only model

Relational responses are sufficiently explained by activated need axes + current magnitude.

Prediction:

No separate problem-form concept adds explanatory power.

### Model B — expectation/strategy model

Needs matter, but the key explanatory structure is expectation + striving + learned pursuit/frustration pattern.

Problem-form is only a human-readable summary.

### Model C — relational problem-form model

A derived relational predicament meaningfully organizes several expectations/motives/defenses at once and predicts uptake/pursuit better than the components considered independently.

### Model D — attractor-dynamics model

Problem-form and strategy are surface descriptions of deeper recurrent basin dynamics; the true explanatory gain lies in transition topology, available routes and capture/recovery.

No model is selected here.

Architecture should not receive a relational-problem object until scenarios discriminate among these explanations.
