## M5-01 — STRUCTURED_LOCAL_CONTEXT

**SOURCE:** Ch. 2 §2.1, pp. 22–28; Ch. 11, pp. 255–286.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical, supported by qualitative case analysis.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** a locally organized social practice with material setting, participants, activities, rules/standards, positions, links and purposes.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** contexts delimit and organize a local scope of possible participation while being linked to other contexts through routes, infrastructures, technologies and participant activities.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** a particular actor faces a context-specific set of accessible activities, relations, counterparts, obligations, exclusions and possible effects on other places.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the context must be actor/time/position bound; mere existence of an institution/place does not establish actor participation.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** persons may use, avoid, negotiate, reinterpret or attempt to change possibilities from their partial positioned grip.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** none directly; downstream V3 may learn/change through actual participation.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** condition/relational configuration, often interval-bearing.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** local practice linked into inter-context structure.

**RECIPROCITY:** participants reproduce or change the local practice through actual coordinated/conflictual action.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** institutionalization, stability, material infrastructure, access/exclusion, local rules, actual counterpart set.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `CONTEXT != GENERIC ENVIRONMENT`; context may not be used as an untyped black box.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** D1 should derive typed context-position ecology; D2 binds validity interval; D3 admits later context changes. Existing V3 owns subjective/action consequences.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`.

## M5-02 — LOCATION_POSITION_SEPARATION

**SOURCE:** Ch. 2 §2.2, especially pp. 29–34; cases throughout Chs. 4, 9–11.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + qualitative illustration.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** actor physically/sociomaterially situated in a context plus structured social relations/positions in that context.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** location specifies concrete situatedness; position specifies socially structured relations, rights, obligations, authority, dependencies and scope. The same location can contain different positions; the same nominal position can have different practical meaning across contexts.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** differential access, influence, counterpart relations, duties, exposure and possible participation.

**REALIZATION LAW:** position must be established by actual relation/rule/role binding, not inferred from co-presence or a demographic label.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** position shapes premises and perspective but does not dictate stance or action.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** none directly.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** relation/condition.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** dyad, family, organization, institution.

**RECIPROCITY:** actors may negotiate or change positions only through social channels.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** rules, authority, membership, legal status, institutional role, actual relation to counterparts.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `LOCATION != POSITION`; `POSITION != PSYCHOLOGICAL IDENTITY`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** D1 `POSITION_BINDING` is strongly supported; candidate tuple should preserve location/context separately rather than silently treating them as position.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE + SHARPEN_NEGATIVE_CEILING`.

## M5-03 — POSITION_SPECIFIC_SCOPE_AND_POWER

**SOURCE:** Ch. 2 pp. 23–28, 31–34; Ch. 4 pp. 71–83.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + qualitative.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** differentiated positions within a shared context.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** positions distribute unequal scopes of participation and unequal capacities to affect arrangements, coparticipants and other contexts. Power is decomposed into concrete scope, access, rights, authority, resources, barriers and effective cross-context reach.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** actor-specific possibilities/constraints, authority applicability, obligations, accessible counterparts and influence channels.

**REALIZATION LAW:** the actor must occupy the relevant position in the valid context/time; formal authority/right remains distinct from exercised authority/right.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** actors can comply, resist, negotiate, ally or abstain; scope does not force a stance.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** downstream experience/practice only when realized.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** relation/condition.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** context/organization/institution.

**RECIPROCITY:** negotiation, conflict and coalition can alter arrangements if they causally change rights/rules/relations.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** institutional enforceability, counterpart response, material resources, coalitions, context links.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `POWER != INTERNAL FORCE`; `FORMAL POWER != EXERCISED CONTROL`; `POSITION != ACTION`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** strong D1 evidence for `AUTHORITY_CONTROL_SANCTION`, `ROLE_OBLIGATION`, `COUNTERPART_FORMATION`, `RESOURCE_DEPENDENCY`.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE / GENERALIZE D1`.

## M5-04 — PARTICIPATION_REALIZATION_LADDER

**SOURCE:** Ch. 2 pp. 28–38; Ch. 4 pp. 71–83; Ch. 5 pp. 84–108.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + qualitative.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** membership/access/position plus contextually available activities.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Dreier separates being part of a practice from actually participating in a particular way. A person can be a member, present, eligible or potentially able to participate without carrying out the activity in question. Participation has mode, degree, recurrence and situated meaning.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** actual counterpart contact, activity, exposure, obligation performance and scope use vary within the same formal membership/position.

**REALIZATION LAW:** require a realized participation/activity fact; do not infer activity from membership, presence or possibility.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** actor concerns, abilities, stance and reasons affect actual participation.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** practice, learning, memory or capacity only downstream and if earned.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** occurrence, recurrent activity, interval.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** local context / cross-context pattern.

**RECIPROCITY:** actual participation contributes to reproducing/changing practice.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** access, timing, ability, competing obligations, counterpart presence, sanctions, actor pursuit.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `MEMBERSHIP != PRESENCE != POSSIBLE_PARTICIPATION != ACTUAL_PARTICIPATION != RECURRENT_PARTICIPATION != CHANGE`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** D1 qualifies possibilities; F3/type-specific realization or existing OEE/practice owners establish actual participation; D2 preserves recurrent identity.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE`.

## M5-05 — PERSONAL_POSSIBILITY_AND_SUBJECTIVE_PREMISE

**SOURCE:** Ch. 2 pp. 31–37; Ch. 5 pp. 99–108.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** contextually arranged social possibilities and actor’s position/history/capabilities.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** socially available possibilities become personally relevant/usable through the person’s situated understanding, concerns and abilities. Reasons for action are formed from how social meanings bear on the person’s concerns, not from rules mechanically causing behavior.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** different actors in the same structural situation may identify, value and use different possibilities.

**REALIZATION LAW:** subjective premise/meaning must be separately established; objective applicability alone cannot mint it.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** central: reasons, concerns, interpretation, resistance and choice.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** belief/knowledge/motive/striving/action orientation—V3 jurisdiction.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** situated uptake/orientation, possibly recurring.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** actor in context.

**RECIPROCITY:** selected actions may later change external arrangements only via D3 channels.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** prior experience, knowledge, capabilities, concerns, perceived consequences, social response.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `OBJECTIVE POSSIBILITY != SUBJECTIVE PREMISE != REASON != ACTION`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** D1 ends before subjective premise. ADP-017/CAN-008/010/011 or exact V3 owners must handle subjective mediation.

**DISPOSITION:** `REJECT_AS_NEW_SEAM_PSYCHOLOGY; REUSE V3`.

## M5-06 — PERSONAL_STANCE_ACROSS_CONTEXTS

**SOURCE:** Ch. 2 pp. 42–46; Ch. 5 §§5.4–5.5, pp. 99–108; Ch. 8 pp. 184–189.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + qualitative.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** repeated participation and comparisons across diverse contexts.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** persons gather and revise stances that help orient how they link, separate, balance and pursue concerns across places. Stances can be relatively stable but are not final and are not directly attached to class/category/position.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** the same structural possibility may be pursued differently across contexts; the person’s activity becomes cross-contextually coordinated without requiring identical behavior everywhere.

**REALIZATION LAW:** stance must be evidenced by actor uptake/practice; cannot be inferred from position.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** stance is itself subjective/personally organized mediation.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** V3 practice/self-model/knowledge/motive/expectation depending exact owner.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** recurrent orientation with revision over trajectory.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person across contexts.

**RECIPROCITY:** stance matters only through actions that enter social practice.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** conflicting concerns, context scope, prior learning, joint response.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `POSITION != STANCE`; `GROUP != PERSONAL_STANCE`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** V3, not D1/D2 storage. D1/D2 provide context-position history over which V3 may form/operate a stance.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE V3`.

## M5-07 — CONDUCT_OF_EVERYDAY_LIFE_COORDINATION

**SOURCE:** Ch. 8 §8.1, pp. 181–189; concrete elaboration Chs. 9–11, pp. 198–286.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + longitudinal qualitative case analysis.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** one person’s simultaneous participation in multiple contexts with different schedules, obligations, resources, relations, opportunities and constraints.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** the person actively composes a livable ordering by linking, separating, balancing and sequencing activities, relationships, concerns and commitments under social arrangements not wholly under personal command.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** limited time/attention, scheduling conflicts, prioritization, routine formation, context switching, deferred/abandoned pursuits, cross-context resource use and practical compromises.

**REALIZATION LAW:** conduct must be instantiated in actual actor action/practice across active context bindings; it is not a structural ecology fact by itself.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** essential—planning, routine, concern, interpretation, improvisation, resistance, accommodation.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** personal practice, skills of coordination, expectations, learning, self-understanding—V3.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** concurrent intervals + recurrence + sequence + transition.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person across household/work/school/therapy/friendship/institutional contexts.

**RECIPROCITY:** conduct changes others’ possibilities when actions are socially realized; then D3 applies.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** time, mobility, material resources, counterpart availability, care duties, institutional schedules, sanctions, personal abilities.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `MULTI_CONTEXT_ECOLOGY != CONDUCT_OF_LIFE`; objective competing demands do not themselves decide coordination.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** composition of D1/D2 outputs with V3 action/practice. No independent D4 is yet proven.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE/COMPOSE; NO_NEW_OWNER_PROVED`.

## M5-08 — TYPED_CROSS_CONTEXT_PROPAGATION

**SOURCE:** Ch. 5 pp. 84–108; Ch. 6 pp. 109–140; Chs. 9–11.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + qualitative process tracing.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** a change/event/activity in Context A plus a concrete link to Context B.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** A changes a resource, time budget, obligation, relation, mobility, stance, information state or actor action that is relevant to participation in B. Effects are transformed by B’s different scope and arrangements.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** changed availability, timing, counterpart relation or activity in B.

**REALIZATION LAW:** require an explicit cross-context carrier/link. Mere temporal succession or common actor identity is insufficient.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** some links are objective (schedule/resource change); others require actor interpretation/action. Keep these separate.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** none automatically; downstream V3 only after realized participation/uptake.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** event-to-condition, condition-to-activity, transition-to-rebinding, recurrent propagation.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** cross-context/domain.

**RECIPROCITY:** B may later feed back to A by the same typed rule.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** transport, schedule, institutional access, communication, relation, actor use, counterpart response.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `CROSS_CONTEXT_EFFECT != GENERIC SPILLOVER`; `CONTENT_IN_A != SAME_CONTENT/EFFECT_IN_B`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** existing candidate registry already includes `CROSS_DOMAIN_PROPAGATION`; D1 provides typed consequence, D2 temporal rebinding, V3 handles subjective coordination.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE / SHARPEN`.

## M5-09 — ROUTINE_RHYTHM_AND_PERIODIC_REBINDING

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + qualitative.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** repeated institutional/family/work/school schedules and recurring activities.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** everyday life is ordered through recurrence at different periods—daily, weekly, fortnightly, monthly, seasonal—plus interruptions, absences, returns and temporary insertions such as therapy.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** recurrently changing active contexts, obligations, counterpart sets and available activity windows.

**REALIZATION LAW:** recurrence identity and valid interval must be preserved; a repeated condition cannot be reconstructed as unrelated events without loss.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** actors routinize, improvise, resist or reorganize the repeated pattern.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** repeated practice can contribute to learning/change through existing V3 history/metabolism.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** recurrent activity/condition + interval + shift.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** multi-context everyday life.

**RECIPROCITY:** repeated actor actions may gradually change the arrangement if external practice changes are realized.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** schedule regularity, season, institutional calendar, interruptions, linked-person demands.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `VARIATION/SHIFT != DURABLE CHANGE`; `RECURRENCE != MULTIPLE UNRELATED EVENTS`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** direct D2 support. D2 must permit overlapping recurrent bindings rather than only one actor-global phase/context.

## M5-10 — PERSONAL_AND_INSTITUTIONAL_TRAJECTORY_SEPARATION

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical + qualitative longitudinal.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** institutions arrange possible/expected sequences, durations, entry/exit and successors. The person’s trajectory unfolds through actual selections, constraints, opportunities, transitions and cross-context pursuits, which need not match the institutional path.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** changing active context set, position, rights, obligations, resources and concerns over time/place.

**REALIZATION LAW:** route possibility/expected transition must be separated from actual transition and from later sustained redirection.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** actors pursue, avoid, interrupt, modify or combine trajectories within constraints.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** biography/history and downstream developmental state only through actual V3 mechanisms.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** transition + trajectory.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** person-in-institutions across life span.

**RECIPROCITY:** actor actions can affect later placement/routes when gate responses admit them.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** age rules, eligibility, prior accomplishments, resources, linked lives, institutional gate decisions.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `INSTITUTIONAL_TRAJECTORY != PERSONAL_TRAJECTORY`; `TRANSITION != TURNING_POINT`; `AGE_STAGE != NATURAL PSYCHOLOGICAL STAGE`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** D2 transition rebinding + ordered typed history. Turning point remains derived, not carrier.

## M5-11 — TRAJECTORY_AS_THREE_GRAINS

**SOURCE:** Ch. 8 pp. 189–197; Chs. 9–11.

**CLAIM STATUS:** worker normalization of Dreier’s theory.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** realized typed history + current arrangement + possible institutional/personal future routes.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** Dreier’s trajectory does three distinguishable jobs: (1) records realized cross-time/cross-place participation history; (2) can be derived as a present view/orientation over that history and possible futures; (3) has causal relevance when prior sequence/duration has changed current position, relation, resources, abilities or stakes.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** path-dependent current ecology and action situation without treating “trajectory” as an occult force.

**REALIZATION LAW:** causal claims must unpack the history-derived current fact that actually mediates the effect.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** current orientation toward the trajectory is V3; objective institutional marks/position changes remain social facts.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** biography/memory/expectation only through V3.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** ordered typed history / derived view / causal process at distinct grains.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** life course across contexts.

**RECIPROCITY:** later action changes subsequent trajectory when socially admitted.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** sequence, duration, timing, context linkage, institutional recognition, actor understanding.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `TRAJECTORY_AS_HISTORY != TRAJECTORY_AS_CURRENT_VIEW != TRAJECTORY_AS_CAUSAL_MEDIATOR`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** existing ordered history can store; D2 rebinds; V3 derives actor-facing meaning; causal use must resolve to typed current facts.

**DISPOSITION:** `GENERALIZE_SEMANTIC_SEPARATION; NO_NEW_TRAJECTORY_OWNER`.

## M5-15 — CONSISTENCY_FROM_CONDUCT_NOT_TRAIT

**SOURCE:** Ch. 8 pp. 188–189; reinforced by Chs. 9–11.

**CLAIM STATUS:** theoretical hypothesis grounded in case analysis.

**STRUCTURAL INPUT:** relatively stable arrangements of recurring participation across contexts.

**MEDIATING MECHANISM:** stable patterns in a person’s functioning can arise because the person repeatedly conducts a similarly organized life, not necessarily because an invariant inner core directly produces identical behavior.

**ACTOR-RELATIVE CONSEQUENCE:** recurrent behavior/pattern under stable context configuration.

**REALIZATION LAW:** require recurrence under comparable bindings; do not infer durable trait from surface regularity alone.

**SUBJECT MEDIATION:** routine, stance, learning and personal practice may contribute.

**DEVELOPMENTAL TARGET:** trait/disposition only if V3 independently earns durability/generalization.

**TEMPORAL FORM:** recurrent activity + stable condition.

**RELATIONAL SCALE:** multi-context life organization.

**RECIPROCITY:** changing arrangements can change the apparent consistency.

**BOUNDARY CONDITIONS:** arrangement stability, repeated positions, context variation, actor history.

**NEGATIVE CEILING:** `BEHAVIORAL CONSISTENCY != INVARIANT TRAIT`.

**RAWLSING MAPPING:** D2 recurrence + V3 durable-change standards; supports anti-direct-trait firewall.

**DISPOSITION:** `REUSE / NEGATIVE_CEILING`.

## Card-level terminal

Dreier yields no mechanism requiring a fourth positive seam capability. His most distinctive addition is a **constraint on composition**: one actor must be representable as simultaneously bound into several context-position-participation structures whose intervals, recurrences and cross-context consequences can overlap, while the person’s coordination of them remains actor-side/V3 rather than being precomputed as structural destiny.
