# 2. Full-volume source court

Each contributed chapter was treated separately. M2 did not transfer one contributor's claims to the editors or to another contributor.

Evidence strength varies materially across the volume.

## Strongest mechanism-bearing evidence

1. **Jemima Repo, ch. 7** — strongest original qualitative evidence for the PRE-V3→V3 seam: interviews with married women in Al-Walajah whose husbands commute through Checkpoint 300, plus policy/statistical context. It yields the clearest linked-life mechanism in the volume.

2. **Mai Taha, ch. 8** — strongest historical/oral-history/archive evidence for recurrent household provisioning, alternative transport/market formation, information networks, internal movement obligations, collective coordination, and suppression. Archival photographs corroborate material scenes such as raid damage and a temporary vegetable market.

3. **Sigrid Vertommen, Weeam Hammoudeh, and Michal Nahman, ch. 9** — strongest reproductive-gate/market cases: legal-policy fertility gates plus two empirical lines, including Nahman's 21 Romanian egg-provider interviews and prisoner/spouse sperm-smuggling/IVF cases.

4. **Susan Ferguson, ch. 6** — strongest historical education/institutional-access synthesis: school closure, teacher routes, alternative classrooms, administrative rebinding, and sanctions against teacher collective action.

## Useful but more evidence-bounded chapters

AbuMezied, Abu Moghli/Rosen, Bhattacharya, and Bitton provide many strong mechanism candidates but rely substantially on secondary synthesis, reports, policy/history, narratives, or theoretical interpretation. Their Rawlsing value rises when the narrower rule/resource/route/task chain is separated from broader political intent or psychological claims.

Gilmore's foreword and Joudah's “Onwards” are not used as independent causal evidence for architectural ownership.

# 9. Top evidence limits / negative ceilings

1. `EDITORSHIP != AUTHORSHIP`: chapter claims remain with named contributors.
2. Political/theoretical labels are not silently converted into causal operators or independently adjudicated by M2.
3. `OBSERVED EFFECT != PROOF OF POLICY INTENT`.
4. `GROUP STATISTIC != ACTOR FACT`.
5. `SOCIAL/DEMOGRAPHIC CATEGORY != WORK/CARE RELATION != PSYCHOLOGY`.
6. `FORMAL RULE != APPLICABILITY != ENCOUNTER != TREATMENT != REALIZED ACCESS != DOWNSTREAM EFFECT`.
7. `INFRASTRUCTURE CONDITION != PSYCHOLOGICAL EFFECT`.
8. `KINSHIP LABEL != SUPPORT/CARE/OBLIGATION`.
9. The volume generally does not provide longitudinal developmental evidence sufficient to map structural conditions directly to child/adult developmental effects.
10. Qualitative/oral-history evidence supports reported pathways and meanings but usually not population prevalence or universal causal effect.
11. Taha's retrospective oral histories retain recall/selection limits; archival photos corroborate scenes, not complete causal explanations.
12. Repo's spouse interviews support the respondents' routines/experiences; they do not directly measure husbands' psychology or establish all households' prevalence.
13. The ch.9 authors explicitly state that “genocidal pronatalism” is not meant to assert that state pronatalism itself is genocidal and that they lack ethnographic data for that leap; M2 preserves that ceiling.
14. `ACTION != SUCCESS`; `ALTERNATIVE PRACTICE != RULE CHANGE`.
15. “Cumulative,” “chronic,” and “intergenerational” require a typed iterative mechanism; labels are not self-executing.
