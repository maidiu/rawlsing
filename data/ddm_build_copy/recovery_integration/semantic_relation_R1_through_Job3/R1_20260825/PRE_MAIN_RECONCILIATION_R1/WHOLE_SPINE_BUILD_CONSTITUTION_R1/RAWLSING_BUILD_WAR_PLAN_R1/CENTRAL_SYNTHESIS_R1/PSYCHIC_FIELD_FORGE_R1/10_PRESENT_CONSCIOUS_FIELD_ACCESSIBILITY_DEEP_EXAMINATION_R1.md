# PSYCHIC FIELD FORGE R1 — PRESENT CONSCIOUS FIELD / ACCESSIBILITY DEEP EXAMINATION R1

**Status:** `PRE_ARCHITECTURE_CONCEPT_EXAMINATION__BANKED__MUST_FACTOR__NOT_ARCHITECTURE_ADOPTION__NO_RUNTIME_AUTHORITY`

## 0. Purpose

This file deeply examines PFF frontier pocket 10, `conscious field / attention / accessibility`, together with its dependencies on V3 actor accessibility, historical V2 salience, live PFF recruitment, self-model, defense, memory, metaperception, and expression.

The central question is:

> What does it mean, in Rawlsing terms, for something to be presently part of the actor's experience, as distinct from merely being causally active, actor-accessible in principle, salient for expression, consciously named, self-attributed, or first-person claimable?

Current conclusions:

`V3_ACCESSIBILITY != PRESENT_CONSCIOUS_FIELD`

`V2_SALIENCE != PRESENT_CONSCIOUS_FIELD`

`CAUSAL_ACTIVITY != CONSCIOUS_PRESENCE`

`CONSCIOUS_PRESENCE != CONCEPTUAL_NAMING`

`CONCEPTUAL_NAMING != SELF_ATTRIBUTION`

`SELF_ATTRIBUTION != CAUSAL_ACCURACY`

`PRESENT_CONSCIOUS_FIELD = MUST_FACTOR`

`PRESENT_CONSCIOUS_FIELD_AS_A_NEW_STORED_DATABASE = NOT_JUSTIFIED`

The strongest present hypothesis is that the present conscious field is a **momentary actor-perspectival projection across current live PFF state, standing conscious self, lawful actor-accessible memory/epistemic material, current perception, and nonpropositional felt effects**. It may be better understood as a dynamic cross-section than as a fifth durable depth layer.

## 1. Governing Rawlsing boundaries

### 1.1 V3 actor accessibility is a hard ceiling, not current attention

ADP-016 and ADP-017 already separate:

`OBJECTIVE EVENT/FACT`
`!= CAUSAL MEMORY`
`!= CURRENT MEMORY ACCESSIBILITY`
`!= ACTOR EPISTEMIC STANCE`
`!= CURRENT CLAIM ACCESSIBILITY`
`!= FIRST-PERSON CLAIM PERMISSION`

An actor can lawfully know or remember something without currently thinking about it.

Therefore:

`ACTOR_ACCESSIBLE(P) != P_CURRENTLY_IN_CONSCIOUS_FIELD`

PFF may not treat all actor-accessible content as simultaneously present.

### 1.2 Historical V2 salience is downstream selection, not phenomenology

CAN-016 explicitly establishes:

`ACCESSIBLE != SALIENT`

`SALIENT != MEMORY`

`SALIENT != MEMORY_ACCESSIBILITY`

`SALIENT != PERSISTENT_CONCERN`

`SALIENT != ACTOR_BELIEF`

`SALIENT != PHENOMENOLOGY`

`SALIENT != EXPRESSION_REGISTER`

`SALIENT != RESPONSE_MOVE`

`SALIENT != CAUSAL_ACTION`

Its set law places salience within an actor-safe candidate set for V2 expression planning.

Therefore PFF must not repurpose V2 salience as consciousness.

`V2_SALIENCE = WHAT CURRENT EXPRESSION PLANNING SELECTS AS RELEVANT`

is distinct from:

`PFF_PRESENT_CONSCIOUS_FIELD = WHAT IS PRESENT TO / FELT BY / EXPERIENCED THROUGH THE ACTOR NOW`

## 2. Present consciousness is not one binary flag

Human-like present experience appears to require several non-collapsible distinctions.

A current item/state may be:

- causally active but entirely inaccessible;
- accessible in principle but not presently activated;
- psychically active but only indirectly felt;
- present as diffuse bodily/affective tone;
- present in the fringe as `something is off`;
- consciously felt but unnamed;
- named but uncertain;
- recognized and self-attributed;
- focal and intrusive;
- deliberately attended;
- consciously recognized but intentionally ignored;
- consciously recognized but actively suppressed from expression;
- easily available if prompted but not presently focal;
- salient for expression while only weakly experienced;
- strongly experienced but irrelevant to the current conversational response.

Therefore:

`CONSCIOUSNESS != FOCUS`

`FOCUS != SALIENCE`

`SALIENCE != REPORTABILITY`

`REPORTABILITY != FIRST_PERSON_PERMISSION`

## 3. Candidate phenomenal distinctions

These are phenomenal categories only, not adopted schema values.

### 3.1 Focal articulated content

The actor is actively thinking something in a conceptually explicit way.

Examples:

- `I need to leave soon.`
- `She seems angry.`
- `I do not want him to know I am upset.`

### 3.2 Focal nonpropositional affect/body state

The actor is clearly experiencing an affective or bodily condition without necessarily representing it as a proposition.

Examples:

- tightness;
- warmth;
- dread;
- agitation;
- attraction-like bodily pull;
- shame heat;
- relief.

### 3.3 Felt-but-unnamed / preconceptual presence

The actor experiences something but lacks or has not yet supplied an explicit conceptual label.

Example:

`I feel weird around her` without access to a more precise explanation.

### 3.4 Peripheral/background presence

Something contributes to the actor's overall field without occupying focal attention.

Examples:

- ongoing grief beneath a work conversation;
- fatigue coloring patience;
- low-level tension around an upcoming meeting;
- awareness that B is watching while attention is nominally on another topic.

### 3.5 Fringe/metacognitive signal

The actor experiences that there is something to know, remember, or resolve without possessing the content.

Examples:

- `something about this reminds me of someone`;
- `I know there is a reason this bothers me`;
- `I cannot put my finger on what is wrong`.

This is especially important for hidden-cause influence without analyst leakage.

### 3.6 Intrusive presence

Material enters or repeatedly returns to focal experience despite the actor's intention to attend elsewhere.

### 3.7 Voluntarily sustained attention

The actor deliberately keeps material in focus because it serves a goal.

### 3.8 Suppressed/deflected presence

Material becomes near-accessible or briefly present and is then actively pushed from focus, relabeled, displaced, or crowded out.

This must remain distinct from total inaccessibility.

## 4. Hidden cause can yield conscious effect without conscious explanation

This is one of the most important PFF laws.

Example:

`latent attraction causally active`
`+ actor has no generated proposition "I am attracted"`
`-> bodily arousal / increased attentiveness / nervous excitement becomes conscious`

Lawful result:

`HIDDEN_CAUSE -> CONSCIOUS_EFFECT`

Forbidden shortcut:

`HIDDEN_CAUSE -> ACTOR KNOWS HIDDEN_CAUSE`

Similarly:

`rejection-sensitive structure`
may produce
`felt dread / urge to withdraw`
without producing
`I am reacting because of my childhood rejection history`.

Thus:

`CONSCIOUS_EFFECT != CONSCIOUS_CAUSAL_MODEL`

This is the main route by which D-band causal depth can influence the present field without becoming actor omniscience.

## 5. Standing conscious self versus present conscious field

The original PFF vision distinguishes the standing conscious self (C-band) from deeper total personhood (D-band), relational activation (B-band), and expression (A-band).

This examination suggests a further clarification:

> The present conscious field may not be another depth band. It may be the **momentary cross-sectional realization** of portions of standing C plus current B activation plus actor-safe memory/perception plus indirect effects of D.

Candidate relation:

`D = total durable person / causal depth`

`C = standing conscious organization / recognized self / accessible life situation`

`B = current relational/live activation, including hidden and conscious processes`

`PRESENT FIELD = momentary actor-perspectival cross-section through C + B + current perception/memory/anticipation, with D allowed to influence through effects but not unauthorized explanation`

`A = resulting expression/action formation`

This is a conceptual refinement only; it does not create a new architecture layer.

## 6. The present field includes world and self, not merely internal emotion

PFF should not reduce consciousness to an internal mood vector.

The actor's current field may include:

- the interlocutor as presently perceived;
- what is happening in the environment;
- current task/goal;
- bodily state;
- affective tone;
- current thought;
- current memory/image;
- anticipated future;
- metaperception (`what B sees in me`);
- self-evaluation;
- competing impulses;
- unresolved concern;
- perceived opportunity/threat;
- conscious intention.

The field is therefore a **situated subjective scene**, not an emotion list.

## 7. Salience and consciousness can cross in both directions

### 7.1 Conscious but not V2-salient

The actor may consciously notice hunger during a conversation where hunger is irrelevant to the response.

### 7.2 V2-salient but weakly conscious

A currently actor-safe relationship fact may be highly relevant to expression planning while not occupying strong phenomenal attention before the response is formed.

### 7.3 Causally active but neither conscious nor V2-salient

A hidden defensive structure may bias perception without becoming actor experience or expression content.

### 7.4 Conscious and salient

A painful accusation becomes both focal experience and central to expression planning.

Therefore downstream expression planning must not define the upstream field.

## 8. Attention, salience, and consciousness should remain theoretically uncollapsed

External cognitive-neuroscience literature remains contested on the exact relation among attention, salience, working memory, reportability, and consciousness.

Useful non-authoritative lessons include:

- attention and consciousness can be at least partly dissociated;
- emotionally significant information can receive processing priority before or without full conscious access;
- attentional gain and salience attribution are themselves distinguishable concepts;
- conscious experience/reportability versus broader cognitive access remains an active scientific debate.

Rawlsing should therefore avoid prematurely selecting one scientific theory of consciousness as project ontology.

The correct pre-architecture requirement is weaker and safer:

> Preserve the observable/phenomenological distinctions required by generated personhood, and let architecture later decide the minimum machinery that can realize them.

## 9. Self-model changes what becomes recognized, not merely what exists

The Enneagram source material provides useful internal logic showing recurring patterns in which actors resist recognizing certain motives or affects because those contents conflict with self-image.

Rawlsing translation:

`CAUSALLY_ACTIVE_CONTENT`
may be transformed by
`SELF_MODEL + DEFENSE`
into
`AVAILABLE_CONSCIOUS_CONTENT`.

Examples:

- anger becomes `concern`;
- attraction becomes `irritation`;
- dependency need becomes `annoyance at the other person's demands`;
- fear becomes `being practical`;
- grief becomes `fatigue`;
- shame becomes `contempt`.

These are examples of possible transformation, not universal mappings.

Thus the field is not simply a transparent readout of live activation.

## 10. Defense may operate at three different locations

A major clarification is that `defense` should not be one downstream cosmetic filter.

### 10.1 Pre-field access defense

Prevents or reduces a causal content's entry into current awareness.

### 10.2 In-field interpretation defense

The actor experiences something but interprets/labels it in a self-protective way.

### 10.3 Post-field expression defense

The actor knows/feels something but chooses to conceal, transform, soften, exaggerate, or posture around it.

One defensive organization may operate across all three.

Therefore:

`DEFENSE != EXPRESSION_STYLE`

and

`CONSCIOUS_FIELD != DEFENSE_FREE_CAUSAL_STATE`.

## 11. Conscious contradiction is lawful

The actor can simultaneously experience:

- love and resentment;
- attraction and fear;
- certainty and doubt about different aspects of the same event;
- desire to disclose and desire to conceal;
- anger and guilt about anger;
- relief and grief;
- trust in one domain and distrust in another.

The conscious field must not force global coherence.

`CONSCIOUS_CONTRADICTION != INVALID_STATE`

This aligns with ADP-017's preservation of contradictory/coexisting epistemic stances rather than forced scalar resolution.

## 12. Current field may be partially structured by accessibility gradients rather than membership only

Without adopting numeric scales, PFF must preserve distinctions such as:

- immediately focal;
- readily available if attention turns;
- peripheral but felt;
- hovering/fringe;
- repeatedly intruding;
- strongly inhibited;
- inaccessible despite causal effect.

A binary `conscious/not conscious` representation may be too coarse.

However, no universal numeric consciousness strength is adopted here.

## 13. Present field and memory

Memory can participate in several ways:

- explicit recalled scene in focus;
- accessible fact supporting current thought;
- emotional/associative trace influencing the field without explicit recall;
- vague familiarity;
- involuntary memory intrusion;
- expected memory that fails to arrive;
- memory whose current meaning differs from its objective event history.

ADP-016 already separates causal memory from accessibility. PFF must build on that distinction rather than recreate memory state.

Critical law:

`PSYCHIC_RESONANCE_WITH_HISTORY != EXPLICIT_MEMORY_RECALL`

## 14. Present field and social metaperception

The actor-model examination established first-order social reading and personal second-order metaperception as core phenomenal requirements.

Those readings can occupy different positions in the field:

- focal: `she can tell I am lying`;
- peripheral: a low-level sense of being watched/judged;
- fringe: `I feel exposed but I am not sure why`;
- absent while still causally shaping defense through hidden recruitment.

Therefore actor modeling and consciousness must be linked without assuming every social inference is consciously articulated.

## 15. The field changes over time without necessarily changing the person

Momentary field transitions include:

- attention shifts;
- one feeling becomes nameable;
- a suppressed motive intrudes;
- reassurance moves a threat from focal to peripheral;
- an interlocutor's remark makes a dormant memory accessible;
- a competing interpretation becomes dominant;
- the actor recognizes after speaking what they were feeling;
- a topic leaves focus but residue remains.

These are live-state transitions, not automatically durable V3 development.

`PRESENT_FIELD_CHANGE != V3_PERSON_CHANGE`

Repeated/admitted consequences may later contribute to V3 change through proper authority.

## 17. Renderer boundary

The final renderer must not receive the hidden causal account merely because hidden causes generated conscious effects.

Conceptually the renderer may receive, subject to later exact contracts:

- actor-permitted conscious/expressible content;
- current situational/social context;
- expression pressures/constraints;
- posture/leakage guidance that does not reveal hidden causes;
- actual surface conversation again.

It must not receive analyst-only explanations that would let it say what the actor does not know.

`HIDDEN_CAUSE_CAN_SHAPE_RENDER_CONSTRAINTS`

but

`HIDDEN_CAUSE_CANNOT_BECOME_FIRST_PERSON_EXPLANATION_WITHOUT_ACTOR_AUTHORITY`.

Before proposing a new `ConsciousFieldState` object, architecture must test whether the phenomenon can be represented through:
