# Who gets the sleeve back?
## The constitution of a person through the work that a life leaves for others

**SORACLE-CO candidate manuscript · November 23, 2026.** The Nadia histories below are alternative fictional constructions. Ages, events, households, shop arrangements and dialogue are stipulated. The constitutive argument is a proposal about possible development. The [genealogy and source court](02_GENEALOGY_AND_SOURCE_COURT.md) gives its inherited sources, empirical limits and contrary cases.

### A good sleeve

At eleven Nadia could run a straight row of stitches down two pieces of cloth and keep their edges together. At home she told a cousin she could put a sleeve into a shirt. She had good reason to think so. Her mother passed her the cut pieces and showed her where to start. Her father took the finished shirts to customers. People wore them. What she had never seen was her mother at the table after she went to bed, easing out the stitches at the shoulder and turning the sleeve a little so a person could reach forward in it. Nadia had learned to feed the cloth quite accurately. Her mother had learned to feel, almost without looking, where her daughter began to pull it tight. Both had become more capable. Each improvement could make it *harder* for Nadia to discover which part of making a wearable shirt was still beyond her.

This is a small but consequential sense in which a person is formed through a relation. Even an observer who had measured the child's hand movements and examined every delivered shirt could attribute the wrong competence to the child. The decisive missing history runs through a different pair of hands at a different hour. It also runs through the price paid per shirt. Correcting it after bedtime cost a tired adult less tonight than holding up the delivery until the child could see the problem tomorrow. This calculation could be completely absent from what either said about teaching. Love, necessity and technical skill met in a particular distribution of the work. Love took the form of sparing the child a failure. Technical competence took the form of an ability to anticipate and correct another person's characteristic error. The arrangement allowed a mistaken expectation of independent competence to become reasonable in the child's daily experience.

Consider a contrary household in which the parent does the same amount of correction while the child watches, handles the seam before and after, and sometimes gets to try again. The difference is neither the quantity of care nor the quantity of error. It is what comes within reach of which participant for a further act. A second contrary history gives Nadia a badly fitted shirt to wear herself. Bodily difficulty may disclose the problem with no instruction whatever. She could still blame the cloth, work out a solution by herself, enjoy the shirt and ignore it, or acquire a habit of avoiding that cut. A third child watches another learner at work and sees the entire sequence without sewing a stitch. One can become perceptive through observation. What the claim requires is access to a consequential difference in a form the particular learner can use. No general requirement to make one's own mistakes follows.

There is a harder counterexample. Mother might show Nadia every stitch, explicitly teach one successful adjustment, hand the work back, and leave her perfectly free to try another. Nadia might reasonably take such a lesson as evidence that she has now been shown all that matters. Bonawitz and colleagues found a related discrimination in preschoolers playing with a novel toy. In their first experiment, after an apparently complete lesson on one function, children discovered an average of 0.72 of the three other functions. After an interrupted lesson the mean was 1.30. The planned contrast compared the teaching condition with three comparison conditions together. In the second experiment exploration also varied according to whom the child saw being taught. The authors interpret the pattern in terms of what children infer from a knowledgeable teacher's selection of evidence. These were brief toy encounters. They establish neither the years-long effect nor the child's belief in this sewing history. [Bonawitz et al., *Cognition*, 2011, experiments 1–2](https://cocolab.stanford.edu/papers/BonawitzEtAl2011-Cognition.pdf)

That comparison changes the account. A correction can be physically available and actionable yet arrive *as somebody's finished answer*. In one kitchen Nadia sees how Mother fixed this shoulder and takes that as the end of the matter. In another Mother tries the identical adjustment, discovers that it fails for the next wearer, and asks the child to hold the cloth while she works out why. The difference is what the child's history entitles her to infer about the completeness of what she has been shown. A learner who treats a gifted teacher's answer as exhaustive can become more competent at a particular repair and less inclined to discover another. Effective opportunity to investigate includes the historically acquired sense that there is still something to find out. Even that sense need not lead to exploration on a night when the next garment is due.

The unity of this life lies in the way its activity produces the conditions of further activity, including conditions made elsewhere, carried by other people, or unavailable to the person whose life they sustain. The particular way a consequence gets back to someone, and the power that person has to do something with it, help determine what can become an object of attention, an emotion, a practiced skill, a judgement or a motive. This relation can itself be changed by what the participants learn. It is explanatory when we can follow who did the work, what became possible because of it, who received which part, and where that course could have failed. A continually developing person has a body in which these histories meet. The world in which they meet has many centers of activity.

### Before anyone could ask whether it fitted

The adults enter this inquiry through a different historical door. In a Rawlsing construction a mother's whole pattern of eligible survey responses at a particular time constrains the fictional adult who could give those responses. The wording, routing, period, missing observations and distribution of comparable cases matter. A reported commitment to equality can coexist with adherence to a religious rule and an unequal division of work. The report does not tell us who took a child to a fitting. To infer the mother in our story, one would also need a plausible history of access to tools, instruction, credit, customers, bodily experience and relationships. A different woman with the same answers could have bought the shirts and never touched a machine. No actual source respondent was selected for this illustration.

Her possible meeting with a future father belongs at a particular time and place. If a proposed meeting conflicts with his work elsewhere, or with her earlier commitments, a statistically plausible pairing does not rescue it. He has a past before he meets her. Either may already have children. Once the two meet, years in which they work together or live apart can form both of them *before* Nadia arrives. In our selected fiction, he learns the customers' routes and brings garments back, although his own livelihood makes a second visit costly. His household work makes his wife's care possible and sometimes deprives her of the chance to question a wearer herself. He might genuinely like visiting people. The division of work does not exhaust him. Once the initial adult histories are selected their subsequent desires and actions have the same historical standing as their daughter's. The adult answers constrain a starting cut. They cannot be used to settle a later quarrel.

Nadia has no corresponding adult answer to match. In her first months someone must feed, carry, warm and let her sleep. The distinction between a hungry body settling and a child recognizing a benefactor arises because providing food, sensing the person nearby, and learning the anticipatory rhythm of feeding can come apart in real time. Father buys the food. Mother feeds her. On another day a relative does both. The familiar approach of hands may come to prepare the infant for a change of posture before any category of helper is available. A delayed feed can leave her hungry without teaching her anything about absence. Relief can occur while she faces away. The father's later account of the sacrifices he made can be truthful and unavailable to the infant whose growth they sustained. The infant in turn pulls away or settles in an unexpected position. Tomorrow a caregiver may change the way they support her, or may notice nothing. The first object of joint history is often something no participant conceived in advance.

It would be a mistake to make this the origin story for the seamstress. Millions of later activities are compatible with such a beginning. The positive continuity is narrower. There is a developing body that has already participated and can consequently meet certain changes with an acquired anticipation. With maturation and different forms of participation, *what can count as a difference* can itself change. Feeling a pull on a sleeve, identifying it as the effect of the orientation of two cut pieces, and learning from another person's account of a discomfort one cannot feel are different acquired operations. A person may become exceptionally good at the first while having no occasion for the third. A learner who signs may discuss a movement in its absence with someone who shares their language. A hearing child who cannot understand the language being used may see an adjustment quite clearly while missing its explanation. Disability, accessible communication and task design vary independently. There is no master sequence from bodily action to spoken commentary to complete knowledge.

There is an empirical check on how far such an argument can go. In the Nicaraguan Sign Language study, ten early-exposed signers from each of three successive cohorts described a cartoon to a peer. Later cohorts more often expressed manner and path of motion as sequential elements. The authors also documented a construction in which one element recurs around another. Representing two aspects separately creates a new problem of expressing that they happened together, as well as a new means of combining them. Children entering a community could encounter expressive possibilities changed by earlier learners, while some earlier signers continued using other forms. This is evidence about the *organization of expressions in these tasks*. It is especially instructive because no solitary child's successful repetition explains the difference between cohorts. The study does not establish how a specific child thought privately, whether the change required correction by a peer, or a law of cognitive superiority. Its authors also propose contributions from children's own ways of analyzing input. A purely institutional explanation would leave that proposal untouched. [Senghas, Kita and Özyürek, 2004, pp. 1780–82 and Fig. 2](https://semantics.uchicago.edu/kennedy/classes/sum07/myths/senghas_kita_ozyurek.pdf)

The same materialist obligation extends to an oral apprenticeship, several institutionally different literacies, early accessible sign, and the project's stipulated viable wolf-den and Montessori counterworlds. What materials can a child handle? Which activities recur, with whom, through what signs? Which operations can later be directed at an absent object? Viability in the den is given. Human written instructions and a fluent signing community are not. Montessori materials can remain untaken on a shelf. Neither world licenses a predicted adult. The commonality grounded here is dependence on a developing, active body in an ecology that affords particular histories. The differences concern what means of making further differences have actually come into being.

### What changes when work can come back unfinished

In one continuation, when Nadia is thirteen her mother takes a morning job. Friday evening becomes the one time they can alter clothes together before Saturday delivery. A wearer, Inés, returns with a jacket. It looks smooth on its hanger. It draws painfully when she lifts a crate. Inés has twenty minutes before her bus. She elects to try it on, raises her arms and declines a second movement in front of a window. Nadia's mother leaves one seam loosely basted. Nadia moves it and asks Inés to repeat only the first movement. The jacket rides up again. On the next attempt Inés can lift the empty crate without the collar pressing into her neck. She catches her bus. At this point there has been a changed garment, a performed comparison and relief for one wearer under one load. Nobody has established what will happen through a full shift, whether Inés likes Nadia, or which part the child could repeat alone.

The decisive change comes across ordinary returns. In one garment the same alteration spoils the fit. A taller man prefers a little restriction to a loose cuff that catches on things. Another customer sends a message through Father, and the place he understood to be tight is the wrong one. Across several Fridays Nadia learns how to leave a particular join open, ask for a movement that separates two possible causes of the pull, and close it only after an answer she can use. She does this later on an unfamiliar coat by varying where she holds the cloth. She never needs to put the movement into tailoring vocabulary. Her skill has acquired a means of finding out *which skill to exercise*. Most of the finished stitches still look the same.

Two antecedent histories have changed their function. From Mother she had learned how to make a seam hold. From fetching work with Father she knew how to keep a customer from missing a bus and how to ask whether everything was all right. Before the fitting, the first operation usually ended the work and the second usually ended the visit. After these encounters a deliberately temporary seam can *open* an inquiry. A question at the door can grant the wearer an effective part in deciding whether the garment is finished. The answer changes an object in a way neither politeness alone nor accurate stitching alone could accomplish. The wearer may want to leave, refuse the trial or propose another end entirely. Knowing how to leave the join open can preserve that choice without forcing a conversation. The method depends on cloth that can be resewn, a fitting place, affordable time and the wearer's bodily authority over what hurts. Some fabrics, jobs and encounters defeat it.

A repeated gesture is weak evidence for this acquisition. Give Nadia the same unusual coat without an audience. Does she keep the critical section open when nobody can praise her? Give her the right place to baste but a wearer who cannot come back. Can she obtain the pertinent difference another way, identify which part she cannot judge, or close the job as best she can? If she leaves the identical opening everywhere even when it cannot affect the stated difficulty, imitation is a better explanation. If she performs the adjustment only after Mother has silently reset it, the relevant power still belongs to their coordinated activity. If she can do everything on the first unfamiliar coat before these Fridays occur, the fittings may have revealed an earlier capacity. And if she returns a good garment because a redesigned pattern fits everyone in this small batch, transfer of learning is unnecessary to explain those outcomes.

The same history can form a motive, but the evidence must change. In the selected continuation, on a free Saturday Nadia makes a reversible pocket for an older neighbor who has trouble reaching into a coat while seated. She had set out to sew for the pleasure of the machine. Only when the neighbor tries the coat does she discover that the question of *where he wants things to be* is interesting to her in its own right. Weeks later she arranges a time to see how a different person carries things, with no commission due and no seam already planned. An incidental consequence of making something has become an object for which she initiates activity. She could have retained a skill and spent all her Saturdays making bright scraps vibrate beneath the needle. That pleasure has its own rhythm and sensuous object. Explaining every free hour through a wound or a commitment to serve others would destroy a real alternative.

### The acts a person can bear to leave undone

There is an affective history inside the same good work. Suppose Mother has always apologized and taken a garment back whenever anyone looked unhappy. Sometimes the apology protected the evening's income. Sometimes she loved the person and wanted to spare them discomfort. Sometimes it purchased peace at her own expense. At fifteen Nadia may feel ashamed of enjoying praise for a garment that cost Mother a night's sleep. The pleasure survives inside the shame. To remove it would change what the shame is *about*. She may come to hear every pause at the counter as a request she has already failed. A relative's familiar pause can bring her hand to the garment before she has named an emotion. Under exam pressure at school the same pause from a friend might scarcely register. Perceptual analogy can be tacit, narrowly accurate, or wrong. Neither the pause nor the history of praise alone tells us when it will operate.

In one evening someone hesitates. Nadia has a sore wrist. She wants to serve the wearer, fears an accusation, enjoys being regarded as quick, and is listening for a bus for her own appointment. These do different work in the act that occurs. Pain changes how she can grip the cloth. The bus changes how much time she has. Her learned anticipation of blame makes her reach before the wearer finishes. Affection changes the *method* of stopping herself: she draws up a chair, says “Show me where,” and waits. The wearer uses the chair to tie a shoe and asks to pay. The shake of Nadia's tired hand was visible. A close friend could recognize the pain correctly and still misread the hurried offer as guilt. Nadia hears herself offer a chair and later laughs at how solemn she sounded. An imagined offer would provide a different private experience and no chair for anyone to use. One act has a single material course and several distinct genealogies down to the choice of its words, tempo, effort and unfinished business.

What could it mean to have become able to leave a dissatisfied person in peace? If Nadia swallows the old apology while privately spending the evening rehearsing it, strategic restraint is real work. If a new employer has taken over complaints, the old situation may simply never occur. If a familiar tone ceases to startle her after repetition, habituation is possible without much change in what she thinks a customer is owed. In another life she has acquired a harder distinction. Several wearers have shown her when they want to solve something themselves. She has seen one positively irritated by her attempt to take over. She has received care from a friend who can accept an unanswered offer. She still wants the work to be good, understands a complaint, feels a brief pang and can continue with what she was doing while the other decides. When help is requested she can return readily. The availability of both continuing and returning is better evidence for changed susceptibility than a calm face or permanent withdrawal. If she cannot bear the person's presence at all, numbing, avoidance, exhaustion and a new compulsion remain rivals. This discrimination comes from R17's account of acquired non-compulsion and changes how the provisional account treats an unanswered consequence. [R17, lines 238–248](../SEAM/30_THREE_AGENT_MULCH_20261119_R1/16_R17_RECONSTITUTED_HOME_CANDIDATE_R1.md#L238-L248)

This is also a limit on a favorite explanation of my own. Learning to keep work answerable to others can make someone extraordinarily intrusive. Nadia can demand a trial, insist that every dissatisfaction be named, follow up with someone who has asked for silence, then feel virtuous because the paperwork says she listened. A wearer may want the old coat exactly as it is because it belonged to a sister who has died. Nobody owes Nadia the history that would make the choice intelligible. The more differentiated capacity is to recognize when a practical answer is hers to act upon, when someone else can decide, and when the uncertainty must be borne. Power over that division can change even as her skill at sewing or her desire for approval persists. There is no inevitable advance toward this form of care.

### The answer that closes the case

At twenty-two, in this fiction, Nadia takes paid work altering uniforms. The shop promises a fitting to each worker. It pays her by completed garment, with a supervisor's signature releasing each job. Inés now works for the company that orders the uniforms. She has to reach overhead and has ten minutes off the line to collect her coat. Nadia recognizes her and asks for the old movement. Inés demonstrates that a seam pulls. Nadia marks it with chalk. A supervisor signs the form recording that a fitting took place. The company collects the uniforms before Nadia has authority to reopen the job. On Monday the coat still pulls. The completed form legally signifies nothing beyond the stipulated shop practice here. Its material effect is precise: the order has been collected and no alteration time has been allocated.

This is an actual contradiction in the organization of work. The fitter fulfills the shop's promise to hear a complaint by the very act which, under its rule, permits the shop to end its responsibility for the complaint. In the family fitting, the wearer's movement changed the criterion of completion. At the shop, the operation Nadia learned can be recruited to furnish evidence of completion even when the wearer has disclosed a defect. Protecting the wearer's time, obtaining consent to try on the coat, understanding the movement and asking a careful question can all be genuine. They can also supply an especially convincing record that the shop has dealt with the problem. Sincerity and tightening control can be products of the same work. The institution can borrow a capacity it never taught. What is taken from the practice is the wearer's effective ability to reopen the task, even as the opportunity to speak is extended.

The supervisor has an independent history. She once secured funding for fittings because ill-fitting coats really did interfere with work. This month she manages a fixed collection contract and has no authority to pay overtime. She may believe most chalk marks are addressed. Inés knows this garment, the lost ten minutes, and her own shoulder. She may take the coat to another worker to alter at lunch without ever telling Nadia. That worker may then lose a break. Father remembers receiving complaints he chose not to pass on because the fares exceeded the fee. Nadia might sincerely judge Mother for never listening to customers when some customers never reached her mother at all. An apparently psychic impermeability can have been produced by the distribution of travel, speech, money and hours. Change who physically carries the garment home and two previously separate lives may become directly related. A new interpretation does not restore a missed break.

When Inés independently comes back with a split seam on Thursday, Nadia has a different next occasion. She can conceal the damage from the supervisor and repair it at home, a genuine benefit that reproduces her mother's unpaid night work. She can direct Inés to a process with no available appointment, reproducing injury in the language of fair access. She can ask whether Inés wants her to bring the coat to the person who controls the order. In the selected branch Inés refuses her name being used but permits the coat to be shown. Nadia presents it without identifying the wearer. The supervisor obtains two paid alteration hours from the buyer. Two coats actually get fixed. Inés's is one of them. A third worker, who could not get to Thursday collection, loses the chance. She has good material grounds for distrusting the reform. Nadia can learn a new way to make another person's difficulty count without disclosing their identity. She can also learn that the shop sustains its promises and become more loyal. Both lessons have evidence. Neither establishes that everyone can now get a repair.

R17's treatment of institutional recuperation makes this last branch indispensable. A real concession can simultaneously change a wearer's bodily life, deepen a fitter's identification, and secure continued cooperation at lower cost to the buyer than revising the contract. Remove the paid hours but keep the praise and the worker's shoulder remains restricted. Keep the hours but require a name and the person may decline. Give all workers enforceable paid returns and material control changes even if Nadia continues to dread every complaint. Keep the rule but let nobody return because the coats fit: zero complaints can now be an honest consequence of good work. An archival comparison of complaint totals alone cannot distinguish the four histories. [R17, lines 415–427 and 572–584](../SEAM/30_THREE_AGENT_MULCH_20261119_R1/16_R17_RECONSTITUTED_HOME_CANDIDATE_R1.md#L415-L427)

The omitted worker matters for a second reason. Suppose, in a different continuation, that several workers start keeping their own list of faults at the change of shift. A mark beside a name means that a coat is still due back. At first this is a memory aid. When they agree that the buyer cannot collect the batch until another worker has checked the outstanding entries, the marks acquire a new use. They can organize a joint withholding of the handover. The coat that Nadia was powerless to reopen by herself can now be left on the rail overnight, if the people responsible for dispatch actually cooperate and can bear the cost. The buyer may fund a second fitting precisely to recover punctual delivery. The material source of that concession would differ from a supervisor's discretionary gift even if the same two workers went home with the same two altered coats. If management can readily collect without their participation or replace the people on dispatch, the marks remain an excellent list and a weak bargaining instrument.

This changes the meaning of recording a defect for the workers too. What had once exposed a complainant can become a way to protect a colleague nobody knows well. Or the list can expose a worker who needed anonymity more than a better coat. Someone who declines to sign may understand that danger more accurately than the most enthusiastic organizer. Solidarity in this example is an acquired capacity to coordinate the next shift's acts around another person's unmet need. It can outlast personal sympathy. It costs time, depends on a material point of cooperation, and can fail through fear, exclusion or ordinary disagreement. Nadia might learn to trust a collective procedure enough to go home on time while continuing to fear criticism face to face. The relation among workers can acquire a power over settlement that no single worker acquires as a feature of her personality.

One December evening a friend asks Nadia to fix a loose cuff. There is no supervisor and no deadline. She leaves a thread loose, asks whether the friend wants to try the coat, and at the friend's refusal hands it back. Her earlier question can now function as an offer whose failure to elicit a complaint she can tolerate. Alternatively she works well into the night, convinced that affection obliges her to obtain an answer. In a third life the Friday fitting was a single interruption in a long history of making hems. Inés never came back, the mother continued correcting the garments, Nadia learned a dependable operation and remained largely unconcerned with fit. She and her friend talk about a film while she hems the cuff. Time has passed, clothes have been made, and bodies have tired. With respect to acquiring a method of inquiry there was genuine nonchange. If we knew only that the case file was blank, that last assertion would exceed the evidence.

### A tune that belongs to neither job

There is another way to test the reach of the argument. At seventeen, years before she signs a fitting sheet, Nadia sometimes stays in the kitchen after the others leave. A loose handle strikes a pan twice when she sets it down. One night she likes the interval between the strokes enough to make it again. She hums across the gap. After a week she can withhold the second stroke and hear where it ought to fall. That moment of silence now has a perceptible shape which an untouched kitchen pan never had for her before. It can disappoint or delight her. She has made a means of directing her own listening. Nobody needs to correct the sound and no one has to profit by it.

Her cousin visits and answers in the gap with a tap of his own. He was waiting for his tea and had no idea she had been working on a tune. When they try it twice more, her pause changes. She can leave it long enough for him to come in, or shorten it to trip him up in play. The same interval now lets two people offer and withhold a turn. Later she invents a second voice alone. Her cousin, who dislikes singing, learns a way of making her laugh. No shared inner composition is necessary. In the contrary history he drums over her phrase, and she waits until he has gone to resume. If he takes the pan away, she may retain an imagined interval but lose the resonance she wanted. If after months of silence she can no longer evoke the gap at all, there can have been an acquired organization which has genuinely ceased to function. The past occurrence alone cannot keep it active.

Now suppose a nephew copies the two taps from the cousin years afterwards. Children make it a rule of a skipping game that the second tap permits the next person to enter. One child is hurt by an early entry, they change the rule, and it survives although no one knows its maker. A practical object can persist by changing hands and purposes while its first maker loses interest. The distinction between an invitation and an order has to be acquired anew by a child who joins the game. None of this requires the sewing apprenticeship or explains why Nadia loved precisely that sound. Kitchen work supplied a resonant object and an unclaimed interval of time. Her own absorption made something of them which the division of household work could never uniquely specify. Conversely, an arrangement that always sent her to bed immediately after washing up could have closed this particular route without demonstrating anything about her musical powers. The argument about the sleeve survives this counterhistory only in its wider form. What unifies the child's practical, sensuous and social lives is the production and reuse of the conditions under which she and others can do something further, including doing something for its own sake.

### Why the parts belong to one life

The sleeve can come back to a person because activity outlasts the moment of making it. A join holds or parts. A wage buys a meal elsewhere. A rule continues to determine who can try again. Someone who never saw the maker bears a cost. A historically formed person also outlasts the moment. Nadia tomorrow can work from a distinction she could not make yesterday, including a mistaken distinction or a practice whose source she cannot recall. These two continuities are independently breakable. A school can lose its premises without its pupils losing their literacy. An old machine can remain usable long after the last person who knows how to use it has died. What connects them in a life is the actual reproduction and alteration of the means of acting: people acquiring capacities while remaking the world of practice that will solicit those capacities, their own and others', on unequal terms.

Several centers of explanation follow from those histories. **Embodied participation** explains how a difference that once demanded a joint activity can become perceptible or practicable by Nadia in another activity. It also explains the reverse limit, a coordination she loses when her partner leaves. **The social distribution of completion, cost and correction** explains which of the effects of her activity become available to learn from, who has to carry the rest, and how materially successful practice can stabilize a confident error. **The acquired organization of concern** explains why the same perceptible defect can invite inquiry, shame, a hurried signature, unsolicited rescue, private pleasure in the technical challenge, or nothing more than a competent repair. This third center changes the first two. A wearer trusted with a genuine decision can alter the design of the next garment. A fitter who can remain present through criticism can help materialize a right to return. A fitter who cannot leave well enough alone can make the same right expensive to use.

These centers were arrived at through different failures. The finished garment defeats individual skill as a sufficient center. The good seam that hurts defeats internal satisfaction. The well-understood complaint with no time to act defeats communication as a sufficient center. The complete lesson that closes exploration defeats physical access to the correction as a sufficient center. The wearer who can now lift a crate but still dreads the supervisor defeats institutional reform as a complete account of liberation. A musician absorbed in a sound only she can hear in imagination defeats a theory that requires an audience's correction to originate anything. Her capacity to sustain and vary the imagined interval may have a social and bodily history. The pleasure immediately organizing it can be her own. Material unity sometimes consists precisely in the provision of an hour, an empty room and an instrument through which a solitary activity can cease to answer to others for a time.

An apparent nonrelation deserves the same precision. Nadia learns to recognize a bad armhole and learns from a funeral how to sit with another person's silence. In one history neither achievement alters the other in the workshop. Different settings, interlocutors and purposes have kept them apart. The shared fact that one body spent particular hours in both settings explains how that differentiation was possible and how practice in one could compete for time with the other. It establishes no direct transfer. Years later a bereaved friend brings a coat. Nadia, who once would have insisted on a fitting, sits still while the friend holds it. If her earlier history with grief is actually recruited there, an old form of being with someone has changed what professional helpfulness can mean. If the shop is closed and she has no tools, the same sitting still may have a simpler cause. Unity obliges us to locate the real crossing or the conditions of separation. It gives us no license to invent a crossing whenever two histories belong to one body.

Finally the person's own changing account belongs among the causes. At thirty Nadia finds a box of garments Mother never finished and recognizes her own early stitches beneath the repairs. Discovering this might correct her claim of early mastery. It cannot prove why Mother stayed up, or erase the pride she actually felt at eleven. She may spend a year reading every act of maternal help as interference and miss a friend's genuine generosity. A different discovery may lead her to teach a child openly how to undo good-looking stitches. Someone can acquire a false way of interpreting future evidence as surely as an accurate one. A counterexample becomes psychologically effective only when it can enter a practice in which a different response is possible. Even then, sometimes it is the critic who is wrong.

The new claim has a deliberate limit. Where the reliability of an activity depends on another person quietly repairing its failures, the *distribution of who gets a consequential difference back in usable form* can explain together the growth of skill, the growth of confident misrecognition, and the continued social need for invisible work. Alter that distribution, and both the skill and what counts as responsible care can acquire a different object. What a returned answer means also matters. If it has acquired the authority of a finished lesson, physical access can increase even as inquiry narrows. Demonstrate that the child already made the relevant distinctions under equal conditions, that an external change alone did the work, or that the wearer has no actual influence despite an apparent right to return, and the proposed acquisition fails in that case. The broader unity survives because these failures reveal whose activity really sustained the life. Nadia need never mend another sleeve for her life to have this form of historical depth.
