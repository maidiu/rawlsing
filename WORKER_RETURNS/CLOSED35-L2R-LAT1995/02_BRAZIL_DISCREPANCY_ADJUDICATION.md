## New archival evidence

### 1. Historical distribution continuity

JDS/ASEP states that its Latinobarómetro archive held annual individual microdata SAV files and official questionnaire/technical documentation. Brazil 1995 is represented as a selectable sample.

This independently confirms that 1995 existed as a distributed country×wave component inside the broader archive.

It does not say `N=1080` or `N=1200` is the operative distributed Brazil count.

### 2. Later replacement of historical files

A concrete version-history lead exists.

JDS/ASEP later announced:
- updates to 1995–2005 files to include Region, City, and Political Party variables fully labelled;
- a later review/harmonization of region/city/political-party codes;
- replacement of historical files on the Latinobarómetro website.

Sources:
- https://www.jdsurvey.net/jds/jdsurveyActualidad.jsp?Idioma=E&NOID=124&SeccionTexto=0401
- https://www.jdsurvey.com/jds/jdsurvey.jsp

This proves that "1995 file" has had a distribution/history layer beyond the original field period.

But the same notice **bounds the documented reason for replacement**. It names coding/documentation/harmonization for region, city, and political party. It does not mention:
- Brazil row additions/removals;
- sample-size corrections;
- interview-number repair;
- weighting construction;
- weighting normalization;
- a `1080→1200` or `1200→1080` version transition.

Therefore:

`HISTORICAL_FILE_REPLACEMENT_PROVED != BRAZIL_120_ROW_VERSION_PROVENANCE_PROVED`

### 3. Contemporary independent total

El País reported on 29 August 1995 that the newly released eight-country Latinobarómetro comprised `9,077` interviews conducted in May-June.

Source:
https://elpais.com/diario/1995/08/29/internacional/809647204_850215.html

This is secondary evidence, but contemporaneous. It corroborates the 9,077 total found in the inherited producer report.

It does not allocate the total among countries independently and therefore does not identify the 120 Brazil cases.

### 4. Later trend-report bases

An archival mirror of the producer-branded 2005 report contains:
- `n=9,070` for at least one 1995–2005 trend;
- `N=9,069` for another 1995 comparison.

Source:
https://es.scribd.com/document/1005735910/F00008560-Informe-Latinobarometro-2005

These are explicitly attached to specific charts/questions. They cannot be promoted into a producer correction of the 1995 wave size.

Their relevance is limited but useful: a printed `n` in a later analytical product may be a question/valid-response base, not a release-row count. Hence no later chart base can explain the Brazil delta without a direct producer statement.
