## Scientific decision

The baseline is a collection of separate source-native joint distributions, with native numerator/denominator counts and optional reviewed categorical recoding. It has no common sample size and no pooled estimator. Complementary constructs can remain separate channels. They cannot enter the shared-joint candidate unless the **entire ordered construct topology**, category meanings and risk-set applicability have been qualified. Afrobarometer citizenship and LiTS satisfaction receive no invented shared estimand.

The one candidate is direct target-stratum standardization of intact source joint distributions. For joint category vector `j`, target stratum `z`, target share `t(z)`, source table `p_s(j|z)` and declared positive source contribution weight `a_s`:

`q(j) = sum_z t(z) * sum_{s admissible at z} [a_s / sum_{r admissible at z} a_r] * p_s(j|z)`.

Contribution coefficients and target/native stratum share ratios are returned at the exact source/stratum grain. Weights express a declared synthesis policy; they are not inferred source reliability, inverse-variance precision weights or respondent sample weights. Survey weights remain inside each source table. Changing either kind of weight has a tested semantic effect. No source-local sample sizes are added into a fictional target sample.

The choice is justified for a categorical sociological joint when differences in population composition are known, the joint law is reasonably invariant conditional on the chosen strata, instruments/routing/measurement are comparable, and positive target support exists. Generalization literature motivates explicit target definition, conditional comparability and positivity for standardization; our descriptive joint law is a separately stated modeling assumption, with no causal-effect claim. See [Lesko et al., Generalizing study results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5466356/).

No shrinkage, latent construct fit, temporal interpolation or additional candidate was attempted. Source-specific joint dependence survives transport; the new target mixture remains **INFERRED**. Cross-source respondents are never joined. The helper for complementary binary channels returns Fréchet bounds plus an explicitly hypothetical independence scenario, without calling any cross-channel joint measured.

Invented A and B both measure material security × employment position, in a region/year-specific common adult work risk set. A uses named categories and a 90/10 majority/minority sample; B uses numeric categories and a 20/80 sample. Target composition is 80/20. Both sources are semantically relevant in this controlled experiment; neither is an actual survey family.

| Leave B out; transfer A to B composition | A 0.44 | 0.18 |

The zero error is an idealized deterministic synthetic check, not out-of-sample empirical calibration. Held-out B never enters the fitting tables; its declared synthetic stratum shares define the transfer target, and its cells are used for validation. Majority/minority subgroup TV remains 0.10/0.20: adjusting composition does not repair stratum-law disagreement. The adverse case is worse than baseline B. These results justify availability of an explicit optional candidate, **no real-source baseline replacement**.

The consumer's modeled `P(formal work | material security = secure)` changes from `29/34` with A only to `18/23` with equal A/B contributions and `112/137` with A weighted three times B. Receipts include model denominator, input probabilities read, value hash and result hash. Native B stays unchanged when A's survey weights change. ## Support, uncertainty and limitations

Every source keeps fieldwork period, boundary/population/unit identity, item/construct/scale/polarity/instrument/response-meaning refs, design/weight/routing/missingness refs, measured joint/coadministration/frontier refs, sample lineage and raw support. Wrong scope/time, unreviewed or changed mappings, overlapping/duplicate sample evidence, modeled marginals masquerading as measured joints and suppressed/nonexhaustive tables cannot silently qualify. Reviewed mapping preimage hashes catch subsequent changes; a hash cannot establish that an initially reviewed interpretation is scientifically correct.

Rare cells are never pruned. A minimum support policy can exclude a source/stratum while preserving its native table and listing the exclusion. Union support can use a remaining qualified source, with its local contribution weight disclosed. If an entire target stratum is missing, output is a covered **subprobability**, an uncovered mass and conservative bounds; no complete target point distribution or conditional service result is issued. Sampling zeros are distinct from unknown/unmapped categories, which return unavailable.

Design-based cell variances must be supplied externally for the mapped joint probabilities. Under disjoint sources/strata and fixed target shares, cell variance is `sum_z,s [t(z)*alpha_s(z)]^2 * Var[p_s(j|z)]`. Missing design variance remains unknown. This implementation supplies diagonal marginal variance only: it cannot estimate conditional uncertainty or covariance, validate a clustered design, repair nonresponse/routing selection, or derive uncertainty in estimated population shares. The synthetic fixture uses an explicitly synthetic fixed-stratum multinomial plug-in variance; this is not a general survey variance estimator. A fourfold input design-variance inflation gives fourfold output variance.

Source disagreement bounds and separate mapping, transport and target-share bias radii remain sensitivity envelopes, not confidence intervals. The selected radii are illustrative and uncalibrated; unsupported true laws can lie outside them. Reference population/risk-set mismatch, omitted transport variables, linguistic differences, scale noninvariance and missing historical coverage can invalidate the candidate. Conditional queries on the inferred joint retain an explicit unknown conditional uncertainty state. No ordinal midpoint is treated as an interval metric. No institution, causal relationship or actor psychology is inferred from an attitude distribution.
