## F7: comparable observations, designs and clocks

F43 invokes the inherited V553 temporal identity, harmonization, multi-wave
authorization, gap and delta machinery through exact EE1. Its synthetic probe
delta remains a probe, never measured source history. There is no claimed
original observed pair or Ghana timeline. Missing years remain gaps rather than

The inherited EE1 equality gate accepts a pair with the same missing identity
fields; its clock gate exempts a `rev1_selected` rule label from the future-rule
publication comparison. F43's candidate pre-guard requires nonempty population,
estimand, response, wording and design identity and valid explicit publication
dates before delegating to the actual native identity/population owners. The
revision label does not waive the publication date. The original EE1 bytes remain
unchanged. These are author-demonstrated candidate corrections, not independent
adjudication or a grant to produce measured comparisons.

Exact design equality is only one defensible branch. Different survey designs
need not imply eternal noncomparability: competent harmonization, population and
design owners can establish compatible estimands and design-based uncertainty.
For qualified repeated-cross-section estimates, a difference is meaningful
without linking respondents. Supplied design variances give
`Var(after-before)=Va+Vb-2Cov`. Independence must be externally justified; it
cannot follow from distinct survey names. Without covariance, F43 reports a
standard-error range `[abs(sqrt(Va)-sqrt(Vb)),sqrt(Va)+sqrt(Vb)]`, not a fabricated
standard error. Missing design variances leave sampling uncertainty unknown.
The optional normal approximation requires independent design qualification and
does not address nonresponse, mode, wording, coverage or temporal identity bias.
Year-only fixture anchors do not qualify real within-year historical cutoffs.

The general distinction between sampling error and other survey error and the
need to account for error when comparing estimates agree with the
[U.S. Census Bureau ACS guidance, chapter 7](https://www.census.gov/content/dam/Census/library/publications/2018/acs/acs_general_handbook_2018_ch07.pdf).
That guidance is methodological context, not authority for any original35 source
or a claim that ACS formulas automatically apply to its complex survey designs.
The missing-data identification framing is consistent with
[Horowitz and Manski, identification with incomplete data](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2005.02.007);
the actual F43 formulas and bounds above have their own explicit derivation and
constructive tests.
