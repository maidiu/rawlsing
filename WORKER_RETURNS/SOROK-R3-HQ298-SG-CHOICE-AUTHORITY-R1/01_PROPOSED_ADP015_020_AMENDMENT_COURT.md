## 3. Construction constraint versus posterior inference — minimum theorem

A constructive search solves an existential predicate:

`exists h in H: FORWARD_V3_VALID(h, W, owners) && SATISFIES(h, necessary_endpoint)`.

One (or multiple) matching histories establishes only a witness of formal admissibility. No likelihood over histories follows.

A posterior requires `P(h|E) proportional P(E|h)P(h)`, including a normalized measure on complete histories, endpoint observation model, proposal inclusion/selection probabilities, rejection/attrition correction and independent adequacy/calibration. None follows from the presence of a mother checkpoint, finite candidate set, ADP-020 uniform sampler, PRE-V3 marginals or a text generator. If history A and B both fit E, their posterior balance is undetermined without those further ingredients.

Finite counterexample (mathematical, **not** an observed or executed person):
- Endpoint E is satisfied by both `h_A` and `h_B` (feasibility Boolean=TRUE for both). One prior law may assign `P(h_A)=9/10`, another `P(h_A)=1/10`; if `P(E|h_A)=P(E|h_B)=1`, posteriors disagree (9/10 versus 1/10) despite *identical feasible histories and endpoint*. Thus feasibility cannot uniquely identify `P(h|E)`.
- Separately, a **hypothetical authorized synthetic policy** `q(A)=q(B)=1/2` with unequal completion chance `a(A)=1`, `a(B)=1/4` yields surviving winners `P(A|successful)= (1/2)/(1/2+1/8)=4/5`, `P(B|successful)=1/5`, **not** 1/2–1/2. Silent retry-until-success rewrites the effective draw law. These are arithmetic illustrations, not real father's demographic frequencies, calibrated event likelihoods or new ADP weight grants.
