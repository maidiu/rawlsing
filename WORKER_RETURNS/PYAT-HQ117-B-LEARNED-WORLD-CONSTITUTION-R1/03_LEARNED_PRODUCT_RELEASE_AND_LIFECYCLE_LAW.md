## 8. Supersession / recertification / correction / withdrawal

Any generic learned-product lifecycle owner must implement P2's typed distinctions without rewriting history.

At minimum:

### Recertification
Same semantic product/claim may receive a new governing authority/evidence instance without pretending the semantic proposition changed.

### Correction
A constitutive semantic change creates a new semantic product identity and revalidates affected descendants.

### Scope change
A scope change is explicit and affects only the governed scope.

### Supersession
Supersession is explicit and nondestructive. Predecessor identity, authorities, replay and historical citations survive.

### Withdrawal
Withdrawal ends current serving/use at the governed scope; it does not erase history.

### Completeness-only supersession
May change what absence/completeness claims are lawful without rewriting unchanged positive product claims.

### Ontology expansion
May add product/relation families without silently reinterpreting old families.

No transition is inferred from Git history, timestamps or version ordering.

## 9. Invalidation and rebuild

Current authority-maintenance mechanics plus C08's durable theorem support:

`CHANGED_EXACT_DETERMINANT -> DIRECT_DEPENDENTS + TRANSITIVE_EXACT_DEPENDENCY_CLOSURE`.

For learned products, this remains a required lifecycle behavior, but generic learned-product determinant registration is not yet proved current.

A source change should not invalidate unrelated products.

A validation-rule change invalidates products that depend on that validation rule, not raw empirical facts.

A cache loss invalidates no semantic product when the cache is performance-only.

A changed semantic determinant may require a new learned-product identity.

Historical replay always pins historical determinants.
